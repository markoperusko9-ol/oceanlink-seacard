Rolling period and GM calculator

Estimate the rolling period from GM, or GM from a timed roll, with the Intact Stability Code formula. Add the waves to compare the encounter period with the rolling period.

Waves (optional)

How it works

A stiff ship with a large GM snaps back quickly and has a short rolling period. A tender ship rolls slowly. The 2008 Intact Stability Code gives this approximate formula in its weather criterion:

T = 2 × C × B ÷ √GM
C = 0.373 + 0.023 × (B ÷ d) − 0.043 × (Lwl ÷ 100)

T is the rolling period in seconds for one complete roll, B the moulded beam, d the mean draught, Lwl the waterline length and GM the metacentric height corrected for free surface, all in metres. Turned round, it gives GM from a roll you have timed: GM = (2 × C × B ÷ T)².

For waves, MSC.1/Circ.1228 gives the wave length and the period at which the ship meets the waves:

Wave length (m) = 1.56 × TW²
Encounter period TE = 3 × TW² ÷ (3 × TW + V × cos α)

TW is the wave period in seconds, V the speed in knots and α the angle between the keel and the wave direction, with 0° a head sea. The circular tells the master to avoid an encounter period close to the rolling period (synchronous rolling) and, in head or following seas, close to the rolling period or half of it (parametric rolling).

Worked example

Beam 32.2 m, draught 12.0 m, waterline length 220 m. C = 0.373 + 0.023 × 2.683 − 0.043 × 2.2 = 0.340. With a GM of 2.50 m the rolling period is 2 × 0.340 × 32.2 ÷ 1.581 = 13.9 s. Steaming at 15 knots into a head sea of 10 s period, the encounter period is 300 ÷ 45 = 6.7 s, which is 0.48 of the rolling period: close to half, the parametric rolling condition.

How this tool was checked

The rolling period formula and its coefficient were checked against the text of the 2008 IS Code as reproduced in the UK MCA instructions to surveyors, and the encounter period against MSC.1/Circ.1228. The encounter period was also derived from first principles for 300 cases: in head and beam seas the IMO formula agrees within 1.1%.

Limits

  • The formula is an approximation. The study behind it found predictions within about 7.5% of measured rolling periods, so a GM worked back from a timed roll can be 15% out. It does not replace the loading computer.
  • The best rolling period is the one you time yourself in calm water, as the circular recommends: several complete rolls, averaged.
  • The encounter formula rounds the wave speed to 3 × TW knots. In following seas, when the ship's speed approaches the wave speed, it becomes very sensitive and should be read as a warning, not a number.
  • Being outside the 10% bands shown here does not mean heavy rolling cannot happen. Real seas contain many periods at once.

FAQ

What is the formula for the rolling period of a ship?

The 2008 Intact Stability Code gives T = 2 x C x B divided by the square root of GM, with C = 0.373 + 0.023 x (B/d) - 0.043 x (Lwl/100). T is in seconds and the dimensions and GM are in metres.

How do you find GM from the rolling period?

Time several complete rolls in calm water and take the average. Then GM equals the square of 2 x C x B divided by the rolling period. It is an estimate, within roughly 15 percent.

What is synchronous rolling?

Synchronous rolling happens when the period at which the ship meets the waves is close to her natural rolling period, so each wave adds to the roll. Altering course or speed changes the encounter period and breaks the match.
