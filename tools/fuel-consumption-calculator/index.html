Fuel consumption and ROB calculator

Turn a consumption figure into tonnes per day, see how many days and miles the fuel on board will last, and check what will be left on arrival.

Consumption

Fuel on board and voyage

How it works

Daily consumption = fuel consumed ÷ hours × 24
Days of fuel = ROB ÷ daily consumption
Range = days of fuel × 24 × speed
Fuel for the voyage = distance ÷ speed ÷ 24 × daily consumption

The cube law estimate says that, for the same ship in the same condition, daily consumption changes roughly with the cube of the speed:

New consumption ≈ consumption × (new speed ÷ speed)³

Worked example

30 t burned in 24 hours with 600 t on board at 14 kn. The fuel lasts 600 ÷ 30 = 20 days, a range of 20 × 24 × 14 = 6,720 nm. A 3,000 nm passage takes 3,000 ÷ 14 = 214.3 hours, 8 days 22 hours, and burns 267.9 t, leaving 332.1 t on arrival. At 12 kn the cube law gives about 30 × (12 ÷ 14)³ = 18.9 t/day.

How this tool was checked

Every figure is tested against the worked example above, worked by hand.

Limits

  • No reserve is taken off. ROB includes fuel you cannot pump, and your company sets the safety margin to keep on arrival.
  • Enter the main engine consumption or the total, but be consistent. Generators and boilers burn fuel in port as well.
  • The cube law is a rough guide for small speed changes. Weather, draught, hull condition and engine load all move the real figure.

FAQ

How do I calculate a ship's daily fuel consumption?

Divide the fuel consumed by the number of hours it covers and multiply by 24. For example 14.5 tonnes in 12 hours is 29 tonnes a day.

How much fuel does slow steaming save?

As a rough guide, daily consumption follows the cube of the speed. Dropping from 14 to 12 knots cuts daily consumption by about 37 percent, although the voyage takes longer, so the saving per mile is smaller.
