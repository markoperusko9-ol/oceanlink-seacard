How it works

This is the manual radar plot done with numbers. Two timed positions of the target, O and A, give its relative track. Extend that line past your own ship: the shortest distance from the centre to the line is the CPA, and the time it takes the target to run from A to that point at its relative speed is the TCPA.

To find what the target is really doing, lay off your own course reversed from O for the distance you ran in the plot interval. That gives W. The line from W to A is the target's true course and speed. Aspect is the relative bearing of your own ship as seen from the target: Red if you see her port side, Green if you see her starboard side.

Worked example

Own ship 000°T at 12 kn. At 1200 the target bears 037°T at 10.0 nm. At 1212 it bears 045°T at 5.7 nm. The relative track runs about 207° at 22 kn. CPA is about 1.8 nm on a bearing of 117°T at about 1227. The target is steering about 232°T at 12.6 kn.

How this tool was checked

The calculation was run on 1,500 simulated encounters with random courses, speeds and starting positions. The CPA and TCPA had to match a brute-force search of the range minute by minute, and the target's true course and speed had to match what the simulation started with. It also reproduces the worked example in the Ocean Link guide.

Limits

  • The answer assumes both ships hold course and speed. Two plots cannot show that the target is altering. Keep plotting.
  • Small errors in bearing matter at long range. A longer plot interval gives a steadier result.
  • CPA and TCPA tell you whether a close-quarters situation is developing. They do not tell you who gives way. That comes from the Rules.

FAQ

What do CPA and TCPA mean?

CPA is the closest point of approach: the shortest distance the target will pass from your ship if neither alters course or speed. TCPA is the time remaining until that moment.

Do I need true or relative bearings?

This calculator uses true bearings. If your radar is head-up and you read relative bearings, add your heading to each bearing first.

Does CPA depend on my own speed input?

No. CPA and TCPA come only from the two plots and the time between them. Your own course and speed are needed to turn the relative track into the target's true course, speed and aspect.
