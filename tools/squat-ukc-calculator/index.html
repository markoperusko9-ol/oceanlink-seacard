How it works

Squat is the extra sinkage and trim of a ship moving through shallow water. The tool uses the quick estimates from Dr Barrass that are taught for bridge planning and exams, with V in knots through the water:

Open shallow water: squat (m) = Cb × V² ÷ 100
Confined channel: squat (m) = 2 × Cb × V² ÷ 100

Then the clearance is built up in the usual order:

Depth of water = charted depth + height of tide
Static UKC = depth of water − deepest static draught
Net UKC = static UKC − squat − other allowances

Put everything your company UKC policy asks for into Other allowances: heel in the turns, wave response, chart and survey accuracy, and the effect of high pressure on the tide.

Worked example

Charted depth 12.40 m, tide 2.10 m, draught 11.20 m, Cb 0.80, 8 kn in a confined channel. Depth of water 14.50 m, static UKC 3.30 m. Squat = 2 × 0.80 × 8² ÷ 100 = 1.02 m. Other allowances are 0.28 m heel, 1.25 m chart accuracy and 0.15 m pressure, 1.68 m in all. Net UKC = 3.30 − 1.02 − 1.68 = 0.60 m. Slowing to 6 kn cuts the squat to 0.58 m.

How this tool was checked

The squat and clearance figures reproduce every worked example in the Ocean Link squat and UKC guides, including the laden bulk carrier at 10 and 7 knots and the container ship at 16 knots.

Limits

  • The Barrass formulas are empirical estimates, not measurements. They are intended to lean to the safe side, and they are meant for a depth to draught ratio of roughly 1.1 to 1.4.
  • Squat depends on speed through the water, not over the ground. A following current hides a high water speed.
  • Your company SMS and the port decide the method and the minimum UKC. Ports and pilots often use more detailed methods.

FAQ

How do you calculate ship squat quickly?

The common bridge estimate is the Barrass approximation. Maximum squat in metres equals Cb × V² ÷ 100 in open shallow water and twice that in a confined channel, with V in knots through the water.

What is the fastest way to gain under keel clearance?

Slow down. Squat grows with the square of the speed, so going from 8 to 6 knots cuts it by almost half. Waiting for more tide is the other lever.
