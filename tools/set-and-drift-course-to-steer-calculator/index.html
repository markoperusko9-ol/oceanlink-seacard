Set and drift and course to steer calculator

Solve the current triangle three ways: the course to steer to hold a track, the set and drift you actually experienced, or the course and speed you will make good.

Time between the two fixes

How it works

All three modes solve the same vector triangle: what you do through the water, plus what the water does, equals what you do over the ground. Every side must cover the same time, so the tool works in one-hour vectors.

Set is the direction the stream flows towards, in degrees true. Rate is its speed in knots. Drift is the distance it carried you.

Course to steer. The part of the stream that pushes you across the track has to be cancelled by pointing the ship the other way:

sin (allowance) = rate × sin (set − track) ÷ speed through water

Set and drift. Lay off what you steered from the first fix to get the DR. The line from the DR to the second fix is the set and drift. Always measure from DR to fix, never the other way.

Worked example

You want to make good 090°T at 10 kn through the water. The stream sets 000°T at 2 kn, straight across the track. sin α = 2 ÷ 10 = 0.2, so α = 11.5°. Steer 101.5°T. Speed made good = 10 × cos 11.5° = 9.8 kn.

How this tool was checked

The three modes were run against each other on 2,000 random cases: the course to steer was fed back into the made-good calculation and had to return the intended track, and the set and rate recovered from the result had to match the stream that went in. They also reproduce both worked examples in the Ocean Link guide.

Limits

  • Leeway is not included. Apply it separately, into the wind.
  • The stream is taken as constant over the leg. For a long leg, work it hour by hour from the tidal stream atlas or the chart diamonds.

FAQ

What is the difference between set, drift and rate?

Set is the direction the stream or current flows towards, in degrees true. Rate is its speed in knots. Drift is the distance it moves you in a given time.

Why can the calculator not find a course to steer?

If the part of the stream across your track is stronger than your speed through the water, no heading can hold the track. The same happens if the stream against you is stronger than your speed along the track.
