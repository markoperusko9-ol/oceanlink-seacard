Propeller slip calculator

Work out apparent slip for the noon report from the propeller pitch, the revolutions and the distance the ship actually ran.

Steaming time (h, min)

How it works

If the propeller turned in something solid it would move the ship forward by one pitch every revolution. That theoretical run is the engine distance. Water gives way, so the ship covers less. The shortfall, as a percentage of the engine distance, is the slip.

Engine distance (nm) = pitch (m) × revolutions ÷ 1,852
Slip (%) = (engine distance − observed distance) ÷ engine distance × 100

Revolutions are average RPM × 60 × hours, or the difference in the revolution counter. Take the steaming time actually run between the two readings, which is 23 or 25 hours on a day the clocks were changed.

Worked example

Pitch 6.0 m, 95 RPM for 24 hours, observed distance 420 nm. Revolutions = 95 × 60 × 24 = 136,800. Engine distance = 6.0 × 136,800 ÷ 1,852 = 443.2 nm. Slip = (443.2 − 420) ÷ 443.2 × 100 = 5.23%.

How this tool was checked

The formulas are tested against the worked example above, worked by hand, and against a negative-slip case.

Limits

  • This is apparent slip. It includes the effect of current, wind, sea, hull fouling and draught, not just the propeller.
  • Use the pitch from the ship's propeller drawing. For a controllable pitch propeller, use the pitch at the setting actually in use.

FAQ

What is a normal propeller slip?

It depends on the ship, her loading and the weather, so compare with your own ship's record. A rising trend at the same draught and weather points to hull or propeller fouling.

Why is my slip negative?

The ship covered more distance than the propeller's theoretical advance. A following current, sea or strong wind astern does this. Check the observed distance and the steaming time as well.
