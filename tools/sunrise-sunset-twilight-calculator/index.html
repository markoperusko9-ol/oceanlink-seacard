Sunrise, sunset and twilight calculator

Enter the date and your position to get sunrise, sunset, both twilights and the time of meridian passage in ship's time, for star sights, the noon sight and the log.

How it works

The tool computes the Sun's position from the date and time, then finds the moments when its altitude crosses the limits the Nautical Almanac uses:

EventAltitude of the Sun's centre
Sunrise and sunset−0°50′ (upper limb on the horizon, with 34′ of refraction and 16′ semi-diameter)
Civil twilight−6°
Nautical twilight−12°

Meridian passage is the moment the Sun is due north or south of you, when its local hour angle is zero. The altitude shown for it is the true altitude of the Sun's centre: 90° minus the difference between your latitude and the declination.

Morning stars are taken between the start of nautical twilight and the start of civil twilight, when both the stars and the horizon are visible. Evening stars are taken between the end of civil twilight and the end of nautical twilight.

Worked example

On 1 September 1994 in 36°14′N 069°26′W, keeping UTC−5, the tool gives sunset at 18:06 ship's time. That is 23:06 UTC, or 18:28 local mean time at that longitude. A US Navy navigation course works the same case from the Nautical Almanac and gets a local mean time of 1827.

How this tool was checked

Sunrise, sunset, both twilights and meridian passage were computed for 275 days between 2026 and 2039 with an independent astronomical library (astropy) and compared with the tool: 1,604 events. Up to 62° of latitude the largest difference was 3 seconds for sunrise and sunset, 10 seconds for civil twilight, 4 seconds for nautical twilight and 2 seconds for meridian passage. Two worked examples taken from the Nautical Almanac in published navigation courses agree within one minute.

Limits

  • Times are for an observer at sea level. From a bridge 20 to 30 m up, sunrise is seen a minute or so earlier and sunset later, more in high latitudes.
  • Refraction near the horizon varies with the weather. A minute or two of difference from what you observe is normal.
  • Enter the position where the ship will be at the event, not where she is now. Every 15′ of longitude moves the times by one minute.
  • Above the polar circles the Sun may stay up or down all day; the tool then says so. Close to those dates the times are sensitive and should be treated as approximate.

FAQ

What is the difference between civil and nautical twilight?

Civil twilight is the time when the Sun is between the horizon and 6 degrees below it. Nautical twilight is when it is between 6 and 12 degrees below. Star sights are taken between the two, when stars and the horizon can be seen together.

How is the time of sunrise defined in the Nautical Almanac?

Sunrise is when the upper limb of the Sun is on the visible horizon for an observer at sea level. Allowing for refraction and the semi-diameter, the centre of the Sun is then 50 minutes of arc below the horizon.

What is meridian passage?

Meridian passage is the moment the Sun crosses your meridian and reaches its highest altitude, bearing due north or south. It is the time of the noon sight for latitude.
