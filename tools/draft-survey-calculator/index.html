How it works

A draft survey weighs the ship by her displacement. The tool follows the standard steps and shows each one.

1. Mean draughts. Port and starboard are averaged at each of the three positions.

2. Correction to the perpendiculars. The marks are rarely on the perpendiculars. With the apparent trim taken between the forward and aft marks:

Correction = apparent trim × distance of the marks from the perpendicular ÷ length between the marks

The tool treats the keel as a straight line through the forward and aft marks and reads it off at the FP, at midships and at the AP, so the signs take care of themselves. The corrected draughts are carried to 0.1 mm, as on a survey sheet.

3. Mean of means. Also called the quarter mean. It allows for hog or sag:

Mean of means = (forward + aft + 6 × midships) ÷ 8

4. Hydrostatics. You read the displacement, TPC, LCF and the two MTC values from your own tables at this draught.

5. Trim corrections.

First trim correction (t) = trim (cm) × LCF from midships (m) × TPC ÷ LBP
Second trim correction (t) = trim² (m) × 50 × (MTC at +0.5 m − MTC at −0.5 m) ÷ LBP

The first correction is added when the LCF lies toward the deeper end, and subtracted when it lies toward the shallower end. The second correction is always added.

6. Density. The tables are for 1.025 unless they say otherwise:

Displacement in dock water = corrected displacement × dock water density ÷ density of the tables

7. Net weight. Take off the ballast, fresh water, fuel and other deductibles. The cargo is the difference between the net figures of the final and the initial survey.

Worked example

The figures shown when the page opens are the worked example in the UK P&I Club's Carefully to Carry chapter on draught surveys. The mean of means is 5.0357 m, the first trim correction is −102.61 t because the LCF is forward and the ship is trimmed by the stern, the second is +3.03 t, and the displacement in water of 1.0185 is 19,669.26 t.

How this tool was checked

The tool returns every figure of the UK P&I Club worked example, from the perpendicular corrections to the final 19,669.26 t, and the worked example in the Ocean Link draft survey guide. Limits

  • The LCF must be a distance from midships. If your tables give it from the AP, subtract half the LBP first.
  • The ship is taken as upright. A heel correction, needed with a large list, is not included.
  • With a very large trim, use the trimmed hydrostatic tables if the ship has them. The corrections here assume a moderate trim.
  • The answer is only as good as the readings: six draughts read in a swell and one density sample are the usual weak points.

FAQ

What is the formula for the mean of means draught?

Add the forward draught, the aft draught and six times the midship draught, all corrected to the perpendiculars and midships, and divide by eight. It is also called the quarter mean.

When is the first trim correction positive?

It is added when the LCF lies on the same side of midships as the deeper draught, for example LCF aft with the ship trimmed by the stern. It is subtracted when the LCF lies toward the shallower end.

What is the second trim correction in a draft survey?

It allows for the LCF moving as the ship trims. It equals the trim in metres squared, times 50, times the difference in MTC at 0.5 metres above and below the mean draught, divided by the LBP. It is always added.
