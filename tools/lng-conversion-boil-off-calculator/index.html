How it works

Every conversion goes through the mass of the cargo. Three figures from the cargo quality certificate fix the rest:

Mass (t) = volume (m³) × density (kg/m³) ÷ 1000
Energy (MMBtu) = mass × heating value per tonne
Gas volume (Nm³) = mass × gas per tonne

A heating value in MJ/kg is changed to MMBtu per tonne with 1 MMBtu = 1,055.056 MJ. Nm³ is gas at 0°C and Sm³ is gas at 15°C, both at 1 atmosphere; 1 Sm³ = 0.9476 Nm³ (ISO 13443).

The boil-off rate is the liquid that evaporates in a day as a percentage of the liquid volume:

Boil-off (% per day) = (volume at start − volume at end) ÷ volume at start ÷ hours × 24 × 100

Worked example

With the International Gas Union's reference figures of 450 kg/m³, 1,300 Nm³ and 53.38 MMBtu per tonne, 1 m³ of LNG is 0.450 t, 585 Nm³ of gas and 24.02 MMBtu. For boil-off, a ship that gauges 170,000 m³ and 169,660 m³ 48 hours later has lost 340 m³: 170 m³ a day, or 0.100% per day, about 76.5 t a day at 450 kg/m³.

How this tool was checked

With the International Gas Union's assumptions the tool reproduces its published LNG conversion table in every direction, and with 52 MMBtu per tonne it matches the S&P Global factor of 0.01923 t per MMBtu. Each conversion was also run forwards and back through all four units for 300 random cargoes and returned the starting figure.

Limits

  • The prefilled 450 kg/m³, 52 MMBtu per tonne and 1,300 Nm³ per tonne are typical values only. Real cargoes range from about 420 to 470 kg/m³. Use the certificate.
  • This is a quantity conversion. The commercial figure comes from the custody transfer measurement: CTMS volumes before and after, density and heating value from the analysed composition, less the energy of the gas displaced.
  • The boil-off rate here is a percentage of the volume at the first gauging. A builder's guaranteed boil-off rate is quoted against the full cargo capacity under set conditions, so the two are not the same number.
  • Gauge at the same trim and list, or correct both volumes first.

FAQ

How many tonnes is one cubic metre of LNG?

About 0.45 tonnes, because LNG density is typically 420 to 470 kilograms per cubic metre. Multiply the volume by the density from the cargo certificate and divide by 1000.

How many MMBtu are in a tonne of LNG?

Roughly 52 MMBtu per tonne for a typical cargo. The exact figure is the gross heating value on the cargo quality certificate; 54.86 megajoules per kilogram equals 52 MMBtu per tonne.

How is the boil-off rate of an LNG carrier calculated?

Divide the volume of liquid lost between two gaugings by the starting volume and by the time in days, then multiply by 100. Modern LNG carriers boil off roughly 0.08 to 0.15 percent of the cargo per day.
