Rate of turn calculator

Find the rate of turn that holds a planned radius at your speed, or the radius you are turning on, with the time and wheel-over distance for a course change.

How it works

On a turn of constant radius the ship moves along an arc. The angle she turns through each minute is her speed along the arc divided by the radius:

Rate of turn (°/min) = speed (kn) ÷ radius (nm) × 57.3 ÷ 60 = 0.955 × speed ÷ radius

On the bridge this is shortened to rate of turn = speed ÷ radius, which reads about 5% high. The tool gives the exact figure and shows the bridge rule beside it.

For a planned course change the rest is geometry:

Time = course change ÷ rate of turn
Distance on the arc = radius × course change (in radians)
Start of the arc before the waypoint = radius × tan(course change ÷ 2)

Worked example

At 12 knots on a 1.0 nm radius the rate of turn is 0.955 × 12 ÷ 1.0 = 11.5°/min. A 45° alteration takes 3 min 56 s and 0.79 nm along the arc. The arc begins 1.0 × tan 22.5° = 0.41 nm before the waypoint, and the wheel goes over earlier than that by the ship's reaction distance.

How this tool was checked

The rate of turn matches the published formula 57.3 × V ÷ (60 × R) and the textbook examples worked with the bridge rule (15 knots on 1.5 nm, 8 knots on 4 cables) to within the 5% of that rule. Time, arc length and tangent distance were tested against the geometry of the turn for 300 random speeds, radii and course changes.

Limits

  • Use speed over the ground. The ship slows in the turn, so the rate of turn has to come down with the speed to hold the radius. Plan with the mean of the speeds before and after.
  • The start of the arc is not the wheel-over point. The ship needs time to answer the helm: add your reaction distance from the manoeuvring data, often about one ship's length.
  • Wind, current and shallow water all change how the ship turns. Monitor the turn with a parallel index or the predictor, not the calculation alone.

FAQ

What is the formula for rate of turn?

Rate of turn in degrees per minute equals 0.955 times the speed in knots divided by the turn radius in nautical miles. The common bridge rule is simply speed divided by radius, which reads about 5 percent high.

How do you calculate the wheel-over point?

The turning arc begins a distance of radius times the tangent of half the course change before the waypoint. The wheel-over point is earlier than that by the distance the ship runs before she starts to answer the helm.

What rate of turn do I need for a 1 mile radius at 15 knots?

About 14.3 degrees per minute, from 0.955 times 15 divided by 1. The bridge rule of speed divided by radius gives 15.
