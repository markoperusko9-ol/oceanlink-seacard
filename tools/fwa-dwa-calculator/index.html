FWA and DWA calculator

Find the fresh water allowance and the dock water allowance for the density at your berth, so you know how far you may submerge the load line.

How it works

A ship floats deeper in fresh water than in salt water. The fresh water allowance is the amount she rises going from water of density 1.000 to 1.025 t/m³. It is on the load line certificate, or it comes from:

FWA (mm) = summer displacement (t) ÷ (4 × TPC)

Dock water is usually somewhere in between. The dock water allowance is the matching share of the FWA:

DWA = FWA × (1025 − dock water density) ÷ 25

You may load until the load line for your zone is under water by the DWA. When the ship reaches sea water she rises to her proper mark.

Worked example

Summer displacement 31,500 t, TPC 42. FWA = 31,500 ÷ (4 × 42) = 187.5 mm, say 188 mm. Dock water 1.010. DWA = 188 × (1025 − 1010) ÷ 25 = 112.8 mm, say 113 mm. With a summer draught of 9.60 m, you may load to about 9.71 m at this berth.

How this tool was checked

The formulas reproduce the worked example in the Ocean Link load lines guide, and are tested at fresh water (the DWA equals the FWA), at 1.025 (no allowance) and in denser water.

Limits

  • The allowance is for density only. Fuel, water and stores to be used before you reach the sea or the next zone are a separate allowance.
  • Take the density with a calibrated hydrometer from a sample clear of the hull discharges, close to the time of completion.
  • The draught line assumes the ship is on an even keel and upright. Hog, sag and trim need the full draught survey treatment.

FAQ

What is the formula for dock water allowance?

DWA equals FWA multiplied by (1025 minus the dock water density in kg per cubic metre), divided by 25. In fresh water the DWA equals the FWA, and in sea water of 1.025 it is zero.

How is the fresh water allowance calculated?

FWA in millimetres equals the summer displacement in tonnes divided by four times the TPC at the summer waterline. It is also stated on the load line certificate.
