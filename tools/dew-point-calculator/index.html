How it works

A wet bulb reads lower than a dry bulb because water evaporating from the wick cools it. The drier the air, the bigger the difference. The tool uses the psychrometer formula in the form published by the US National Weather Service, at standard pressure of 1013.25 hPa:

Saturation vapour pressure es(T) = 6.112 × exp(17.67 × T ÷ (T + 243.5)) hPa
Vapour pressure e = es(wet) − 1013.25 × 0.00066 × (dry − wet) × (1 + 0.00115 × wet)
Relative humidity = 100 × e ÷ es(dry)
Dew point = 243.5 × ln(e ÷ 6.112) ÷ (17.67 − ln(e ÷ 6.112))

The dew point rule for cargo holds compares two dew points. Ventilate when the dew point of the outside air is lower than the dew point of the air in the hold. Do not ventilate when it is higher, because the incoming air would give up its moisture on the cooler cargo or steel.

Worked example

Outside, the dry bulb reads 28°C and the wet bulb 25°C. The vapour pressure works out at 29.6 hPa, the relative humidity at 78% and the dew point at 23.9°C. In the hold the readings are 24°C and 20°C, a dew point of 18.0°C. The outside air is the wetter of the two, so the dew point rule says keep the hold closed.

How this tool was checked

The formula was worked by hand from the National Weather Service document and matches two published psychrometric chart readings to within one percent of relative humidity and one degree Fahrenheit of dew point. It was also compared with the ASHRAE Handbook method for 856 pairs of readings: for dew points above freezing the two never differed by more than 0.7°C of dew point or 1 point of relative humidity.

Limits

  • The wet bulb must be properly ventilated: a whirling (sling) or aspirated psychrometer, whirled until the reading stops falling. A still wet bulb in a screen reads high and makes the air look wetter than it is.
  • Use clean fresh water and a clean wick. Salt on the wick spoils the reading.
  • Pressure is taken as 1013.25 hPa. At sea level the barometer changes the dew point by a tenth of a degree or two at most.
  • The dew point rule is one of two accepted methods. When you cannot get readings in the hold, the three degree rule is used instead: ventilate when the outside dry bulb is at least 3°C below the cargo temperature at loading. Follow the shipper's carrying instructions and your company procedures.

FAQ

How do you find the dew point from wet and dry bulb temperatures?

Work out the vapour pressure from the wet bulb reading and the difference between the two thermometers, then find the temperature at which that vapour pressure is the saturation pressure. Dew point tables and this calculator do that for you.

What is the dew point rule for cargo ventilation?

Ventilate the hold when the dew point of the outside air is lower than the dew point of the air inside the hold. Do not ventilate when the outside dew point is higher.

What is the three degree rule?

Ventilate when the dry bulb temperature of the outside air is at least 3 degrees Celsius cooler than the average cargo temperature taken at loading. It is used when hold dew points cannot be measured.
