Horizon and light range calculator

Enter your height of eye to get the distance to the sea horizon. Add the height of a light for its dipping range, and the scanner height for the radar horizon.

How it works

The Earth's curve hides the sea beyond a distance that depends on your height. Light bends slightly round the curve, so you see a little farther than the geometry alone allows. With heights in metres and distances in nautical miles:

Sea horizon = 2.08 × √(height of eye)
Dipping range of a light = 2.08 × (√(height of eye) + √(height of light))
Radar horizon = 2.21 × √(scanner height)

A light dips when it sinks below your horizon, or rises above it. That happens where your horizon and the light's horizon meet, so the two distances are added. Radar waves bend a little more than light, which is why the radar horizon is slightly farther.

Why tables disagree

The figure 2.08 depends on how much refraction is assumed, and publications do not agree. IALA uses 2.03. Bowditch gives 2.07 as a formula, while its Table 12 works out at 2.12. Reeds and the RYA use 2.08. The tool shows the range from 2.03 to 2.12 beside the answer so you can see the spread, which is about 2% either way.

Worked example

Height of eye 25 m: the horizon is 2.08 × 5 = 10.4 nm away. A light 40 m high has its own horizon at 2.08 × 6.32 = 13.2 nm, so it dips at about 23.6 nm. With the scanner at 30 m the radar horizon is 2.21 × 5.48 = 12.1 nm.

How this tool was checked

Using the constant behind Bowditch Table 12, the same code reproduces 17 values of that table from 1.5 m to 152 m to the printed tenth of a mile. The constants 2.08 and 2.21 were checked against the formulas in Bowditch and the published dipping distance formula.

Limits

  • Refraction changes with the weather. In abnormal conditions the true distance can differ by far more than the 2% spread between tables.
  • Charted elevations of lights are above high water. At a lower tide the light is higher above the sea and dips a little farther off.
  • The dipping range is a geographical limit. A weak light may not be seen that far: check its nominal range and the visibility.
  • A dipping distance with a bearing gives an approximate position only. Confirm it with another fix.

FAQ

What is the formula for distance to the horizon at sea?

In nautical miles it is about 2.08 times the square root of the height of eye in metres, or 1.17 times the square root of the height in feet. Different publications use constants from 2.03 to 2.12 because they assume different refraction.

What is the dipping distance of a light?

It is the distance at which a light just appears on or disappears below the horizon. Add the horizon distance for your height of eye to the horizon distance for the height of the light.

Why is the radar horizon farther than the visible horizon?

Radar waves are bent round the Earth slightly more than light. The radar horizon in nautical miles is about 2.21 times the square root of the scanner height in metres.
