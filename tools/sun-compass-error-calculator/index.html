Compass error by the Sun: azimuth and amplitude calculator

How it works

The tool works out where the Sun is for your date and UTC time, so you do not need an almanac. From the Sun's declination and Greenwich hour angle and your position it finds the local hour angle, then solves the navigational triangle for the true azimuth:

tan Z = sin LHA ÷ (cos Lat × tan Dec − sin Lat × cos LHA)

Compass error is always true minus compass. If the true bearing is the larger, the error is East, and a gyro is reading low. If the true bearing is the smaller, the error is West, and a gyro is reading high. For a magnetic compass, deviation = compass error − variation. Deviation belongs to the heading you were on, so log the heading with it.

Amplitude

The amplitude lines give the Sun's true bearing at theoretical sunrise and sunset, when its centre is on the celestial horizon:

sin Amplitude = sin Declination ÷ cos Latitude

At that moment the Sun's lower limb is still about half a diameter above the visible horizon. If you enter the exact time of your observation instead, the azimuth at the top is already the true bearing for that instant.

Worked example

Latitude 20°00'N, declination 15°00'N. sin Amp = 0.2588 ÷ 0.9397 = 0.2754, so the amplitude at sunset is W 16.0° N and the true bearing is 286.0°. The gyro showed 287.0°. Error = 286.0 − 287.0 = 1.0° W, gyro high.

How this tool was checked

The Sun position was compared with a reference astronomical ephemeris for 1,500 random times and positions between 2026 and 2040. With the Sun above the horizon and below 80° altitude, the azimuth always agreed within 0.02°. Declination agreed within 0.2′ and GHA within 0.6′. That is ample for compass error, which is logged to 0.1° or 0.5°. It is not almanac grade, so do not use these figures for sight reduction.

Limits

  • The time must be UTC. A one-minute clock error can move the azimuth by a quarter of a degree, and by more when the Sun is high.
  • Take the bearing with the Sun low, ideally below 30° altitude.
  • Sun only. Stars, planets and the Moon are not included.

FAQ

Do I need a nautical almanac for this calculator?

No. The Sun's declination and GHA are computed from the date and UTC time you enter. They agree with reference ephemeris data to a fraction of a minute of arc, which is ample for compass error.

How do I name the compass error?

Subtract the compass bearing from the true bearing. If true is larger the error is East. If true is smaller the error is West. For a gyro, East means the gyro reads low and West means it reads high.

When should I take an amplitude?

When the Sun's centre is on the celestial horizon, which is when its lower limb is about half a diameter above the visible horizon. If you enter the exact time of the observation instead, the tool gives the true azimuth for that moment.
