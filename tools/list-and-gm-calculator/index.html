How it works

Solid GM = KM − KG
Free surface correction = free surface moments ÷ displacement
Fluid GM = solid GM − free surface correction

KM comes from the hydrostatic tables for the present draught. KG comes from the loading computer or a moments calculation. The free surface moments for each slack tank are in the tank tables of the stability booklet. Add them up.

When a weight already on board is moved across the ship, the centre of gravity moves with it and the ship lists until the centre of buoyancy is under the new G:

GG₁ = weight × distance ÷ displacement
tan (list) = GG₁ ÷ fluid GM

Worked example

Displacement 13,500 t, KM 8.40 m, KG 7.193 m, one slack tank with a free surface moment of 5,765.6 t·m. Solid GM = 8.40 − 7.193 = 1.207 m. Free surface correction = 5,765.6 ÷ 13,500 = 0.427 m. Fluid GM = 0.78 m. Now 50 t is moved 10 m to starboard: GG₁ = 500 ÷ 13,500 = 0.037 m, tan θ = 0.037 ÷ 0.78 = 0.0475, so the list is 2.7° to starboard.

How this tool was checked

The formulas reproduce the worked examples in the Ocean Link GM guide and the list and loll guide, and are tested with a negative GM, where no list is given.

Limits

  • The list formula is for small angles, up to about 10°, where the metacentre can be treated as fixed.
  • It is for a weight already on board that is moved across. Loading or discharging a weight also changes the displacement, KG and KM.
  • A negative GM gives an angle of loll, not a list. Treat it by lowering G, never by shifting weight to the high side.
  • The loading computer and the approved stability booklet are the authority for the ship's actual condition.

FAQ

How do you calculate the angle of list?

Find the shift of G: weight multiplied by the distance moved across, divided by displacement. Then tan of the list equals that shift divided by the fluid GM. The formula holds for small angles with positive GM.

What is the free surface correction?

It is the loss of GM caused by liquid moving in slack tanks. It equals the total free surface moments divided by the displacement, and it is subtracted from the solid GM to give the fluid GM.
