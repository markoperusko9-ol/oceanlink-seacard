Pilot Ladder and Combination Arrangements: Rigging, Checks and Rules

Quick answer

A pilot ladder is used where the pilot's climb from the boat is between 1.5 m and 9 m. If the point of access is more than 9 m above the sea, a combination arrangement is used, normally an accommodation ladder together with a pilot ladder. SOLAS Chapter V Regulation 23 and IMO Resolution A.1045(27) set the requirements. A responsible officer must supervise every transfer, and the ladder must be properly secured, inspected, recorded and lit.

Why it matters and which rules apply

Getting the pilot on board is one of the most dangerous routine jobs at sea. Pilots have been killed and badly injured when ladders parted, steps broke or the ladder was secured to something that gave way. Port State Control inspectors look closely at pilot transfer arrangements. Pilots do refuse to board when the rig is unsafe, which can mean a missed slot, a delay and a report to the company.

The main instruments are:

  • SOLAS Chapter V, Regulation 23 (Pilot transfer arrangements). This is the mandatory requirement. The current text came from the 2010 amendments (MSC.308(88)), which applied to new installations from 1 July 2012.
  • IMO Resolution A.1045(27). This gives the detailed specifications and the required rigging arrangements. It is illustrated by the familiar IMO/IMPA poster.
  • ISO 799 series. This is the international standard for pilot ladder design, maintenance and attachments, which manufacturers certify against.

IMO adopted further amendments to Regulation 23, with new performance standards, in 2023. Entry into force is set for 2028. Some details, such as winch reels and securing arrangements, will change. Always check which version your flag and company SMS currently apply. Older installations may also be covered by transitional arrangements that your flag can confirm.

Main components

Pilot ladder (A.1045(27) specification, in summary):

  • Steps are hardwood free of knots, or another material with equivalent properties. The lowest four steps may be rubber of adequate strength. Each step has an effective non-slip surface.
  • Step dimensions are at least 400 mm between the side ropes, 115 mm wide and 25 mm deep, excluding any non-slip device.
  • Step spacing is equal, not less than 310 mm and not more than 350 mm.
  • Side ropes are two uncovered ropes on each side, of manila or equivalent, at least 18 mm in diameter. Each rope is continuous, with no joints below the top step.
  • Spreaders are at least 1.8 m long and stop the ladder twisting. The lowest spreader is on the fifth step from the bottom, and there are no more than nine steps between spreaders.
  • Identification is by a tag or permanent marking. A record is kept on board of the date each ladder entered service and any repairs made.

Associated equipment to be ready at the boarding station:

  • Two man-ropes of not less than 28 mm and not more than 32 mm diameter, rigged if the pilot asks for them.
  • A lifebuoy with a self-igniting light.
  • A heaving line.
  • Adequate lighting of the ladder overside and of the position on deck where the pilot boards.

Access to deck:

  • A bulwark ladder with two handhold stanchions, or an equivalent arrangement.
  • The stanchions are 0.70 to 0.80 m apart, at least 32 mm in diameter, and extend at least 1.2 m above the top of the bulwark.
  • Shipside doors used for pilot access must not open outwards.

Combination arrangement:

  • An accommodation ladder rigged together with a pilot ladder.
  • Some ships use a trapdoor in the lower platform instead. Specific rigging requirements apply to trapdoors, so follow A.1045(27) and the manufacturer's drawings.

The numbers you must know

Item Requirement
Climb on pilot ladder Not less than 1.5 m, not more than 9 m
When access point is more than 9 m above sea Accommodation ladder with pilot ladder, or other equally safe and convenient means
Single length of ladder Must reach the water in all loading and trim conditions with an adverse list of 15°
Accommodation ladder slope (combination) Not more than 45°
Lower platform (combination) Horizontal; at least 5 m above the sea (A.1045)
Pilot ladder above lower platform At least 2 m (A.1045)
Position along the hull Within the parallel body length and, as far as practicable, the midship half-length; clear of all discharges

The accommodation ladder in a combination should lead aft. Its lower end must rest firmly against the ship's side within the parallel body.

The pilot station sets the height it wants the bottom of the ladder above the water. Check the Admiralty List of Radio Signals or sailing directions, the port information or the agent's instructions, or ask on VHF. Do not guess.

Rigging step by step

Pilot ladder only (climb 9 m or less):

  1. Confirm the boarding side, ladder height, time and speed with the bridge and the pilot station. The ship makes a lee as requested.
  2. Inspect the ladder as you flake it out. Check every step, the side ropes, the spreaders and the ID tag.
  3. Check the boarding position. It must be within the parallel body and clear of discharges, overboard outlets, belting and flare, so the ladder lies flat against the hull.
  4. Secure both pairs of side ropes to strong points on deck, such as dedicated securing points or eye plates. Never secure them to handrails, and never take the weight on a step or a shackled step.
  5. If a winch reel is fitted, it must not take the load. Secure the ladder independently to strong points and brake or lock the drum.
  6. Set the bottom height as requested, allowing for roll and the ship's trim.
  7. Rig the bulwark ladder or prepare the access door. Lay out the lifebuoy with light, the heaving line and the man-ropes. Switch on the overside lighting and aim it so it does not dazzle the pilot or the boat crew.
  8. The responsible officer stands by with a radio to the bridge. The officer oversees the boarding and arranges for the pilot to be escorted by a safe route to and from the bridge.

Combination arrangement (access more than 9 m):

  1. Lower the accommodation ladder so it leads aft, with its lower platform horizontal and its lower end resting against the hull.
  2. Rig the pilot ladder alongside the lower platform. Extend it at least 2 m above the platform and secure it to the ship's side so it cannot swing away.
  3. Check that the gap and the step-over from pilot ladder to platform are safe. Rails and stanchions must be fitted and tight.
  4. Recheck the platform height and the ladder slope as the draught, trim or list changes.

Worked scenario: 11 m freeboard in ballast

You are 3rd Officer on a bulk carrier arriving in ballast. The point of access is 11 m above the sea. The pilot station asks for the ladder 1.5 m above the water on the starboard side.

  • Can you use a pilot ladder alone? No. The climb exceeds 9 m, so you must rig the combination arrangement.
  • What do you rig? Rig the starboard accommodation ladder leading aft, with the lower platform horizontal and at least 5 m above the sea. Rig the pilot ladder alongside the platform, extending at least 2 m above it and secured to the hull. The bottom of the pilot ladder hangs 1.5 m above the water as requested.
  • What do you check?
    • The ladder slope does not exceed 45°.
    • The platform is horizontal and its lower end is firmly against the hull.
    • Both pilot ladder side ropes are made fast to strong points.
    • Rails and stanchions are tight.
    • The lighting, lifebuoy with light and heaving line are ready.
  • What if the ship rolls? Report to the bridge. A change of heading to improve the lee is the master's decision. Never send a rig you are unhappy with.

Routine checks, maintenance and storage

  • Before every use, visually and physically check the steps. Look for cracks, splits, looseness, worn non-slip surfaces and chocks that have slipped.
  • Check the side ropes for chafe, cuts, kinks, hardness, discolouration, rot or chemical damage.
  • Check the spreaders for length, security and position.
  • Check the ID tag is legible and matches the ladder record.
  • After use, wash off salt water with fresh water if the ladder is wet with spray. Dry the ladder before stowing.
  • Store it rolled, in a dry, ventilated space away from sunlight, heat, paint, chemicals and oil.
  • Never paint, varnish or coat the side ropes. Never cover the non-slip surface of the steps.
  • Repairs must follow the manufacturer's instructions and A.1045(27). The resolution limits the number of replacement steps secured by a method different from the original construction. If in doubt, replace the ladder.
  • Record each repair and the date the ladder entered service, as SOLAS requires.
  • Accommodation ladder, winch, falls, limit switches and davit must be examined, tested and maintained in accordance with SOLAS II-1/3-9 and MSC.1/Circ.1331. This is implemented through the company planned maintenance system.

SOLAS does not set a fixed replacement interval for pilot ladders. Follow the manufacturer's instructions, ISO 799 guidance, the company SMS and the condition of the ladder.

Common faults and what they look like

Fault Symptom or consequence
Ladder secured to handrails or by shackles on steps Rail bends or step pulls out under load; pilot may refuse
Worn or rotten side ropes Hairy, stiff, discoloured or soft fibres; risk of parting
Cracked or loose steps Step tilts or moves when weighted
Missing or short spreaders Ladder twists as the pilot climbs
Ladder not flat against hull Swings out at flare or belting; feet trapped
Rigged near discharges Pilot soaked or slips on fouled steps
Winch reel taking the weight Ladder slips if the brake fails
Combination platform not horizontal Awkward, dangerous step-over
Poor lighting Dark ladder, or floodlight blinding pilot or boat crew
Man-ropes tied to the ladder Ladder is pulled away from hull; man-ropes useless
Unattended rig No supervision, no communication with bridge

Many ships fail Port State Control or pilot inspections on these simple points. Several of them often appear together on the same rig.

Oral exam questions

Typical questions an examiner might ask, with the points to cover:

  • When must a combination arrangement be used? When the access point is more than 9 m above sea level.
  • What are the step dimensions, step spacing and spreader rules?
    • Steps at least 400 x 115 x 25 mm.
    • Steps 310 to 350 mm apart.
    • Spreaders at least 1.8 m long, the lowest on the fifth step, no more than nine steps between them.
  • Where on the hull should the ladder be rigged? Within the parallel body and, where practicable, the midship half-length, clear of discharges.
  • What equipment must be at the boarding station? Man-ropes ready, a lifebuoy with self-igniting light, a heaving line and lighting.
  • Who supervises the transfer? A responsible officer in communication with the bridge. The officer also arranges for the pilot to be escorted by a safe route to and from the bridge.
  • What records are kept? The date each identified ladder entered service and any repairs.
  • What would you do if you found a cracked step five minutes before the pilot arrives? Do not use the ladder. Rig the spare, inform the bridge and record the defect.

Examiners want the reason as well as the number. The 9 m limit exists because climbing further is exhausting. The securing and spreader rules exist because twisting ladders and anchorages that give way kill pilots.

Key points
  • Pilot ladder climb must be between 1.5 m and 9 m; above 9 m a combination arrangement is required.
  • Secure side ropes to strong points on deck, never to handrails, steps or a winch reel drum.
  • Rig within the parallel body, clear of discharges, with the ladder lying flat against the hull.
  • Steps are 310 to 350 mm apart, spreaders at least 1.8 m long, the lowest spreader on the fifth step.
  • In a combination rig the accommodation ladder leads aft at no more than 45°, with its lower platform horizontal.
  • A responsible officer in contact with the bridge must supervise every transfer.
  • Every ladder is identified, and its in-service date and repairs are recorded on board.

Frequently asked questions

What is the maximum height for a pilot ladder alone?

SOLAS V/23 limits the climb on a pilot ladder to no less than 1.5 m and no more than 9 m. If the point of access is more than 9 m above the sea, use an accommodation ladder in combination with a pilot ladder, or another equally safe and convenient means.

Can the pilot ladder be tied to the ship's handrail?

No. Handrails, shackles on steps and winch reel drums (even with the brake on) are not acceptable as securing points. The side ropes must be made fast to strong points independent of the reel. Pilots and PSC inspectors will reject anything else.

How often must a pilot ladder be replaced?

SOLAS does not set a fixed replacement interval. Replace the ladder based on its condition, the manufacturer's guidance, ISO 799 guidance and your company SMS. Remove any ladder with damaged ropes or steps from service immediately, and keep the in-service and repair record up to date.

Who decides which side and what height to rig the ladder?

The pilot station normally specifies the side and the ladder height above the water. The master manoeuvres the ship to give a lee. If the instructions are unclear, confirm them by VHF before rigging.

Are the pilot ladder rules changing?

Yes. IMO adopted amendments to SOLAS V/23 and new performance standards in 2023, with entry into force set for 2028. Check with your flag and company which requirements apply to your ship and when.

Test yourself

Above what height of the access point above sea level must a combination arrangement be used?

Where the access point is more than 9 m above the sea, an accommodation ladder in combination with a pilot ladder, or equivalent, is required. SOLAS Chapter V, Regulation 23.

To what should the pilot ladder side ropes be secured?

The side ropes must be made fast to strong points; handrails, steps and reel drums are not acceptable. IMO Resolution A.1045(27).

What is the required spacing between pilot ladder steps?

Steps must be equally spaced, not less than 310 mm and not more than 350 mm apart. IMO Resolution A.1045(27).

In a combination arrangement, what is the maximum slope of the accommodation ladder?

The accommodation ladder used with a pilot ladder must not exceed a slope of 45°. SOLAS Chapter V, Regulation 23.

Where is the lowest spreader on a pilot ladder fitted?

The lowest spreader is on the fifth step from the bottom, with no more than nine steps between spreaders. IMO Resolution A.1045(27).
