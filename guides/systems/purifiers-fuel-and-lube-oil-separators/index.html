Purifiers (Fuel and Lube Oil Separators) on Merchant Ships

Quick answer

A purifier is a high-speed centrifugal separator. It removes water and solids from fuel and lube oil before they reach the engines. It uses a spinning bowl and disc stack to throw the heavier water and dirt outward, while clean oil moves inward and is pumped out. Most separator problems come down to wrong temperature, wrong feed rate, wrong gravity disc or a bowl that will not seal, so those are the four things to check first.

Why ships need purifiers

Residual and distillate fuels arrive on board carrying water, sludge, rust, sand and catalytic fines (hard aluminium and silicon particles left over from refinery cracking). Lube oil picks up water from coolers and condensation, plus soot, wear metals and combustion products. If they are not removed:

  • Fuel pumps and injectors wear fast, and catfines can cause severe abrasive wear of liners and rings in a very short time when concentrations are high.
  • Water in fuel causes poor combustion, corrosion and, in bad cases, engine stoppage.
  • Contaminated lube oil damages bearings and journals and shortens oil life.

Gravity settling in the settling tank does part of the job, but it is far too slow for fine particles. A centrifuge applies thousands of times the force of gravity, so separation that would take days in a tank happens in seconds.

The principle is Stokes' law. The settling velocity of a particle increases with the square of its diameter and with the density difference between particle and oil. It falls as viscosity rises. Heating the oil cuts viscosity and widens the density gap with water. That is why separation temperature matters so much.

Purifier or clarifier – and the main components

The same machine can usually run in either mode, depending on the disc fitted at the top of the bowl.

Mode Removes Outlets Key part
Purifier Water and solids Clean oil + separated water Gravity disc (sets the oil/water interface)
Clarifier Mainly solids, small amounts of water Clean oil only Clarifier disc / dam ring (no water outlet)

Main components:

  • Bowl: the rotating body, driven through a vertical spindle at high speed.
  • Disc stack: conical discs with small spacers between them. They divide the oil into thin layers, so particles travel only a short distance before reaching a disc surface and sliding outward.
  • Distributor: guides dirty oil to the outer part of the stack, often through holes in the discs.
  • Gravity disc: in purifier mode, its hole size positions the oil/water interface.
  • Paring discs (centripetal pumps): stationary discs that skim the rotating clean oil (and water) and pump it out under pressure.
  • Sliding bowl bottom and operating water system: hydraulically closes the bowl and opens it for sludge discharge.
  • Drive: an electric motor driving the spindle, through a centrifugal friction clutch on many older units or directly on many modern ones. Belt and gear drives are both common.
  • Ancillaries: feed pump, heater (steam, thermal oil or electric), three-way recirculation valve, control unit with alarms. On self-adjusting designs there is also a water transducer in the clean oil outlet.

How it works, step by step

Inside the bowl (purifier mode):

  1. Heated dirty oil enters through the distributor and is flung outward into the disc stack.
  2. Solids and water, being denser, move outward along the underside of the discs. Oil moves inward along the upper surfaces.
  3. Separated water builds a ring at the periphery and leaves over the gravity disc. Solids pack in the sludge space at the bowl wall.
  4. Clean oil collects near the centre and is discharged by the oil paring disc.

The interface and water seal. Before oil is admitted, the bowl is primed with seal water. This stops oil escaping through the water outlet. The radius where oil meets water is the interface. It should sit just outside the outer edge of the disc stack. If it moves out beyond the outer edge of the top disc, oil can pass into the water path and the seal breaks.

  • Interface too far out (oil too dense, temperature too low, disc hole too big): the seal breaks and oil pours out of the water outlet.
  • Interface too far in: the outer part of the disc stack fills with water and separation is poor.

The general rule is to fit the gravity disc with the largest hole that does not cause a seal break, chosen from the maker's nomogram using oil density and separation temperature. Denser oil needs a smaller hole.

Self-adjusting separators have no gravity disc. They run as clarifiers and monitor water in the clean oil outlet. When water is detected, they drain it or trigger a discharge. This lets them handle denser fuels than conventional purifiers, up to the limit stated by the maker.

Typical start sequence (always follow the maker's manual and your SMS):

  1. Check the oil level in the gear/sump, the brake released, the operating water supply and the heater ready.
  2. Start the motor. Watch the current come down as the bowl reaches rated speed. On older units, also confirm speed with the revolution counter.
  3. Close the bowl with operating water and give seal water (purifier mode).
  4. Open the feed slowly, with the oil at separation temperature.
  5. Check the water outlet for oil, check the back pressure and confirm the sludge discharges are working.

Stopping: shut off the feed (oil recirculates to the tank), give displacement water if specified, do a final discharge, stop the motor and apply the brake if your procedure requires it. Never open the bowl until it has completely stopped.

Routine checks and maintenance

Each watch / daily:

  • Separation temperature steady at the set point. For heavy fuel this is usually close to 98°C. For lube oil it is lower, at whatever the maker specifies.
  • Feed rate as set. A lower throughput means a longer residence time and better removal of catfines.
  • Clean oil back pressure, operating water pressure and motor current all normal.
  • Water outlet sight glass clear of oil. No oil appearing in the sludge.
  • Gear/sump oil level. No abnormal noise or vibration.
  • Sludge discharge interval suits the fuel condition. Sludge tank level noted.

Periodic (by PMS running hours):

  • Open and clean the bowl and disc stack, and inspect the discs for damage or deposits.
  • Renew the bowl seal rings, the sliding bowl bottom seal and the operating water device O-rings.
  • Clean the operating water nozzles and strainers.
  • Inspect the paring discs and the gravity disc for erosion.
  • Check the spindle bearings, the friction pads (if fitted), the gear/belt drive and the vibration dampers.
  • Change the gear oil.
  • Check bowl height setting and lock ring markings. Mismatched or over-worn lock ring marks are a serious danger sign.

Bowl parts are balanced as a set. Do not mix parts between bowls unless the maker allows it. An unbalanced bowl at full speed can cause heavy damage.

Common faults and what they look like

Symptom Likely causes
Oil out of water outlet (seal break) Too little seal water; separation temperature too low; gravity disc hole too large for oil density; feed opened too fast; bowl not fully closed
Water in clean oil Gravity disc hole too small; feed rate too high; water content beyond capacity; temperature low
Bowl will not close / oil escapes to sludge outlet Low operating water pressure; blocked nozzles; worn sliding bowl bottom seal; dirt on seal faces
Bowl does not open for discharge Blocked operating water passages; faulty solenoid valve; sludge packed solid
High vibration Uneven sludge build-up; damaged or wrongly assembled bowl; worn bearings or springs
Slow to reach speed / speed drops Worn friction pads; slipping belt; motor fault; brake partly on
Excessive sludge or frequent alarms Poor fuel quality; heater fouled; discharge interval too long

Worked scenario. You bunker a new fuel that is noticeably denser than the last one. Soon after you change over, the purifier water outlet shows oil and the sludge tank level rises quickly. The cause is that the interface has moved outward and the seal is breaking. Check that the separation temperature is correct. Then confirm the bunker density at 15°C from the BDN or lab report. Select the gravity disc from the nomogram, which will be a smaller hole than before. Restart with the full seal water quantity and open the feed gradually. If the fuel is above the density limit for gravity-disc operation, run the machine as the maker advises.

Safety precautions and regulations

Separators handle hot oil next to hot surfaces and spin heavy bowls at high speed. They are a known engine room fire and injury risk.

  • Fire: leaks from heated fuel lines, overflowing sludge or a burst hose can ignite. Keep screening and insulation in place. Know where the quick-closing valves and local fire-fighting controls are.
  • Overflow: a failed discharge cycle or a seal break can flood the sludge tank or drip tray. Never leave a newly started purifier unwatched.
  • Mechanical: isolate electrically and lock out before opening (see lock-out tag-out). Confirm the bowl has stopped. Use the maker's lifting tools, and follow the correct lock ring direction and torque.
  • Burns and skin contact: use heat-resistant gloves and eye protection. Hot oil and water leave the outlets during discharge.

Regulatory points:

  • SOLAS Chapter II-2 sets out fuel oil arrangement requirements, including where purifiers for heated oil are placed. It also requires a fixed local application fire-fighting system in larger machinery spaces, and the protected areas include purifier zones.
  • MARPOL Annex I requires a sludge tank to receive oil residues, including separator sludge. Collection and disposal of those residues must be recorded in the Oil Record Book Part I.

Exact layout details are set by the flag administration and the class society, and running procedures by your company SMS.

Oral exam questions

Examiners for watchkeeping and senior engineer certificates like purifiers because they test theory and practical sense together.

  • Explain the difference between a purifier and a clarifier. A purifier separates two liquids plus solids and has a water outlet with a gravity disc. A clarifier removes solids, with no water outlet.
  • How do you select a gravity disc? Use the oil density and separation temperature on the maker's nomogram. Pick the largest hole that does not break the seal.
  • What happens if you increase separation temperature? Viscosity falls and the density difference grows, so separation improves, within the maker's and the fuel's limits.
  • Why reduce throughput for poor fuel? Residence time increases, so smaller particles such as catfines have time to be removed.
  • Name the causes of a seal break (see the fault table), and explain what you would do first.
  • Describe how the bowl opens for sludge discharge. Operating water drains from under the sliding bowl bottom, the bottom drops, sludge ejects through the ports, and operating water closes the bowl again.
  • What would you check before opening a bowl? Motor isolated and locked out, bowl stopped, feed and heater off, lines drained, correct tools ready.
Key points
  • Purifiers use centrifugal force and a disc stack to remove water and solids from fuel and lube oil.
  • Separation improves with higher temperature (lower viscosity, Stokes' law) and with a lower feed rate (longer residence time).
  • The gravity disc sets the oil/water interface: use the largest hole that does not break the water seal.
  • Denser oil needs a smaller gravity disc hole; follow the maker's nomogram.
  • Most faults trace back to temperature, feed rate, wrong disc or a bowl that will not seal.
  • Never open a bowl until it has stopped and the motor is locked out.
  • Separator sludge goes to the sludge tank and is recorded in the Oil Record Book Part I.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a purifier and a clarifier on a ship?

A purifier separates oil from water and solids and has a separate water outlet controlled by a gravity disc. A clarifier mainly removes solids and has no water outlet. Traditionally the clarifier was run after the purifier as a polishing stage. The same separator can usually be set up for either mode.

What is the correct separation temperature for heavy fuel oil?

For heavy fuel, makers usually recommend a temperature close to 98°C, because it gives low viscosity without boiling the water in the oil. Always use the figure in your separator manual and company procedures. Lube oil and distillate fuels are separated at lower temperatures.

Why does a purifier lose its water seal?

The seal breaks when the oil/water interface moves outward beyond the outer edge of the top disc, so oil can flow into the water outlet path. Common causes are insufficient seal water, oil too cold, a gravity disc hole too large for the oil density, a bowl not properly closed, or the feed opened too quickly. Oil then appears at the water outlet.

How often should a purifier bowl be cleaned?

Follow the planned maintenance system, which is based on the maker's running-hour intervals. Bring it forward if fuel quality is poor or performance drops. Self-cleaning separators discharge sludge automatically, but the disc stack still needs periodic manual cleaning and the seals need renewing.

Can a purifier remove catalytic fines from fuel?

It is the main defence on board, but removal is never complete. Efficiency improves with the correct temperature, a reduced feed rate and regular sludge discharges. Good settling and service tank management, plus regular draining, also help keep catfines away from the engine.

Test yourself

In purifier mode, what does the gravity disc control?

The gravity disc's hole size sets the radius where oil meets water inside the bowl. Speed, discharge interval and temperature are set elsewhere. Reference: separator maker's manual / standard marine engineering practice.

A denser fuel is bunkered and the purifier starts losing its seal. What gravity disc change is normally needed?

Denser oil pushes the interface outward, so a smaller hole is needed to bring it back in. Raising the feed rate would make separation worse. Reference: maker's gravity disc nomogram.

According to Stokes' law, which change improves separation of particles from oil?

Settling velocity rises with density difference and particle size and falls with viscosity. Heating the oil and reducing throughput both help. Reference: Stokes' law as applied to centrifugal separation.

Oil is continuously leaking out of the sludge ports while the purifier is running. What is the most likely cause?

If the sliding bowl bottom cannot hold the bowl shut, oil escapes through the discharge ports. A small gravity disc hole would cause water in the clean oil, not sludge port leakage. Reference: separator maker's fault-finding guide.

Where must the collection and disposal of purifier sludge be recorded?

Machinery space operations, including oil residue (sludge) handling, go in ORB Part I. Part II is for cargo and ballast operations on oil tankers. Reference: MARPOL Annex I, Regulation 17.
