Breathing Apparatus (SCBA) and EEBD on Merchant Ships

Quick answer

The SCBA is the compressed-air set in the fire-fighter's outfit, used to enter smoke-filled or unsafe atmospheres for fire-fighting and rescue. The EEBD is a short-duration escape hood or mask that gets you out of a smoke-filled space and must never be used to go in. Both are covered by SOLAS Chapter II-2 and the FSS Code, Chapter 3, while your company SMS and the maker's manual set the detailed checks.

SCBA vs EEBD: what each one is for

Candidates mix these two up more than almost anything else in fire-fighting orals. Keep the distinction simple:

SCBA EEBD
Purpose Entering and working in smoke, toxic or oxygen-deficient atmospheres Escaping from a space with a hazardous atmosphere
Typical use Fire party, enclosed space rescue Engine room or accommodation escape
Duration At least 1,200 litres of free air, or at least 30 minutes for other types At least 10 minutes
Training needed Yes, worn in drills Familiarisation, often with training units
Use for entry? Yes Never

The SCBA is part of the fire-fighter's outfit. The outfit also includes protective clothing, boots, gloves, helmet, safety lamp, axe and a fire-resistant lifeline.

The EEBD is a self-contained escape set, usually a hood with a small cylinder. It protects your eyes, nose and mouth for long enough to reach fresh air. It is not for fighting fire or entering tanks or voids. It is not a substitute for an SCBA in any circumstances.

Main components and how an SCBA works

Shipboard SCBA sets are open-circuit compressed air sets. You breathe ordinary compressed air, not oxygen, and exhale it to atmosphere.

Main components:

  • Backplate and harness: carry the cylinder and spread the load.
  • Cylinder and cylinder valve: steel or composite. Working pressure is commonly 200 or 300 bar, marked on the cylinder.
  • Pressure reducer (first stage): drops cylinder pressure to a medium pressure. It usually has a relief valve.
  • Medium-pressure hose: runs to the demand valve.
  • Lung demand valve (second stage): supplies air when you inhale. Most modern sets are positive pressure, keeping the mask slightly above ambient so any leak blows outward.
  • Face mask: full face piece with visor, inner mask, inhalation and exhalation valves, and a speech diaphragm.
  • Pressure gauge: on a high-pressure line to the shoulder strap so you can read it while working.
  • Low-pressure warning device: normally a whistle.

Step by step in use:

  1. Opening the cylinder valve charges the reducer and hose.
  2. Donning the mask and taking the first breath activates the demand valve, which switches to positive pressure.
  3. Each inhalation opens the demand valve. Each exhalation leaves through the exhalation valve.
  4. As the cylinder empties, the gauge falls. At the set pressure, the warning whistle sounds and keeps sounding until the air is exhausted.

Warning device rule: SOLAS amendments in force from 2014 added a requirement to the FSS Code. The SCBA must have an audible alarm and a visual or other device that warns the user before the air in the cylinder drops to 200 litres. Existing ships had a phase-in period.

Communications: SOLAS II-2/10.10.4 (in force 2014) requires a minimum of two two-way portable radiotelephone apparatus for each fire party. They must be explosion-proof or intrinsically safe.

Worked scenario: how long will the air last?

Examiners like a quick duration sum. Here is a common one, using figures the examiner gives you.

Given: a 6.8 litre cylinder charged to 300 bar, with the whistle set at 55 bar. Assume a consumption of 40 litres per minute.

  1. Free air available = water capacity × pressure = 6.8 × 300 ≈ 2,040 litres. (This is the simple exam method; at 300 bar real gas behaviour means actual free air is roughly 10% less, about 1,800 L.)
  2. Reserve at the whistle = 6.8 × 55 ≈ 374 litres. Usable air before the whistle ≈ 2,040 − 374 ≈ 1,666 litres.
  3. Working time ≈ 1,666 ÷ 40 ≈ 42 minutes.

The 200-litre figure is the regulatory latest point for the warning; actual whistle settings usually leave a larger reserve. Always use the whistle pressure of your own set.

Now say what really happens. Heavy work, heat, stress, ladders and dragging a casualty can push consumption far above 40 L/min. Real working time can be well under half the calculated figure.

That is why the BA control officer logs these for every wearer:

  • time in
  • starting pressure
  • expected time of whistle

A team withdraws when the first member's whistle sounds, or earlier on the SMS turnaround pressure. Do not wait for the last member.

Note that the calculation ignores the small residual pressure you can never use. It also assumes a full charge, which is why the pre-use pressure check matters.

Routine checks and maintenance

Inspection intervals come from three places:

  • the company SMS
  • the maker's manual
  • IMO guidance on maintaining fire-protection equipment (MSC.1/Circ.1432)

The flag may add its own. Follow your vessel's planned maintenance system. The typical content is below.

Pre-use (donning) checks, every time:

  • Visual check of harness, hoses, mask and visor for damage.
  • Cylinder valve open. The gauge should show near full, or at least the minimum your SMS sets.
  • High-pressure leak test: open the cylinder valve, then close it and watch the gauge for one minute. The pressure drop must stay within the maker's limit.
  • Whistle test: with the valve closed, bleed air slowly through the demand valve. The whistle must sound at the maker's set pressure, which is commonly somewhere around 50–60 bar. Check your set's manual.
  • Face seal (negative pressure) test: put on the mask and inhale with the air supply shut off or the demand valve disconnected, as the maker specifies. The mask should pull in and stay in. If it doesn't, adjust the straps and repeat.

Periodic checks (as per PMS):

  • Cylinders fully charged and spare charges complete.
  • Cylinder hydrostatic test dates in date. Intervals depend on cylinder type, the standard it was made to and the flag.
  • Composite cylinders within their marked service life.
  • Masks cleaned and disinfected after use.
  • Air quality from the onboard BA compressor tested to a recognised breathing-air standard (EN 12021 is widely used). Check that the intake is away from engine exhausts.
  • Shore servicing of reducers and demand valves at the maker's intervals.

Spare charges: SOLAS Chapter II-2, Regulation 10 requires two spare charges for each required breathing apparatus. Cargo ships and passenger ships carrying not more than 36 passengers that have suitable means of fully recharging cylinders free from contamination need carry only one spare charge per set. Passenger ships carrying more than 36 passengers must carry at least two spare charges per set.

EEBD checks:

  • Pressure gauge in the green, or tamper seal intact on gauge-less types.
  • Hood packaging undamaged.
  • Set in its marked location and signage visible.
  • Training units clearly marked and stowed separately from live units.

Common faults and their symptoms

Symptom Likely cause Action
Gauge drops fast in leak test Leaking cylinder valve seal, hose connection or reducer Do not use. Tag the set and change or repair it per PMS
Continuous free flow from demand valve Demand valve stuck or bypass left open Check the bypass. If it persists, take the set out of service
Whistle does not sound, or sounds at the wrong pressure Whistle blocked, damaged or out of adjustment Set unserviceable until repaired by a competent person
Mask fails negative pressure test Poor strap adjustment, hair or beard in the seal, perished seal, exhalation valve fault Readjust and remove the obstruction. Replace the mask if the leak persists
Visor misting Inner mask poorly fitted or missing Refit or replace the inner mask
Cylinder valve hard to turn Damaged or dirty valve Replace the cylinder and send it for service
Cylinder not full after charging Compressor fault, or charged hot and cooled down Top up after cooling. Investigate the compressor
EEBD gauge in red or seal broken Discharged, damaged or tampered with Replace with a spare and report

Facial hair and seal: facial hair crossing the seal line defeats the face seal on a full face mask. Many companies require wearers to be clean-shaven where the seal sits. Ordinary spectacles also break the seal. Use the spectacle kit if one is supplied.

Safety precautions in use

  • Never enter alone. BA wearers work in pairs or teams with a standby team ready.
  • BA control is mandatory practice. Tally or entry board, times, pressures and a communications plan should all be in place before anyone goes in.
  • Lifeline or guide line as the situation requires. The fireproof lifeline for each breathing apparatus must be at least 30 m long and attaches by snaphook to the BA harness or a separate belt.
  • Withdraw on the first whistle in the team, or at the SMS turnaround pressure, whichever comes first.
  • Keep the mask on until you are in clean air. Gas and smoke can linger in corridors well beyond the fire.
  • Enclosed space rescue: only SCBA, never an EEBD, for going in. Use rescue sets and stretchers as your enclosed-space procedure lays out.
  • Recharge only from approved compressors with tested air. Never fill a cylinder that is out of test or damaged.
  • EEBD: grab, don the hood, and go. Do not stop to fight a fire or search compartments with it on.

Drills are required under SOLAS Chapter III. They include checking the fire-fighter's outfits, and crew are expected to don and use the SCBA, not just look at it.

Oral exam: questions you should expect

Q: What is the minimum air capacity of a shipboard SCBA? At least 1,200 litres of free air for a compressed-air set, or at least 30 minutes for other types. The requirement is in FSS Code Chapter 3.

Q: What does the warning device do, and why does it matter? It alerts the wearer before the remaining air falls to 200 litres, by audible alarm plus visual or other means. It gives a margin to withdraw safely.

Q: Take me through your pre-use checks. Work through these in order:

  • visual inspection
  • cylinder pressure
  • high-pressure leak test
  • whistle test
  • face seal test
  • positive pressure confirmed after donning

Q: Can you use an EEBD to rescue a man from a ballast tank? No. EEBDs are for escape only, with a minimum 10-minute duration. Rescue entry needs SCBA under the enclosed space entry procedure.

Q: Where are EEBDs located? As marked on the Fire Control Plan and by the guidelines for EEBDs (MSC/Circ.849). Typical locations are in the accommodation and machinery spaces, readily visible and on escape routes, with spares carried. Numbers depend on ship type and layout.

Q: What is in a fire-fighter's outfit? SCBA, protective clothing, boots, gloves, rigid helmet, approved safety lamp, axe and fire-resistant lifeline.

Q: Why positive pressure? Any leak at the face seal blows air outward, so smoke and gas are kept out of the mask.

Key points
  • SCBA is for entry and fire-fighting. EEBD is for escape only and must never be used to enter a space.
  • A shipboard SCBA must give at least 1,200 litres of free air (or 30 minutes for other types). An EEBD must last at least 10 minutes.
  • Pre-use checks: visual, pressure, high-pressure leak test, whistle test and face seal test, every time.
  • Newer requirements add a low-air warning before 200 litres remain and two-way radios for fire parties.
  • The team withdraws on the first wearer's whistle or the SMS turnaround pressure, under BA control.
  • Cylinder test dates, composite service life and breathing-air quality must all be kept in date per PMS and flag.
  • Facial hair or spectacles in the seal area defeat the mask seal.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between SCBA and EEBD on a ship?

The SCBA is a full compressed-air breathing set used by fire parties and rescue teams to enter hazardous atmospheres. The EEBD is a short-duration escape device, usually a hood, used only to get out of a smoke-filled space. An EEBD must never be used for entry or fire-fighting.

How long does an EEBD last?

The FSS Code requires an EEBD to have a service duration of at least 10 minutes. Actual duration depends on the model and your breathing rate, so use it only to escape by the shortest route.

Does an SCBA contain oxygen?

No. Shipboard fire-fighting SCBA sets are open-circuit sets charged with compressed breathing air, the same air we breathe normally. Pure oxygen would be a serious fire hazard in this role.

At what pressure should the SCBA whistle sound?

The rule-based requirement is that the warning operates before the remaining air falls to 200 litres. The actual whistle pressure is set by the manufacturer and is commonly somewhere around 50–60 bar. Always check the figure in your set's manual.

How many spare cylinders must be carried for each SCBA?

SOLAS Chapter II-2, Regulation 10 requires two spare charges for each required breathing apparatus. Cargo ships and passenger ships carrying not more than 36 passengers that have suitable means of fully recharging cylinders free from contamination need carry only one spare charge per set. Passenger ships carrying more than 36 passengers must carry at least two spare charges per set.

Test yourself

What is the minimum quantity of free air required for a compressed-air SCBA under the FSS Code?

The FSS Code requires a compressed-air SCBA to hold at least 1,200 litres of free air, or other types to function for at least 30 minutes (FSS Code, Chapter 3).

A crew member collapses in a cargo hold with a suspected oxygen-deficient atmosphere. What should the rescue team wear to enter?

EEBDs are for escape only and filter masks give no oxygen. Rescue entry requires SCBA (FSS Code, Chapter 3; SOLAS Chapter III drill requirements).

What is the minimum service duration of an EEBD?

The FSS Code, Chapter 3, requires an EEBD to have a service duration of at least 10 minutes.

In the high-pressure leak test, what are you looking for after closing the cylinder valve?

A pressure drop beyond the maker's limit shows a leak in the high-pressure or medium-pressure system. The set should not be used (maker's instructions; MSC.1/Circ.1432 maintenance guidance).

Why are modern shipboard SCBA demand valves positive pressure?

Positive pressure keeps the mask slightly above ambient, so a leak pushes air out rather than drawing contaminants in. A face seal test is still required (maker's instructions).
