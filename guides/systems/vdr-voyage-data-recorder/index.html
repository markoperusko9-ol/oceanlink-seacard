VDR (Voyage Data Recorder) on Merchant Ships

Quick answer

The voyage data recorder (VDR) is the ship's 'black box'. It continuously records bridge audio, VHF, radar and ECDIS images, position, heading, speed, alarms and machinery orders into protective capsules so investigators can reconstruct events after a casualty. It is required under SOLAS Chapter V, must pass an annual performance test, and the master must be able to save its data after an incident.

What a VDR is and why it is fitted

A VDR is a fixed recording system that keeps a continuous, time-stamped record of what was happening on the bridge and around the ship. Its purpose is to help casualty investigators work out what happened and why. It is not there to catch out the officer of the watch, although every OOW should assume that everything said on the bridge is being recorded.

Carriage is required by SOLAS Chapter V, Regulation 20. In broad terms it covers passenger ships and cargo ships of 3,000 gt and above. Exact applicability depends on ship type, tonnage and build date. Some older cargo ships were permitted a simplified VDR (S-VDR) that records fewer data items. Check your ship's Safety Equipment or Passenger Ship Safety Certificate record of equipment to confirm what is fitted and to which standard.

The detailed performance standard depends on when the unit was installed. VDRs installed on or after 1 July 2014 follow IMO Resolution MSC.333(90). Older units were approved to earlier standards and are still acceptable on the ships they were fitted to. The differences show up mainly in recording duration and capsule arrangement, as covered below.

Main components

Layouts vary by maker, but a typical modern installation has the following parts:

  • Main unit / data acquisition unit (DAU): usually in a bridge cabinet or electronics room. It collects all inputs, time-stamps them, compresses them and writes them to the recording media.
  • Fixed protective capsule: mounted on an open deck, usually on the compass deck or monkey island. It is bright orange and built to survive fire, impact and deep-water pressure. It carries an underwater locating beacon (an acoustic pinger) so divers or ROVs can find it on the seabed.
  • Float-free capsule: required on units built to MSC.333(90). It sits in a bracket with a hydrostatic release, floats free if the ship sinks, and has a means of indicating its position.
  • Long-term recording medium: inside or alongside the main unit. It holds a longer history than the capsules and can be accessed on board.
  • Bridge microphones: positioned so that conversation at the conning position, chart table, radar and bridge wing workstations is captured.
  • Sensor interfaces: serial (IEC 61162 / NMEA) links, LAN connections to radar and ECDIS, and analogue or digital alarm inputs.
  • Dedicated reserve power source: a battery or UPS inside or next to the main unit.
  • Bridge alarm / control panel: shows system status, raises fault alarms and usually carries the save / backup function.

The data items depend on the standard and on what sensors the ship actually has. If the ship has the equipment, the VDR should normally record it:

An S-VDR records a reduced set of items. Your ship's VDR documentation and the annual test report list exactly which inputs are connected.

How it works, step by step

  1. Acquisition: sensors send their data continuously to the DAU. Radar and ECDIS screens are captured as images at set intervals, and audio channels are sampled continuously.
  2. Time-stamping: every item is tagged against one reference time so that the timeline can be rebuilt later.
  3. Recording: data is written to the fixed capsule, the float-free capsule and the long-term medium. The capsules work as a rolling loop, so the oldest data is overwritten by new data.
  4. Recording duration: units to MSC.333(90) keep at least 48 hours on the capsules and at least 30 days on the long-term medium. Older units installed before 1 June 2008 had to keep at least 12 hours. Units installed from 1 June 2008 under the amended standards keep at least 48 hours. Check your own equipment's specification.
  5. Monitoring: the system checks itself continuously. A lost input, a microphone fault, a capsule communication failure or a power problem raises an alarm on the bridge panel.
  6. Power failure: if both main and emergency supplies are lost, the VDR switches to its dedicated reserve source and keeps recording bridge audio for 2 hours. It then stops automatically.
  7. Save: after an incident, the master or OOW uses the save function so the relevant period is preserved and not overwritten. Data is then downloaded with the maker's playback software.

Routine checks and the annual performance test

Daily and watch checks. These are usually set out in the company SMS bridge checklists.

  • The status panel shows normal operation with no unacknowledged alarms.
  • Investigate any fault alarm, log it and report it to the master. Do not keep silencing it.

Periodic checks. Frequency depends on the SMS and the maker's manual.

  • Microphone test using the built-in test function where fitted.
  • Visually inspect the fixed capsule. It should be secure, clean and undamaged, with legible markings.
  • Check the expiry dates on the underwater locating beacon battery and on the float-free capsule's hydrostatic release and battery. Replace them as the label and manual require.
  • Make sure the float-free capsule is clear to float free, with no lashings, paint or added obstructions.
  • Check the date of the reserve power battery.

Annual performance test (APT). SOLAS Chapter V, Regulation 18 requires the VDR, including all its sensors, to undergo an annual performance test by an approved testing or servicing facility. The technician checks that every input is being recorded, that data can be recovered and played back, that the audio is intelligible, and that the protective capsules and beacons are serviceable. A certificate of compliance is issued and must be kept on board.

PSC point. Port state control commonly finds an expired APT certificate, an inoperative VDR or a beacon past its battery date. Track these dates in the planned maintenance system.

A defective VDR is a SOLAS equipment defect. The master normally informs the company and, where the SMS requires, the flag Administration or RO. Repairs, or a condition imposed by the flag, may be needed before departure. How this is handled varies by flag and company, so follow your SMS.

After an incident and safety precautions

Worked scenario. At 0315 your ship has a close-quarters situation, and later a contact with a fishing vessel is reported. What should the bridge team do?

  1. Make the ship and people safe first. The VDR comes after the emergency.
  2. The master, or the OOW on the master's instruction, operates the save function as soon as practicable so that the period is not overwritten.
  3. Record the time of the save in the log book.
  4. Do not switch off, reset or tamper with the VDR. Do not try to download or delete data without the company's instruction.
  5. Inform the company. It will arrange download and preservation, and investigators may require the data.

Under the IMO Casualty Investigation Code, investigating authorities can obtain VDR data. The shipowner owns the data, but the SMS will require it to be preserved and made available. Deliberately losing VDR data after an accident is treated very seriously.

Safety precautions when working on the VDR

  • Isolate power under the ship's lock-out tag-out and permit-to-work procedures before opening the main unit.
  • Work at the fixed or float-free capsule usually means working aloft on the monkey island, so the working aloft precautions apply.
  • Do not paint over capsules, release units or labels.
  • Do not open capsules on board. Only the maker or investigators open them.

Oral exam style questions

Q: What is the purpose of the VDR? A: To keep a secure, retrievable record of the ship's position, movement, physical status, command and control in the period around an incident, for casualty investigation.

Q: Name the data items recorded. A: Give the list in a logical order: date/time, position, speed, heading, bridge audio, VHF audio, radar, ECDIS, depth, alarms, rudder order and response, engine order and response, hull openings, watertight and fire doors, hull stress, wind, AIS and rolling motion. Say 'where fitted' for the optional items.

Q: What happens on total power failure? A: The dedicated reserve source keeps bridge audio recording for 2 hours, then recording stops automatically.

Q: Which regulation requires annual testing, and who does it? A: SOLAS Chapter V, Regulation 18. An approved testing or servicing facility carries it out, and a certificate of compliance is kept on board.

Q: You have just had a collision. What do you do with the VDR? A: After immediate safety actions, save the data, log the time, do not tamper with the unit, and inform the company.

Q: Difference between fixed and float-free capsules? A: The fixed capsule stays with the wreck and is located by its underwater beacon. The float-free capsule releases hydrostatically, floats and indicates its position.

Examiners want structured answers and the correct regulation chapter. If you are unsure of a resolution number or duration, do not guess. Say 'per the equipment's approved standard' instead.

Key points
  • The VDR is required by SOLAS Chapter V, Regulation 20; applicability depends on ship type, tonnage and build date.
  • It records position, heading, speed, bridge and VHF audio, radar/ECDIS images, alarms, and helm and engine orders and responses.
  • Units to MSC.333(90) keep at least 48 hours on the capsules and at least 30 days on the long-term medium.
  • On total power loss, the reserve source keeps bridge audio recording for 2 hours.
  • SOLAS V/18 requires an annual performance test by an approved facility, with a certificate kept on board.
  • After an incident, save the data, log the time, never tamper, and inform the company.
  • Track beacon battery, HRU and APT expiry dates; they are frequent PSC deficiencies.

Frequently asked questions

Is the VDR the same as a 'black box' on an aircraft?

The idea is the same: a protected recorder used to reconstruct events after an accident. A ship's VDR records a wider range of navigational data, including radar and ECDIS images and bridge audio. Besides its protective capsules, it also has a long-term medium on board.

How long does a VDR record?

It depends on the standard the unit was approved to. VDRs to MSC.333(90), installed on or after 1 July 2014, keep at least 48 hours on the capsules and at least 30 days on the long-term medium. Units installed before June 2008 may hold only 12 hours, while those installed between 2008 and 2014 generally hold 48 hours. Check your equipment documentation.

Who can access VDR data after an incident?

The shipowner owns the data. Casualty investigation authorities can obtain it under the IMO Casualty Investigation Code and national law. On board, the master saves the data and the company arranges download. Crew should not download or delete data without instruction.

What happens if the VDR is defective before sailing?

It is a SOLAS equipment defect. The master reports it to the company, and the flag Administration or RO may need to be informed. The exact process, and whether the ship may sail, depend on the flag and the company SMS.

Can the crew test the VDR themselves?

Crew carry out routine checks such as the status display, microphone self-tests and capsule inspection, as the SMS and maker require. The annual performance test under SOLAS V/18 must be done by an approved testing or servicing facility.

Test yourself

Which SOLAS chapter contains the carriage requirement for the VDR?

VDR carriage is in SOLAS Chapter V (Safety of Navigation), Regulation 20. Chapter III covers life-saving appliances and Chapter IV covers radiocommunications.

After a total loss of main and emergency power, what does the VDR continue to record from its reserve source?

The dedicated reserve power source keeps bridge audio recording for 2 hours, after which recording stops automatically.

Who must carry out the VDR annual performance test?

SOLAS Chapter V, Regulation 18 requires an approved testing or servicing facility to carry out the annual performance test. A certificate of compliance is kept on board.

After a collision, once the immediate emergency is under control, what is the correct action regarding the VDR?

Saving prevents the data being overwritten. Switching off, unauthorised download or removing the capsule risks losing or compromising the evidence.

For VDRs to MSC.333(90), what is the minimum recording duration on the long-term recording medium?

MSC.333(90) requires at least 30 days on the long-term medium. 48 hours applies to the capsules, and 12 hours was the minimum for units installed before June 2008.
