Working on a Fishing Vessel

Quick answer

Fishing vessels catch, process and store fish at sea. They are governed largely by their own instruments (STCW-F and ILO C188) rather than the merchant STCW and MLC framework. Expect hard physical work, long trips, serious hazards from gear and stability, and certification rules that differ from merchant ships and from one flag to another.

What a fishing vessel does and how it is laid out

A fishing vessel finds, catches, handles and preserves fish. Larger vessels also process the catch on board. Unlike a cargo ship, the 'cargo' is caught by the crew, so the deck is a working factory floor as much as a ship.

Main types you will meet:

  • Trawlers tow a net along the seabed (bottom trawl) or through the water column (midwater/pelagic). Freezer and factory trawlers process and freeze at sea.
  • Purse seiners encircle a shoal with a net, then close the bottom. Tuna seiners usually carry a skiff and use a power block to haul.
  • Longliners set lines carrying thousands of baited hooks, hauled with a line hauler.
  • Gillnetters, pot/creel boats and pole-and-line vessels are common in coastal and some distant-water fisheries.
  • Carriers/reefers take catch from fishing vessels for transport. Rules for transhipment depend on the fishery and flag.

The typical layout has a wheelhouse forward or amidships with good all-round visibility. Aft is a working deck with:

  • trawl winches, net drums and a gantry or A-frame
  • often a stern ramp

Below are the fish handling and processing spaces and the holds. Holds may be insulated and iced, refrigerated sea water (RSW) tanks, or freezer holds served by plate or blast freezers. The refrigeration plant is a major part of the engine department's job.

Which rules apply (and which do not)

This is the first thing merchant officers get wrong. Several familiar instruments exclude fishing vessels or apply only partly:

Instrument Position for fishing vessels
STCW Convention Fishing vessels are excluded. Certification is covered by STCW-F 1995, which applies to personnel on seagoing fishing vessels. Skipper and deck officer certification applies on vessels of 24 m and over, and basic safety training applies to all fishing vessel personnel.
MLC 2006 Excludes vessels engaged in fishing. Fishers' working and living conditions are covered by ILO Work in Fishing Convention, 2007 (C188) where the flag has ratified it.
SOLAS Chapter I excludes fishing vessels from most chapters. Chapter V (navigation safety) applies to all ships unless it says otherwise.
COLREG Applies in full. Fishing-specific provisions appear in Rules 3, 9(c), 10(i), 18, 26 and 35 and in Annex II.
MARPOL Applies, with requirements depending on size and annex.

The international safety convention for fishing vessels, the Cape Town Agreement 2012, has not entered into force at the time of writing. Construction and equipment standards therefore come mainly from flag state rules. Some regions, such as the EU, have their own regimes.

Bottom line: ask what the flag requires. Do not assume your merchant certificates cover you.

Certificates and experience usually expected

STCW-F sets minimum standards for:

  • skippers and deck officers on fishing vessels of 24 m and over
  • chief and second engineer officers on fishing vessels of 750 kW propulsion power or more
  • radio personnel

It also requires basic safety training for all fishing vessel personnel before they are assigned shipboard duties.

In practice you should expect:

  • Basic safety training (survival, fire, first aid, personal safety). Some administrations accept STCW basic training, and others want a fishing-specific course.
  • A medical fitness certificate issued to the standard the flag accepts. C188 requires fishers on larger or longer-voyage vessels to hold one.
  • A fishing CoC for skipper, mate or engineer roles, issued or recognised by the flag. Some administrations will endorse or convert a merchant STCW CoC. Others require separate fishing exams. Check before you sign.
  • GMDSS radio certification if you will keep a radio watch.
  • Practical deck experience for deckhand roles: splicing, net mending, knife and gear handling. Many skippers value this above paperwork.

A career warning for cadets and junior officers: sea service on fishing vessels may not count towards a merchant STCW CoC. Some administrations accept part of it and some none. Confirm with your administration in writing before you take a fishing contract to build sea time.

Operations and key hazards

Fishing is widely recognised, including by the ILO, as one of the most dangerous occupations. The main hazards are:

  • Winches, warps and blocks. Trawl warps under tension can part or jump a sheave. Snap-back zones and pinch points on net drums cause crushing injuries and amputations. Never step over a moving warp.
  • Stability. Several factors erode stability:
    • catch on deck
    • water trapped behind bulwarks
    • free surface in RSW tanks and holds
    • topside icing in cold regions
    • heavy gear lifted from a high point

The most dangerous case is a fastener: trawl gear snagged on the seabed. A heavy pull on the warps, especially from abeam, can heel and capsize a vessel very quickly. Know where your warp release or brake is and when to use it. - Man overboard. Low freeboard, open stern ramps and wet decks make falls overboard a real risk, especially at night and in heavy weather. Wear your PFD and use safety lines where the SMS or skipper requires them. - Enclosed spaces. Fish holds and RSW tanks can be oxygen-deficient or contain toxic gases such as hydrogen sulphide from decomposing catch. Refrigeration plants may use ammonia or other refrigerants. Treat holds as enclosed spaces and test before entry. - Fatigue. Hauling continues as long as the fish do. Fatigue drives knife injuries, falls and navigation errors. C188 sets minimum rest requirements, and the flag sets the detail. - Cold, wet and manual handling, plus knife and processing-line injuries in factory spaces.

Crew composition and life on board

Crew size varies hugely, from a handful on a coastal boat to large crews on factory trawlers and tuna seiners. A typical distant-water vessel carries:

  • Skipper (master) and one or more mates. The skipper usually also directs the fishing.
  • Chief engineer and second engineer, plus motormen. Refrigeration duties often fall to the engine department or a dedicated refrigeration engineer.
  • Bosun and deckhands/fishermen handling gear and catch.
  • Factory or processing crew on processing vessels.
  • Cook.
  • Sometimes a fisheries observer, who is not crew but must be accommodated and given access.

Life on board is different from merchant ships:

  • Trips can run for weeks or months, and some vessels stay at sea for long periods while carriers take the catch.
  • Watches give way to the fishing cycle.
  • Accommodation is usually tighter.
  • Communication with home may be limited.
  • Pay arrangements may include a share of the catch. Make sure your written work agreement states exactly how pay is calculated.

C188 requires a written work agreement and decent accommodation and food. It also requires access to medical care and repatriation, where the flag has implemented it.

How to get a first contract safely

Step by step:

  1. Decide your route. Coastal or national fishing builds skills and local certificates. Distant-water vessels under foreign flags are a bigger commitment.
  2. Get the certificates the flag actually requires. Ask the agency to name the flag, then confirm the requirements with that administration or your own.
  3. Use only a licensed recruitment agency under your national rules. In the Philippines, check the agency with the Department of Migrant Workers. In India, check with the DG Shipping and the relevant authorities.
  4. Refuse to pay recruitment fees. C188 says fishers should not be charged for recruitment.
  5. Read the work agreement before departure. Check the vessel name and flag, duration, pay basis and payment method, rest hours, food, medical cover and repatriation.
  6. Keep your passport and documents. Hand them over only as required by law, and keep copies at home.
  7. Know who to contact. Before you sail, note your agency, your embassy or consulate, and a seafarers' welfare organisation.

Abuse on some distant-water fleets, including withheld wages and confiscated passports, is well documented. Checking first protects you.

Oral exam style: questions examiners like

Q: Is a vessel trolling 'engaged in fishing'? No. Rule 3 excludes trolling lines and other apparatus that do not restrict manoeuvrability. That vessel shows ordinary underway lights and gets no Rule 18 privileges.

Q: Lights for a trawler making way? She shows:

  • two all-round lights in a vertical line, green over white
  • a masthead light abaft and higher than the green, which is mandatory at 50 m or more in length and optional below
  • sidelights and sternlight

Q: Fishing other than trawling, gear extending more than 150 m? She shows red over white all-round lights. She also shows an all-round white light or a cone apex upwards in the direction of the gear.

Q: Sound signal in restricted visibility? One prolonged followed by two short, at intervals of not more than two minutes. It is also given when engaged in fishing at anchor.

Q: What do you do if the trawl comes fast on the bottom? You need to say three things:

  • reduce power
  • bring the vessel head to the warps and avoid any pull from abeam
  • be ready to slack or release the warps rather than lose stability
Key points
  • STCW and MLC 2006 exclude fishing vessels; STCW-F and ILO C188 cover them instead.
  • COLREG applies in full; fishing-specific provisions appear in Rules 3, 9(c), 10(i), 18, 26 and 35 and in Annex II.
  • Stability is the killer: catch on deck, free surface, icing and snagged gear can capsize a vessel.
  • Treat fish holds and RSW tanks as enclosed spaces and test before entry.
  • Fishing sea time may not count towards a merchant CoC, so confirm with your administration first.
  • Use a licensed agency, pay no recruitment fees and read your work agreement before sailing.

Frequently asked questions

Can I work on a fishing vessel with my merchant STCW certificates?

Sometimes, but not automatically. STCW does not apply to fishing vessels, so certification falls under STCW-F and flag state rules. Some flags accept or convert merchant CoCs and STCW basic training, while others require fishing-specific courses or exams. Check with the flag administration before signing.

Does sea time on a fishing vessel count towards my merchant CoC?

It depends entirely on your national administration. Some accept part of it under conditions, and others accept none. Get written confirmation before using a fishing contract to build sea time for a merchant certificate.

Is the MLC 2006 applicable on fishing vessels?

No. MLC 2006 excludes vessels engaged in fishing. Fishers are covered by the ILO Work in Fishing Convention, 2007 (C188) where the flag state has ratified and implemented it, and otherwise by national law.

What is the most dangerous part of working on a trawler?

Gear handling and stability are the biggest risks. Moving warps, drums and blocks cause serious injuries. Snagged gear, heavy catch on deck or free surface can heel or capsize the vessel. Falls overboard from low working decks are another major cause of deaths.

Should I pay an agency to get a fishing job abroad?

No. C188 provides that fishers should not be charged recruitment fees. Use only agencies licensed under your national rules, such as the Department of Migrant Workers in the Philippines. Never hand over your passport except where the law requires it.

Test yourself

Under COLREG, which vessel is NOT 'engaged in fishing'?

Rule 3 excludes trolling lines and other apparatus that do not restrict manoeuvrability. Reference: COLREG Rule 3(d).

Which instrument sets certification standards for skippers of seagoing fishing vessels of 24 m and over?

STCW excludes fishing vessels. STCW-F covers fishing vessel personnel. Reference: STCW-F 1995, Chapter II.

What all-round lights does a vessel engaged in trawling show in a vertical line?

Trawling is green over white. Red over white is for fishing other than trawling. Reference: COLREG Rule 26.

A trawler's gear comes fast on the seabed. What is the main danger to avoid?

A snagged trawl pulling from abeam can rapidly destroy stability, so the warps must be eased or released if needed. Reference: flag state fishing vessel stability guidance and company procedures.

In restricted visibility, what sound signal does a vessel engaged in fishing give?

One prolonged and two short, at intervals of not more than two minutes, underway or at anchor. Reference: COLREG Rule 35(c) and (d).
