Fuel Changeover for ECA Compliance: A Practical Guide for Marine Engineers

Quick answer

Ships without an approved equivalent compliance method (such as a scrubber) or a compliant alternative fuel such as LNG must burn fuel of 0.10% m/m sulphur or less inside an Emission Control Area. The fuel system must be fully flushed of higher-sulphur fuel before the ship crosses the ECA boundary, following a written changeover procedure, with times, positions and tank volumes recorded. The engineering challenge is doing this without thermal shock, viscosity problems or fuel incompatibility that can stop the engine at the worst possible moment.

Why changeover is needed: the regulatory picture

MARPOL Annex VI, Regulation 14 sets two sulphur limits:

  • Outside ECAs: 0.50% m/m (global cap since 1 January 2020).
  • Inside SOx ECAs: 0.10% m/m (since 1 January 2015).

The established SOx ECAs are the Baltic Sea, North Sea, North American and United States Caribbean Sea areas. The Mediterranean Sea SOx ECA took effect on 1 May 2025. Further ECAs, such as the Canadian Arctic and Norwegian Sea, have been adopted with later effective dates. Always check the current list in company circulars and the passage plan.

A common misconception is that VLSFO (0.50%) is ECA-compliant. It is not. Inside an ECA you need one of two things:

  • a 0.10% fuel, usually MGO, or MDO/ULSFO grades certified at 0.10% S or less; or
  • an approved equivalent, such as an exhaust gas cleaning system.

Where a ship uses separate fuels, Regulation 14 requires:

  • a written changeover procedure on board;
  • enough time for the fuel oil service system to be fully flushed of non-compliant fuel before entering the ECA;
  • the volume of low-sulphur fuel in each tank, plus the date, time and position when changeover is completed before entry (or started after exit), recorded in the logbook prescribed by the Administration.

Which book that is varies by flag and company SMS. It is often the engine log, the deck log or a dedicated ECA/fuel changeover record.

Port States also apply local rules. For example, the EU requires 0.10% fuel at berth under Directive (EU) 2016/802, and California has its own state regulation. Check port requirements during passage planning.

How the changeover works in the system

On a typical two-stroke installation, the changeover is made at a three-way valve upstream of the supply pumps. This valve selects either the HFO/VLSFO service tank or the MGO service tank.

Downstream, fuel passes through the mixing (venting/de-aeration) tank, circulating pumps, heaters or viscosity controller, filters and the engine fuel pumps. Excess fuel returns to the mixing tank.

Because the circulating loop returns to the mixing tank, the old fuel is not pushed out in one plug. It is diluted gradually. New fuel enters only at the rate the engines consume it, so the sulphur content of the loop falls roughly exponentially:

S(t) ≈ S_new + (S_old − S_new) × e^(−Q·t / V)

where Q is the consumption rate and V is the volume of the mixing loop.

Illustrative worked example only. Use your ship's own figures and the maker's or company's calculation tool. Suppose you change from 0.50% VLSFO to 0.05% MGO and target 0.08% at the boundary, leaving a margin below 0.10%:

  1. Fraction of old fuel allowed = (0.08 − 0.05) / (0.50 − 0.05) ≈ 0.067.
  2. ln(1/0.067) ≈ 2.7, so the loop needs about 2.7 loop-volumes of consumption.
  3. Add the plug-flow time for the pipework between the three-way valve and the mixing tank.
  4. Add a margin for slower consumption, for example if the bridge reduces speed.

Some key points follow from this:

  • Changeover time depends on loop volume and consumption, not on a fixed number of hours.
  • At low load the flush takes longer.
  • Any change in arrival time at the boundary means recalculating.

Step-by-step: changing over before entering an ECA

Your company procedure governs the details. A typical sequence is:

Planning (days and hours before)

  • Agree the boundary crossing time with the bridge and calculate the start time, including a margin.
  • Confirm the MGO quantity in each tank is enough for the whole ECA transit plus a reserve.
  • Confirm the bunker delivery note and the fuel analysis report (from the commercial or ship's sample) show 0.10% or less.
  • Check compatibility between the outgoing and incoming fuels. A spot test kit or laboratory advice is useful, especially when going from VLSFO or HFO to a paraffinic fuel.
  • Test the MGO side: tank valves, supply pumps, MGO cooler or chiller, and filters.
  • Check whether boilers and auxiliary engines change over at the same time. Boilers often need a different atomiser or settings for distillate.
  • Note the maker's instructions on cylinder oil (BN) and any recommended load reduction during changeover.

Execution

  • Inform the bridge and the Chief Engineer. Some companies require the engine on bridge control with an engineer standing by in the ECR.
  • Reduce heating gradually. Switch the viscosity controller to the mode the maker specifies and cut steam tracing to the lines that will carry distillate.
  • Operate the three-way valve. Some systems are automated and ramp gradually.
  • Monitor the rate of temperature change at engine inlet and keep within the maker's limit. Also monitor viscosity, supply and circulating pressures, and filter differential pressure.
  • Bring the MGO cooler in as inlet temperature falls, so viscosity stays above the engine maker's minimum.
  • Change cylinder lubricating oil to the BN and feed rate the maker specifies, at the time they specify.

Completion

  • Changeover is complete when the system is fully flushed, not when the valve is turned.
  • Record date, time, position and the volume of compliant fuel in each tank in the prescribed log.
  • Draw an in-use sample if your company procedure requires one.

On exit the process is reversed. Start the changeover only after leaving the ECA. Bring heating up gradually, take the cooler out, restore steam tracing and record the same details.

Faults, symptoms and causes

Most changeover problems come from three things: temperature, viscosity and compatibility.

Symptom Likely cause
Fuel pump plunger or injection valve sticking, engine slowdown or shutdown Thermal shock from changing temperature too fast; differential expansion of plunger and barrel
Low fuel pressure, poor starting or slow response at low load Viscosity too low. Distillate is too hot, increasing internal leakage in pumps and injectors
Pressure fluctuations, vapour or gassing in the circulating system Distillate overheated in a loop still carrying HFO-level heat
Rapid rise in filter differential pressure, sludge in filters or purifiers Incompatibility. Asphaltenes precipitating when residual and distillate mix
Boiler flame failure or unstable flame Burner settings, atomiser or pressure not suited to distillate
Increased wear or scuffing (long term) Low lubricity of very low-sulphur distillate; wrong cylinder oil BN
Leaks at flanges or seals after changeover Seal shrinkage or temperature cycling of fittings

In traffic near the coast, a loss of propulsion at the ECA boundary is a navigational emergency, not just an engine room fault. Port States have specifically flagged changeover-related propulsion losses as a concern.

Checks, maintenance and safety

Routine checks and maintenance

  • Exercise the MGO system before you need it, particularly if the ship has been running on VLSFO for months. Check valves, pumps, cooler, filters and level gauges.
  • Keep the three-way valve and any automatic changeover unit in the PMS, and test the indication.
  • Maintain the MGO cooler or chiller. A fouled cooler means you cannot hold minimum viscosity.
  • Keep the fuel injection equipment in good condition. Worn pumps lose more delivery on low-viscosity fuel.
  • Keep MARPOL samples, BDNs and fuel analysis reports in order. Port State inspectors will ask for them alongside the changeover records.

Safety

  • Hot surfaces and fuel leaks are the classic engine room fire combination. Check screening and jacketed pipes after changeover.
  • SOLAS Chapter II-2, Regulation 4 generally limits fuel flashpoint to not less than 60°C. Distillate still needs careful handling and good leak containment.
  • Do not take shortcuts on gradual heating or cooling to save time. Start earlier instead.
  • If compliant fuel cannot be obtained despite best efforts, MARPOL Annex VI, Regulation 18 provides for a fuel oil non-availability report to the flag and the relevant port. It is not an automatic exemption.

Oral exam: questions examiners like to ask

  • What is the sulphur limit inside an ECA, and since when? 0.10% m/m, since 1 January 2015, under MARPOL Annex VI Regulation 14.
  • When must changeover be complete? Before entering the ECA, with the system fully flushed. On exit, it may only begin after leaving.
  • What must be recorded? Volume of low-sulphur fuel in each tank, plus date, time and position of completion or commencement, in the log prescribed by the Administration.
  • Why not change over instantly? Thermal shock to fuel pumps and injectors, viscosity falling below minimum, gassing, and incompatibility sludge.
  • How do you calculate flushing time? From loop volume and consumption (dilution), plus pipework volume and a margin. Lower load means a longer flush.
  • What else changes besides the fuel? Heating, steam tracing, viscosity control mode, MGO cooler, cylinder oil BN and feed rate, and boiler burner arrangements.
  • What if compliant fuel is unavailable? Report under Regulation 18 with evidence of the efforts made to obtain it.

Examiners usually follow up with: 'The engine slows down five minutes after you opened the three-way valve. What do you check?'

Inform the bridge first. Then talk through the inlet temperature trend, viscosity, pressures, filter differential pressure and fuel pump condition.

Key points
  • Inside an ECA the limit is 0.10% m/m sulphur; 0.50% VLSFO is not compliant.
  • The system must be fully flushed before the boundary; turning the valve is not completion.
  • A written changeover procedure and logged times, positions and tank volumes are mandatory.
  • Flushing time depends on loop volume and consumption, so recalculate if speed or ETA changes.
  • Change temperature gradually and hold viscosity above the engine maker's minimum, using the MGO cooler.
  • Check fuel compatibility and watch filter differential pressure for sludge.
  • Remember the cylinder oil BN, the boilers and the auxiliary engines, not just the main engine.

Frequently asked questions

Is VLSFO compliant inside an Emission Control Area?

No. VLSFO is a 0.50% sulphur fuel, which meets the global cap but not the 0.10% ECA limit. Inside an ECA you need 0.10% fuel (MGO, or MDO or ULSFO meeting 0.10%) or an approved equivalent such as an exhaust gas cleaning system.

How long before the ECA boundary should fuel changeover start?

There is no fixed number of hours in MARPOL. The time depends on the volume of the fuel system, the engine consumption, the sulphur content of both fuels and a safety margin. Use the ship's calculation tool or the company procedure, and recalculate if the ETA or speed changes.

Where do I record the ECA fuel changeover?

MARPOL requires the record in the logbook prescribed by the Administration. Depending on flag and company SMS, this may be the engine log, the deck log or a dedicated changeover record. Record the date, time and position, and the volume of compliant fuel in each tank.

Why does the engine sometimes stop during fuel changeover?

The usual causes are thermal shock seizing fuel pump plungers, viscosity dropping too low so pumps lose delivery, gassing of hot distillate, or filters choking with incompatibility sludge. Gradual temperature change, a working MGO cooler and a compatibility check prevent most cases.

Do ships with scrubbers need to change over fuel?

Not if the exhaust gas cleaning system is approved and operating so that emissions are equivalent to the ECA limit. If the scrubber fails, the ship must follow its flag and company contingency procedure, which commonly involves changing to compliant fuel and notifying the relevant authorities.

Test yourself

What is the maximum fuel sulphur content for a ship without a scrubber operating inside a SOx ECA?

The ECA limit has been 0.10% m/m since 1 January 2015; 0.50% is the global cap. Ref: MARPOL Annex VI, Regulation 14.

When is a fuel changeover for ECA entry considered complete?

The regulation requires enough time for the service system to be fully flushed before entry; turning the valve only starts the dilution. Ref: MARPOL Annex VI, Regulation 14.

Why must the rate of temperature change be controlled during changeover from heated residual fuel to distillate?

Rapid cooling causes differential expansion between plunger and barrel, which can seize them. The maker's limit on the rate of change must be followed.

Which of these is required to be recorded for an ECA changeover?

Regulation 14 requires the volume of compliant fuel in each tank, with the date, time and position of completion or commencement, in the log prescribed by the Administration.

After changeover, filter differential pressure rises rapidly and the purifiers show heavy sludge. What is the most likely cause?

Mixing incompatible fuels can destabilise asphaltenes and form sludge, which chokes filters and purifiers. A compatibility check before changeover reduces the risk.
