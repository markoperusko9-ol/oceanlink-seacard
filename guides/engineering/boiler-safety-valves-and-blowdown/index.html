Boiler Safety Valves and Blowdown: A Practical Guide for Marine Engineers

Quick answer

Boiler safety valves are the last protection against over-pressure. They must be correctly set, sealed, kept free to lift and tested to the surveyor's satisfaction. Blowdown is how you remove sludge, floating contamination and excess dissolved solids so the boiler water stays within the limits of the treatment programme. This guide explains both, how to operate and maintain them safely, and what examiners ask.

Why boilers need safety valves and blowdown

A boiler is a pressure vessel holding a large mass of water at saturation temperature. If pressure rises unchecked, for example from a burner that will not cut out or a stop valve shut against a firing boiler, the stored energy can be released violently.

SOLAS Chapter II-1, Regulation 32 requires every steam boiler and unfired steam generator to have at least two safety valves of adequate capacity. The Administration may accept a single valve, having regard to the boiler's output or other features, if it is satisfied that adequate over-pressure protection is provided. Class rules then govern the design, capacity, setting and survey of those valves.

Blowdown deals with a slower threat. Feed water brings in dissolved and suspended solids, treatment chemicals add more, and evaporation concentrates them all in the boiler. Left alone, they cause:

  • Sludge and scale on heating surfaces, which leads to overheating and tube failure
  • Foaming and priming, which carries water over into the steam lines and can cause water hammer
  • Corrosion where deposits trap concentrated chemicals

You fight this with two tools working together. Chemical treatment keeps impurities in a harmless form. Blowdown physically removes them.

How a boiler safety valve works

Most marine boilers use spring-loaded safety valves. Older and smaller installations often have the improved high-lift type. Larger plant may have full-lift or pilot-operated designs. The basic sequence is the same for all of them.

  1. Closed. Spring force, set by the compression nut, holds the valve lid on its seat against boiler pressure.
  2. Set pressure reached. Steam force on the lid equals the spring force. The lid starts to lift, and a small simmer may come first.
  3. Rapid lift. As the lid rises, escaping steam acts on a larger area, such as a lip, a huddling chamber or a floating ring. In the improved high-lift type it also acts on a waste piston. The valve snaps open to give full discharge capacity instead of just cracking open.
  4. Discharge. Steam goes through the waste steam pipe to atmosphere above the funnel.
  5. Reseat. The valve closes only when pressure has fallen somewhat below the set pressure. That difference is the valve's blowdown, a different meaning of the word from boiler blowdown. It stops the valve chattering on and off. On some designs it can be adjusted with a blowdown or adjusting ring.

Other fittings and arrangements to know:

  • Easing gear lets you lift the valve by hand, usually from a safe remote position. You use it to prove the valve is free and to release pressure in an emergency.
  • Waste steam pipe drain. There is a drain at the lowest point of the escape pipe. Condensate collecting there adds weight and back-pressure, and it can freeze or cause water hammer. Keep this drain clear.
  • Superheater safety valve. Where a superheater is fitted, its valve is set to lift before the drum valves. Steam then keeps flowing through the superheater tubes, which would otherwise overheat with no flow through them.
  • Exhaust gas economiser. An economiser that can be isolated from the boiler needs its own over-pressure protection. Check your ship's arrangement drawing.

Setting, sealing and the accumulation test

Safety valves are set and sealed under class supervision, normally at boiler survey after overhaul. The working pressure, the set pressures and any permitted tolerances come from the boiler's approved plans and class rules. Take them from the boiler certificate and maker's manual, not from memory.

Typical setting sequence (follow the maker's and surveyor's instructions):

  1. Overhaul the valves. Lap the seats and lids, check the spindle for straightness, check the spring and confirm it is free in its guides.
  2. Raise boiler pressure slowly with the main stop valve controlled.
  3. Gag the valve or valves you are not setting, using the maker's gag only.
  4. Adjust the compression nut on the valve being set until it lifts and reseats at the required pressure. Use a calibrated test gauge.
  5. Repeat for the other valves. The superheater valve is set to lift first.
  6. Remove every gag and have it physically checked and counted.
  7. Fit the locking arrangement and seal. The surveyor records the set pressures.
  8. Test the easing gear.

Accumulation of pressure test. The main steam stop is shut and the boiler is fired at full rate. The safety valves must discharge all the steam generated, so that pressure does not rise beyond the limit class allows above working pressure within the test period. This proves the valves have enough capacity, not just the right set pressure.

On boilers with superheaters, firing with no steam flow can damage the superheater tubes. Class may accept a capacity calculation or a modified test instead.

Boiler blowdown: bottom blow and scum blow

There are two kinds of blowdown, and they do different jobs.

Bottom blowdown Scum (surface) blowdown
Takes water from Lowest point of drum or shell Just below normal water level
Removes Settled sludge and deposits Floating oil, foam, suspended matter, high dissolved solids
When Intermittent, at low firing rate or when sludge has settled As needed, e.g. after oil contamination or on high TDS/chlorides
Effect on level Large, quick drop Smaller, more controlled

Bottom blowdown works best when circulation is slow, so that sludge has settled. Doing it at high firing rate disturbs circulation in water-tube boilers and is less effective.

When to blow down depends on boiler water test results: chlorides, dissolved solids, alkalinity and treatment chemical reserve. Compare these with the limits in the chemical supplier's programme and the company SMS. Do not blow down on a fixed habit.

Step-by-step bottom blow. This is a typical sequence. Always follow your ship's procedure.

  1. Check that overboard discharge is permitted in your current position. Local port rules may prohibit it.
  2. Inform the watchkeeper or bridge as your procedure requires. Check the water level is high in the glass and the feed pump is available.
  3. Open the ship's side (overboard) valve fully.
  4. Open the blowdown valve nearest the boiler fully.
  5. Crack open the second (regulating) valve slowly to warm the line and avoid water hammer. Then open it as required.
  6. Watch the gauge glass continuously. Do not let the level fall below the safe working minimum.
  7. Close in reverse order: regulating valve, boiler valve, then ship's side valve.
  8. Restore the level and record the blowdown in the log.

Blowdown cocks are often designed so the key or spanner can only be removed with the cock closed. Where several boilers share a blowdown line, non-return arrangements stop one boiler blowing into another. Never leave a blowdown valve open and walk away.

Checks and planned maintenance

Your PMS and the maker's manual set the intervals. The jobs below are typical.

Safety valves

  • Test the easing gear at the interval set in your PMS, as far as company procedure allows, to prove the valve is free.
  • Check visually for simmering or weeping at the waste steam pipe outlet.
  • Keep the waste steam pipe drain clear. Water at the outlet when the valve has not lifted means a leak.
  • Check seals and locks are intact. A broken seal must be reported.
  • Overhaul the seats, lids, springs and spindles, then reset the valves, at boiler survey.

Blowdown and gauge glasses

  • Feel or measure the temperature of the blowdown line downstream of a closed valve. A hot line means the valve is passing.
  • Watch for unexplained high make-up feed consumption.
  • Blow through the gauge glasses regularly to prove both the steam and water connections are clear. After blowing through, the level should return promptly and settle. A sluggish or false level means a choked connection.
  • Keep remote level indicators agreeing with the local glasses.

Boiler water

  • Test the water at the frequency in the treatment programme. Act on the trend, not a single reading.

Faults, symptoms and likely causes

Symptom Likely causes Action
Safety valve simmering or leaking below set pressure Damaged or dirty seat, weak spring, distorted spindle, debris Report and plan overhaul. Never 'cure' it by over-tightening or gagging.
Valve chattering (rapid open/close) Blowdown ring wrongly set, excessive inlet pressure loss, high back-pressure from a blocked or waterlogged waste pipe Check the drain and pipe. Adjust only under proper supervision.
Valve fails to lift at set pressure Seized spindle, corrosion, deposits, gag left in, paint on moving parts Treat as an emergency. Reduce firing, control pressure, inform the Chief Engineer.
Valve will not reseat Debris on seat, blowdown setting wrong, mechanical damage Reduce pressure. The boiler may need to be shut down.
Hot blowdown line, high make-up Passing blowdown valve Isolate, report and overhaul at the first safe opportunity.
Water hammer during blowdown Valve opened too fast, cold line Open slowly and warm through.
Priming or foaming High dissolved solids, oil contamination, high water level Scum blow, test the water, look for the source of oil (e.g. a heating coil leak).

Worked scenario. On watch you see the boiler pressure gauge reading above the normal working range. The burner is still firing and no safety valve has lifted.

  1. Stop the burner manually.
  2. Check the pressure switch and the burner control.
  3. Use the easing gear to relieve pressure if it is still rising.
  4. Call the Chief Engineer.

A valve that has not lifted above its set pressure is a serious defect. It must be recorded and reported, and the boiler must not stay in service on the strength of the second valve alone.

Safety rules that are not negotiable

  • Never gag, wedge or tie down a safety valve in service. Gags are for setting under supervision only and must be accounted for afterwards.
  • Never adjust a sealed valve without the Chief Engineer's authority and class involvement as required.
  • Stand clear of the waste steam outlet and blowdown discharge areas when testing.
  • Blowdown valves: open slowly, stay at the valves for the whole operation and keep your eyes on the water level.
  • Work on boiler mountings needs pressure released, double isolation where fitted, lock-out tag-out and a permit to work under your SMS.
  • Entering a boiler, drum or furnace is enclosed space entry, with full procedures.
  • Steam leaks at superheated temperature may be invisible near the source. Approach with caution.

Oral exam: questions examiners like

  • Why at least two safety valves? SOLAS II-1/32 requires it, giving redundancy if one sticks. The Administration may accept one valve, having regard to output or other features, if over-pressure protection is adequate.
  • Why is the superheater safety valve set to lift first? To keep steam flowing through the superheater tubes so they do not overheat.
  • What is accumulation of pressure and how is it tested? It is the rise in pressure with the stop valve shut and the boiler at full firing. It proves the valves have enough relieving capacity within the limit class allows.
  • What does 'blowdown' mean for a safety valve? The difference between the set (lifting) pressure and the reseating pressure.
  • What is the purpose of the drain on the waste steam pipe? To remove condensate that would add back-pressure, cause water hammer or freeze.
  • Bottom blow versus scum blow? A bottom blow removes settled sludge. A scum blow removes floating oil, foam and dissolved solids near the surface.
  • In what order do you open blowdown valves, and why? Ship's side first, then the boiler valve fully, then regulate with the second valve. Close in reverse order. This keeps the boiler valve's seat protected from wire-drawing and leaves the line safe.
  • What would make you suspect a passing blowdown valve? A hot line downstream and high make-up feed.
  • How do you prove a gauge glass is reading correctly? Blow through the steam and water sides. The level should return promptly and agree with the other indicators.
Key points
  • SOLAS II-1/32 requires at least two safety valves of adequate capacity per boiler; the Administration may accept one, having regard to output or other features, if over-pressure protection is adequate.
  • The superheater safety valve is set to lift before the drum valves to protect the superheater tubes.
  • Safety valves are set, sealed and capacity-tested (accumulation) under class supervision, and all gags must be removed and accounted for.
  • A bottom blow removes settled sludge and a scum blow removes surface contamination; both are driven by water test results.
  • Open the ship's side valve first, then the boiler valve, then regulate; close in reverse and never leave blowdown unattended.
  • A hot blowdown line or rising make-up feed points to a passing blowdown valve.
  • Never gag or over-tighten a safety valve in service; a valve that fails to lift is an emergency to report.

Frequently asked questions

How often should boiler safety valves be tested on board?

Easing gear checks are set by your PMS, the maker and company procedures. Full setting and sealing is done at boiler survey under class. Intervals differ between class societies and ships, so check your survey status and PMS rather than relying on a general figure.

What is the difference between safety valve blowdown and boiler blowdown?

Safety valve blowdown is the pressure drop between the valve lifting and reseating. Boiler blowdown is draining water from the boiler to remove sludge and impurities. Same word, completely different meaning.

Can I blow down the boiler in port?

Only if local regulations and your company procedures allow it. Many ports restrict overboard discharges. Check the port information and get permission as your SMS requires.

Why should bottom blowdown be done at low load?

At low firing rate circulation is slow, so sludge settles at the bottom where the blowdown connection can remove it. At high firing it stays in suspension, and in water-tube boilers a heavy blow can disturb circulation.

What should I do if a safety valve is leaking steam continuously?

Report it to the Second or Chief Engineer, monitor it and plan an overhaul. Never stop the leak by tightening the compression nut or gagging the valve, as that alters the set pressure and defeats the protection.

Test yourself

Why is the superheater safety valve set to lift before the drum safety valves?

If the drum valves lifted first, flow through the superheater could stop and the tubes would overheat; setting the superheater valve lower keeps steam flowing. Ref: class boiler rules and maker's instructions.

What does the accumulation of pressure test prove?

With the stop valve shut and the boiler at full firing, the valves must keep the pressure rise within the class limit, which proves relieving capacity. Ref: SOLAS II-1/32 (adequate capacity) and class rules.

When carrying out a bottom blowdown, which valve should be opened first?

Opening the ship's side valve first means the line is clear to discharge before the boiler valves are opened. Ref: maker's instructions and ship's SMS procedures.

A safety valve is simmering slightly below its set pressure. What is the correct action?

Tightening or gagging changes or defeats the set pressure; the valve must be overhauled and reset correctly. Ref: class survey requirements, maker's instructions and company SMS.

Which is the main purpose of scum (surface) blowdown?

The scum blow draws from just below the water surface, removing oil, foam and high dissolved solids; bottom blow handles sludge. Ref: boiler maker's manual and water treatment programme.
