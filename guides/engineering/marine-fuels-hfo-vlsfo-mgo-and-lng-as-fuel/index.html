Marine fuels: HFO, VLSFO, MGO and LNG as fuel

Quick answer

HFO is high-sulphur residual fuel. It needs heating and purifying, and since the 0.50% global sulphur cap it can only be burned with an approved equivalent, in practice an exhaust gas cleaning system (scrubber). VLSFO is the compliant blended alternative, and the main problems with it are stability, compatibility and cat fines. MGO is the distillate used in ECAs and needs attention to viscosity and lubricity. LNG is a cryogenic gas fuel governed by the IGF Code, with its own engines, hazards and training.

Why fuel knowledge matters in the engine room

Fuel is the most expensive consumable on board and a common root cause of engine trouble. A badly handled bunker can lead to:

  • Stuck fuel pump plungers
  • Scuffed liners
  • Choked filters
  • Blackouts
  • A port state detention for a sulphur non-compliance

Examiners expect you to know four things for every fuel type:

  1. What it is
  2. How it is treated before it reaches the engine
  3. What goes wrong with it
  4. Which rules control it

The international quality specification most bunker contracts refer to is ISO 8217. It divides marine fuels into two families:

  • Distillate grades (DM), such as DMA and DMZ.
  • Residual grades (RM), such as RMG and RMK.

Always check which edition of ISO 8217 your charter party or bunker contract quotes, because the limits have changed between editions.

Sulphur content is regulated separately under MARPOL Annex VI, Regulation 14:

  • Outside Emission Control Areas, the global limit has been 0.50% m/m since 1 January 2020.
  • Inside ECAs designated for SOx, the limit is 0.10% m/m.
  • Since March 2020, a ship without an approved equivalent such as a scrubber may not even carry fuel above 0.50% for its own use.

The four fuels compared

Fuel What it is Main handling need Typical use today
HFO (high-sulphur residual) Residue from refining, high viscosity and density Heating for storage, transfer, purification and injection Ships with approved scrubbers
VLSFO (0.50% S) Mostly a blend of residual and distillate components Heating (usually less than HFO), purification, careful segregation Main fuel for non-scrubber ships outside ECAs
MGO / MDO (distillate) Distillate fuel, low viscosity, normally low sulphur Cooling in some installations, lubricity and viscosity watch ECAs, auxiliary engines, emergency generator, boilers in port
LNG Natural gas, mostly methane, liquefied at cryogenic temperature (around −162 °C at atmospheric pressure) Cryogenic storage, vaporisation, gas valve unit, gas safety systems Dual-fuel and gas-fuelled ships under the IGF Code

Flashpoint. SOLAS chapter II-2 sets a minimum flashpoint of 60 °C for liquid fuel oil, with limited exceptions such as some emergency generator fuel. LNG and other low-flashpoint fuels are only permitted through the IGF Code route.

VLSFO quality. VLSFO is not one product. Two VLSFOs from different suppliers can have very different viscosity, density, pour point and aromatic content. That is why you cannot treat VLSFO as "just thinner HFO".

How residual fuel gets from bunker tank to injector

This is the classic oral question. Walk the examiner through the system in order:

  1. Bunker tanks. Fuel is kept warm enough to pump, above its pour point. Tanks are not overheated, which wastes steam and can impair fuel stability.
  2. Transfer pump to settling tank. The settling tank is heated and allows water and heavy sludge to settle. It is drained regularly.
  3. Purifiers and clarifiers. The fuel is heated to the separation temperature recommended by the maker. Centrifuging removes water, sludge and catalytic fines. Conventional gravity-disc purifiers have a density limit. The heaviest residual grades need separators designed for high-density fuel.
  4. Service tank. Clean fuel ready for use. It is drained for water as per routine.
  5. Supply (booster) module. This includes:
    • Supply pumps
    • A mixing (venting) tank
    • Circulating pumps
    • Heaters controlled by a viscosity controller
    • Fine or automatic filters
  6. Engine. Fuel reaches the high-pressure pumps and injectors at the viscosity the engine maker specifies. Excess fuel returns to the mixing tank.

Viscosity, not temperature, is the controlled variable. The viscosity controller adjusts heating until the viscosity at the engine inlet is in the maker's range. Because VLSFOs vary so much, the heating needed for one stem may be quite different from the last.

Distillates are different. MGO may need cooling rather than heating to keep its viscosity above the engine maker's minimum. Some ships have fuel coolers or chillers fitted for this.

Checks, testing and good practice

Before and during bunkering

  • Check the Bunker Delivery Note against the order: grade, quantity, density, sulphur and flashpoint.
  • Under Annex VI Regulation 18, the BDN is kept on board for three years.
  • The MARPOL delivered sample stays under the ship's control until the fuel is substantially consumed, and for not less than 12 months.
  • Draw commercial and testing samples as per company procedure. Send the lab sample promptly.
  • Never bunker into a tank that still holds a different VLSFO unless compatibility has been checked. Segregate wherever possible and wait for the analysis before use, if the voyage allows.

Routine checks

  • Drain settling and service tanks for water.
  • Monitor purifier performance: back pressure, sludge discharge interval and water content after treatment.
  • Track differential pressure across filters. A sudden rise after changing to a new stem is a warning sign.
  • Do a spot test or use an onboard compatibility kit before mixing fuels, if your ship carries one.
  • Match cylinder oil BN to fuel sulphur on two-strokes, following the engine maker's guidance:
    • High-BN oil with low-sulphur fuel can build deposits and polish the liner.
    • Low-BN oil with high-sulphur fuel invites cold corrosion.
  • Check scavenge spaces and piston rings during inspections. They are your evidence of how the fuel and lube oil combination is behaving.

Fuel changeover (HFO/VLSFO to MGO before an ECA)

  • Follow the ship's written procedure.
  • Respect the engine maker's limit on the rate of temperature change. Thermal shock can seize fuel pump plungers.
  • Record the changeover as Annex VI requires: date, time, position and the volume of compliant fuel in each tank.

LNG as fuel: what changes

Which code applies

  • Ships that are not gas carriers but burn LNG are governed by the IGF Code, made mandatory through SOLAS.
  • Gas carriers burning their cargo boil-off fall under the IGC Code instead.
  • Crew with responsibilities for gas-fuelled ships need STCW basic or advanced IGF training under STCW Regulation V/3.

How the fuel system works

  1. LNG is stored in insulated tanks.
  2. It is vaporised and heated to gas.
  3. The gas passes through a gas valve unit. Double block and bleed valves isolate and vent the supply.
  4. It reaches the engine through double-walled pipes or ventilated ducts. These have gas detection and automatic shutdown.

Two main engine types

  • Low-pressure dual-fuel (Otto cycle). Gas is admitted at low pressure and ignited by a small pilot quantity of liquid fuel. These engines can usually meet NOx Tier III in gas mode without aftertreatment. They are sensitive to knocking and misfire, and they have methane slip, which is unburnt methane in the exhaust, a strong greenhouse gas.
  • High-pressure gas injection (Diesel cycle). Gas is injected at high pressure near top dead centre. Methane slip is lower, but the gas supply system works at much higher pressure.

Hazards are different from oil

  • Cryogenic burns and brittle fracture of ordinary steel from LNG spills
  • Flammable gas clouds
  • Asphyxiation in enclosed spaces
  • Boil-off gas pressure build-up if gas is not being consumed

LNG has virtually no sulphur, so it meets the SOx limits without a scrubber.

Safety points for every fuel

Fire

  • Fuel spray on hot surfaces is the classic engine room fire.
  • SOLAS requires jacketed high-pressure fuel lines with leak collection, and insulation of hot surfaces.
  • Report damaged lagging or a leak alarm from jacketed piping immediately. Do not just reset it.

Hydrogen sulphide (H2S)

  • Residual fuels can release H2S into tank vapour spaces.
  • Treat tank vents, sounding pipes and tank entry as gas hazards.
  • Use a personal H2S monitor where company procedures require it.

Enclosed spaces

  • Fuel tanks are enclosed spaces. Follow the full permit and atmosphere-testing procedure before entry.

Pollution

  • Bunkering spills come under SOPEP.
  • Bunkering of fuel must be recorded in Oil Record Book Part I (code H). Record other operations as MARPOL and company procedures require.

Gas

  • On LNG ships, never defeat gas detection or ventilation interlocks.
  • Loss of ventilation in the double-walled pipe or duct annular space (or the relevant conditions in an ESD-protected machinery space) shuts off the gas supply by design.

Oral exam: questions you should be ready for

  • What is the difference between a purifier and a clarifier?
    • A purifier separates oil from water and solids and continuously discharges water.
    • A clarifier removes solids and small water traces.
    • They can be run in series (purifier followed by clarifier as a safeguard) or in parallel at reduced throughput, as the separator maker recommends. Low throughput improves cat-fine removal.
  • Why do we control viscosity and not temperature before the engine? Correct atomisation depends on viscosity. The temperature needed to reach that viscosity changes from bunker to bunker.
  • What are cat fines and why are they dangerous? They are catalyst particles from the refining process, aluminium and silicon compounds. They are hard and abrasive and damage fuel pumps, injectors, liners and rings. The separators are your main defence.
  • What is meant by fuel stability and compatibility?
    • Stability is a fuel's ability to keep asphaltenes dissolved.
    • Compatibility is whether two fuels stay stable when mixed.
    • Incompatible mixtures drop sludge.
  • What MARPOL samples must the ship keep, and for how long?
    • The delivered sample is retained until the fuel is substantially consumed and at least 12 months.
    • Ships must also have designated in-use sampling points under Annex VI Regulation 14 (phased in from 2022/2023), so that in-use or onboard samples can be drawn when the Administration requests them.
  • How would you prepare for entering an ECA?
    • Follow the written changeover procedure and calculate start time from tank volumes.
    • Make the gradual temperature change, then confirm compliance at the engine inlet.
    • Make the logbook entries.
    • Adjust cylinder oil BN as the maker advises.
  • What is methane slip? It is unburnt methane in the exhaust of gas engines. It is highest in low-pressure Otto-cycle designs, particularly at low load.
  • Which code applies to a container ship burning LNG? The IGF Code. The IGC Code applies to gas carriers.
Key points
  • The global sulphur limit is 0.50% m/m and 0.10% m/m in ECAs. High-sulphur HFO may only be carried for use with an approved equivalent such as a scrubber.
  • Residual fuels are heated, settled, purified and delivered at the engine maker's viscosity. Viscosity, not temperature, is the controlled variable.
  • VLSFO's main risks are instability, incompatibility between stems, cat fines and cold flow. Segregate and test before mixing.
  • MGO may need cooling to stay above the minimum injection viscosity. Watch lubricity and fuel pump wear.
  • Match cylinder oil BN to fuel sulphur to avoid both cold corrosion and deposits.
  • LNG-fuelled ships fall under the IGF Code and need STCW Regulation V/3 training. Methane slip and cryogenic hazards are the new issues.
  • Keep the BDN for three years and the MARPOL sample for at least 12 months.

Frequently asked questions

Can a ship still burn HFO after 2020?

Yes, but only if it has an approved equivalent such as an exhaust gas cleaning system (scrubber) that achieves the same SOx reduction. Without one, the ship may not even carry fuel above 0.50% sulphur for its own use. Some ports and coastal states restrict open-loop scrubber discharge, so check local rules for the voyage.

Why is mixing two VLSFOs from different suppliers risky?

VLSFOs are blends and can be very different in composition. A mixture can become unstable, and asphaltenes then drop out as sludge that chokes purifiers and filters. Segregate stems where possible and check compatibility before mixing.

What is the difference between MGO and MDO?

Both are distillate fuels covered by the DM grades of ISO 8217. MGO is normally a pure distillate. MDO-type grades can contain a small amount of residual component and may be slightly heavier and darker. The bunker contract grade, not the trade name, defines what you have received.

Do I need special training to work on an LNG-fuelled ship?

Yes. STCW Regulation V/3 requires basic IGF Code training for seafarers with safety duties related to the gas fuel. Advanced training is required for those with direct responsibility for fuel operations, typically master, engineer officers and anyone directly handling the fuel. Your flag administration issues or endorses the certificate, so check its specific requirements.

How long must the bunker delivery note and MARPOL sample be kept?

Under MARPOL Annex VI Regulation 18, the bunker delivery note is kept on board for three years. The MARPOL delivered sample is retained under the ship's control until the fuel is substantially consumed, and in any case for not less than 12 months.

Test yourself

What is the variable controlled by the viscosity controller in a residual fuel booster module?

The controller adjusts heating to give the engine maker's required viscosity for atomisation. The temperature needed varies with each bunker (engine maker's fuel recommendations; ISO 8217 fuel properties).

What is the maximum sulphur content of fuel used inside a SOx Emission Control Area by a ship without a scrubber?

MARPOL Annex VI Regulation 14 sets 0.10% m/m inside ECAs. The 0.50% limit applies globally outside ECAs.

What is the main purpose of the fuel separators with respect to cat fines?

Centrifuging is the main onboard defence against catalytic fines, which are hard and highly abrasive. Separators do not change flashpoint, sulphur or ignition quality (ISO 8217 cat fines limits; engine maker guidance).

A container ship (not a gas carrier) burns LNG as fuel. Which instrument governs its gas fuel system?

The IGF Code applies to ships, other than gas carriers, using gases or other low-flashpoint fuels. The IGC Code applies to gas carriers (SOLAS chapter II-1; IGF Code).

What is the most likely result of using a high-BN cylinder oil for a long period with very low-sulphur fuel?

Excess alkaline additive with little acid to neutralise forms hard deposits that can polish the liner. Cold corrosion is the opposite risk, from low BN with high-sulphur fuel (engine maker cylinder lubrication guidance).
