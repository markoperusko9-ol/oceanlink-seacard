Heat Exchangers on Ships: How They Work, What Goes Wrong and What Examiners Ask

Quick answer

Ship heat exchangers move heat from one fluid to another without mixing them. They cool lube oil, jacket water and charge air, and they heat fuel. Most are shell-and-tube or gasketed plate types. Their performance falls as fouling builds up, and leaks show up as contamination between the two fluids, so the engineer's job is to watch temperatures, pressures and tank levels and to clean and test the units before they fail.

What heat exchangers do on board

Almost every running machine on a ship depends on a heat exchanger. You will find them in three broad duties:

  • Coolers: main engine lube oil, jacket cooling water, piston cooling (where fitted), charge (scavenge) air, generator engines, stern tube oil, compressors, and the central coolers where sea water takes heat from the low-temperature fresh water circuit.
  • Heaters: fuel oil heaters before the purifiers and the main engine, lube oil purifier heaters and tank heating coils. The heating medium is usually steam or thermal oil.
  • Condensers and evaporators: the steam condenser or cascade tank cooler, refrigeration and air-conditioning condensers and evaporators, and the fresh water generator, which is itself two heat exchangers.

On most modern ships a central cooling system is fitted. Sea water passes through the central coolers and a small number of units such as the fresh water generator condenser, and most other coolers are served by fresh water. This keeps sea water, with its corrosion, fouling and marine growth, out of most of the engine room pipework. On older ships, or on some auxiliary systems, sea water may still cool individual units directly.

How it works: the principle and the main types

Heat flows from the hotter fluid to the colder one through a separating wall, which is a tube or a plate. How much heat is transferred depends on three things:

  • Surface area of the wall.
  • Temperature difference between the fluids. Examiners expect you to know the log mean temperature difference.
  • Overall heat transfer coefficient. This falls as fouling, oil films, scale or air build up on either side.

Counter-flow arrangements, where the fluids move in opposite directions, give a better mean temperature difference than parallel flow. That is why most marine coolers are piped counter-flow or multi-pass.

Type Construction Strengths Weaknesses
Shell-and-tube A tube bundle between tube plates, inside a shell, with water boxes at the ends Robust, tolerates higher pressures and dirty fluids, individual tubes can be plugged Bulky, needs withdrawal space for the bundle, lower efficiency per volume
Gasketed plate Corrugated plates (often titanium for sea water) clamped in a frame, with gaskets directing the flows Compact, efficient, easy to add or remove plates, easy to open Gaskets age and leak, narrow passages foul and block, the clamping dimension is critical
Finned tube / plate-fin Water in tubes, air over fins Suited to charge air coolers, where the air side needs a large area The air side fouls with oil and soot, and condensate must be removed

Expansion. In a shell-and-tube cooler the tubes and the shell expand by different amounts. Designs allow for this with a floating head, U-tubes or an expansion bellows in the shell. A fixed tube plate without one of these relies on a small temperature difference.

Control and normal operation

Temperature control is normally done by a three-way temperature control valve that sends part of the fresh water or oil through the cooler and bypasses the rest. The sea water flow is usually left steady, and the cooled fluid is regulated instead. Some systems also vary sea water pump speed or the number of pumps running for energy saving. Know your own ship's arrangement.

Operating points every watchkeeper should understand. Use the maker's figures and the ship's normal values, not a number from a textbook:

  • Sea water velocity. If the flow is too low, the tubes silt up and foul quickly. If it is too high, the tube inlets erode. Do not throttle the sea water inlet valve to control temperature. If the system design requires throttling, it is normally done on the outlet side so the cooler stays full and does not draw air.
  • Pressure differential. On an oil cooler, the oil side is traditionally kept at a higher pressure than the cooling water side. A leak then sends oil into the water, where it can be seen, rather than water into the oil, where it damages bearings. Check what your maker and company SMS say for each cooler.
  • Venting. High points must be vented after filling or opening up. Trapped air blanks off part of the surface, and the cooler underperforms for no obvious reason.
  • Warming and cooling slowly. Avoid thermal shock, especially with steam heaters and when bringing a cold cooler into service.

Trend the readings, do not just record them. For each cooler, log the inlet and outlet temperatures on both sides, the pressure drop across the unit and the temperature control valve position. One reading means little. A slow creep over weeks tells you the cooler is fouling.

Faults and their symptoms

Fault What you see Typical checks
Fouling (water or oil side) Hot-side outlet temperature creeping up. Control valve sending more and more flow through the cooler. Pressure drop across the unit rising. Sea water temperature rise across the cooler falling. Compare with trend and sea water temperature. Check sea chest strainers, pump discharge pressure and filter differentials.
Air lock Sudden or unexplained poor cooling, hot spots on the shell, noisy flow Vent high points. Check for air drawn in on pump suction or through a low expansion tank.
Tube or plate leak, oil into water Oil sheen in the fresh water expansion tank or at a sea water sampling point. Oil level in the sump or service tank falling for no reason. Isolate and pressure test. Compare tank levels. Check sea water discharge where applicable.
Tube or plate leak, water into oil Water in lube oil. Purifier showing more water than usual. Oil analysis showing water, or sodium if the water is sea water. Lube oil analysis, purifier operation, crackle test, expansion tank level falling
Sea water into fresh water Expansion tank level rising. Chloride content up in the cooling water test. Cooling water test kit, then isolate coolers one at a time
Gasket failure (plate type) External drip from the leakage area between double gaskets, or weeping at the frame Inspect gaskets, check the plate pack dimension, replace the gaskets
Charge air cooler fouling or leak Higher scavenge air temperature and pressure drop across the cooler, rising exhaust temperatures. An abnormal amount of water at the scavenge drains, or salty or continuous drainage, if a tube leaks (some condensate is normal). Check cooler differential pressure, mist catcher and drains, water/air side cleaning
Corrosion or erosion Pitting at the tube ends, anodes consumed quickly, repeated leaks Inspect anodes and water boxes. Review sea water velocity and materials.

Gasketed plate exchangers are usually built with a double-gasket leakage space vented to atmosphere. A failed gasket in that area therefore leaks visibly to the outside rather than mixing the two fluids.

Maintenance and opening-up, step by step

Intervals come from the planned maintenance system and the maker's manual, so follow those. A typical shell-and-tube cleaning job goes like this:

  1. Plan and permit. Do a risk assessment and raise a permit to work as your SMS requires. Make sure a standby cooler or reduced-load arrangement is in place if the machinery must keep running.
  2. Isolate both sides. Close and secure the inlet and outlet valves on both fluids. Apply lock-out/tag-out to the valves and to any pump that could pressurise the unit. Sea water valves are often near or below the waterline, so be certain of them before you break a joint.
  3. Cool, drain and depressurise. Open the drains and vents and confirm zero pressure. Collect oily drainage in a drip tray or container and transfer it to the sludge or dirty oil tank, not into the bilge.
  4. Open up. Remove the water box covers. Note the tube pass partitions and the gasket positions.
  5. Inspect before cleaning. Look at the anodes, tube ends, tube plate, coatings and the type of deposit. Photograph them for the PMS record.
  6. Clean. Use tube brushes or rods for water-side deposits. Chemical cleaning, if the maker approves it, must be done with the correct product, PPE and Safety Data Sheet. Do not use tools that score the tube bore.
  7. Test. Pressure test the shell side, using water or as the maker specifies, and watch the tube ends for leaks. Plug leaking tubes at both ends with approved plugs. Record the number of plugs, because each one reduces capacity, and the maker or class may limit how many are allowed.
  8. Renew anodes and gaskets, box up, vent thoroughly, and return to service slowly while you check for leaks and normal temperatures.

Plate exchangers. Before slackening the tie bolts, record the plate pack tightening dimension. Open and close the pack evenly, keep the plates in order (they are numbered for a reason) and clean them with soft brushes. Re-tighten to the maker's dimension, never by feel. Over-tightening crushes gaskets and can crack the plates.

Worked scenario: main engine lube oil inlet temperature rising

You are on watch at full sea speed. The main engine lube oil inlet temperature is a few degrees above its usual value and still creeping up, and the temperature control valve shows full flow through the cooler.

  1. Is the heat load higher? Check the engine load and that nothing has changed, such as a purifier heater left on or a bearing running warm (look at the bearing temperatures and oil mist detector).
  2. Is the coolant temperature higher? Check the LT fresh water temperature at the cooler inlet. If that is high too, the problem is upstream in the central cooler or sea water side. Check the sea water temperature, sea chest strainers, sea water pump pressure and the central cooler differential.
  3. Is the coolant flow lower? Look at pump pressures, valve positions and possible air in the system. Check the expansion tank level.
  4. Is the cooler fouled? Compare the temperature differences and pressure drop with your trend. If the cooler is fouled and a standby cooler exists, change over in line with the procedure.
  5. Inform the Second Engineer or Chief Engineer, log the readings and actions, and plan cleaning at the next opportunity. Reduce load if the temperature approaches the alarm or slowdown values.

The lesson for orals is to work outward from the symptom: heat load, then coolant temperature, then coolant flow, then the cooler itself.

Oral exam questions you should be ready for

  • Why is lube oil pressure kept higher than cooling water pressure in an oil cooler? So that a leak sends oil out into the water, rather than letting water into the oil.
  • How would you find a leaking tube? Isolate and drain the water side, pressurise the shell side, and watch the tube ends. Some makers also allow other leak tests.
  • Why counter-flow? It gives a higher mean temperature difference, so more heat is transferred for the same surface area.
  • Why not control temperature by throttling sea water inlet? It risks low pressure, air ingress, a partly empty cooler and silting. Temperature is normally controlled on the cooled-fluid side with a three-way valve.
  • What are sacrificial anodes for and what material? They corrode in preference to the tubes and tube plate. Zinc or soft iron are common, as specified by the maker. If anodes are being consumed very fast, look for stray currents or a material problem.
  • How do you know a plate cooler gasket has failed without mixing the fluids? By leakage from the double-gasket drain area to the outside.
  • What allows for thermal expansion in a shell-and-tube cooler? A floating head, U-tubes or an expansion bellows.
  • What are the effects of a fouled charge air cooler? Higher scavenge air temperature, lower air density, higher exhaust temperatures and higher fuel consumption.

Answer from your own ship's system where you can. Examiners respect a candidate who can describe a real cooler they have opened.

Key points
  • Heat transfer depends on surface area, mean temperature difference and the overall heat transfer coefficient, and fouling attacks the coefficient.
  • Central cooling confines sea water to the central coolers and a few units such as the fresh water generator condenser, so fresh water serves most other coolers.
  • Trend the inlet and outlet temperatures, pressure drop and control valve position; a slow drift warns of fouling.
  • Keep oil-side pressure above water-side pressure on oil coolers unless the maker or SMS states otherwise.
  • Isolate both sides, use lock-out/tag-out, drain and depressurise before opening, and take special care with sea water valves.
  • Record plugged tubes and plate pack dimensions, and follow maker's limits and PMS intervals.
  • For a rising temperature, check heat load, then coolant temperature, then coolant flow, then the cooler.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a shell-and-tube and a plate heat exchanger on a ship?

A shell-and-tube cooler passes one fluid through a tube bundle and the other around it inside a shell. It is robust and suits higher pressures and dirtier fluids. A plate heat exchanger uses a pack of corrugated plates with gaskets, which is more compact and efficient but sensitive to gasket condition and blockage of its narrow passages.

How do you know when a cooler needs cleaning?

Watch the trend. The hot-side outlet temperature rises, the temperature control valve sends more flow through the cooler, and the pressure drop across the unit increases while the sea water temperature stays the same. Clean at least as often as the PMS interval requires, and sooner if the trend shows fouling; the trend tells you the real condition.

How can I tell if a lube oil cooler is leaking?

Oil in the fresh water expansion tank or at sea water sampling points, or an unexplained fall in sump level, points to oil leaking into the water. Water in the oil shows up in purifier behaviour and in lube oil analysis. Confirm by isolating the cooler and carrying out a pressure test.

Can I plug leaking tubes in a cooler and keep using it?

Yes, plugging both ends of a leaking tube with approved plugs is normal practice. Each plug reduces cooling capacity, though, and the maker or classification society may set a limit. Record every plug, and plan retubing or replacement as the number grows.

Why are anodes fitted in sea water coolers?

Sacrificial anodes, commonly zinc or soft iron, corrode in place of the tubes, tube plates and water boxes. Inspect and renew them at each opening. If they are consumed unusually fast, investigate stray currents or material compatibility.

Test yourself

Why is the lube oil pressure normally kept higher than the cooling water pressure in a lube oil cooler?

Water in lube oil damages bearings, so the pressure difference is arranged so that any leak goes from oil to water. Reference: maker's instructions; competence in STCW Code, Table A-III/1.

In a gasketed plate heat exchanger with a double-gasket arrangement, how does a failed gasket in that area usually show itself?

The space between the double gaskets is vented to atmosphere, so a failure leaks visibly to the outside rather than mixing the fluids. Reference: maker's manual.

A main engine lube oil cooler shows a slowly rising outlet temperature over several weeks, its temperature control valve is moving towards full cooler flow and its pressure drop is rising, while sea water temperature is unchanged. What is the most likely cause?

A gradual loss of performance with increasing pressure drop is the classic sign of fouling. Reference: engine room trend monitoring under the PMS and STCW Code, Table A-III/1.

What is the main purpose of a floating head in a shell-and-tube heat exchanger?

The tubes and the shell run at different temperatures, and the floating head lets the tube bundle expand freely. U-tubes and shell bellows do the same job. Reference: maker's manual.

What does a falling fresh water expansion tank level combined with water in the lube oil most likely indicate?

Fresh water is being lost from the circuit and is turning up in the oil, which points to a leak from the water side into the oil side. Confirm by lube oil analysis and pressure testing. Reference: STCW Code, Table A-III/1.
