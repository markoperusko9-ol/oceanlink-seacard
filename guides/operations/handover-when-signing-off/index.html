Handover When Signing Off: A Practical Guide for Officers and Ratings

Quick answer

A sign-off handover passes your duties, open jobs, defects and accountable items to your relief so they can take over safely from the first hour. Prepare written notes well before the relief arrives, verify figures and inventories together, close or formally transfer anything open, and do not leave until your relief and head of department accept the handover. Your company SMS sets the exact forms and sign-offs, and it always takes precedence over general guidance.

Why the sign-off handover matters

Most of us have joined a ship and found a 'handover note' that says little more than all in order, good luck. Then the surprises start: a fuel figure that doesn't match the soundings, a permit nobody closed, a PSC deficiency due in the next port, a narcotics register that doesn't balance. The person who walked down the gangway is now on a plane, and the problem belongs to you.

A proper handover is not a courtesy. Under the ISM Code (Section 6) the company must have procedures so that people new to the ship, or transferred to new duties, are properly familiarised with their safety and environmental duties. STCW Regulation I/14 places similar responsibilities on the company. Your handover is the most direct part of that familiarisation for the specific job, and the company SMS will normally have a handover form or checklist for each rank.

There is also a personal side. Your signature on the handover, the logbooks and the inventories is your record of what you left behind. If something later goes wrong, a clear, countersigned handover protects both you and your relief.

Before your relief arrives: preparation

Overlap time varies a great deal. Some companies allow a day or more; on a tight port call you may get a couple of hours, or occasionally none if the relief is delayed and the master decides otherwise. Assume you will have little time and prepare in advance.

Start a running handover note a couple of weeks before sign-off. Add to it as things happen. Cover:

  • Open jobs and their status (who, what, next step)
  • Outstanding defects, with PMS or defect-report reference numbers
  • Open PSC deficiencies, flag or class conditions, and internal or external audit findings that fall to your department
  • Upcoming surveys, inspections, certificate expiries and drills due
  • Spare parts and stores ordered, received, or still awaited
  • Quirks of the ship and its equipment: the alarm that trips for no reason, the valve that must be opened slowly, the crane limit switch that needs watching
  • Port- or charter-specific requirements for the coming voyage

Bring records up to date. That means logbooks, PMS entries, Oil Record Book entries (engine), ECDIS and chart corrections (2nd Officer), cargo and ballast records (Chief Officer), and training and drill records. A relief should not have to finish your paperwork.

Step-by-step handover on board

The sequence below reflects general industry practice. Your company SMS form takes priority over it.

  1. Introduce your relief to the HoD and the master if they have not yet met. Make sure the relief has completed, or is booked for, safety familiarisation under the SMS. That includes being told their muster station and emergency duties, as required by SOLAS Chapter III.
  2. Talk through the written note in a quiet place. Do not do it on the gangway.
  3. Walk the spaces together. For deck that means the bridge, cargo control room, deck stores, LSA and FFA lockers. For engine it means the engine room, steering gear, emergency generator, workshop and stores. Point out the equipment and its problems in person.
  4. Verify accountable items jointly. Take soundings and ROB figures, count keys and controlled items, and agree the inventories. Count physically. Do not copy last month's figures.
  5. Deal with open work. Close permits to work and lock-out/tag-out isolations, or transfer them formally in line with the SMS, with the relief signing as the new responsible person.
  6. Hand over passwords and access for the PMS, the company system and the ECDIS user account, following company IT and security policy.
  7. Complete and sign the handover form. Both of you sign, and normally the HoD or master countersigns. Keep a copy or photo if the company allows it.
  8. Leave contact details so the relief can reach you once you are ashore.

Rule of thumb: if your relief is not satisfied with something, sort it out or put it in writing before you leave. Do not wave it through.

Typical items are listed below. The actual list depends on ship type, company and flag.

Typical hazards and lessons from the deck plates

These are composite scenarios of the kind any examiner or superintendent will recognise.

Scenario 1: the open isolation. A 3rd Engineer signs off with a pump still isolated and tagged for a seal job, and he mentions it only verbally. The relief forgets, and two days later someone else sees the tag, assumes the job is finished and tries to restore it. Lesson: every isolation and permit is closed or formally transferred in writing.

Scenario 2: copied ROB figures. A Chief Engineer hands over bunker ROB taken from the last noon report. The relief later sounds the tanks and finds a discrepancy before a bunker survey. Lesson: sound and calculate together, and both sign the figures.

Scenario 3: the medical locker. A 2nd Officer hands over without counting the controlled drugs. The relief later finds that the register and the stock don't match. Lesson: where you are responsible for controlled items, count them jointly and sign the register. Flag and company rules on custody vary, so follow yours.

Scenario 4: no overlap. The relief's flight is delayed and the ship sails. Lesson: this is why the written note exists. A good note should let a competent officer take over even without you there.

Scenario 5: the hidden PSC item. An open deficiency has to be rectified before the next port, but it is buried in an email thread. Lesson: list every external finding with its deadline at the top of the note.

Your own sign-off paperwork and personal items

Separate from the job handover, make sure your own affairs are in order before you leave:

  • Discharge entry in your Continuous Discharge Certificate / Seaman's Book, signed and stamped as your flag and company require
  • Record of employment. Under MLC 2006, Standard A2.1 you are entitled to a document recording your employment on board. Check that dates and rank are correct.
  • Sea service and onboard training records. Cadets should have training record books signed off. Officers should collect any testimonials or sea-time letters they will need for their next CoC application.
  • Final account of earnings and deductions, checked and signed
  • Return PPE, keys, tools and any company equipment
  • Leave your cabin clean and clear

Check the details before you sign. Corrections are much harder to arrange from ashore.

Oral exam style questions

Examiners often bring handover into ISM, watchkeeping or 'what would you do' questions. Practise answering these aloud:

  • What does the ISM Code require regarding new personnel? The company must have procedures so that new or transferred personnel are familiarised with their safety and environmental duties (Section 6). The handover is part of meeting this.
  • Your relief arrives and you have a hot-work permit still open. What do you do? Close it if the work is complete. If it is not, either close it and have it reissued to the relief, or transfer it formally if the SMS allows, with a fresh assessment and the relief taking responsibility in writing. Never leave it open without an owner.
  • The relieving officer appears unfit, for example exhausted or under the influence. What do you do? Do not hand over. Inform the master, and your HoD. For watch handover this mirrors the principle in STCW Code Section A-VIII/2: you do not hand over to someone you believe is not capable of carrying out the duties, and the master is notified.
  • What must be physically verified, not just read off a sheet? Soundings and ROB, cash, controlled drugs, keys and critical inventories.
  • The relief disagrees with your ROB figure. Who resolves it? Re-sound together, recalculate, and if it is still unresolved report it to the Chief Engineer or master and record the discrepancy before you leave.
Key points
  • The company SMS handover form always takes precedence over general guidance.
  • Start a written handover note weeks before sign-off, because overlap time is never guaranteed.
  • Verify ROB, cash, controlled drugs, keys and inventories jointly and physically, not from old figures.
  • Close or formally transfer every permit to work and lock-out/tag-out isolation in writing.
  • List open PSC deficiencies, audit findings and class conditions with their deadlines at the top of the note.
  • Do not hand over to a relief you believe is unfit. Report it to the master and your HoD.
  • Check your discharge entry, record of employment and final account before leaving the ship.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a handover take when signing off?

There is no single international figure. The overlap period is set by the company and depends on the rank and the port call. Senior officers often get longer overlaps than ratings. Prepare written notes in advance so that a short overlap still works.

What if my relief does not arrive before the ship sails?

The master decides whether you sail on or sign off, following company instructions. If you leave without meeting your relief, your written handover note, updated records and signed inventories become the whole handover, so they must be complete. Leave contact details for questions.

Is there a mandatory handover form?

No international convention sets out one standard form. The ISM Code requires the company to have familiarisation procedures, and most companies meet this with rank-specific handover checklists in the SMS. Use your company's form.

Who signs the handover?

Normally the off-signer and the relief both sign, and the head of department or master countersigns. Exact requirements vary by company SMS. Some companies also require the handover to be sent to the office.

Can I refuse to accept a handover as the relief?

You should not accept items you cannot verify. Record the discrepancies on the handover form and report them to your HoD or the master. That does not mean refusing to work; it means taking over with the problems documented.

Test yourself

Which instrument requires the company to have procedures for familiarising new or transferred personnel with their safety duties?

ISM Code Section 6 covers resources and personnel, including familiarisation of new and transferred personnel. STCW Regulation I/14 supports this.

You are signing off and a cold-work permit is still open because the job is unfinished. What is correct?

Open permits must never be left without a responsible person. Depending on the company permit-to-work procedure under the SMS, the permit is either closed and reissued to the relief or formally transferred.

During handover, how should bunker ROB be confirmed?

Accountable quantities should be physically verified and agreed by both parties, because a copied or remote figure may hide discrepancies. Follow the company SMS handover procedure.

The relieving officer appears unfit to take over. What should the outgoing officer do?

STCW Code Section A-VIII/2 states that a watch is not handed over if there is reason to believe the relieving officer is not capable of carrying out the duties, and that the master shall be notified. The same principle applies to a sign-off handover: report it to the master (and HoD).

Which document are you entitled to on leaving the ship as a record of your employment?

MLC 2006, Standard A2.1 entitles seafarers to a document containing a record of their employment on board.
