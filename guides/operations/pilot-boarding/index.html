Pilot Boarding: A Practical Guide for Deck Officers and Ratings

Quick answer

Pilot boarding means rigging a compliant transfer arrangement, giving the pilot boat a good lee at a safe speed, and having a responsible officer with a radio supervise the transfer and arrange for the pilot to be escorted by a safe route to and from the bridge. Most of the rules are in SOLAS Chapter V, Regulation 23 and IMO Resolution A.1045(27), and your company SMS and the pilot station's instructions set the detail. Many pilot transfer accidents are linked to poorly rigged or poorly maintained arrangements, not bad luck.

Why pilot boarding matters

A pilot climbing a rope ladder up a moving hull at night is doing one of the most dangerous routine jobs in shipping. Pilots have been killed and seriously injured in falls, often because of something the ship's crew could have prevented:

  • a ladder secured to the wrong point
  • worn or twisted side ropes
  • steps not resting flat against the hull
  • a ladder rigged too close to a discharge or a flared section of hull

For the ship, a non-compliant arrangement has other costs. Pilots are entitled to refuse to board, which means delay, a possible report to the authorities and a likely Port State Control interest. Pilot organisations such as IMPA run regular safety campaigns that record non-compliant arrangements, and deficient ships may be reported to the authorities.

Pilot boarding is also a bridge team event. It is a high-workload period, often in converging traffic close to the pilot station. The ship is slowed down and manoeuvring for a lee while the OOW is also dealing with VHF calls, the pilot boat and the deck party.

What the rules require (in plain terms)

The core requirements sit in SOLAS Chapter V, Regulation 23 and the detailed standard in IMO Resolution A.1045(27). In summary:

  • Pilot ladder limits. A pilot ladder alone may be used where the climb is not less than 1.5 m and not more than 9 m above the water. Above 9 m you need a combination arrangement (accommodation ladder plus pilot ladder) or another equally safe means approved by the Administration. Plan for the ship's trim and list.
  • Construction. The ladder must be certified by the manufacturer as complying with the standard. Steps are spaced between 310 mm and 350 mm apart, and the ladder must be a single length, not a joined-up pair. Spreader steps must be at least 1.8 m long. The fifth step from the bottom is a spreader, and further spreaders are fitted at intervals of not more than nine steps.
  • Position. Every step must rest firmly against the ship's side. The ladder goes within the parallel body length and, as far as practicable, within the midship half-length, clear of discharges.
  • Securing. Side ropes are made fast to dedicated strong points on deck, not to rails, stanchions or the bulwark ladder.
  • Safe access to deck. There must be safe, unobstructed passage from the head of the ladder onto the deck, with handhold stanchions or a bulwark ladder as fitted.
  • Equipment at hand. Keep these at the boarding station, ready for immediate use:
    • two man-ropes, rigged only if the pilot requests them
    • a lifebuoy with a self-igniting light
    • a heaving line
  • Lighting. At night the ladder, the water at its foot and the deck position must be adequately lit, without dazzling the pilot or the boat crew.
  • Supervision. A responsible officer, in radio contact with the bridge, supervises rigging and the transfer. The officer also arranges for the pilot to be escorted by a safe route to and from the bridge.
  • No mechanical pilot hoists. These are prohibited.

Amendments to Regulation 23 were adopted in 2023 and take effect from 2028, with further requirements phased in for existing ships. From 2028, new IMO performance standards for pilot transfer arrangements replace A.1045(27) for the applicable ships. Check with your company and flag which standard applies to your vessel and when. Flag administrations, ports and pilotage authorities may add their own requirements, such as freeboard notification or side preference.

Step-by-step: from ETA to pilot on the bridge

This is a general sequence. Your company SMS and the pilot station's instructions always take priority.

1. Before arrival - Send the ETA and any information the pilot station requires: draught, freeboard at the boarding point and similar. - Confirm the boarding position, time, side, ladder height above water, speed and whether a combination arrangement is needed. - Check the passage plan up to the boarding point. Also check the abort or holding options if the pilot is delayed.

2. Brief the team - Name the responsible officer and the deck party. - Agree the radio channel, the PPE to be worn and the timing for rigging. - Cover the weather and sea state.

3. Rig and inspect Only trained personnel should rig the ladder. Inspect it before every use (see the checklist below), rig it to the requested height and check its position and securing.

4. Bridge preparation - Have the pilot card and wheelhouse poster up to date. - Test the steering, engines and communications. - Display the correct signal flag for the situation. - Post a lookout, and reduce speed in good time. Do not do it at the last moment.

5. Make the lee Alter course as needed to give the pilot boat calm water on the boarding side, at the speed the pilot asks for. In a heavy swell, agree the heading with the pilot boat by VHF.

6. The transfer - The officer reports to the bridge when the boat is alongside, when the pilot is on the ladder and when the pilot is safely on deck. - Nobody handles the ladder while the pilot is on it. - Keep the lifebuoy and heaving line ready.

7. Escort and exchange The pilot is escorted to the bridge by a safe route. The Master and pilot then exchange information, broadly as described in IMO Resolution A.960(23). This covers:

  • ship's particulars and manoeuvring characteristics
  • known defects
  • the pilot's intended route, speeds and tug plan
  • local traffic and conditions

8. Secure Recover or stow the ladder as instructed and log the times.

For disembarkation, run the same steps in reverse. Remember the pilot is going down onto a moving boat, which is often the more hazardous part.

Ladder and boarding station checklist

Use your company checklist. These are the points that inspectors and pilots look at first.

Item What to check
Certification The manufacturer's certificate is on board and the ladder is identifiable
Side ropes No cuts, chafe, rot, chemical damage or excessive stiffness; no splicing or knots added
Steps Not cracked, split or loose; spacing equal; non-slip surface intact; all horizontal
Spreaders At least 1.8 m long; lowest spreader is the 5th step from the bottom; further spreaders at intervals of not more than 9 steps; fitted as manufactured, not lashed on
Securing Side ropes to dedicated strong points; no shackles or lashings that could slip
Height At the level requested, allowing for swell and list
Hull Ladder clear of overboard discharges, belting, flare and fenders
Access Bulwark ladder and stanchions secure; deck clear and non-slip
Lighting Ladder and water lit; deck light not shining into the pilot's eyes
Safety gear Lifebuoy with self-igniting light, heaving line, man-ropes if requested
Combination Accommodation ladder leading aft, lower platform horizontal and secured to the ship's side, pilot ladder rigged as required by the standard

If you are not sure a ladder is fit, do not use it. Rig the spare and tell the Master.

Do not make improvised repairs. If the side ropes are damaged, withdraw the ladder from use. Any step replacement must follow the manufacturer's original method and A.1045(27), as your SMS directs. If in doubt, replace the ladder.

Typical hazards and lessons from incidents

These failures come up again and again in pilot transfer incidents and near-miss reports:

  • Ladder secured to steps or rails. Shackling or lashing the side ropes to bulwark rails, or tying the ladder by its steps, puts the load on parts never designed to carry it. Use the dedicated strong points.
  • Worn ladders left in service. UV light, salt and stowage on deck all degrade rope. A ladder can look fine from a distance and still fail under load.
  • Wrong height. If the ladder is too long, it trails in the water and gets caught by the pilot boat. If it is too short, the pilot has a dangerous step-across.
  • Poor lee or excessive speed. The pilot boat is pinned against the hull or thrown around. Agree the speed and heading with the pilot boat; do not guess.
  • Discharges and hull obstructions. A sanitary or cooling water discharge near the ladder soaks the pilot and the steps. Belting or flare can stop the steps resting flat.
  • No officer present, or no radio. If the bridge cannot be told that the pilot is on the ladder, it may alter course or speed at exactly the wrong moment.
  • Crew working without fall protection. Those rigging the ladder near an open bulwark gate are also at risk. Wear a harness and lifejacket as your SMS requires.

Worked scenario: You are the 3rd Officer and the pilot boat is ten minutes out. You notice one side rope is chafed near the fourth step. Report it to the bridge immediately. Rig the spare ladder and inspect it to the same standard, then tell the Master so that the pilot station can be warned of a short delay if needed. Taking a few extra minutes is far better than an accident or a pilot refusing to board.

The bridge side: the pilot is aboard, now what?

Under the STCW Code, Section A-VIII/2, having a pilot on board does not relieve the Master or the OOW of their duties and obligations for the safety of the ship.

In practice that means the OOW should:

  • keep plotting positions and monitoring progress against the passage plan
  • keep checking that helm and engine orders are carried out correctly
  • query any pilot order that seems unclear or unsafe, and tell the Master at once if in doubt
  • keep a proper lookout and monitor traffic, including the outbound pilot boat
  • record pilot boarding and disembarkation times accurately

Bridge Resource Management applies to the pilot too. The pilot is a temporary member of the team, not a replacement for it.

Oral exam style questions

Examiners like pilot boarding because it tests regulation, seamanship and bridge management together. Expect questions like these:

  • Q: When is a combination arrangement required? When the distance from the water to the point of access exceeds 9 m. Also explain how list and trim affect that figure.
  • Q: What must be ready at the boarding station? A lifebuoy with a self-igniting light, a heaving line and man-ropes if the pilot requests them, plus adequate lighting. A responsible officer with a radio must also be present.
  • Q: Where do you secure the side ropes? To dedicated strong points on deck, never to rails, stanchions or the bulwark ladder.
  • Q: How do you make a lee? Alter course so that the boarding side is sheltered from wind and sea, at the speed agreed with the pilot. Allow for drift and the pilot boat's approach.
  • Q: The pilot orders something you think is dangerous. What do you do? Query it immediately. If you are still concerned, call the Master. Remember the Master's responsibility remains with a pilot on board.
  • Q: What does the pilot card contain, and why does it matter? The ship's particulars, draught, manoeuvring data and known equipment defects. It is the basis of the Master–pilot information exchange.

A good answer quotes the principle, then adds what you personally would do on deck or on the bridge.

Key points
  • Pilot transfer arrangements are governed by SOLAS V/23 and IMO Resolution A.1045(27); the company SMS and pilot station instructions set the detail.
  • A pilot ladder alone covers a climb of 1.5 m to 9 m; above 9 m you need a combination arrangement or another approved means.
  • Side ropes go to dedicated strong points, never to rails, stanchions or the steps.
  • Inspect the ladder before every use; never make improvised repairs, and replace it in line with the maker's instructions and your SMS.
  • A responsible officer with a radio supervises the transfer and arranges for the pilot to be escorted by a safe route to and from the bridge.
  • Make a proper lee at the agreed speed, and do not alter course or speed while the pilot is on the ladder.
  • A pilot on board does not relieve the Master or OOW of responsibility for the ship's safety.

Frequently asked questions

Can the pilot refuse to board if the ladder is unsafe?

Yes. Pilots are entitled to refuse a transfer arrangement they consider unsafe. This usually means delay and may lead to a report to the port or Port State Control. Rig the ladder correctly the first time.

What height should the pilot ladder be rigged at?

Rig it at the height above the water that the pilot station requests. Requests vary with the pilot boat type and the sea state. Allow for swell, list and trim, and confirm by VHF if conditions change.

Who is allowed to rig the pilot ladder?

Crew who have been instructed in safe rigging procedures, under the supervision of a responsible officer. Your company SMS sets who does it on your ship and how it is recorded. Fall protection and a lifejacket are normally required near open bulwark gates.

Are man-ropes always required?

They must be available at the boarding station, but they are only rigged if the pilot asks for them. Many pilots prefer not to use them. Always ask, or follow the pilot station's standing instructions.

Does the Master still have responsibility when the pilot has the con?

Yes. The Master and OOW keep their responsibility for the safety of the ship, and the OOW must keep monitoring the ship's position and the pilot's orders. If in doubt, query the pilot and inform the Master immediately.

Test yourself

Above what height from the water to the point of access must a pilot ladder no longer be used on its own?

Above 9 m, a combination arrangement or another equally safe means is required. Reference: SOLAS Chapter V, Regulation 23.

What is the current position on mechanical pilot hoists?

Mechanical pilot hoists are prohibited. Reference: SOLAS Chapter V, Regulation 23 as amended.

Who must supervise the rigging of the pilot ladder and the pilot transfer?

SOLAS requires a responsible officer with means of communication with the bridge to supervise the transfer and arrange for the pilot to be escorted. Reference: SOLAS Chapter V, Regulation 23.

What is the required spacing between pilot ladder steps?

Steps must be equally spaced between 310 mm and 350 mm. Reference: IMO Resolution A.1045(27).

With a pilot on board conducting the navigation, who remains responsible for the safety of the ship?

Having a pilot on board does not relieve the Master or OOW of their duties for the ship's safety. Reference: STCW Code, Section A-VIII/2.
