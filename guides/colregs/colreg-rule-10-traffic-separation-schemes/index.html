COLREG Rule 10 – Traffic Separation Schemes

Quick answer

Rule 10 sets out how to use an IMO-adopted traffic separation scheme. You follow the correct lane in the direction of traffic flow, keep clear of separation zones, join or leave at small angles, and cross on a heading as near as practicable to a right angle. It gives no right of way, so the steering and sailing rules still decide who keeps clear of whom inside a TSS.

What Rule 10 covers and where it sits

Rule 10 is the last rule in Section I of Part B. Section I covers the conduct of vessels in any condition of visibility. Rule 10 applies to traffic separation schemes (TSS) adopted by the IMO. Under Rule 1(d), IMO may adopt traffic separation schemes for the purpose of the Rules. The general provisions on ships' routeing are in IMO Resolution A.572(14) as amended, and SOLAS Chapter V Regulation 10 covers ships' routeing.

A TSS separates opposing streams of traffic using:

  • Traffic lanes, each with a defined direction of flow (shown by arrows on the chart).
  • Separation zones or separation lines between the lanes.
  • In some schemes, an inshore traffic zone (ITZ) between the landward boundary of the scheme and the coast.

The first paragraph of the rule is the most important point. Rule 10 does not relieve any vessel of her obligations under any other rule. It controls where and how you navigate in the scheme. It does not decide who gives way.

Some schemes are not IMO-adopted, for example some port or harbour schemes. Local or special rules made under Rule 1(b) may govern these. Check the chart, sailing directions and local regulations for any scheme on your passage.

The rule paragraph by paragraph (paraphrased)

Para What it requires, in plain words
(a) Applies to IMO-adopted schemes. Does not override any other rule.
(b)(i) Proceed in the correct lane, in the general direction of traffic flow for that lane.
(b)(ii) So far as practicable, keep clear of the separation line or zone.
(b)(iii) Normally join or leave at the end of a lane. If joining or leaving from the side, do it at as small an angle to the traffic flow as practicable.
(c) So far as practicable, avoid crossing lanes. If you must cross, do it on a heading as nearly as practicable at right angles to the traffic flow.
(d)(i) Do not use an ITZ when you can safely use the adjacent lane. Exceptions: vessels under 20 m, sailing vessels and vessels engaged in fishing may use it.
(d)(ii) Any vessel may use the ITZ when bound to or from a port, offshore installation, pilot station or other place within it, or to avoid immediate danger.
(e) Do not normally enter a separation zone or cross a separation line. Exceptions: when crossing, joining or leaving; in an emergency to avoid immediate danger; or to fish within the separation zone.
(f) Navigate with particular caution near the ends of a scheme.
(g) So far as practicable, avoid anchoring in a TSS or near its ends.
(h) If you are not using the TSS, avoid it by as wide a margin as practicable.
(i) A vessel engaged in fishing must not impede any vessel following a traffic lane.
(j) A vessel under 20 m or a sailing vessel must not impede the safe passage of a power-driven vessel following a lane.
(k) A vessel restricted in her ability to manoeuvre (RAM) is exempted to the extent necessary while doing maintenance for safety of navigation in the TSS.
(l) A RAM vessel is exempted to the extent necessary while laying, servicing or picking up submarine cable in the TSS.

What Rule 10 does not do: right of way

Many candidates believe a ship following a lane has priority over everything else. This is wrong, and it is the most common misunderstanding of Rule 10.

  • Power-driven vessel against power-driven vessel. If you are following the lane and another power-driven vessel crosses it, Rule 15 applies normally. If she is on your starboard side and risk of collision exists, you are the give-way vessel. Being in the lane gives you no privilege.
  • 'Not impede' vessels. Under Rule 10, fishing vessels must not impede any vessel following a lane, and vessels under 20 m and sailing vessels must not impede the safe passage of a power-driven vessel following a lane. They should act early to leave sea room. Rule 8(f) explains how this works:
    • The vessel that must not impede stays under that duty even when risk of collision develops.
    • The vessel following the lane still has to comply fully with the steering and sailing rules once risk of collision exists.
  • Rule 18 still applies when vessels are in sight of one another. A vessel not under command or restricted in her ability to manoeuvre in the lane must be kept clear of in the normal way.
  • Restricted visibility. Rule 19 applies. There is no stand-on or give-way vessel, whether you are in the lane or crossing it.
  • Rule 9 does not apply automatically. A TSS is not a narrow channel unless it is also one. The overtaking sound signals in Rule 34(c) are tied to narrow channels and fairways.

Applying Rule 10 on the bridge

Passage planning

  • Mark each TSS clearly on the chart or ECDIS. Plan to join and leave at the lane ends where practicable.
  • If you must cross a scheme, plan the crossing at a right-angle heading. Choose a point where lane traffic is lightest and visibility of the scheme is best.
  • Find any mandatory ship reporting system (SOLAS V/11) or VTS (SOLAS V/12) for the area. Note the reporting points and VHF channels.
  • Mark the scheme ends as areas needing extra caution. Converging traffic, pilot boarding and ships altering for the next leg all concentrate there.
  • Check the Master's standing orders and the company SMS. Many require calling the Master, manual steering or extra lookouts in or near a TSS. These requirements differ between companies.

Execution

  • Keep in the lane but off the separation zone. Leave enough sea room to manoeuvre without entering the zone.
  • When overtaking in the lane, you remain the give-way vessel under Rule 13. Plan the pass so you are not forced into the separation zone or the opposite lane.
  • Watch for crossing traffic, fishing vessels working in the separation zone, and small craft near the ITZ boundary.
  • Make reports to VTS as required. A VTS call does not replace action under the Rules. Do not use VHF agreements as a substitute for early, substantial action.

Worked scenarios

1. Crossing with a strong set along the lane

You must cross a lane that runs 045°/225°. A 3-knot tidal stream is setting towards 045°.

  • Rule 10(c) asks for a heading near 135° or 315°, not a track at right angles.
  • Steer the right-angle heading and accept that your track will be oblique.
  • Reasons:
    • Lane traffic sees your lights and radar aspect as a clear crossing vessel.
    • You spend the least time in the lane.
  • Do not crab into the stream to make good a perpendicular track.

2. Following the lane, power-driven vessel crossing from starboard

A power-driven vessel crosses the lane on a right-angle heading, fine on your starboard bow. The bearing is steady and the range is closing.

  • Rule 15 makes you the give-way vessel.
  • Take early, substantial action under Rules 8 and 16. Usually that means an alteration to starboard to pass astern of her, or a reduction in speed.
  • Check first that a starboard turn does not put you into conflict with other lane traffic or the edge of the scheme.

3. Yacht in the lane ahead

A sailing yacht is crossing ahead of you while you follow the lane in a power-driven vessel.

  • Under Rule 10(j) she must not impede your safe passage, and she should act early.
  • If she does not, and risk of collision develops, Rule 8(f) means you are fully bound by Part B.
  • Do not stand on and argue the point. If in doubt and the vessels are in sight of one another, sound at least five short and rapid blasts (Rule 34(d)), and take avoiding action in time.

4. Main engine failure in the lane

  • Show not-under-command lights or shapes (Rule 27).
  • Inform VTS and the Master. Broadcast your status.
  • Other vessels in sight of you must keep out of your way under Rule 18; in restricted visibility Rule 19 applies.
  • Avoid anchoring in the lane so far as practicable. If drifting towards danger, anchoring is still correct seamanship.

Common mistakes watchkeepers make

  • Treating the lane as giving right of way over crossing power-driven vessels.
  • Crossing at an angle to save distance, or steering to make good a right-angle track instead of a right-angle heading.
  • Joining from the side at a broad angle, which looks to lane traffic like a crossing vessel.
  • Hugging the separation line to pass slower traffic, leaving little room to manoeuvre without entering the separation zone.
  • Using the ITZ as a short cut when the adjacent lane could safely be used.
  • Running parallel to a scheme just outside its boundary, instead of keeping clear by as wide a margin as practicable.
  • Relaxing at the end of the scheme, exactly where Rule 10(f) asks for particular caution.
  • Relying on VHF arrangements with other ships instead of taking action under the Rules.

Oral exam points

Examiners commonly probe the following.

  • Q: Does Rule 10 give a vessel in the lane right of way?
    • No. Rule 10(a) preserves all other rules.
    • Fishing vessels must not impede any vessel following a lane (Rule 10(i)). Vessels under 20 m and sailing vessels must not impede the safe passage of a power-driven vessel following a lane (Rule 10(j)).
  • Q: How do you cross a TSS, and why on a heading?
    • As nearly as practicable at right angles to the traffic flow, measured by heading.
    • This gives a clear aspect to lane traffic and the shortest time in the lane.
  • Q: Who may use an inshore traffic zone?
    • Vessels under 20 m, sailing vessels and vessels engaged in fishing.
    • Any vessel going to or from a port, installation, pilot station or other place within the ITZ.
    • Any vessel to avoid immediate danger.
  • Q: When may you enter a separation zone?
    • When crossing, joining or leaving.
    • In an emergency to avoid immediate danger.
    • To engage in fishing within the zone.
  • Q: Which vessels are exempted from Rule 10, and to what extent?
    • RAM vessels doing maintenance for safety of navigation, or working submarine cable, within the scheme.
    • The exemption applies only to the extent necessary for the operation.
  • Q: Does Rule 10 apply in restricted visibility?
    • Yes. It is in Section I, which applies in any visibility.
    • Rule 19 then governs the avoiding action.

Answer in your own words, cite the paragraph letter, and link it to Rules 8(f), 15, 18 and 19 where relevant.

Key points
  • Rule 10 applies to IMO-adopted schemes and does not override any other COLREG rule.
  • Follow the correct lane in the direction of flow and keep clear of the separation zone or line.
  • Join or leave at lane ends where possible; from the side, use as small an angle as practicable.
  • Cross on a heading as nearly as practicable at right angles to the traffic flow, not a right-angle track.
  • A lane-following vessel has no right of way over a crossing power-driven vessel; Rule 15 still applies.
  • Fishing vessels must not impede any lane vessel; under-20 m and sailing vessels must not impede power-driven lane traffic (Rule 8(f) applies).
  • Take particular caution near scheme ends, and avoid anchoring in or near a TSS so far as practicable.

Frequently asked questions

Does a ship in a traffic lane have right of way under COLREG Rule 10?

No. Rule 10 states it does not relieve any vessel of her obligations under other rules. Between two power-driven vessels, Rule 15 and the other steering rules apply as normal. Fishing vessels must not impede any vessel following a lane, and vessels under 20 m and sailing vessels must not impede the safe passage of a power-driven vessel following a lane.

Why must you cross a TSS on a heading at right angles rather than a track?

The rule refers to heading. A right-angle heading shows lane traffic a clear crossing aspect by lights and on radar, and it keeps your time in the lane to a minimum. With a current setting along the lane, your track will be oblique, and that is accepted.

Can a large ship use the inshore traffic zone?

Only if she cannot safely use the adjacent traffic lane. A large ship may also use it when going to or from a port, offshore installation, pilot station or other place within the zone, or to avoid immediate danger. Otherwise the ITZ is for vessels under 20 m, sailing vessels and vessels engaged in fishing.

Is fishing allowed inside a traffic separation scheme?

Yes. A vessel may enter a separation zone to engage in fishing there. However, a vessel engaged in fishing must not impede the passage of any vessel following a traffic lane. Lane traffic must still comply with the steering rules if risk of collision develops.

Do Rule 10 requirements change in fog?

Rule 10 is in Section I of Part B, so it applies in any condition of visibility. In restricted visibility, Rule 19 governs avoiding action and there is no stand-on or give-way vessel. Check also your Master's standing orders and any VTS requirements for the area.

Test yourself

You must cross a traffic lane where a strong tidal stream sets along the direction of flow. How should you cross?

Rule 10(c) refers to the heading, not the track. Steering a right-angle heading gives a clear aspect to lane traffic and the shortest time in the lane (COLREG Rule 10(c)).

You are following a lane in a power-driven vessel. Another power-driven vessel is crossing the lane from your starboard side with risk of collision. What applies?

Rule 10(a) preserves the other rules. Between power-driven vessels, the crossing rule applies inside a TSS as anywhere else (COLREG Rules 10(a) and 15).

Which vessel may use an inshore traffic zone even though she could safely use the adjacent lane?

Vessels under 20 m, sailing vessels and vessels engaged in fishing may use the ITZ. Other vessels may use it only for the specific purposes in Rule 10(d)(ii) (COLREG Rule 10(d)).

A vessel engaged in fishing is in your lane and risk of collision is developing. What is the correct understanding?

Rule 10(i) places a duty not to impede on the fishing vessel. Rule 8(f) keeps that duty in force when risk develops, and also requires the lane vessel to comply with Part B (COLREG Rules 8(f) and 10(i)).

When may a vessel that is not crossing, joining or leaving enter a separation zone?

Rule 10(e) allows entry into a separation zone only in an emergency to avoid immediate danger or to engage in fishing within the zone (COLREG Rule 10(e)).
