COLREG Rule 20 – Application of Lights and Shapes Explained

Quick answer

Rule 20 sets out when the lights and shapes of Part C must be shown. Lights are shown from sunset to sunrise, and also by day in restricted visibility. Shapes are shown by day, all Part C rules apply in all weathers, and no other light may be shown that could be mistaken for a navigation light, spoil its visibility or hamper the look-out.

What Rule 20 actually says

Rule 20 is the gateway to Part C (Rules 20 to 31). It does not tell you which lights or shapes to show. Rules 23 to 31 do that. Rule 20 tells you when and to what standard. In paraphrase it has five paragraphs:

  • 20(a): The rules in Part C must be complied with in all weathers. Fog, rain and heavy seas do not excuse you.
  • 20(b): The rules on lights apply from sunset to sunrise. During that time you must not show any other light unless it meets three tests. It must not be mistakable for a prescribed light. It must not impair the visibility or distinctive character of your navigation lights. It must not interfere with a proper look-out.
  • 20(c): If you carry the prescribed lights, you must also show them from sunrise to sunset in restricted visibility. You may show them at any other time when you judge it necessary.
  • 20(d): The rules on shapes apply by day.
  • 20(e): Lights and shapes must meet the technical provisions of Annex I.

Keep in mind that lights and shapes are information signals. They tell another watchkeeper what you are and what you are doing. Showing the wrong signal, or none, removes that information at exactly the moment he needs it.

Points that catch people out:

  • Sunset and sunrise mean the actual times, not twilight. They are not "when it starts getting dark" either. Take them from the almanac or your bridge software for your position.
  • Sunset is the latest you may switch on. Many masters' standing orders require lights earlier, and Rule 20(c) allows this. Follow your standing orders and company SMS.
  • In daytime fog you show both lights and shapes. Restricted visibility does not cancel the shapes requirement, because shapes apply "by day" with no exception for weather.
  • Shapes are not shown at night, because they are useless in darkness. They also mean nothing to a vessel that cannot see you in fog. Your sound signals under Rule 35 and your radar plot do the real work there.

"No other lights": deck lights, accommodation and searchlights

Rule 20(b) is where most practical problems arise. The rule does not ban every other light. It bans lights that confuse, dazzle or blind. Questions to ask on the bridge:

  • Could it be mistaken for a navigation light? A red light high on the mast could be read as part of a NUC, RAM or constrained-by-draught signal. A green one could be read as a trawling or mine-clearance signal. Either could also be taken for a sidelight. So could a white light that looks like a second masthead light.
  • Does it impair your own navigation lights? Bright deck floodlights near the masthead or sidelights can wash out their colour or arc when seen from another vessel.
  • Does it hamper the look-out? Accommodation lighting that spills onto the bridge front or reflects off the windows destroys night vision. Back-scatter from forward deck lights in rain or spray does the same.

Some exceptions and special cases are written into the Rules themselves:

  • At anchor, Rule 30 allows a vessel to light her decks with working lights. A vessel of 100 m or more in length is required to do so.
  • Signals to attract attention under Rule 36 must not be mistakable for an aid to navigation. High-intensity flashing or revolving lights such as strobes are to be avoided.
  • Local or special rules made under Rule 1(b) may require extra lights in particular waters, for example canal transit lights. These are set by the relevant authority, so check the port or canal regulations rather than assuming.

On passage at night, deck lights should normally be off or shielded unless there is a working reason for them. Curtains on accommodation portholes facing forward should be closed.

Annex I: the standard your lights and shapes must meet

Rule 20(e) links you to Annex I, which covers vertical and horizontal positioning, screening, colour specifications, intensity, and the dimensions of shapes. Some well-established points:

  • Shapes are black. A ball, cone and cylinder each have a diameter of not less than 0.6 m. Vessels under 20 m may use smaller shapes in proportion to their size.
  • Spacing between shapes in a vertical line is at least 1.5 m, again with a reduction allowed for small vessels.
  • A diamond is two cones base to base.
  • Construction and installation: the construction of lights and shapes and the installation of lights on board are to the satisfaction of the appropriate authority of the flag State.

Visibility ranges of lights are in Rule 22, not Rule 20. For the watchkeeper, the practical consequence of 20(e) is simple. A shape that is undersized, faded or grey, or hoisted where the funnel hides it, is not compliant. Neither is a light with a cracked lens, a wrong-coloured bulb, or a broken screen.

Your vessel's procedures and the flag Administration's requirements govern:

  • checking navigation lights and indicator panels before sunset;
  • carrying spare lamps;
  • shifting to secondary lights after a failure.

Follow your SMS checklist.

Worked scenarios

1. Anchored, fog rolls in at 1400.

  • Keep the black ball hoisted, because it is day.
  • Switch on the anchor lights, because it is day in restricted visibility and the lights are carried.
  • Start the Rule 35 anchor signal.
  • Call the master as per standing orders.

2. Heaving up anchor at 0800.

  • Once the anchor is aweigh you are no longer at anchor. By Rule 3 you are underway.
  • Lower the ball at that point. A ball left up while you are underway tells other vessels you are stationary.
  • A common practice is to assign one person in the bridge team to the shape on the anchor-station checklist.

3. Main engine failure just before sunrise.

  • Switch off the masthead light(s). A vessel not under command does not exhibit them (Rule 27(a)).
  • You exhibit two all-round red lights as NUC under Rule 27.
  • When the sun rises, you hoist two black balls in a vertical line.
  • You may also keep the lights on in poor light under Rule 20(c).
  • If you have stopped and are not making way, you switch off sidelights and stern light. If you are still making way through the water, you keep them on.

4. Clear weather, but heavy rain squalls ahead in the late afternoon.

  • Strictly, lights are not yet required.
  • Rule 20(c) lets you switch them on early if you judge it necessary.
  • Most masters expect you to do so without asking.
  • Once you are in the squall and visibility is restricted, lights are no longer optional. Rule 20(c) requires them (if carried) from sunrise to sunset in restricted visibility.

5. Cargo work at night on a general cargo ship at sea, preparing hatches.

  • Deck lights are switched on forward.
  • The OOW goes to the bridge wing and checks for glare on the bridge windows.
  • He also checks whether the masthead or sidelights are being washed out. If they are, shield or switch off the offending lights.
  • Note the decision in the logbook if your SMS requires it.

Common mistakes on board

  • Forgetting shapes entirely. This happens at anchor, when NUC by day, and when RAM during a slow job such as launching a survey sensor. Missing or incorrect shapes can be raised as a deficiency during inspections.
  • Leaving shapes up after weighing anchor, refloating, or completing a RAM operation.
  • Assuming fog cancels shapes. It does not. By day, both apply.
  • Switching on at "dusk" after sunset has already passed.
  • Treating optional shapes as compulsory, or the reverse. Some Part C provisions say a vessel may exhibit something, others say shall. Read the exact rule for the vessel type.
  • Unshielded accommodation and deck lighting that destroys the look-out's night vision. This also makes your own aspect hard to read from another ship.
  • Navigation light failure not acted on. Any alarm means switch to the secondary light at once. Then inform the master and log it, per your SMS.

Oral exam: what the examiner will ask

Q: When must navigation lights be shown? From sunset to sunrise, and from sunrise to sunset in restricted visibility. They may be shown at any other time when deemed necessary.

Q: You are at anchor by day in fog. What do you show? The ball and the anchor lights, plus the Rule 35 sound signals.

Q: What other lights may you show at night? Only lights that cannot be mistaken for prescribed lights, do not impair the visibility or character of your own lights, and do not interfere with the look-out. Then mention the Rule 30 deck lights at anchor and the Rule 36 strobe warning.

Q: Where do you find the size of a ball shape? Annex I, through Rule 20(e). Give the 0.6 m minimum diameter.

Q: Does Rule 20 tell you which lights to show? No. It covers application and timing only. Rules 23 to 31 specify the signals, and Annex I sets the technical standards.

Examiners like candidates who connect rules. Rule 20 with Rule 3 tells you when "underway" starts. Rule 20 with Rule 19 and Rule 35 tells you what to do in fog. Rule 20 with Rule 1(b) covers local light requirements.

Key points
  • Part C applies in all weathers; Rule 20 sets when signals are shown, not which ones.
  • Lights are shown from sunset to sunrise, judged by actual times, not twilight.
  • By day in restricted visibility, show both your lights (if carried) and your shapes.
  • Lights may be shown by day at any time you judge it necessary, such as squalls, haze or low light.
  • Other lights at night must not be mistakable, must not impair navigation lights, and must not hamper the look-out.
  • Lights and shapes must meet Annex I; shapes are black, and ball, cone and cylinder are at least 0.6 m in diameter for vessels of 20 m and over.
  • Lower shapes promptly when the condition ends, for example on weighing anchor.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to show navigation lights in daytime fog?

Yes. Rule 20(c) requires the prescribed lights, if carried, to be shown from sunrise to sunset in restricted visibility. Your shapes are still required because it is day, and you must also make the Rule 35 sound signals.

Can I switch on navigation lights before sunset?

Yes. Rule 20(c) allows lights to be shown at other times when deemed necessary. Many masters' standing orders and company procedures require lights on before sunset or in poor light, so follow them.

Are deck working lights allowed at night under COLREG?

Yes, provided they cannot be mistaken for navigation lights, do not impair your navigation lights and do not interfere with the look-out. At anchor, Rule 30 allows deck lighting, and requires it for vessels of 100 m or more in length.

Do shapes need to be shown at night?

No. Rule 20(d) applies the shapes rules by day only. At night the corresponding lights do the job.

Where are the sizes of day shapes specified?

In Annex I, which Rule 20(e) makes compulsory. For vessels of 20 m and over, a ball, cone or cylinder has a diameter of at least 0.6 m and shapes are spaced at least 1.5 m apart vertically. Smaller vessels may use smaller shapes in proportion to their size.

Test yourself

Under Rule 20, between which times must the rules on lights be complied with?

Rule 20(b) applies the lights rules from sunset to sunrise, judged by actual times. Earlier switching on is permitted under Rule 20(c).

A vessel is at anchor by day in restricted visibility. What does Rule 20 require her to exhibit?

Rule 20(c) requires lights in restricted visibility by day, and Rule 20(d) requires shapes by day regardless of weather. Rule 35 sound signals are additional.

Which of the following lights may be exhibited at night under Rule 20(b)?

Rule 20(b) prohibits other lights unless they cannot be mistaken for prescribed lights, do not impair their visibility or character, and do not interfere with the look-out.

Where are the technical requirements for the size of day shapes found?

Rule 20(e) requires lights and shapes to comply with Annex I, which sets shape dimensions and spacing. Rule 22 covers visibility ranges of lights.

Clear weather, mid-afternoon, with heavy rain squalls approaching. What does Rule 20 allow regarding navigation lights?

Rule 20(c) permits lights to be exhibited in all other circumstances when deemed necessary. COLREG sets no visibility figure for this.
