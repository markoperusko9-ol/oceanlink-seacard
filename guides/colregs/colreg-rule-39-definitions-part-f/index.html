COLREG Rule 39 – Definitions (Part F)

Quick answer

Rule 39 defines four terms used in Part F of the COLREGs: audit, Audit Scheme, Code for Implementation and Audit Standard. Part F covers how IMO audits governments on how well they implement the Collision Regulations. It places no duty on ships, but examiners ask about it to see whether you know the full structure of the COLREGs and the difference between a State audit and the ISM audits you see on board.

Where Rule 39 sits in the COLREGs

Most watchkeepers know Parts A to D thoroughly and stop at Rule 38. Part F was added later. It was adopted by the IMO Assembly in 2013 and entered into force on 1 January 2016, when the IMO Member State Audit Scheme became mandatory.

Part Rules Subject
A 1–3 General
B 4–19 Steering and sailing
C 20–31 Lights and shapes
D 32–37 Sound and light signals
E 38 Exemptions
F 39–41 Verification of compliance with the Convention

Annexes I to IV follow Part F.

Part F has three rules:

  • Rule 39: definitions
  • Rule 40: application, which requires Contracting Governments to use the Code for Implementation
  • Rule 41: verification of compliance, which covers periodic audits of every Contracting Party

Rule 39 gives the vocabulary the other two rules rely on.

What Rule 39 says, in plain terms

Rule 39 defines four terms. In paraphrase:

  • Audit: a systematic, independent and documented process. It gathers evidence and assesses it objectively to see how far the agreed audit criteria are met. It is a structured check, not a casual inspection.
  • Audit Scheme: the IMO Member State Audit Scheme. IMO established it and it takes account of IMO's own guidelines.
  • Code for Implementation: the IMO Instruments Implementation Code, known as the III Code, adopted by IMO Assembly resolution A.1070(28).
  • Audit Standard: the Code for Implementation. In other words, the III Code is the yardstick against which a State is audited.

The key point is that every one of these terms concerns States, not ships. The III Code sets out what a government should do in its roles as:

  • a flag State
  • a coastal State
  • a port State

This includes having legislation, resources, procedures and records in place to give effect to the conventions it has ratified.

How this touches the bridge (honestly: indirectly)

Rule 39 creates no duty for the officer of the watch. No light, shape, sound signal or manoeuvre changes because of Part F. Your obligations on watch still come from:

  • Parts A to E
  • the Annexes
  • your flag State's rules
  • your company SMS

The link to your daily work is indirect. The audit checks whether your flag Administration does its job properly in areas such as:

  • giving the COLREGs force in national law
  • handling exemptions under Rule 38
  • determining closest possible compliance for vessels of special construction or purpose under Rule 1(e)
  • surveying navigation lights and sound appliances against Annexes I and III
  • investigating collisions and taking enforcement action

If a flag does these things badly, it shows up in the ships flying that flag. One example is lights that were never properly checked against Annex I.

Equivalent audit provisions were added to other mandatory IMO instruments, including SOLAS, MARPOL, STCW and the Load Line Convention, and entered into force on the same date, 1 January 2016. The definitions you learn here therefore turn up again in those conventions.

Worked scenarios

Scenario 1: the cadet's question during a quiet watch

A cadet reading the COLREG book asks whether the ship needs to prepare for a 'Part F audit'. The answer is no:

  • The IMO audit is of the Administration. Auditors look at the government's systems, not at individual vessels.
  • Ship staff will normally never be involved.

Point the cadet instead to what the ship does face: ISM internal and external audits, flag State inspections and Port State Control.

Scenario 2: confusing two kinds of audit

The ship has an ISM external audit by the Recognised Organisation next week. A junior officer says this is 'the COLREG Rule 41 audit'. It is not:

ISM audit IMO Member State audit (Part F)
Who is audited Company and ship SMS The Member State government
Audit standard ISM Code III Code
Who audits Company, flag or RO on flag's behalf IMO audit team
Ship involvement Direct Normally none

Scenario 3: why a flag cares about your Annex I lights

A newbuild's masthead light positioning is questioned. Any decision on equivalence or alternative compliance comes from the flag Administration, within what the COLREGs permit. The quality and consistency of that kind of decision-making is the sort of thing the III Code expects a flag State to manage properly. You report the defect through your SMS. You do not quote Part F at the surveyor.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking Part F applies to ships. It applies to Contracting Parties, meaning governments.
  • Mixing up the III Code and the ISM Code. The ISM Code is about company safety management. The III Code is about how a State implements IMO instruments.
  • Confusing 'Audit Standard' with 'Audit Scheme'.
    • The Audit Scheme is the IMO programme itself.
    • The Audit Standard is the III Code, the benchmark used in that programme.
  • Believing the COLREGs end at Rule 38. Older textbooks and memory aids may stop there, because Part F was added later. Exam papers set on the current amended text may include it.
  • Equating 'verification of compliance' with Port State Control. PSC is a port State inspecting foreign ships. Part F is IMO auditing the State. The III Code does, however, cover how a State carries out its port State duties.
  • Inventing details. Audit intervals, scheduling and procedures are set by IMO guidance and the Secretary-General's schedule. If you don't know a specific figure, say so in an oral exam rather than guess.

Oral exam points

Examiners rarely spend long on Rule 39. When they do ask, it is usually to check you know the structure of the COLREGs and can separate State-level duties from ship-level duties.

Q: How many parts do the COLREGs have, and what is Part F? Six parts, A to F, plus four Annexes. Part F, Rules 39 to 41, deals with verification of compliance through the IMO Member State Audit Scheme.

Q: What terms does Rule 39 define? Audit, Audit Scheme, Code for Implementation and Audit Standard.

Q: What is the Audit Standard? The Code for Implementation, the III Code, adopted by resolution A.1070(28).

Q: Does Part F place any obligation on the master or OOW? No. It concerns Contracting Governments. The master and OOW comply with the steering, lighting and signalling rules as given effect by their flag State and company SMS.

Q: Who is audited, and by whom? Each Contracting Party is audited by IMO under the Audit Scheme. Rule 41 provides for periodic audits, administered by the Secretary-General.

A good candidate answers briefly, then links back to practical seamanship. For example: 'It doesn't change my watch, but it is why my flag must have proper procedures for things like Rule 38 exemptions and Annex I surveys.'

Key points
  • Rule 39 defines four terms: audit, Audit Scheme, Code for Implementation and Audit Standard.
  • Part F (Rules 39–41) entered into force on 1 January 2016 with the mandatory IMO Member State Audit Scheme.
  • The Audit Standard is the III Code, adopted by IMO resolution A.1070(28).
  • Part F audits governments as flag, coastal and port States, not ships or companies.
  • It creates no new duty for the OOW; the steering, lighting and signalling rules are unchanged.
  • Do not confuse the III Code with the ISM Code, or Part F audits with ISM audits or Port State Control.

Frequently asked questions

What is COLREG Rule 39?

Rule 39 is the definitions rule of Part F of the COLREGs. It defines 'audit', 'Audit Scheme', 'Code for Implementation' and 'Audit Standard', which are used in Rules 40 and 41 on verifying how States implement the Convention.

Does Part F of the COLREGs apply to my ship?

Not directly. Part F places obligations on Contracting Governments, which must use the III Code and are subject to periodic IMO audits. Your ship's duties under the COLREGs come from Parts A to E and the Annexes, as applied by your flag State.

What is the III Code?

The III Code is the IMO Instruments Implementation Code, adopted by resolution A.1070(28). It sets out what a State should do as flag, coastal and port State to give effect to IMO instruments. Rule 39 names it as the Audit Standard.

When was Part F added to the COLREGs?

The amendments adding Part F were adopted by the IMO Assembly in 2013 and entered into force on 1 January 2016. Equivalent audit provisions were added to other mandatory IMO instruments, including SOLAS, MARPOL, STCW and the Load Line Convention, and entered into force on the same date, 1 January 2016.

Is a Part F audit the same as an ISM audit or a PSC inspection?

No. An ISM audit checks a company's and ship's safety management system against the ISM Code. A PSC inspection is a port State checking a foreign ship. A Part F audit is IMO auditing a Member State government against the III Code.

Test yourself

Under COLREG Rule 39, what is the 'Audit Standard'?

Rule 39 defines the Audit Standard as the Code for Implementation, the III Code adopted by resolution A.1070(28). Ref: COLREG Rule 39.

Who is the subject of audits under Part F of the COLREGs?

Part F concerns audits of Contracting Parties under the IMO Member State Audit Scheme. It does not audit ships, companies or ROs directly. Ref: COLREG Rules 39 and 41.

Which rules make up Part F of the COLREGs?

Part F, verification of compliance, comprises Rules 39 (definitions), 40 (application) and 41 (verification of compliance). Rule 38 is Part E, Exemptions. Ref: COLREG Part F.

What does Rule 39 mean by 'Audit Scheme'?

Rule 39 defines the Audit Scheme as the IMO Member State Audit Scheme, established by IMO taking account of its guidelines. Ref: COLREG Rule 39.

How does Part F change the officer of the watch's duties on the bridge?

Part F deals only with how Contracting Parties implement the Convention and are audited. Ship-level duties remain in Parts A to E and the Annexes. Ref: COLREG Rules 39–41.
