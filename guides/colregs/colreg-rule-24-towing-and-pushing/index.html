COLREG Rule 24 – Towing and Pushing

Quick answer

Rule 24 sets out the lights and shapes for a power-driven vessel towing astern, pushing ahead or towing alongside, and for the vessels or objects being towed. The key triggers are the type of tow, whether the units are rigidly connected, and whether the tow is longer than 200 metres measured from the towing vessel's stern to the after end of the tow. Rule 24 is only about signals. It gives the towing vessel no special right of way unless she also qualifies as restricted in her ability to manoeuvre under Rule 27.

What Rule 24 actually covers

Rule 24 sits in Part C (Lights and Shapes). It tells you what a towing or pushing vessel and her tow must display, so other vessels can tell three things:

  • that a tow exists
  • roughly how long it is
  • which way it is heading

The rule falls into four groups:

  • Power-driven vessel towing astern: paragraphs (a) and (d).
  • Pushing ahead or towing alongside, including the special case of a rigidly connected composite unit: paragraphs (b), (c) and (d).
  • The vessel or object being towed, including inconspicuous, partly submerged objects: paragraphs (e), (f) and (g).
  • Situations where the prescribed lights are impracticable, such as an emergency tow of a vessel in distress: paragraphs (h) and (i).

Rule 24 gives a towing vessel no privileged status. Rule 18 gives a vessel engaged in towing no special category; she is a power-driven vessel. She becomes a vessel restricted in her ability to manoeuvre only when the tow severely restricts her and her tow in their ability to deviate from course. That case is covered by Rule 3(g)(vi) and Rule 27(c), and she must then show the additional RAM signals.

Towing astern: lights and shapes on the towing vessel

A power-driven vessel towing astern shows the lights below. Length of tow is measured from the stern of the towing vessel to the after end of the tow.

Item Tow 200 m or less Tow over 200 m
Masthead lights in a vertical line Two Three
Sidelights Yes Yes
Sternlight Yes Yes
Towing light (yellow) vertically above sternlight Yes Yes
Day shape None under Rule 24 One diamond where it can best be seen

Points to remember:

  • Where the vertical masthead lights go. They replace either the forward masthead light or the after masthead light of Rule 23.
  • The other masthead light (Rule 24(d)). A towing vessel of 50 m or more must also carry the other Rule 23 masthead light, so you may see the vertical pair or three forward with a single higher light aft, or a single light forward with the vertical group aft. A vessel under 50 m is not obliged to carry it but may.
  • The towing light. Rule 21 defines it as a yellow light with the same characteristics as the sternlight, showing over the same arc aft.
  • Visibility ranges. Ranges for each light depend on the vessel's length and are set out in Rule 22. Positioning and spacing details, including the diamond's minimum size, are in Annex I.

Pushing ahead, towing alongside and composite units

Composite unit (Rule 24(b)). When a pusher and the vessel pushed ahead are rigidly connected in a composite unit, they are treated as a single power-driven vessel. They show ordinary Rule 23 lights appropriate to the combined length:

  • no vertical masthead group
  • no towing light

In restricted visibility they also give power-driven vessel sound signals.

Not rigidly connected (Rule 24(c)). A power-driven vessel pushing ahead or towing alongside, where the units are not a composite unit, shows:

  • two masthead lights in a vertical line, in place of one Rule 23 masthead light
  • sidelights
  • a sternlight
  • the other masthead light under Rule 24(d) if she is 50 m or more

There is no towing light here. The yellow light is only for towing astern, and the 200 m distinctions do not apply.

The vessels being pushed or towed alongside (Rule 24(f)). Any number of vessels pushed in a group, or towed alongside in a group, are lit as one vessel:

  • Pushed ahead (not a composite unit): sidelights at the forward end.
  • Towed alongside: a sternlight, plus sidelights at the forward end.

Lights and shapes on the tow itself

Ordinary vessel or object towed astern (Rule 24(e)) shows:

  • sidelights
  • a sternlight
  • a diamond where it can best be seen, if the tow is over 200 m

There are no masthead lights on the tow. On a long tow, the absence of masthead lights is how you tell the towed vessel from the tug.

Inconspicuous, partly submerged vessels or objects (Rule 24(g)) are things like dracones or barely awash barges. Sidelights would mean little on these, so all-round white lights outline the object:

  • Breadth under 25 m: one all-round white light at or near each end. A dracone need not show the forward one.
  • Breadth 25 m or more: two additional all-round white lights at or near the extremities of the breadth.
  • Length over 100 m: additional all-round white lights between them, so the gap never exceeds 100 m.
  • By day: a diamond at or near the aftermost end of the last vessel or object towed. If the tow exceeds 200 m, add a second diamond as far forward as practicable.

When the prescribed lights are impracticable (Rule 24(h)). If the tow cannot show the lights or shapes for a sufficient reason, all possible measures must be taken to light it, or at least to indicate it is there. Examples of sufficient reason are damage or an unmanned hulk with no power. Portable battery lights and illuminating the hull or towline are typical measures.

Emergency towing by a vessel not normally a tug

Rule 24(i) recognises that a merchant ship rarely has towing lights fitted. This matters when she takes a disabled vessel or a vessel in distress in tow.

If it is impracticable, she is not required to show the Rule 24(a) or (c) lights. She must still take all possible measures to indicate the relationship between herself and the tow, using signals authorised by Rule 36. In particular, she should illuminate the towline.

In practice, on the bridge:

  1. Inform the Master and follow the company SMS emergency towing procedure. SOLAS II-1/3-4 requires ships to carry a ship-specific emergency towing procedure.
  2. Consider whether you are now restricted in your ability to manoeuvre. If so, show the Rule 27(c) signals if the vessel is equipped to do so. Not all merchant ships have red-white-red all-round lights fitted, so check before you need them.
  3. Rig a searchlight or deck floodlights on the towline. Make a VHF Sécurité broadcast and request the coast station to promulgate a navigational warning, and advise VTS if you are in a VTS area.
  4. In restricted visibility, sound the towing signal under Rule 35.
  5. Log the lights shown and the reasons.

Worked scenarios on the bridge

Scenario 1 – Three white lights in a vertical line, yellow over white astern. At night you see three white masthead lights in a vertical line and a red sidelight, with a higher white light aft. Later you see a yellow light above a white light. This is a power-driven vessel towing astern, tow over 200 m. She is probably 50 m or more, since she shows a second masthead light. You are on her port side. Look further astern for the tow's sidelights and sternlight. Never pass between a tug and her tow. Treat the towline as invisible and the tow as yawing.

Scenario 2 – Two white lights in a vertical line. From ahead or on the bow you see two white masthead lights in a vertical line. This could be a vessel towing astern with a tow of 200 m or less, or a vessel pushing ahead or towing alongside. You cannot tell which from these lights alone. Look for a tow astern, or for sidelights at the forward end of a pushed barge. From astern, a sternlight with no yellow towing light above it indicates pushing ahead or towing alongside rather than towing astern.

Scenario 3 – Daylight, a tug with no shape at all. This is legal if the tow is 200 m or less and the tug is not RAM. You must identify the tow visually, by radar, or by AIS if transmitted. If she shows ball–diamond–ball, she is also RAM under Rule 27, and Rule 18 responsibilities change.

Scenario 4 – Two all-round white lights low on the water, well astern of a tug. These are likely the ends of an inconspicuous, partly submerged tow under 25 m in breadth. Radar returns may be weak. Give a wide berth and assess risk of collision against the whole length of the tow, not just the tug.

Scenario 5 – Fog, one prolonged and two short blasts, then one prolonged and three short. A vessel is towing (prolonged, two short). The manned last vessel of the tow is answering (prolonged, three short) straight after. Proceed at a safe speed. If you hear the fog signal of another vessel apparently forward of your beam, and you have not determined that risk of collision does not exist, Rule 19(e) applies. Reduce speed to the minimum at which you can keep your course, take all way off if necessary, and navigate with extreme caution until danger of collision is over. Rule 19(d) is a separate case, for a vessel detected by radar alone. There you take avoiding action in ample time and, so far as possible, avoid an alteration to port for a vessel forward of the beam other than one being overtaken.

Common mistakes and oral exam points

Mistakes examiners hear regularly:

  • Saying a towing vessel is automatically RAM or always has right of way. She is not, unless the tow severely restricts her ability to deviate from course.
  • Fitting the towing light when pushing ahead or towing alongside. It is for towing astern only.
  • Measuring tow length from the tug's bow, or including the tug's own length. It runs from the tug's stern to the after end of the tow.
  • Giving a composite unit two or three vertical masthead lights. It shows Rule 23 lights only.
  • Putting masthead lights on the towed vessel.
  • Forgetting that the diamond is shown on both the towing vessel and the tow when the tow is over 200 m.

Typical oral questions:

  • Describe the lights of a 60 m tug towing a barge 300 m astern, seen from right ahead. Three vertical masthead lights forward, plus a second masthead light aft and higher (she is over 50 m), and both sidelights. The barge shows sidelights.
  • What day signals for the same tow? A diamond on the tug and a diamond on the barge, where best seen.
  • What is a composite unit and how is it lit? A pusher and pushed vessel rigidly connected. It is lit as one power-driven vessel under Rule 23.
  • How do you light a dracone? All-round white light at the after end (forward one optional), extra lights as breadth and length require, and the diamond rules by day.
  • You are towing a disabled ship with no towing lights. What do you do? Explain Rule 24(i), Rule 36 and illuminating the towline, plus Rule 27 if restricted.
Key points
  • Tow length runs from the towing vessel's stern to the after end of the tow; over 200 m means three vertical masthead lights and diamonds.
  • Towing astern adds a yellow towing light vertically above the sternlight; pushing ahead or alongside does not.
  • A rigidly connected composite unit is lit as one power-driven vessel under Rule 23.
  • Towed vessels show sidelights and a sternlight but never masthead lights.
  • Inconspicuous, partly submerged tows are outlined with all-round white lights and carry a diamond at the after end by day.
  • A towing vessel has no special right of way unless she is also restricted in her ability to manoeuvre under Rule 27.
  • In an emergency tow where lights are impracticable, illuminate the towline and indicate the relationship as Rule 36 allows.

Frequently asked questions

When does a towing vessel show three masthead lights?

When she is towing astern and the tow exceeds 200 metres, measured from her stern to the after end of the tow. With 200 m or less she shows two in a vertical line. When pushing ahead or towing alongside, she shows two regardless of length.

What is the yellow towing light and where is it fitted?

It is a yellow light with the same characteristics as the sternlight, as defined in Rule 21. It is fitted vertically above the sternlight. Only a vessel towing astern shows it.

Does a tug always show a day shape when towing?

No. Under Rule 24 a diamond is required only when the tow exceeds 200 metres. A tug with a shorter tow shows no shape, unless she is also restricted in her ability to manoeuvre, in which case she shows ball-diamond-ball under Rule 27.

How is a pusher and barge composite unit lit?

If the pusher and barge are rigidly connected as a composite unit, they are regarded as a single power-driven vessel and show Rule 23 lights for their combined length. If they are not rigidly connected, the pusher shows two vertical masthead lights, sidelights and a sternlight, and the barge shows sidelights at its forward end.

Is a vessel engaged in towing a stand-on vessel?

Not by virtue of towing alone. Rule 18 gives a vessel engaged in towing no special category; she is a power-driven vessel. If her tow severely restricts her ability to deviate from course, she qualifies as restricted in her ability to manoeuvre under Rule 3(g)(vi), must show the Rule 27(c) signals, and gains the corresponding Rule 18 standing.

Test yourself

At night a towing vessel shows three white masthead lights in a vertical line. What does this tell you?

Three vertical masthead lights are shown only when towing astern with a tow length over 200 m. Pushing ahead or towing alongside uses two. (COLREG Rule 24(a) and (c))

By day, a tug is towing a barge 150 metres astern and is not restricted in her ability to manoeuvre. What shape must she show?

The diamond is required only when the tow exceeds 200 m. Ball-diamond-ball applies only if she is RAM. (COLREG Rule 24(a)(v), Rule 27(c))

How is a pushing vessel and a vessel pushed ahead, rigidly connected in a composite unit, lit?

A rigidly connected composite unit is regarded as a power-driven vessel and shows Rule 23 lights. (COLREG Rule 24(b))

Where is the towing light exhibited?

The yellow towing light is shown in a vertical line above the sternlight of a vessel towing astern. (COLREG Rule 24(a)(iv), Rule 21(d))

Which lights does an ordinary vessel being towed astern show at night?

A towed vessel shows sidelights and a sternlight, never masthead lights. All-round red lights would indicate not under command. (COLREG Rule 24(e))
