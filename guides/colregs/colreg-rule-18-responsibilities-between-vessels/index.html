COLREG Rule 18 – Responsibilities between vessels

Quick answer

Rule 18 sets the order of responsibility between vessels in sight of one another. A power-driven vessel keeps out of the way of vessels not under command, restricted in their ability to manoeuvre, engaged in fishing and sailing. A sailing vessel keeps clear of the first three, and a fishing vessel keeps clear of NUC and RAM as far as possible. Vessels other than NUC and RAM avoid impeding a vessel constrained by her draught. The rule gives way to Rules 9, 10 and 13, and applies only to vessels in sight of one another. When vessels are not in sight in or near restricted visibility, Rule 19 applies instead.

What Rule 18 says, in plain terms

Rule 18 is the 'pecking order' of the Steering and Sailing Rules. It opens with a qualifier: it applies except where Rules 9 (narrow channels), 10 (traffic separation schemes) and 13 (overtaking) otherwise require. It then sets out these responsibilities:

  • (a) A power-driven vessel underway keeps out of the way of a vessel not under command (NUC), a vessel restricted in her ability to manoeuvre (RAM), a vessel engaged in fishing and a sailing vessel.
  • (b) A sailing vessel underway keeps out of the way of NUC, RAM and a vessel engaged in fishing.
  • (c) A vessel engaged in fishing, when underway, keeps out of the way of NUC and RAM, so far as possible.
  • (d) Any vessel other than NUC or RAM shall, if the circumstances admit, avoid impeding the safe passage of a vessel constrained by her draught (CBD) that is showing the Rule 28 signals. The CBD vessel in turn must navigate with particular caution, having full regard to her special condition.
  • (e) A seaplane on the water shall, in general, keep well clear of all vessels and avoid impeding their navigation. Once risk of collision exists, she complies with the Steering and Sailing Rules.
  • (f) A WIG craft keeps well clear of all other vessels and avoids impeding them when taking off, landing and in flight near the surface. When operating on the water surface, she complies as a power-driven vessel.

When it applies and when it does not

In sight only. Rule 18 sits in Section II of Part B. Rule 11 limits that section to vessels in sight of one another. When vessels are not in sight of one another in or near an area of restricted visibility, Rule 19 governs, and Rule 19 has no hierarchy at all. A radar target with an AIS status of 'engaged in fishing' gets no stand-on privilege in fog while you cannot see her.

Overridden by Rules 9, 10 and 13.

  • Overtaking: an overtaking vessel keeps out of the way of the vessel being overtaken, whatever their types. A sailing vessel overtaking a power-driven vessel must keep clear.
  • Narrow channels: a vessel under 20 metres or a sailing vessel must not impede a vessel that can safely navigate only within the channel. A vessel engaged in fishing must not impede any other vessel navigating within the channel.
  • Traffic separation schemes: a fishing vessel must not impede any vessel following a traffic lane. A vessel under 20 metres or a sailing vessel must not impede the safe passage of a power-driven vessel following a lane.

Status comes from the definitions, not from what a vessel claims. Each status has a definition in Rule 3:

  • A vessel engaged in fishing is one using gear that restricts her manoeuvrability. Trolling lines do not count.
  • A sailing vessel is one under sail with any machinery not being used.
  • A yacht motor-sailing is a power-driven vessel.

In practice you judge status by the lights and shapes shown, backed up by what you actually observe.

Local rules may modify the position. Rule 1(b) allows special rules for harbours, rivers and inland waters. Check the local regulations and the port's requirements.

'Keep out of the way' versus 'not impede'

Examiners like this distinction.

Keep out of the way creates a give-way and stand-on relationship:

  • The give-way vessel acts under Rule 16: early and substantial action, well clear.
  • The other vessel stands on under Rule 17. She keeps her course and speed, may take action once it is apparent the give-way vessel is not acting, and must act when collision cannot be avoided by the give-way vessel alone.

Not impede, used for the CBD vessel and in Rules 9 and 10, works differently. Rule 8(f) explains it:

  • The vessel required not to impede must take early action so there is enough sea room for the other vessel's safe passage.
  • She is not relieved of that duty if the vessels get close enough to involve risk of collision. When taking action she must have full regard to the steering rules.
  • The vessel whose passage is not to be impeded remains fully obliged to comply with the steering rules once risk of collision exists.

The aim of 'not impede' is to act so early that risk of collision never develops. If it does develop, both vessels have obligations at the same time. A CBD vessel is by definition power-driven, so on paper Rule 18(a) still applies to her in relation to fishing and sailing vessels. The other vessel's duty not to impede is meant to stop the situation ever getting that far.

Applying Rule 18 on the bridge – step by step

  1. Confirm you are in sight. If visibility is restricted and you are tracking by radar or AIS only, you are in Rule 19. Do not use Rule 18.
  2. Identify status from lights, shapes and behaviour. Use binoculars and check the Rule 26–28 signals. Use AIS navigational status only to support what you see, never to replace it. AIS status is entered manually and is often wrong.
  3. Check whether Rule 9, 10 or 13 applies. Ask whether you are overtaking or being overtaken, in a narrow channel, or in a traffic lane. If so, that rule takes priority where it says otherwise.
  4. Work out the relationship under Rule 18. Decide whether you keep out of the way, stand on, or must avoid impeding.
  5. Assess risk of collision under Rule 7. Take compass bearings and use ARPA CPA/TCPA. Remember that a fishing vessel's course and speed can change suddenly.
  6. Act early and substantially. If giving way, make a bold alteration that is obvious to the other vessel. Consider sea room and what gear the vessel may be towing or laying.
  7. Keep monitoring. If you are the stand-on vessel, watch for the moment when Rule 17(a)(ii) or 17(b) comes into play.
  8. Follow the Master's standing orders and the company SMS on CPA limits and calling the Master. These vary between companies.

Worked scenarios

1. Trawler crossing from your port side. You are on a power-driven bulk carrier by day. A trawler shows two cones apexes together and is crossing from your port bow on a steady bearing. Under Rule 15 alone you would be stand-on. Rule 18(a), however, makes you keep out of the way of a vessel engaged in fishing.

  • Alter early and boldly.
  • Allow for gear streamed astern of the trawler. Avoid passing close astern of a fishing vessel.

2. 'Sailing' yacht showing a cone apex downward. The yacht is under sail and engine, and the cone shows she is motor-sailing. She is a power-driven vessel, so Rules 13–15 apply between you, not Rule 18(a).

  • Stay alert to the risk that her skipper believes sail gives her priority.

3. Fishing vessel crossing a traffic lane. You are following the lane in a TSS when a fishing vessel engaged in fishing crosses ahead. Rule 10(i) requires her not to impede you, and Rule 18(a) requires you to keep out of the way of her.

  • Under Rule 8(f) both duties exist together.
  • She should have acted early. If risk of collision develops, you are still obliged to take avoiding action.
  • Reduce speed or alter early, and keep a good margin. Do not hold course on the assumption that she must yield.

4. Fog and an AIS target showing 'engaged in fishing'. You are not in sight, so Rule 19 applies.

  • If a close-quarters situation is developing, take action under Rule 19(d), avoiding the alterations that rule warns against.
  • Sound the Rule 35 signals.
  • Rule 18 gives that target no priority while she is not in sight.

5. Ship showing three all-round red lights in a vertical line. She is constrained by her draught in a deep-water route and is exhibiting the optional Rule 28 signals. You are on a power-driven ship approaching to cross.

  • Because she is exhibiting the Rule 28 signals, Rule 18(d) applies. You shall avoid impeding her if the circumstances admit, by acting early.
  • If you leave it late and risk of collision develops, the normal steering rules apply to both vessels.

Common mistakes

  • Treating Rule 18 as a fog rule. It applies only in sight. When vessels are not in sight of one another in restricted visibility, Rule 19 gives no vessel any privilege.
  • Forgetting the opening qualifier. An overtaking sailing vessel or fishing vessel does not stand on because of Rule 18.
  • Trusting AIS navigational status over lights, shapes and observed behaviour.
  • Assuming any fishing boat is 'engaged in fishing'. A boat steaming home with gear stowed, or trolling, is a power-driven vessel (or a sailing vessel if under sail alone), not a vessel engaged in fishing.
  • Treating 'not impede' as giving permanent right of way to a CBD vessel or a lane-follower. Rule 8(f) keeps both vessels in the game once risk of collision exists.
  • Expecting NUC or RAM vessels to give way to each other under Rule 18. The rule is silent on that. Rule 2 and good seamanship govern, along with VHF contact where it is safe and appropriate.
  • Treating an anchored or aground vessel as part of the hierarchy. Rule 18 deals with vessels underway (and seaplanes and WIG craft). You keep clear of an anchored vessel through ordinary seamanship and Rules 2 and 8.

Oral exam points

Expect questions like these at OOW and Chief Mate/Master orals. Flag-state examination formats differ, but the content of the rule does not.

  • "Give me the order of responsibility." NUC and RAM, then CBD (not to be impeded), then fishing, then sailing, then power-driven. Add seaplanes and WIG craft. State the Rule 9, 10 and 13 exceptions without being prompted.
  • "Does Rule 18 apply in fog?" Not between vessels that are not in sight of one another. Section II applies only to vessels in sight (Rule 11), so in fog Rule 19 governs until the vessels can see each other visually. Once they are in sight, Rule 18 applies.
  • "Who keeps clear between a sailing vessel and a fishing vessel?" The sailing vessel, under Rule 18(b).
  • "Explain 'not impede'." Paraphrase Rule 8(f) in three parts: early action; no release from the duty if risk develops; the other vessel is still bound by the steering rules.
  • "What is a vessel constrained by her draught?" A power-driven vessel severely restricted in her ability to deviate from her course because of her draught relative to the available depth and width of water. She may show the Rule 28 signals in addition to her power-driven lights: three all-round red lights in a vertical line, or a cylinder by day. Rule 18(d) protects her only when she is exhibiting them.
  • "Can the stand-on vessel act?" Yes, under Rule 17. She may act once it is apparent the give-way vessel is not taking appropriate action. She must act when collision cannot be avoided by the give-way vessel alone.

Answer from the rule's structure and paraphrase it accurately. Examiners mark down vague 'right of way' language because the COLREGs do not use that term.

Key points
  • Rule 18 sets responsibilities only between vessels in sight of one another. Vessels not in sight of one another in or near restricted visibility are governed by Rule 19, which has no hierarchy.
  • Power-driven vessels keep out of the way of NUC, RAM, fishing and sailing vessels.
  • Sailing vessels keep clear of NUC, RAM and fishing; fishing vessels keep clear of NUC and RAM so far as possible.
  • All vessels except NUC and RAM shall, if the circumstances admit, avoid impeding the safe passage of a vessel constrained by her draught showing Rule 28 signals.
  • Rules 9, 10 and 13 override Rule 18 where they require otherwise, so an overtaking vessel always keeps clear.
  • 'Not impede' means early action, and both vessels keep their steering-rule duties if risk of collision develops (Rule 8(f)).
  • Judge status from the Rule 3 definitions, lights and shapes, not from AIS status alone.

Frequently asked questions

Does a power-driven vessel always give way to a sailing vessel?

Only when they are in sight of one another and Rules 9, 10 and 13 do not require otherwise. A sailing vessel overtaking a power-driven vessel must keep clear. In a narrow channel or traffic lane, a sailing vessel must not impede certain power-driven vessels. A yacht using her engine is a power-driven vessel, even with sails set.

Does Rule 18 apply in restricted visibility?

Not while the vessels are out of sight of one another. Rule 18 is in Section II of Part B, which Rule 11 limits to vessels in sight of one another. When vessels are not in sight in or near an area of restricted visibility, Rule 19 applies to all vessels equally, regardless of status. If they come into visual sight of each other, Rule 18 applies again.

Who gives way between a vessel not under command and a vessel restricted in her ability to manoeuvre?

Rule 18 does not say. Both vessels must rely on Rule 2, good seamanship and the general duty under Rule 8 to avoid collision. In practice, early communication and whatever manoeuvring ability each vessel has decide the outcome.

Must a fishing vessel keep clear of a ship constrained by her draught?

A vessel engaged in fishing must, if the circumstances admit, avoid impeding the safe passage of a CBD vessel showing the Rule 28 signals. Rule 8(f) says this means taking early action. The CBD vessel must still navigate with particular caution and comply with the steering rules if risk of collision develops.

Is a fishing boat on passage with its gear stowed a 'vessel engaged in fishing'?

No. Under Rule 3, a vessel is engaged in fishing only when using nets, lines, trawls or other gear that restricts her manoeuvrability. A fishing boat on passage, or one fishing with trolling lines, is a power-driven or sailing vessel for the purposes of the rules.

Test yourself

In dense fog your radar shows a target, identified on AIS as 'engaged in fishing', closing on a steady bearing on your starboard bow. Which rule governs your action?

Rules 11–18 apply only to vessels in sight of one another. When vessels are not in sight of one another in or near an area of restricted visibility, Rule 19 governs and there is no hierarchy (COLREG Rules 11 and 19).

A sailing vessel is overtaking a power-driven vessel in clear weather. Which vessel must keep out of the way?

Rule 18 applies except where Rules 9, 10 and 13 otherwise require. Under Rule 13, any overtaking vessel keeps out of the way of the vessel being overtaken.

What is the duty of a vessel engaged in fishing towards a vessel constrained by her draught showing the Rule 28 signals?

Rule 18(d)(i) requires any vessel other than NUC or RAM, if the circumstances admit, to avoid impeding a CBD vessel showing Rule 28 signals. Rule 18 applies only to vessels in sight of one another, so option C is wrong.

A yacht under sail is showing a conical shape, apex downwards, by day. How should a power-driven ship treat her under the steering rules?

The cone apex downward shows a vessel proceeding under sail and machinery (Rule 25(e)). Under Rule 3(c), a vessel using propelling machinery is not a sailing vessel.

Under Rule 8(f), what is the position of a vessel whose passage must not be impeded when risk of collision develops?

Rule 8(f)(iii) keeps the vessel whose passage is not to be impeded fully bound by the steering rules once vessels approach so as to involve risk of collision.
