COLREG Rule 26 – Fishing Vessels: Lights, Shapes and Bridge Practice

Quick answer

Rule 26 sets out the lights and shapes for a vessel engaged in fishing. A trawler shows green over white all-round lights, and other fishing vessels show red over white. Both show two cones apex-to-apex by day and add sidelights and a sternlight only when making way. The rule only applies while the vessel is actually engaged in fishing as defined in Rule 3(d), and it tells the watchkeeper which vessel he is dealing with under Rule 18.

What Rule 26 says, in plain terms

Rule 26 has five paragraphs. Paraphrased:

  • (a) Only these signals. A vessel engaged in fishing shows only the lights and shapes in this Rule, whether she is underway or at anchor. A fishing vessel that is fishing at anchor therefore does not show anchor lights or an anchor ball. She shows her fishing signals.
  • (b) Trawling. Trawling means dragging a dredge net or other fishing appliance through the water. A trawler shows:
    • two all-round lights in a vertical line, green over white, or by day two cones with apexes together;
    • a masthead light abaft of and higher than the green light. This is compulsory at 50 m and over. Below 50 m it is optional;
    • sidelights and a sternlight when making way through the water.
  • (c) Fishing other than trawling (gillnetting, longlining, purse seining and so on). The vessel shows:
    • all-round red over white, or the same two-cone shape;
    • when outlying gear extends more than 150 m horizontally, an all-round white light, or by day a cone apex upwards, in the direction of the gear;
    • sidelights and a sternlight when making way. She shows no masthead light.
  • (d) Fleets. Vessels fishing in close proximity to each other may use the additional signals in Annex II.
  • (e) Not fishing. When she is not engaged in fishing, a fishing vessel must not show these signals. She shows only the lights and shapes for a vessel of her length, normally those of a power-driven vessel.

The classic memory aids are "green over white – trawling tonight" and "red over white – fishing at night".

Who counts as "engaged in fishing"?

Rule 26 depends entirely on the definition in Rule 3(d). Paraphrased, a vessel is engaged in fishing when she is using nets, lines, trawls or other apparatus that restrict her manoeuvrability. Trolling lines and other gear that do not restrict manoeuvrability do not count.

In practice:

  • A trawler towing her gear is engaged in fishing.
  • A longliner shooting or hauling is engaged in fishing.
  • A purse seiner working her net is engaged in fishing.
  • A boat trolling lines for tuna or game fish is not engaged in fishing. She is an ordinary power-driven or sailing vessel.
  • A trawler steaming to the grounds with her gear on deck is not engaged in fishing. Under Rule 26(e) she must show power-driven vessel lights.

The privilege in Rule 18 exists because the gear restricts the vessel's manoeuvrability. The vessel's type does not matter. A fishing boat on passage has no more rights than any other power-driven vessel.

Points to note:

  • The day shape is the same for trawlers and other fishing vessels. Only the lights distinguish them.
  • Minimum visibility ranges for these lights depend on the vessel's length and are set by Rule 22. Positioning and spacing are set by Annex I.
  • Lights are shown from sunset to sunrise and in restricted visibility, and shapes by day, under Rule 20.

Applying it on the bridge: the duty to keep clear

Rule 26 itself contains no steering rules. It is an identification rule. The obligations come from elsewhere.

  • Rule 18: A power-driven vessel underway keeps out of the way of a vessel engaged in fishing. So does a sailing vessel. In turn, a fishing vessel keeps out of the way of vessels not under command or restricted in their ability to manoeuvre, so far as possible. She also avoids impeding a vessel constrained by her draught, if circumstances admit.
  • Rule 18 opens with a qualification. It applies except where Rules 9, 10 and 13 otherwise require.
    • Rule 9(c): a fishing vessel must not impede the passage of any other vessel navigating within a narrow channel or fairway.
    • Rule 10(i): a fishing vessel must not impede the passage of any vessel following a traffic lane.
    • Rule 13: a fishing vessel that is overtaking must keep out of the way of the vessel being overtaken.
  • Rule 8(f): a vessel required not to impede must act early. If risk of collision develops anyway, both vessels must still apply the steering and sailing rules. The vessel not to be impeded is not excused from her obligations.
  • Rule 19: in restricted visibility, with vessels not in sight of one another, there is no stand-on or give-way. Rule 18 does not apply, and fishing signals give no privilege.
  • Rule 35(c) and (d): in or near restricted visibility, a fishing vessel sounds one prolonged blast followed by two short blasts, at intervals of not more than 2 minutes. She sounds this underway or at anchor. It is the same signal used by NUC, RAM, CBD, sailing and towing vessels.

Good practice when giving way to fishing vessels:

  • Act early and make the alteration large, as Rule 16 requires.
  • Remember that the gear is often far from the hull. A trawl can be well astern of the vessel towing it, so do not cut close under a trawler's stern.
  • Treat a fishing fleet as an area to avoid. Do not try to thread through it. Plan for it at the passage planning stage where fleets are known to work.

Worked scenarios

1. Night, open sea, fine on the starboard bow. You see two all-round lights, green over white, with a white light higher and further aft. Below them is a red sidelight.

  • The green over white means she is trawling.
  • The higher white light abaft the green is her masthead light. She may be 50 m or over, though a smaller trawler may also show one.
  • The red sidelight means she is making way, and you are seeing her port side.
  • You are power-driven, so you give way under Rule 18. Make a bold alteration early, for example to starboard to pass astern with a generous margin. Remember the warps and net trailing behind her. If you cannot pass well clear, consider reducing speed instead.

2. Gillnetter with gear out. You see red over white, no sidelights, and a separate all-round white light, 2–6 m off to one side and no higher than the white of the red-over-white pair.

  • She is fishing other than trawling and is not making way.
  • The separate white light marks outlying gear extending more than 150 m in that direction.
  • Pass on the opposite side from that light. By day, look for a cone apex upwards displayed on the side where the gear extends.

3. Fishing vessel in a traffic lane. You are following the lane, and a vessel showing red over white is ahead, slightly on your port bow.

  • Under Rule 10(i) she must not impede you.
  • Do not stand on blindly. If she is not moving clear and risk of collision is developing, Rule 8(f) still requires you to apply Part B.
  • Call the Master early. Reduce speed if necessary. Make your intentions clear with Rule 34 signals where they apply.

4. Fog, radar shows a cluster of echoes ahead. You hear one prolonged blast and two short blasts, apparently forward of the beam.

  • That signal could come from several categories of vessel, and you cannot see her. Rule 19 applies, and there is no right of way.
  • Reduce speed to the minimum at which you can keep your course. Take all way off if necessary. Navigate with extreme caution until danger of collision is over.
  • Avoid altering towards a vessel abeam or abaft the beam. Avoid altering to port for a vessel forward of the beam, unless you are overtaking her.

Annex II: fishing in close proximity

Rule 26(d) points to Annex II. That annex provides extra lights for vessels fishing close together. These lights are placed lower than the Rule 26 lights, and the annex specifies visibility of at least 1 mile. In outline:

  • Trawlers:
    • shooting nets: two white lights in a vertical line;
    • hauling nets: white over red;
    • net fast upon an obstruction: two red lights in a vertical line.
    • Annex II distinguishes between trawlers of 20 m and over and smaller trawlers on whether these signals are mandatory. Check the annex text for the exact wording.
  • Pair trawlers: when using Annex II signals, each directs a searchlight by night forward and in the direction of the other vessel of the pair. Each also shows the shooting, hauling or net-fast lights above as appropriate. For vessels under 20 m these signals are optional. Check the annex text for the exact wording.
  • Purse seiners: two yellow lights in a vertical line, flashing alternately. Only a purse seiner hampered by her gear may show them.

In many coastal areas worldwide, small and artisanal fishing craft may not show textbook lights. You may also see bright working lights, unlit boats and unmarked nets. Treat what you see with caution. Rule 2 (good seamanship) and Rule 5 (proper look-out) matter as much as Rule 26.

Common mistakes and oral-exam points

Mistakes examiners hear regularly:

  • Mixing up red over white with white over red.
    • Red over white is fishing other than trawling.
    • White over red is a pilot vessel on duty under Rule 29. Remember "white hat, red nose – pilot".
  • Giving the non-trawler a masthead light. Only the trawler has one under Rule 26.
  • Saying a trawler under 50 m must show a masthead light. She may, but she is not obliged to.
  • Saying a fishing vessel at anchor shows anchor lights. Under Rule 26(a) she shows only her fishing signals.
  • Calling a trolling boat "engaged in fishing". Check the Rule 3(d) definition.
  • Claiming the fishing vessel has right of way in a TSS or narrow channel. Rules 9 and 10 put the duty not to impede on her.
  • Applying Rule 18 in fog to vessels not in sight. Rule 19 governs instead.

Typical oral questions:

  1. What lights does a 60 m trawler show when making way? Green over white all-round, a masthead light abaft and above the green, sidelights and a sternlight.
  2. A vessel shows red over white with an extra white light to the east. What does that mean? She is fishing other than trawling, with outlying gear more than 150 m to the east. Pass to the west.
  3. Is a fishing vessel steaming home a privileged vessel? No. Under Rule 26(e) she shows power-driven lights and has the same obligations.
  4. What fog signal does a fishing vessel at anchor sound? One prolonged and two short, as for underway, under Rule 35(c) and (d).
  5. What day shape does a trawler show, and how is it different from a longliner's? Two cones apex to apex. The shape is identical for both.
Key points
  • Trawling: all-round green over white, plus a masthead light abaft and higher (compulsory at 50 m and over, optional below).
  • Fishing other than trawling: all-round red over white, and never a masthead light.
  • Sidelights and a sternlight are added only when making way through the water.
  • Outlying gear more than 150 m out: all-round white light (or cone apex up) towards the gear, so pass on the other side.
  • Day shape is two cones apexes together for every type of fishing vessel.
  • Rule 26 applies only while actually engaged in fishing (Rule 3(d)); trolling and passage do not count.
  • Fishing vessels must not impede vessels in narrow channels (Rule 9) or traffic lanes (Rule 10), and in restricted visibility, where vessels are not in sight of one another, Rule 19 applies and Rule 18 gives no privilege.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between green over white and red over white lights?

Green over white all-round lights show a vessel engaged in trawling, meaning she is dragging a net or other appliance through the water. Red over white shows a vessel engaged in fishing other than trawling, such as gillnetting, longlining or purse seining. Only the trawler shows a masthead light, and both show sidelights and a sternlight only when making way.

Does a fishing vessel always have right of way under COLREG?

No. A power-driven or sailing vessel must keep out of her way under Rule 18 only while she is engaged in fishing. Even then, the fishing vessel must not impede vessels in a narrow channel or a traffic lane (Rules 9 and 10). An overtaking fishing vessel must keep clear (Rule 13). In restricted visibility, when vessels are not in sight of one another, Rule 19 applies instead and there is no give-way or stand-on vessel.

What does an extra white light on a fishing vessel mean?

On a vessel showing red over white, an additional all-round white light marks outlying gear extending more than 150 m horizontally in that direction. By day the equivalent is a cone apex upwards. Keep to the opposite side and give the gear a wide berth.

Does a fishing vessel at anchor show anchor lights?

Not if she is fishing. Rule 26(a) says a vessel engaged in fishing, underway or at anchor, shows only the lights and shapes of Rule 26. If she is at anchor and not fishing, she shows normal anchor lights and shapes for her length.

What is the fog signal for a vessel engaged in fishing?

One prolonged blast followed by two short blasts, at intervals of not more than 2 minutes, under Rule 35(c). Under Rule 35(d), the same signal is used when she is fishing at anchor, instead of the bell and gong. Because NUC, RAM, CBD, sailing and towing vessels sound the same signal, you cannot identify her by sound alone.

Test yourself

A 40 m trawler is trawling and making way at night. Which lights is she required to show?

A trawler under 50 m need not show the masthead light but may do so, and adds sidelights and a sternlight when making way. Reference: COLREG Rule 26(b).

A longliner has gear extending 300 m from the vessel. What additional signal does Rule 26 require towards the gear?

Outlying gear extending more than 150 m horizontally is marked by an all-round white light or a cone apex upwards in the direction of the gear. Reference: COLREG Rule 26(c)(ii).

In restricted visibility, which sound signal does a vessel engaged in fishing at anchor make?

Rule 35(d) requires a vessel engaged in fishing at anchor to give the Rule 35(c) signal instead of the anchor bell signal. Reference: COLREG Rule 35(c) and (d).

A small power-driven vessel is trolling lines for tuna. Is she a 'vessel engaged in fishing' under COLREG?

The definition covers apparatus that restricts manoeuvrability and expressly excludes trolling lines or other apparatus that do not. Reference: COLREG Rule 3(d).

A vessel engaged in fishing is working inside a traffic lane of a TSS. Which statement is correct?

Fishing in a TSS is not banned, but the fishing vessel must not impede vessels following a traffic lane. Rule 18 applies only where Rule 10 does not otherwise require. Reference: COLREG Rule 10(i) and Rule 18.
