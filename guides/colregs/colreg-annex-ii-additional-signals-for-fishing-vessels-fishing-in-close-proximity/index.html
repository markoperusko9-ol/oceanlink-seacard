COLREG Annex II – Additional signals for fishing vessels fishing in close proximity

Quick answer

Annex II gives fishing vessels working close to each other extra all-round lights that show what they are doing with their gear: shooting, hauling, net fast on an obstruction, pair trawling, or a purse seiner hampered by its net. These lights go below the normal Rule 26 fishing lights and are weaker than them. A deck officer mainly needs to recognise them at night and understand what the gear is doing, so the officer can keep well clear.

Where Annex II fits in the Rules

Annex II is not a standalone rule. Rule 26(d) brings it in. That paragraph says a vessel fishing in close proximity to other fishing vessels may show the additional signals described in Annex II.

Keep three points in mind:

  • It adds to Rule 26 and replaces nothing. The Rule 26 lights stay on: green over white for trawling, red over white for other fishing, plus sidelights and sternlight when making way.
  • It is lights only. Annex II has no day shapes and no sound signals. In restricted visibility, a vessel engaged in fishing still uses the Rule 35 signal: one prolonged blast followed by two short.
  • It is aimed at fishing vessels talking to each other. The original purpose was to warn other fishermen on crowded grounds what each boat's gear is doing. A merchant ship passing through a fishing fleet benefits because the same lights tell you where the gear is and how the vessel is likely to move.

The Annex covers three things: general positioning and visibility, signals for trawlers (including pair trawlers), and signals for purse seiners.

General requirements: position and range

Section 1 of the Annex sets out how the extra lights are carried:

  • They are placed where they can best be seen.
  • The lights in each pair are at least 0.9 m apart.
  • They sit at a lower level than the Rule 26 fishing lights. That means below the green-over-white of a trawler, or below the red-over-white of a vessel fishing other than by trawling.
  • They are all-round lights, visible at at least 1 mile. That range must be less than the range of the lights the Rules otherwise require for fishing vessels.

This matters in practice. On approach you will usually see the Rule 26 lights first. The Annex II lights appear later and lower. If you pick up a white-over-red, or a pair of reds, without the fishing lights above them, look again. You are probably looking at something else.

Trawlers: shooting, hauling and net fast

Section 2 deals with vessels engaged in trawling, whether the gear is demersal (on or near the bottom) or pelagic (midwater).

A trawler of 20 m or more in length uses these signals. Under the current text it is required to show them when it uses the Annex. A trawler under 20 m may show the same lights.

Gear activity Additional lights (vertical line, below Rule 26 lights)
Shooting nets White over white
Hauling nets White over red
Net fast on an obstruction Red over red

What each signal tells the officer of the watch:

  • Shooting: gear is being paid out astern. The vessel is normally on a steady course at low speed, and the warps and net are lengthening behind her.
  • Hauling: she is slowing or stopping, may turn, and the gear is coming in. Her movements are less predictable than when she is steadily towing.
  • Net fast: the gear is snagged on the seabed or a wreck. The vessel is effectively tethered by her warps. She may lie stopped, swing, or manoeuvre hard to free the net. Treat her as close to immobile, with heavily loaded wires leading away from her.

In all three cases, remember the gear itself. Never pass close astern of a trawler. On the net-fast signal in particular, the direction of the warps may have nothing to do with her heading.

Pair trawling and purse seiners

Pair trawling. Two vessels tow one net between them. Each vessel of 20 m or more engaged in pair trawling shows:

  • by night, a searchlight directed forward and in the direction of the other vessel of the pair; and
  • when shooting, hauling or net fast, the same Annex II lights as a single trawler.

Vessels under 20 m may show these signals.

The practical lesson is simple: never pass between two vessels that are pair trawling. A net and warps span the gap. A searchlight beam between two fishing vessels is your strongest clue at night. This searchlight use is specifically provided for in Annex II. It is separate from the Rule 36 provision for signals to attract attention.

Purse seiners. Section 3 allows a vessel fishing with purse seine gear to show two yellow lights in a vertical line. These lights:

  • flash alternately every second, with equal periods of light and darkness; and
  • may be shown only when the vessel is hampered by her fishing gear.

A purse seiner is fishing other than by trawling, so the yellow lights sit below her red-over-white. When you see them, she has a net set around a shoal and cannot manoeuvre freely. Give the whole area a wide berth, not just the hull. Do not confuse these lights with the single all-round flashing yellow light of an air-cushion vessel in non-displacement mode under Rule 23. The purse seiner shows two lights flashing alternately, below fishing lights.

Worked scenario: night passage through a fishing fleet

You are OOW on a bulk carrier at night in open water, approaching a cluster of fishing vessels. Visibility is good.

  1. Identify the Rule 26 lights first. Ahead, fine on the starboard bow, you see green over white, with a masthead light abaft and above them, so she is probably 50 m or more. You also see her red sidelight. Sidelights mean she is making way, and the red tells you that you are on her port side.
  2. Look below for Annex II lights. Closer in, two white lights in a vertical line appear under her fishing lights. She is shooting, so her gear is streaming astern and getting longer.
  3. Apply the steering rules. As a power-driven vessel you keep out of the way of a vessel engaged in fishing (Rule 18). Annex II does not change that. It only tells you more about her gear.
  4. Plan the action. Pass ahead or well clear on the side away from the gear, never close astern. Make the alteration early and large, in line with Rule 8.
  5. Watch for change. Twenty minutes later, two vessels to port each show green over white, and each shows a searchlight directed forward and towards the other. They are pair trawling. Your planned track passes between them, so re-plan around the outside of the pair.
  6. Log and report. Note the fleet's position and activity, and brief the next watch. Call the Master if the density of fishing vessels exceeds what the standing orders allow you to handle alone.

Safe speed (Rule 6) and a proper look-out (Rule 5) drive every step. In a fleet, use binoculars to resolve the lower, shorter-range Annex II lights. Do not rely on radar or AIS alone. Many small fishing vessels do not transmit AIS.

Oral exam: what examiners ask

Candidates for OOW and Chief Mate orals are regularly shown a light diagram and asked to identify it. Expect questions like these:

  • "What does Annex II apply to?" Fishing vessels fishing in close proximity to other fishing vessels. It is invoked by Rule 26(d).
  • "Is it mandatory?" Rule 26(d) says the vessel may exhibit the signals. Within the Annex, trawlers of 20 m or more use the trawling signals, and those under 20 m may use them. Purse seiner lights are permissive.
  • "Where are the lights placed?" Below the Rule 26 fishing lights, at least 0.9 m apart, all-round, visible at least 1 mile but at less range than the fishing lights.
  • "Green over white, then red over red below. What is it?" A trawler whose net is fast on an obstruction. Do not call it not under command: the green-over-white above tells you it is a fishing vessel.
  • "Red over white, with two yellow lights flashing alternately below?" A purse seiner hampered by her gear.
  • "Do these lights change who gives way?" No. Responsibilities still come from Rule 18, with Rules 9 and 10 for narrow channels and traffic separation schemes.
  • "Any day signals?" No. Annex II is lights only.

Answer clearly. Identify the Rule 26 lights first, then the Annex II lights, then say what action you would take.

Key points
  • Annex II is brought in by Rule 26(d) for fishing vessels fishing in close proximity to each other.
  • The extra lights go below the Rule 26 fishing lights, at least 0.9 m apart, all-round, visible at least 1 mile but at less range than the fishing lights.
  • Trawlers: white over white means shooting, white over red means hauling, red over red means net fast on an obstruction.
  • Pair trawlers show a searchlight directed forward and towards the other vessel of the pair. Never pass between them.
  • Purse seiners may show two yellow lights flashing alternately every second, only when hampered by their gear.
  • Annex II has no day shapes or sound signals and does not alter the Rule 18 responsibilities.

Frequently asked questions

What is COLREG Annex II about?

It sets out optional additional lights for fishing vessels fishing close to other fishing vessels. Trawlers can show what their gear is doing (shooting, hauling, or net fast), pair trawlers use a searchlight, and purse seiners can show two alternately flashing yellow lights. These lights are shown under the normal Rule 26 fishing lights.

What do two red lights below a trawler's green over white mean?

The trawler's net has come fast on an obstruction, such as a seabed snag or a wreck. The vessel is effectively held by her gear and may be stopped or manoeuvring to free it. Keep well clear, especially of the area where her warps lead.

Do Annex II lights give a fishing vessel extra right of way?

No. Who keeps out of whose way is decided by Rule 18, together with Rules 9 and 10 where they apply. Annex II only gives more information about the gear and the vessel's likely movements, which helps you choose a safe passing distance and side.

Are there day shapes for shooting or hauling nets?

No. Annex II contains lights only. By day, a fishing vessel shows the Rule 26 shapes. In restricted visibility she gives the Rule 35 sound signal for a vessel engaged in fishing.

Why are Annex II lights hard to see from a merchant ship?

They are placed lower than the Rule 26 lights and must have a shorter range, with a minimum visibility of 1 mile. You will usually identify the fishing lights first and only resolve the Annex II lights when closer, so use binoculars and keep a good look-out in fishing areas.

Test yourself

Which Rule allows a fishing vessel to exhibit the additional signals in Annex II?

Rule 26(d) provides that a vessel fishing in close proximity to other fishing vessels may exhibit the Annex II signals. (COLREG Rule 26(d); Annex II)

A trawler shows green over white, with white over red in a vertical line below. What is she doing?

Under Annex II, white over red below the trawling lights indicates hauling nets. (COLREG Annex II, Section 2)

Where are Annex II lights positioned relative to the Rule 26 fishing lights?

Annex II requires the lights to be at least 0.9 m apart and at a lower level than the Rule 26(b)(i) and (c)(i) lights. (COLREG Annex II, Section 1)

What additional signal may a purse seiner hampered by her gear exhibit?

Purse seiners may show two vertical yellow lights flashing alternately every second, with equal light and occultation, only when hampered by their gear. A single flashing yellow light is an air-cushion vessel under Rule 23. (COLREG Annex II, Section 3)

By night, what additional signal does each vessel of 20 m or more engaged in pair trawling show?

Annex II requires each pair trawler of 20 m or more to show, by night, a searchlight directed forward and towards the other vessel of the pair. (COLREG Annex II, Section 2(b))
