COLREG Rule 4 – Application (Section I): Rules That Never Switch Off

Quick answer

Rule 4 states that the Rules in Section I of Part B (Rules 4 to 10) apply in any condition of visibility. Look-out, safe speed, risk of collision, action to avoid collision, narrow channels and traffic separation schemes therefore apply in clear weather, in fog and in patchy visibility. The steering and sailing rules of Section II apply only when vessels are in sight of one another, and Rule 19 applies when they are not in sight in or near restricted visibility.

What Rule 4 actually says

Rule 4 is one line long. It states that the Rules in Section I apply in any condition of visibility. It sets no duty of its own. It tells you when the next six Rules apply.

Section I of Part B is titled Conduct of vessels in any condition of visibility and covers:

  • Rule 5: Look-out
  • Rule 6: Safe speed
  • Rule 7: Risk of collision
  • Rule 8: Action to avoid collision
  • Rule 9: Narrow channels
  • Rule 10: Traffic separation schemes

In practice, these duties apply on every watch. Brilliant sunshine, thick fog and a drizzly night with visibility coming and going make no difference.

Rule 4 does not decide where the Rules apply. That is Rule 1: high seas and connected navigable waters, subject to special rules made by an appropriate authority. Rule 4 only deals with the visibility condition.

Where Rule 4 sits: the three sections of Part B

Part B (Steering and Sailing Rules) is split by visibility. Each section has its own application rule.

Section Rules Applies when Application rule
I 4–10 Any condition of visibility Rule 4
II 11–18 Vessels in sight of one another Rule 11
III 19 Vessels not in sight of one another, navigating in or near an area of restricted visibility Rule 19(a)

Two definitions in Rule 3 drive this split:

  • In sight of one another: one vessel can be observed visually from the other. A radar echo or an AIS target does not count.
  • Restricted visibility: visibility reduced by fog, mist, falling snow, heavy rainstorms, sandstorms or similar causes.

Section I is the constant layer. Section II is added for each vessel you can see. Section III (Rule 19) is added for vessels not in sight when you are in or near restricted visibility.

A radar-only target beyond visual range in clear weather is governed by Section I alone until she comes into sight. Rule 2 (Responsibility) sits over everything.

Applying Section I on the bridge, clear or thick

Each Section I rule has wording that only makes sense if it applies in all visibility:

  • Rule 5: Look-out is by sight and hearing and all available means. In fog, hearing and radar carry more of the load, but the eyes stay on the job. In clear weather, the radar does not get switched to standby.
  • Rule 6: Safe speed factors include the state of visibility itself. There is also a separate list of factors for vessels with operational radar. Safe speed is reassessed whenever conditions change, not only when fog closes in.
  • Rule 7: This expects proper use of radar, including long-range scanning and radar plotting or equivalent systematic observation. It also warns against assumptions based on scanty radar information. A steady compass bearing indicates risk whether the target is visual or radar-only.
  • Rule 8: Action must be large enough to be readily apparent to a vessel observing visually or by radar. Avoid a succession of small alterations. The rule also covers slackening speed or stopping, and checking the effectiveness of action until the other vessel is finally past and clear.
  • Rule 9: Keep as near to the outer limit of the channel or fairway on your starboard side as is safe and practicable. Do not cross if it impedes a vessel that can navigate only within the channel. Small and sailing vessels must not impede vessels that can only navigate within the channel. Give the prolonged blast when approaching a bend where other vessels may be obscured.
  • Rule 10: Lane discipline, crossing on a heading as nearly as practicable at right angles, and the not-impede duties of small and sailing vessels all continue in fog.

Rule 19(c) reinforces Rule 4. When complying with Section I in restricted visibility, every vessel must have due regard to the prevailing circumstances and conditions of restricted visibility. In other words, Section I still applies in fog, but you read it through the fog.

Rule 19(b) adds two further duties in restricted visibility:

  • Every vessel must proceed at a safe speed adapted to the conditions.
  • A power-driven vessel must have her engines ready for immediate manoeuvre.

Worked scenarios

Scenario 1: clear day, crossing situation

Visibility is excellent. A power-driven vessel is on your starboard bow, bearing steady, range closing.

  • Section I applies: You keep a proper look-out (Rule 5), proceed at a safe speed (Rule 6), confirm risk by compass bearing and ARPA (Rule 7), and make a substantial, early alteration (Rule 8).
  • Section II also applies: You are in sight, so Rule 15 makes you give-way and Rule 16 tells you to take early and substantial action.

Scenario 2: dense fog in a traffic separation scheme

Visibility is about 0.5 nm. A radar target is fine on your port bow, about 6 nm, with a steady bearing. You are following the north-east bound lane.

  • Section I still applies: Look-out, safe speed, systematic radar observation and readily apparent action all apply. Rule 10 still requires you to follow the lane in the general direction of traffic flow.
  • Section II does not apply: You cannot see the other vessel, so there is no give-way vessel and no stand-on vessel.
  • Rule 19 governs the manoeuvre: You must decide whether a close-quarters situation is developing or risk exists. If so, take avoiding action in ample time. For a vessel forward of the beam, avoid altering to port so far as possible, unless she is being overtaken. Rule 35 fog signals also apply.

Scenario 3: patchy visibility

You are plotting a radar target in fog. The fog thins and you see her masthead lights at 3 nm. She is crossing from your port side.

  • From the moment you are in sight of one another, Section II applies to that vessel. Under Rule 15 she is the give-way vessel, and Rule 17 governs your conduct as stand-on.
  • Section I never stopped applying.
  • Watch for another target still hidden in the fog bank. Rule 19 continues to govern that vessel. You may be under two regimes at once, one for each vessel.

Scenario 4: narrow channel in fog

  • Rule 9's keep-to-starboard and not-impede duties still apply, and so does the bend signal.
  • The overtaking agreement signals referred to in Rule 9(e) are the Rule 34(c) signals. Those are only for vessels in sight of one another, so they are not used in fog.
  • Any overtaking in thick weather is a matter of caution, local rules and VTS instructions. A VHF arrangement never replaces the Rules.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking the steering rules apply in fog to vessels not in sight. Rules 13 to 17 are in Section II. There is no stand-on vessel in restricted visibility between vessels not in sight of one another.
  • Thinking Rule 19 replaces Section I. It does not. Rule 19 adds to Section I.
  • Treating a radar or AIS target as "in sight". Rule 3 requires visual observation. Until you can see her, Section II does not apply. In or near restricted visibility, Rule 19 governs your manoeuvre.
  • Applying the Rule 18 hierarchy in fog. Rule 18 is in Section II, so its "who keeps out of whose way" list applies when vessels are in sight. The not-impede duties in Rules 8(f), 9 and 10 sit in Section I and apply in any visibility.
  • Leaving safe speed and radar discipline to fog only. Rule 4 makes safe speed, proper radar use and a full look-out continuous obligations. Night, sea clutter, rain and traffic density all count, not just fog.
  • Confusing Rule 4 with Rule 1. Rule 1 sets the geographical scope. Rule 4 sets the visibility scope for Section I only.

Oral exam points

Examiners rarely ask "recite Rule 4". They test whether you understand the structure. Expect questions like these.

  • Q: Which Rules apply in restricted visibility? A: These all apply:
    • Part A (Rules 1 to 3)
    • Section I (Rules 4 to 10)
    • Section III (Rule 19), for vessels not in sight
    • Part C (lights)
    • Part D (sound signals), including Rule 35
    • Part E

Section II applies only to vessels in sight of one another. Rule 2 applies throughout. - Q: You are in fog and a vessel appears on radar crossing from starboard. Are you the give-way vessel? A: No. Rule 15 does not apply because you are not in sight. If a close-quarters situation is developing, take action under Rule 19 in ample time, with regard to Rule 19(d). Apply Rules 6, 7 and 8 as well. - Q: Does Rule 8 apply in fog? A: Yes, through Rule 4. Its wording deliberately includes action readily apparent to a vessel observing by radar. - Q: Does a vessel under 20 m still have to avoid impeding you in a TSS in fog? A: Yes. Rule 10 is in Section I and applies in any visibility. - Q: What changes when the other vessel comes into sight? A: Section II starts to apply to that vessel. Section I continues. While you remain in or near restricted visibility, Rule 19 still applies to any vessel not yet in sight.

A strong answer names the section, the application rule, and the definition from Rule 3 that triggers it.

Practical habits for the watchkeeper

  • For each target, ask two questions. Am I in sight of her? Am I in or near restricted visibility? The answers decide whether Section II, Rule 19, or Section I alone applies.
  • Keep radar plotting and bearing checks going in clear weather. Rule 7 does not wait for fog.
  • Reassess safe speed when visibility, traffic or sea state changes. Log the reasoning if your company SMS requires it.
  • Know your master's standing orders and company SMS triggers for restricted visibility. Examples include calling the master, putting the engine on standby, manual steering and an extra look-out. These differ by company and ship and sit on top of the Rules, never below them.
  • In patchy weather, expect to apply different regimes to different vessels at the same moment.
Key points
  • Rule 4: Rules 4 to 10 (Section I) apply in any condition of visibility.
  • Section I covers look-out, safe speed, risk of collision, action to avoid collision, narrow channels and TSS.
  • Section II (Rules 11 to 18) applies only when vessels are in sight of one another, meaning visual observation.
  • Between vessels not in sight of one another in or near restricted visibility, Section I plus Rule 19 apply, and there is no give-way or stand-on vessel.
  • Not-impede duties in Rules 8(f), 9 and 10 apply in fog. The Rule 18 hierarchy applies only in sight.
  • In patchy visibility, different vessels can fall under different regimes at the same time.

Frequently asked questions

What does COLREG Rule 4 say?

Rule 4 states that the Rules in Section I of Part B apply in any condition of visibility. That covers Rules 4 to 10: look-out, safe speed, risk of collision, action to avoid collision, narrow channels and traffic separation schemes.

Do the crossing and overtaking rules apply in fog?

No, not between vessels that cannot see each other. Rules 13 to 17 are in Section II, which applies only to vessels in sight of one another. In restricted visibility you manoeuvre under Rule 19 while still complying with Section I.

Does seeing a vessel on radar count as being in sight?

No. Rule 3 defines vessels as in sight of one another only when one can be observed visually from the other. In or near restricted visibility, a radar or AIS contact alone keeps you under Rule 19 for that vessel. In clear weather, a radar-only target beyond visual range is governed by Section I until she comes into sight.

Does Rule 10 for traffic separation schemes apply in restricted visibility?

Yes. Rule 10 is in Section I, so Rule 4 makes it apply in any visibility. Lane discipline, crossing rules and the not-impede duties of small and sailing vessels all continue in fog.

What is the difference between Rule 1 and Rule 4?

Rule 1 sets where the Regulations apply: the high seas and connected navigable waters, subject to special local rules. Rule 4 sets when Section I applies, which is in any condition of visibility.

Test yourself

Which Rules does COLREG Rule 4 make applicable in any condition of visibility?

Rule 4 applies to Section I of Part B, which contains Rules 4 to 10. COLREG 1972, Rule 4.

In dense fog, a radar target is crossing from your starboard bow with a steady bearing. What is your status?

Section II applies only to vessels in sight of one another. In restricted visibility, Rule 19 governs the manoeuvre. COLREG Rules 3(k), 11 and 19.

When are two vessels 'in sight of one another' under the COLREGs?

Rule 3 defines 'in sight' by visual observation only. Radar, AIS and VHF contact do not count. COLREG Rule 3(k).

In restricted visibility, which of these duties still applies through Rule 4?

Rule 10 is in Section I, so its not-impede duties apply in any visibility. Rule 18, Rule 17 and the Rule 34(c) signals depend on vessels being in sight. COLREG Rules 4 and 10(j).

Visibility improves and a vessel you were plotting comes into sight, while another target is still hidden in fog. What applies?

The regime is decided vessel by vessel. Section I applies in any visibility. COLREG Rules 4, 11 and 19(a).
