COLREG Annex I – Positioning and Technical Details of Lights and Shapes

Quick answer

Annex I to COLREG is the technical specification behind Part C. It sets where navigation lights are fitted, how far apart they are, how bright they are, their colours and sectors, and the size and spacing of day shapes. A deck officer does not need every chromaticity coordinate, but should know the key heights, separations, shape dimensions and sector cut-offs, because they explain what you see from another ship and what your own ship must display.

What Annex I is and why a watchkeeper should care

Rules 20 to 31 tell you which lights and shapes a vessel shows. Annex I tells you how: where they are mounted, how far apart, how bright, what colour, and how sharply they cut off at the edges of their sectors. Rule 20(e) requires lights and shapes to comply with Annex I, which forms part of the Regulations, so it is binding, not guidance.

It is written mainly for designers, shipyards and flag administrations. Its final section says that construction of lights and shapes, and the installation of lights, must satisfy the flag State's appropriate authority. In practice, the details are checked at newbuilding and survey, and each flag decides what lantern approval it accepts.

You need it on the bridge for three reasons:

  • Interpreting other ships. The spacing of masthead lights is what lets you judge another vessel's heading and spot an alteration at night.
  • Checking your own ship. It covers shape sizes, all-round light obscuration and replacement lanterns.
  • Exams. Shape dimensions and light separations are standard oral questions.

Annex I has 14 sections:

  1. definition of height above the hull
  2. vertical positioning
  3. horizontal positioning
  4. direction-indicating lights for fishing vessels, dredgers and vessels engaged in underwater operations
  5. screens for sidelights
  6. shapes
  7. colour specification
  8. intensity
  9. horizontal sectors
  10. vertical sectors
  11. non-electric lights
  12. manoeuvring light
  13. high-speed craft
  14. approval

Vertical positioning: the heights and spacings that matter

Section 1 defines height above the hull as height above the uppermost continuous deck, measured vertically beneath the light.

The key vertical requirements in Section 2 are:

Item Requirement (paraphrased)
Forward (or only) masthead light, power-driven vessel 20 m or more Not less than 6 m above the hull. If breadth exceeds 6 m, not less than the breadth, but need not exceed 12 m
After masthead light At least 4.5 m vertically higher than the forward one
Visibility of the pair In all normal trim, the after light is seen over and separate from the forward light at 1,000 m from the stem, viewed from sea level
Masthead light, power-driven vessel 12 m to under 20 m Not less than 2.5 m above the gunwale
Sidelights, power-driven vessel No higher than three-quarters of the height of the forward masthead light, and not low enough to be interfered with by deck lights
Lights in a vertical line, vessel 20 m or more At least 2 m apart. The lowest is at least 4 m above the hull, unless a towing light is required
Lights in a vertical line, vessel under 20 m At least 1 m apart. The lowest is at least 2 m above the gunwale, unless a towing light is required
Three lights in a vertical line Equally spaced
Forward anchor light (when two carried) At least 4.5 m above the after one. On vessels 50 m or more, at least 6 m above the hull

Two further points are worth knowing:

  • Masthead lights go above and clear of all other lights and obstructions. There is one exception. If the NUC or CBD all-round lights (Rules 27(b)(i) and 28) cannot practicably be carried below the masthead lights, they may go above the after masthead light, or between the forward and after masthead lights. If they go between, they must be offset athwartships (see the next section).
  • Fishing vessels. The lower of the two all-round fishing lights must be above the sidelights by at least twice the vertical gap between the two all-round lights.

Horizontal positioning and direction-indicating lights

Section 3 governs fore-and-aft and athwartship placement:

  • Two masthead lights. They must be separated horizontally by at least half the vessel's length, though no more than 100 m is required. The forward light must be no more than a quarter of the length from the stem.
  • One masthead light only. It goes forward of amidships. A vessel under 20 m need not do this, but must carry it as far forward as practicable.
  • Sidelights on power-driven vessels of 20 m or more. They must not be forward of the forward masthead light, and they go at or near the ship's side.
  • NUC/CBD all-round lights between the masthead lights. They must be at least 2 m athwartships from the fore-and-aft centreline.

Section 4 deals with lights that point to gear or obstructions:

  • Fishing vessels with outlying gear (Rule 26(c)(ii)). The light showing the direction of the gear is placed 2 to 6 m horizontally from the red-over-white all-round lights. It is no higher than the white all-round light and no lower than the sidelights.
  • Dredgers and vessels engaged in underwater operations (Rule 27(d)). The obstructed-side and safe-side lights or shapes go as far out horizontally as practicable, and never less than 2 m from the ball-diamond-ball or red-white-red signal. The upper of them must be no higher than the lowest of those three.

Section 5 requires inboard screens, painted matt black, on the sidelights of vessels of 20 m or more. Smaller vessels need them only if necessary to meet the sector requirements. A combined lantern with a single vertical filament and a very narrow red/green division need not have external screens.

Day shapes: sizes and spacing

Section 6 sets the dimensions of shapes. All shapes are black.

Shape Minimum size
Ball Diameter 0.6 m
Cone Base diameter 0.6 m, height equal to the diameter
Cylinder Diameter 0.6 m, height twice the diameter
Diamond Two cones as above, sharing a common base

The vertical distance between shapes is at least 1.5 m. Vessels under 20 m may use smaller shapes in proportion to their size, with correspondingly reduced spacing.

These are minimum sizes. A collapsible ball that has lost its shape, or a home-made substitute, may not meet them. When you take over the shapes locker as a junior officer, check what is actually there:

  • three balls
  • a diamond
  • a cylinder, if the ship may be constrained by her draught (Rule 28 – optional signal; note some national inland rules do not use it)
  • a cone, for vessels that may proceed under sail and power (Rule 25(e)); the diamond above also serves for a tow exceeding 200 m (Rule 24)

Also check the halyards and the spacing arrangement.

Colour, intensity and sectors: the technical sections in plain terms

Colour (Section 7). White, green, red and yellow are each defined by an area on the CIE chromaticity diagram. You will never measure this on board. It is why only an approved lantern and the correct lamp or LED module should be fitted.

Intensity (Section 8). A formula links luminous intensity to the required range, taking account of atmospheric transmissivity. A table then gives the minimum intensity for ranges of 1 to 6 miles. This is how the ranges in Rule 22 become a specification for the lantern maker. Intensity may be limited to avoid glare, but not by a variable control. You cannot dim navigation lights to suit yourself.

Horizontal sectors (Section 9). These requirements explain several things you see on watch:

  • Sidelights forward. Each must show its minimum intensity right ahead and fade to a practical cut-off between 1° and 3° outside its sector. Very close to dead ahead, you may briefly see both sidelights, or one sidelight slightly across the bow.
  • Masthead lights, sternlights, and sidelights at 22.5° abaft the beam. Full intensity is held to within 5° of the sector limits. Inside that last 5° it may fall by up to 50%. It must cut off no more than 5° outside the sector. So near the overtaking boundary of 22.5° abaft the beam there is a genuine zone of ambiguity. This is one reason Rule 13 says to assume you are overtaking if in doubt.
  • All-round lights. They must not be obscured by masts or structures over sectors of more than 6°. Anchor lights are partly excepted, because they need not be carried impracticably high. If one lantern cannot achieve this, two may be used, positioned or screened so that they look like one light at a mile.

Vertical sectors (Section 10). Electric lights must give:

  • full minimum intensity from 5° above to 5° below the horizontal
  • at least 60% of it from 7.5° above to 7.5° below

Sailing vessels underway have a wider requirement, to allow for heel: full intensity within ±5°, and at least 50% within ±25°. Non-electric lights should meet these figures as closely as possible (Sections 10 and 11).

Manoeuvring light (Section 12). If fitted under Rule 34(b), it goes in the same fore-and-aft vertical plane as the masthead lights, with a specified vertical separation from them. On a single-masthead-light vessel it goes where it can best be seen.

High-speed craft (Section 13). HSC may carry the masthead light lower than Section 2 requires, relative to their breadth. The condition is that, seen end-on, the triangle formed by the sidelights and masthead light has base angles of not less than 27°. HSC of 50 m or more in length may also reduce the 4.5 m masthead separation, subject to a formula.

Using Annex I on watch: a worked scenario

Scenario. It is a dark night in open water. Fine on your starboard bow you see two white lights, the lower one to the left of the higher one, and a red light below.

The Annex I geometry tells you the following:

  1. She is a power-driven vessel, probably 50 m or more. Rule 23 requires a second masthead light at that length, though smaller vessels may carry one. The after light is always the higher of the two.
  2. She is showing her port side. You see red, and her forward (lower) light is to the left of her after light. Her bow is pointing to your left, so she is crossing from starboard to port. If risk of collision exists, you are the give-way vessel under Rule 15.
  3. Watch the masthead lights. The horizontal separation of at least half her length gives a sensitive heading indication. If they close up and move towards a vertical line, she is turning towards you. If they open, she is turning away. You will often detect this before radar shows a change in her course.
  4. Masthead lights in line plus both sidelights means she is heading straight at you. If she is also ahead or nearly ahead of you (check the bearing), that is the head-on situation in Rule 14.
  5. Do not rely on the lights alone. Use compass bearings and radar or ARPA (Rule 7). Remember the 1° to 3° sidelight cut-off: a ship nearly head-on can show a misleading glimpse of the other sidelight.

Own-ship checks, every watch and at sunset:

  • Confirm on the navigation light panel that each light is lit and that no failure alarm is active.
  • Visually confirm the lights from the bridge wings where you can.
  • Know where the spare lamps and the secondary or emergency circuit are.
  • Replace a failed lamp or LED unit with the correct approved type. A different lamp can change both colour and intensity.
  • Report any obstruction of an all-round light, such as new deck cargo, a crane jib or a container stack. On box ships, stack height can screen lights, so check the stowage plan against the anchor and NUC light positions.

Oral exam: what examiners typically ask

For OOW and Chief Mate orals, expect quick-fire questions. Answer in substance; you are not expected to recite the wording.

  • What is the minimum size of a ball, and how far apart are shapes? A 0.6 m diameter ball. Shapes at least 1.5 m apart vertically.
  • Describe a cylinder. Black, at least 0.6 m in diameter, with height twice the diameter.
  • Required horizontal separation of masthead lights? At least half the vessel's length, but need not exceed 100 m. The forward light is within a quarter of the length from the stem.
  • Vertical separation? The after light is at least 4.5 m above the forward one, and visible over and separate from it at 1,000 m from the stem at sea level.
  • Where must sidelights be on a power-driven vessel of 20 m or more? Not forward of the forward masthead light, at or near the side, with matt black inboard screens. Their height is no more than three-quarters of the forward masthead light's height.
  • Can NUC lights be above the masthead lights? Yes, if it is impracticable to carry them below. If placed between the masthead lights, they must be at least 2 m off the centreline.
  • What are the sidelight cut-off requirements forward? A practical cut-off between 1° and 3° outside the sector.
  • Who approves navigation lights? The flag State's appropriate authority (Section 14).

Also be ready to link Annex I to the Rules it supports: Rule 21 (sector arcs), Rule 22 (ranges), Rule 23 (power-driven vessels) and Rule 38. Rule 38 gave older vessels transitional exemptions from some Annex I repositioning requirements.

Key points
  • Annex I is binding and sets the technical detail behind Part C: positions, spacing, colours, intensities, sectors and shapes.
  • The after masthead light is at least 4.5 m higher than the forward one, and they are at least half the ship's length apart horizontally (need not exceed 100 m).
  • Shapes are black: ball and cone 0.6 m minimum diameter, cylinder twice as tall as its diameter, diamond two cones base to base, spaced at least 1.5 m.
  • Sidelights cut off 1° to 3° outside their sector forward. Near sector limits, intensity may fade, so lights alone can mislead.
  • All-round lights must not be obscured over more than 6°. If one lantern cannot achieve this, two may be used to appear as one at a mile.
  • Navigation light intensity must not be reduced by a variable control. Fit only approved lanterns and lamps.
  • Construction and installation of lights and shapes is to the satisfaction of the flag State.

Frequently asked questions

What is the size of a black ball under COLREG Annex I?

A ball must have a diameter of not less than 0.6 m. Shapes shown in a vertical line must be at least 1.5 m apart. Vessels under 20 m may use smaller shapes, in proportion to their size, with reduced spacing.

How far apart must the two masthead lights be?

Horizontally, at least half the vessel's length, but no more than 100 m is required. The forward light is no more than a quarter of the length from the stem. Vertically, the after light is at least 4.5 m higher than the forward one.

Why can I sometimes see both sidelights of a ship that is not exactly head-on?

Annex I allows sidelights to fade to a practical cut-off between 1° and 3° outside their sector forward. A vessel within a few degrees of head-on may therefore briefly show both sidelights, or the 'wrong' one. Confirm the situation with compass bearings and radar rather than relying on lights alone.

Can the not under command lights be placed above the masthead lights?

Yes, if it is impracticable to carry them below the masthead lights. They may then go above the after masthead light, or between the forward and after masthead lights. If they go between, they must be at least 2 m athwartships from the centreline.

Who approves navigation lights and shapes on board?

Annex I leaves approval of construction and installation to the appropriate authority of the flag State. Lantern type-approval and acceptable replacement lamps or LED units therefore depend on the flag administration's requirements. Check your flag's rules and the maker's documentation before changing any fitting.

Test yourself

What is the minimum diameter of a ball shape on a vessel of 20 m or more?

A ball must be at least 0.6 m in diameter. The 1.5 m figure is the minimum vertical spacing between shapes. (COLREG Annex I, Section 6)

What is the minimum vertical distance between shapes?

Shapes are at least 1.5 m apart. The 2 m figure applies to lights in a vertical line on vessels of 20 m or more. (COLREG Annex I, Section 6(b))

When two masthead lights are carried, what horizontal separation is required?

The separation must be at least half the vessel's length, but need not be more than 100 m. A quarter of the length is the limit for the forward light's distance from the stem. (COLREG Annex I, Section 3(a))

Within what range of angles outside their sector must sidelights reach practical cut-off in the forward direction?

Forward, sidelights cut off between 1° and 3° outside the prescribed sector. The 5° figure applies at other sector boundaries. (COLREG Annex I, Section 9(a))

What are the proportions of a cylinder shape?

A cylinder is at least 0.6 m in diameter, with a height twice its diameter. A cone's height equals its base diameter. (COLREG Annex I, Section 6(a))
