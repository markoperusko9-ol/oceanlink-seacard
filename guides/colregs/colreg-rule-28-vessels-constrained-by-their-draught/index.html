COLREG Rule 28 – Vessels constrained by their draught

Quick answer

Rule 28 allows a power-driven vessel constrained by her draught to show three all-round red lights in a vertical line, or a black cylinder by day, in addition to her normal Rule 23 lights. Other vessels, except those not under command or restricted in their ability to manoeuvre, must then avoid impeding her if circumstances allow. This does not make her a stand-on vessel. If risk of collision develops, the normal steering and sailing rules apply in full to both vessels.

What Rule 28 actually says

Rule 28 is one of the shortest rules in the book. In substance, a vessel constrained by her draught may, in addition to the lights for a power-driven vessel under Rule 23, show:

  • By night: three all-round red lights in a vertical line, placed where they can best be seen.
  • By day: a cylinder.

Three points follow straight from the wording:

  • The signals are in addition to Rule 23 lights. She keeps her masthead light(s), sidelights and sternlight. This is unlike a vessel not under command, which shows two all-round reds instead of masthead lights.
  • The word is may, not shall. Displaying the signals is optional. However, the duty of other vessels under Rule 18(d) is tied to a vessel exhibiting the signals in Rule 28. A vessel that is constrained but shows nothing gets no special consideration from Rule 18.
  • Under Annex I, shapes are black. The cylinder has a diameter of at least 0.6 m and a height of twice its diameter.

Under Rule 22, all-round red lights on a vessel of 50 m or more must be visible at a minimum of 3 miles. In practice, almost every vessel that uses Rule 28 is well over 50 m.

Who qualifies: the Rule 3(h) definition

Rule 3(h) defines the status, paraphrased as follows. It is a power-driven vessel which, because of her draught in relation to the available depth and width of navigable water, is severely restricted in her ability to deviate from the course she is following.

Break that down the way an examiner will:

  • Power-driven only. A sailing vessel cannot claim this status, however deep her keel.
  • Depth and width together. Being deep-laden is not enough. What matters is the relationship between draught and the water available either side of the track. A VLCC in 200 m of open ocean is not constrained. The same ship in a dredged deep-water route with shoals close on both sides may well be.
  • Severely restricted in deviating from her course. She may still be able to alter speed. The restriction is on leaving her track.

The decision to display the signals rests with the master, based on under-keel clearance, predicted squat, channel width, tidal height and traffic. Some port and coastal authorities have local rules, VTS reporting requirements or designated deep-water routes that affect how and where the status is used. These differ by area, so check the pilot books, port information and company SMS for the waters you are in.

Not every jurisdiction recognises the category in its local rules. The US Inland Navigation Rules, for example, have no equivalent of Rule 28. Always confirm which rules apply where you are.

Where CBD sits in the Rule 18 hierarchy

Rule 18(d) gives the status its meaning:

  • Any vessel other than a vessel not under command (NUC) or restricted in her ability to manoeuvre (RAM) shall, if circumstances admit, avoid impeding the safe passage of a vessel constrained by her draught showing the Rule 28 signals.
  • The vessel constrained by her draught must herself navigate with particular caution, having full regard to her special condition.

"Not impede" is not the same as "keep out of the way". Rule 8(f) explains the difference:

  • A vessel required not to impede must take early action to leave sufficient sea room for the other vessel's safe passage.
  • That vessel is not relieved of her obligation if the two vessels nevertheless approach so as to involve risk of collision. When taking action, she must have full regard to the rules of Part B.
  • The vessel whose passage is not to be impeded remains fully obliged to comply with the Part B rules once risk of collision exists.

In short, the obligation not to impede applies before risk of collision develops. If you leave it too late and a close-quarters crossing exists, Rules 13 to 17 apply in the normal way. The CBD vessel may then be the give-way vessel.

Rule 18 also starts with an exception for Rules 9, 10 and 13. A CBD vessel overtaking another vessel is still the overtaking vessel and must keep out of the way.

Applying it on the bridge

When you sight a CBD vessel

  1. Identify her early. By night you will see three reds in a vertical line plus masthead lights and a sidelight or sternlight. By day look for the cylinder. Cross-check her AIS navigational status, which has a "constrained by her draught" option, but never rely on AIS alone.
  2. Assess her likely track. She will be following the deep water, so look at the chart, the deep-water route or the channel axis. Do not assume she can alter towards you or towards shallower water.
  3. Act early and substantially. Adjust course or speed well before any risk of collision develops, so the situation never becomes a close-quarters crossing. Small, late alterations defeat the purpose of Rule 8(f).
  4. Communicate where useful. VTS or a brief VHF exchange can confirm intentions. Remember that VHF agreements do not override the rules.
  5. Keep monitoring. Use radar, ARPA and visual bearings. If risk of collision develops anyway, apply Part B fully.

When your own ship is constrained

  1. The master decides, using UKC calculations, squat predictions and the passage plan. These limits should already have been identified at the appraisal and planning stage.
  2. Display the lights or cylinder, and update the AIS navigational status.
  3. Inform VTS or the pilot as required locally.
  4. Navigate with particular caution. Use safe speed (bearing squat in mind), a proper lookout and early use of the Rule 34(d) signal if another vessel's intentions are unclear.
  5. Remove the signals and reset AIS as soon as she is no longer constrained, and log the times.

Worked scenarios

Scenario 1: night, approach to a deep-water route. You are OOW on a loaded handysize bulker. Fine on your port bow at about 7 miles you see two masthead lights, a green sidelight and three all-round reds in a vertical line. ARPA shows a steady bearing and a CPA under 0.5 mile. She is a deep-draught tanker in the route, constrained by her draught.

Action: You must avoid impeding her. Take early, substantial action. Typically this means reducing speed markedly (or stopping) so that she passes clear ahead, possibly combined with a bold alteration to starboard to open the CPA. Avoid altering to port towards a vessel on your port bow. Do it now, while there is plenty of room, rather than standing on as though she were simply a give-way vessel crossing from port. Keep checking the bearing until she is past and clear.

Scenario 2: fishing vessel in the channel approaches. A trawler showing green-over-white is working near the edge of a dredged channel as a CBD vessel approaches. The trawler is still bound by Rule 18(d) and must, if circumstances admit, avoid impeding the CBD vessel. The CBD vessel must navigate with particular caution and should not assume the trawler can manoeuvre quickly with gear down.

Scenario 3: restricted visibility. Visibility drops to 0.5 mile and you detect a vessel by radar alone. You hear one prolonged blast followed by two short blasts at intervals of not more than two minutes (Rule 35(c)). That signal is shared by NUC, RAM, CBD, sailing, fishing and towing vessels, so you cannot tell which she is from sound alone. Rule 18 is in Section II and applies only to vessels in sight of one another. In fog with no visual contact, Rule 19 governs and there is no hierarchy. Proceed at safe speed and take early avoiding action per Rule 19(d).

Common mistakes

  • Treating CBD as a privileged stand-on vessel. She has a right not to be impeded, not a right of way. Once risk of collision exists, Part B applies to both vessels.
  • Confusing three reds with two reds. Two all-round reds in a vertical line mean NUC. She shows no masthead lights at all, and adds sidelights and sternlight only when making way through the water. Three reds in a vertical line, with masthead lights, mean CBD. Red-white-red means RAM.
  • Showing the signals just because the ship is deep-laden. The status depends on depth and width of available water. Leaving the signals on in open water misleads other traffic.
  • Forgetting the day shape. The cylinder is often left in the locker on a clear afternoon.
  • Leaving it late. Waiting for the situation to become a crossing case defeats Rule 8(f). Early action is the whole point.
  • Applying Rule 18 in fog. The not-impede duties apply only to vessels in sight of one another.
  • Assuming every jurisdiction recognises CBD. Local and inland rules may not. Check them.

Oral exam points

Examiners for OOW and Chief Mate/Master orals typically probe the following:

  • Define a vessel constrained by her draught. Give the Rule 3(h) definition in your own words: power-driven, draught relative to depth and width, severely restricted in deviating from her course.
  • What does she show? Rule 23 lights plus three all-round reds in a vertical line, or a cylinder by day. The signals are optional ("may").
  • Who must not impede her? Everyone except NUC and RAM vessels, if circumstances admit, under Rule 18(d).
  • Explain "not impede" versus "give way". Use Rule 8(f): early action to allow sea room, and full Part B obligations on both vessels once risk of collision develops.
  • Sound signal in restricted visibility? One prolonged followed by two short, at intervals of not more than two minutes (Rule 35(c)).
  • Can a sailing vessel be CBD? No, the status applies to power-driven vessels only.
  • A CBD vessel is overtaking. Who keeps clear? The CBD vessel does. Rule 13 overrides Rule 18.
  • What must the CBD vessel herself do? Navigate with particular caution, having full regard to her special condition (Rule 18(d)(ii)).

Expect to be shown a lights card at night. Be ready to give the aspect, the status and your action in one clear answer.

Key points
  • Rule 28 allows a power-driven vessel constrained by her draught to show three all-round reds in a vertical line, or a cylinder by day.
  • These signals are additional to the normal Rule 23 lights, so masthead lights stay on.
  • Only power-driven vessels qualify, and the test is draught relative to both depth and width of navigable water.
  • All vessels except NUC and RAM must avoid impeding a CBD vessel showing the signals, if circumstances admit.
  • "Not impede" means early action; once risk of collision exists, Part B applies fully to both vessels.
  • The CBD vessel must navigate with particular caution and still keeps clear when overtaking.
  • In restricted visibility Rule 19 governs; the CBD sound signal is one prolonged blast followed by two short.

Frequently asked questions

What lights does a vessel constrained by her draught show?

She shows the normal power-driven vessel lights under Rule 23 (masthead light or lights, sidelights and sternlight), plus three all-round red lights in a vertical line where they can best be seen. By day she may show a black cylinder instead.

Is a vessel constrained by her draught the stand-on vessel?

Not automatically. Other vessels, except NUC and RAM, must avoid impeding her by taking early action. If risk of collision still develops, Rule 8(f) means both vessels must comply fully with the steering and sailing rules, and depending on the encounter she may be the give-way vessel.

Does a vessel constrained by her draught have to show the Rule 28 signals?

No, Rule 28 says she may show them. However, other vessels' duty under Rule 18(d) applies to a CBD vessel exhibiting the signals. A ship that is constrained but shows nothing cannot expect others to recognise her status.

What is the difference between NUC and constrained by draught signals?

A NUC vessel shows two all-round reds in a vertical line and never shows masthead lights. She adds sidelights and sternlight only when making way. By day she shows two balls. A CBD vessel shows three all-round reds in addition to her masthead lights and sidelights, or a cylinder by day, and NUC vessels are not required to avoid impeding her.

What sound signal does a vessel constrained by her draught make in fog?

Under Rule 35(c) she sounds one prolonged blast followed by two short blasts at intervals of not more than two minutes. NUC, RAM, sailing, fishing and towing vessels use the same signal. Because Rule 18 applies only to vessels in sight, Rule 19 governs conduct when vessels are not in sight of each other.

Test yourself

What day signal may a vessel constrained by her draught display?

Rule 28 provides for a cylinder by day. Two balls indicate NUC, and a diamond appears in towing signals. Reference: COLREG Rule 28 and Annex I.

Which vessel has no Rule 18(d) duty to avoid impeding a vessel constrained by her draught?

Rule 18(d)(i) applies to any vessel other than a NUC or RAM vessel, so fishing and sailing vessels are included. Reference: COLREG Rule 18(d).

In restricted visibility, what sound signal does a vessel constrained by her draught make?

Rule 35(c) groups CBD with NUC, RAM, sailing, fishing and towing vessels for one prolonged followed by two short. Reference: COLREG Rule 35(c).

Which vessel can qualify as a vessel constrained by her draught?

Rule 3(h) limits the status to power-driven vessels restricted by draught relative to the available depth and width of water. Reference: COLREG Rule 3(h).

A vessel required not to impede a CBD vessel fails to act early, and risk of collision develops. What applies?

Rule 8(f) keeps the not-impede vessel's obligation in place and fully binds the vessel not to be impeded once risk of collision exists. Reference: COLREG Rule 8(f).
