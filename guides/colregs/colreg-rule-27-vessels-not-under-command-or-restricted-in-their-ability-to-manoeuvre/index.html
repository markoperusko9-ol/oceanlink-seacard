COLREG Rule 27 – Vessels Not Under Command or Restricted in Their Ability to Manoeuvre

Quick answer

Rule 27 sets the lights and shapes for a vessel not under command (NUC) and a vessel restricted in her ability to manoeuvre (RAM), including dredgers, divers, restricted tows and mineclearance vessels. NUC shows two all-round red lights or two balls. RAM shows red-white-red all-round lights or ball-diamond-ball. Sidelights, sternlight and (for RAM only) masthead lights are added when making way.

What Rule 27 actually covers

Rule 27 sits in Part C (Lights and Shapes). It tells you how to show that you are NUC or RAM. It does not decide who gives way. That is Rule 18, and it only applies when vessels are in sight of one another. The definitions come from Rule 3:

  • Not under command (Rule 3(f)): a vessel that, through some exceptional circumstance, cannot manoeuvre as the Rules require and so cannot keep out of the way. Typical examples are main engine failure, steering gear failure or a fouled propeller.
  • Restricted in her ability to manoeuvre (Rule 3(g)): a vessel that, because of the nature of her work, cannot manoeuvre as required and so cannot keep out of the way. The Rule lists examples:
    • laying, servicing or picking up a navigation mark, submarine cable or pipeline
    • dredging, surveying or underwater operations
    • replenishment or transferring persons, provisions or cargo while underway
    • launching or recovering aircraft
    • mineclearance
    • a towing operation that severely restricts the tug and tow in deviating from their course

The key distinction for the exam:

  • NUC is about something going wrong.
  • RAM is about the work the vessel is doing.

A ship that is merely slow, deep-laden, or awkward in a narrow channel is neither. The last of these may be constrained by her draught under Rule 28, which is a different status.

The lights and shapes, paragraph by paragraph

Paragraph (a): NUC - Two all-round red lights in a vertical line, where they can best be seen. - By day, two balls (or similar shapes) in a vertical line. - When making way through the water, add sidelights and a sternlight. There are no masthead lights.

Paragraph (b): RAM (other than mineclearance) - Three all-round lights in a vertical line: red over white over red. - By day, three shapes: ball over diamond over ball. - When making way, add masthead light(s), sidelights and sternlight. - At anchor, add the Rule 30 anchor light(s) or ball.

Paragraph (c): restricted tow - A power-driven vessel on a towing operation that severely restricts her and her tow shows the Rule 24(a) towing lights or shapes plus the RAM lights or shapes from (b)(i) and (ii).

Paragraph (d): dredging or underwater operations, when RAM - Shows the RAM signals in (b)(i), (ii) and (iii). - Where an obstruction exists, she also shows: - two all-round reds, or two balls, on the side where the obstruction is - two all-round greens, or two diamonds, on the side where others may pass - At anchor, these lights or shapes are shown instead of the Rule 30 anchor signals.

Paragraph (e): small diving vessels - If the vessel is too small to show everything in (d), she shows red-white-red all-round lights. - By day, she shows a rigid replica of International Code flag 'A', not less than 1 metre high, arranged so it is visible all round.

Paragraph (f): mineclearance - Shows the Rule 23 power-driven lights, or the Rule 30 anchor signals as appropriate, plus three all-round greens or three balls. - One is shown near the foremast head and one at each end of the fore yard. - These signals mean it is dangerous to approach within 1000 m.

Paragraph (g): small vessels - Vessels under 12 m are not required to show Rule 27 signals, except when engaged in diving operations.

Paragraph (h): not distress signals - Rule 27 signals do not mean distress or a request for assistance. Distress signals are in Annex IV.

Applying it on the bridge

When you see the signals - Identify the vessel's status first. Then work out her aspect from the sidelights and masthead lights she shows. - Then apply the steering and sailing rules: - Under Rule 18, a power-driven vessel and a sailing vessel underway keep out of the way of NUC and RAM vessels. - A fishing vessel does so as far as possible. - Act early and substantially, as Rule 8 requires. A RAM vessel may have divers down, a cable or pipe on the bottom, or a tow behind her. Give her the widest berth the navigable water allows.

When you are the one showing them The details are normally set out in your company SMS checklists, so follow those. A typical sequence on engine or steering failure underway:

  1. Inform the Master and the engine room immediately.
  2. Switch on the NUC lights (most bridge panels have a dedicated switch). Hoist the two balls by day.
  3. Switch off the masthead lights. Check that the sidelights and sternlight stay on only while the ship is still making way.
  4. Change the AIS navigational status to 'not under command'.
  5. In restricted visibility, sound one prolonged blast followed by two short blasts at intervals of not more than 2 minutes, as Rule 35(c) requires.
  6. Broadcast a navigational warning on VHF and inform any VTS. Prepare the anchor if depth allows.
  7. Record times and actions in the deck log.

Restricted visibility Rule 35(c) gives the same sound signal to several categories:

  • NUC
  • RAM
  • constrained by draught
  • sailing
  • fishing
  • towing or pushing

You therefore cannot tell from the whistle which one you are hearing.

Under Rule 35(d), a RAM vessel at anchor carrying out her work, and a fishing vessel at anchor, sound this same signal instead of the anchor bell and gong signals.

Worked scenarios

Scenario 1: night, open sea - You see two red all-round lights in a vertical line and a green sidelight. There are no masthead lights. - This is a NUC vessel making way, showing you her starboard side. - As a power-driven vessel, you keep out of her way under Rule 18. Alter early, and preferably pass astern of her. Her movements may be unpredictable, so do not cut close ahead.

Scenario 2: night, coastal - You see red-white-red all-round lights, two masthead lights and a red sidelight. - This is a RAM vessel making way (two masthead lights, so probably 50 m or more in length), showing you her port side. She could be a survey ship running lines or a vessel replenishing. - Keep clear. Call her on VHF if her intentions or the extent of her work are unclear. Remember that VHF agreements never override the Rules.

Scenario 3: daytime, harbour approach - A dredger shows ball-diamond-ball. On her port side she shows two balls; on her starboard side, two diamonds. - The obstruction is on her port side. Pass on her starboard side, the diamond side, reducing speed to limit wash.

Scenario 4: your own ship, in a TSS lane - Your main engine trips and you lose way. - Show the NUC signals, set the AIS status and inform VTS. - Being NUC does not cancel Rule 2. You must still use every means available to avoid collision, including anchor, bow thruster and early VHF warnings.

Scenario 5: tug with a long tow - At night you see three masthead lights in a vertical line and red-white-red all-round lights. - She is towing with a tow over 200 m and is severely restricted, under Rule 27(c). - Keep well clear of both the tug and the tow. Never pass between them.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving the masthead lights on when NUC. Rule 27(a) adds only sidelights and a sternlight. Masthead lights belong to RAM making way, not NUC.
  • Keeping sidelights on when stopped. Both NUC and RAM add sidelights and sternlight only when making way through the water. Being underway is not the same as making way (Rule 3(i)).
  • Confusing NUC with aground. A vessel aground shows two all-round reds plus the anchor light(s), and three balls by day (Rule 30(d)). Two reds alone, with no anchor lights, means NUC.
  • Claiming NUC for convenience. Slow speed, manoeuvring with a pilot or heavy traffic are not exceptional circumstances. Misuse may later be judged by investigators or a court.
  • Thinking NUC or RAM status gives priority when not in sight. Rule 18 applies only to vessels in sight of one another (including in restricted visibility once visual contact is made). When vessels are not in sight of one another in or near an area of restricted visibility, Rule 19 governs every vessel equally, whatever her status.
  • Reading Rule 27 signals as distress. Paragraph (h) is explicit that they are not. A NUC ship that needs help must use an Annex IV signal or GMDSS.
  • Passing a dredger on the wrong side. Remember the pairs: red or balls = obstruction; green or diamonds = clear side.

Oral exam points

Examiners typically ask you to draw or identify the lights from several aspects. Then they move to the 'what would you do' questions. Be ready for these:

  • Define NUC and RAM. Give the reason for each: an exceptional circumstance versus the nature of the work. List at least four RAM examples from Rule 3(g).
  • Lights for a NUC vessel stopped, and then making way. Answer: two reds only; then two reds plus sidelights and sternlight.
  • Day signal for RAM. Ball-diamond-ball. Add the Rule 30 anchor ball if at anchor, except dredgers under 27(d), which use their obstruction shapes instead.
  • Sound signal in fog, underway and at anchor. One prolonged and two short at intervals of not more than 2 minutes. A RAM vessel at anchor doing her work uses it instead of the bell.
  • Who keeps out of the way of whom. Walk through Rule 18 in order. Note that NUC and RAM vessels are not required to avoid impeding a vessel constrained by her draught. Rule 18(d) applies to vessels other than NUC or RAM.
  • The mineclearance distance. 1000 m.
  • The diving flag. A rigid replica of flag 'A', not less than 1 m high, all-round visible.
  • Rule 10 links. Rule 10(k) and (l) exempt RAM vessels from Rule 10, to the extent necessary, when they are maintaining navigational safety or working on submarine cables inside a TSS.

Tip: when describing lights, always state the aspect you are viewing from. Then say what action you would take and under which rule. Examiners want the reasoning, not just the colours.

Key points
  • NUC: two all-round reds or two balls; add sidelights and sternlight only when making way, never masthead lights.
  • RAM: red-white-red or ball-diamond-ball; add masthead, sidelights and sternlight when making way, and anchor signals at anchor.
  • Dredgers and underwater operations: two reds or balls mark the obstruction side, two greens or diamonds mark the side to pass.
  • Mineclearance vessels show three greens or three balls; keep more than 1000 m off.
  • Rule 27 signals are not distress signals; Rule 18 keep-out-of-the-way duties apply only when vessels are in sight of one another.
  • Two reds plus anchor lights means aground, not NUC.
  • In restricted visibility, NUC and RAM sound one prolonged and two short at intervals of not more than 2 minutes.

Frequently asked questions

Does a NUC vessel show masthead lights?

No. A NUC vessel shows two all-round red lights. When making way through the water she adds only sidelights and a sternlight. Masthead lights are added by a RAM vessel making way, not by a NUC vessel.

What is the difference between not under command and restricted in ability to manoeuvre?

NUC results from an exceptional circumstance, such as engine or steering failure. RAM results from the nature of the vessel's work, such as dredging, cable laying, replenishment underway or a restricted tow. Both are owed the same give-way obligations under Rule 18 when in sight.

Which side do I pass a dredger on?

Pass on the side showing two all-round green lights by night or two diamonds by day. The side showing two reds or two balls has the obstruction, such as a pipeline, anchor wires or the dredge head. Reduce speed and wash when passing.

Does a NUC or RAM vessel have right of way in fog?

Not while you cannot see each other. Rule 18 applies only to vessels in sight of one another. When vessels are not in sight in or near restricted visibility, every vessel follows Rule 19, whatever her status. If you come into sight of each other, Rule 18 applies again. NUC and RAM status still affects the Rule 35 sound signals.

When should my ship display NUC signals?

Display them when an exceptional circumstance, such as main engine, steering or propeller failure, prevents you from manoeuvring as the Rules require. The decision and procedure are normally set out in your company SMS and taken with the Master. Display them promptly, update AIS and warn traffic, and remove them as soon as the ship can manoeuvre normally again.

Test yourself

At night a vessel is underway but stopped, not making way, because of a steering failure. Which lights should she show?

A NUC vessel adds sidelights and sternlight only when making way through the water, and never masthead lights. Reference: COLREG Rule 27(a).

A RAM vessel making way through the water shows which combination?

Unlike a NUC vessel, a RAM vessel making way adds masthead lights as well as sidelights and sternlight. Reference: COLREG Rule 27(b)(iii).

A dredger shows two balls on her starboard side and two diamonds on her port side. How should you pass?

Balls (or red lights) mark the obstruction side; diamonds (or green lights) mark the side where others may pass. Reference: COLREG Rule 27(d).

In dense fog your radar shows a target whose AIS status is 'not under command'. You cannot see her. What governs your conduct?

Rule 18 responsibilities apply only to vessels in sight of one another. In restricted visibility with no visual contact, Rule 19 applies. Reference: COLREG Rules 11, 18 and 19.

At night you see two all-round red lights in a vertical line together with anchor lights. What is she?

A vessel aground shows the anchor light(s) plus two all-round reds. By day she shows three balls. Reference: COLREG Rule 30(d).
