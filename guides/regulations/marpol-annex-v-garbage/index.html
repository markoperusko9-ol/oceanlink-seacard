MARPOL Annex V – Garbage: What Every Seafarer Needs to Know

Quick answer

MARPOL Annex V controls garbage from ships. Since the revised Annex entered into force on 1 January 2013, discharging any garbage into the sea is prohibited unless the Annex expressly allows it. On board it creates three things you will deal with: placards, a Garbage Management Plan and the Garbage Record Book. PSC officers check all three and compare your records with what is actually stored and landed.

What Annex V is and when it came in

Annex V is the garbage annex of MARPOL. It was an optional annex, which meant states could ratify MARPOL without accepting it. It entered into force on 31 December 1988.

The original version banned plastics and then allowed most other garbage to be discharged beyond set distances from land. The revised Annex V turned that round. It entered into force on 1 January 2013 and starts from a general prohibition: no garbage goes into the sea unless a specific regulation permits it. Most of the confusion on board comes from people who still think in the old way: "it's not plastic, so it can go."

The Annex has been amended since 2013. Two later changes matter most on board:

  • 2018: the Garbage Record Book format was revised, e-waste became its own category, and criteria were introduced for classifying solid bulk cargo residues as harmful to the marine environment (HME).
  • 1 May 2024: amendments extended the Garbage Record Book requirement to smaller ships.

Always work from the current consolidated text and your flag state's instructions.

How the Annex is structured

The revised Annex V is short. In outline:

  • Definitions and application. Garbage covers food, domestic and operational wastes, plastics, cargo residues, incinerator ash, cooking oil, fishing gear and animal carcasses. Sewage and oil are excluded because other annexes cover them.
  • General prohibition, plus a ban on discharging plastics anywhere. This includes synthetic ropes, fishing nets, plastic bags and incinerator ash from plastic products.
  • Discharge outside special areas.
  • Special requirements for fixed and floating platforms, and for ships alongside or within 500 m of them.
  • Discharge within special areas.
  • Exceptions. These cover safety of the ship and people, accidental loss from damage where reasonable precautions were taken, and accidental loss of fishing gear.
  • Reception facilities.
  • Port state control on operational requirements.
  • Placards, garbage management plans and record-keeping.

The special areas are:

  • Mediterranean Sea
  • Baltic Sea
  • Black Sea
  • Red Sea
  • Gulfs area
  • North Sea
  • Wider Caribbean Region
  • Antarctic area

A special area's stricter rules only take effect once IMO sets a date, after enough reception facilities have been notified. Check the current status for your trading area. Do not assume every area is live.

Discharge rules in practice

The table below is a simplified summary. Distances are from the nearest land, and the ship must be en route unless stated otherwise.

Garbage type Outside special areas Inside special areas
Plastics, incl. synthetic rope and nets Prohibited Prohibited
Food waste, comminuted (passes 25 mm screen) ≥ 3 nm ≥ 12 nm
Food waste, not comminuted ≥ 12 nm Prohibited
Cargo residues not HME ≥ 12 nm Only under strict conditions (see below)
Cleaning agents in hold/deck wash water, not HME Permitted Permitted under conditions
Animal carcasses As far from land as possible Prohibited
Domestic waste, cooking oil, incinerator ash, operational waste, fishing gear, e-waste Prohibited Prohibited

Key points that catch people out:

  • Cooking oil is not food waste. Its discharge is prohibited everywhere.
  • Mixed garbage takes the strictest rule. If food waste is contaminated with plastic, it is treated as plastic.
  • Antarctic area: avian products must not be introduced unless they have been treated to make them sterile.
  • Platforms: at fixed or floating platforms, and for ships alongside or within 500 m of them, only comminuted food waste may be discharged, and only at ≥ 12 nm from land.

In a special area, cargo residues in wash water may only go over the side if all of these conditions are met:

  • Both the departure port and the next port are inside the special area.
  • The ship will not transit outside the area between those ports.
  • Neither port has adequate reception facilities.
  • The residues are not HME.
  • The ship is ≥ 12 nm from land and en route.

Records and documents on board

Annex V does not issue a certificate. There is nothing equivalent to an IOPP certificate. What it requires instead is:

1. Placards

  • Required on every ship of 12 m length overall and above, and on platforms.
  • They tell crew and passengers the discharge rules.
  • They must be in the working language of the crew. Ships on international voyages must also display them in English, French or Spanish.

2. Garbage Management Plan (GMP)

  • Required on every ship of 100 GT and above, every ship certified to carry 15 or more persons, and on platforms.
  • It covers:
    • minimising waste at source
    • collection, storage, processing and disposal
    • use of on-board equipment
  • It names the person in charge of carrying it out.

3. Garbage Record Book (GRB)

  • Originally required on ships of 400 GT and above, ships certified to carry 15 or more persons on international voyages, and platforms.
  • The 2024 amendments brought ships of 100 GT and above into scope. Confirm with your flag state how this applies to your ship.
  • Part I covers all garbage except cargo residues.
  • Part II covers cargo residues and is used by ships carrying solid bulk cargoes.

The GRB uses these categories:

Code Category
A Plastics
B Food wastes
C Domestic wastes
D Cooking oil
E Incinerator ashes
F Operational wastes
G Animal carcasses
H Fishing gear
I E-waste
J Cargo residues (non-HME)
K Cargo residues (HME)

Record-keeping rules:

  • Each discharge, incineration or delivery ashore is entered with date, time, position, category and estimated amount in cubic metres.
  • The officer in charge signs each entry, and the Master signs each completed page.
  • Accidental or exceptional discharges are recorded with the circumstances and reasons.
  • Keep the receipts from reception facilities with the book.
  • The GRB is retained on board for two years from the last entry.
  • Colour-coded bins and onboard storage arrangements usually come from the company SMS and IMO guidelines, not from the Annex itself. Follow your GMP.

Worked scenario: bulk carrier washing holds

Your ship has discharged a bulk cargo and the Chief Officer wants to wash holds on passage. Work through it in this order:

  1. Check the cargo's HME status. The shipper's declaration should state whether the cargo is HME. If it is HME, the residues and wash water go ashore. Full stop.
  2. Check where you are. Outside a special area, non-HME residues may be discharged ≥ 12 nm from land while en route. Inside one, apply all of the conditions listed in the discharge rules section, and accept that in most cases it will not be allowed.
  3. Check the cleaning agents. They must not be HME. Keep the product safety data on file.
  4. Record it in GRB Part II. Enter start and stop times and positions, the category (J), the estimated quantity, and the reference to the hold washing.
  5. Check the next port's rules. Some coastal states and ports have stricter local requirements, and these apply in their waters.

If the voyage is short and inside a special area, plan for retention and delivery ashore. Do not start washing and then try to make the rules fit.

What port state control typically checks

PSC inspectors work from the documents to the deck. Expect them to:

  • Inspect placards: present, legible and in the right languages.
  • Read the GMP: ship-specific, current, and naming the responsible person.
  • Review the GRB:
    • complete and correctly categorised entries
    • positions consistent with permitted distances
    • signatures in place
    • receipts that match the entries
  • Compare quantities. Does what you stored, incinerated and landed make sense for your crew size and time since the last port? A month at sea with almost nothing landed raises questions.
  • Look at the stores: segregation, storage condition, and whether plastics are mixed with food waste.
  • Check the incinerator, if fitted: working, and whether operators know what they can and cannot burn.
  • Interview crew. Under the PSC provisions, inspectors can test whether crew are familiar with garbage procedures. Ratings may be asked where plastics go or how far off food waste can be discharged.

Serious failings can lead to detention. They can also lead to prosecution under the coastal state's law. Falsified record books have resulted in criminal cases in several jurisdictions.

Oral exam: questions examiners like

  • "Can you throw food waste overboard 5 nm off the coast in the Mediterranean?" No. Inside a special area, food waste must be comminuted and the ship must be ≥ 12 nm from land and en route.
  • "Is cooking oil food waste?" No. It has its own category, and its discharge is prohibited.
  • "Which certificate covers Annex V?" None. Compliance is shown through placards, the GMP, the GRB and receipts.
  • "Who decides whether a bulk cargo residue is HME?" The shipper classifies it against the Annex V criteria and declares it.
  • "You lost a fishing net overboard in heavy weather. What do you do?" Record it in the GRB. Annex V also requires accidental loss or discharge of fishing gear posing a significant threat to the environment to be reported to the flag state and, where relevant, the coastal state. Your SMS will set out how.
  • "What is the general principle of revised Annex V?" All discharge of garbage is prohibited unless it is expressly permitted.
Key points
  • Revised Annex V (in force 1 January 2013) prohibits all garbage discharge unless expressly permitted.
  • Plastics, cooking oil, incinerator ash, domestic waste, e-waste and fishing gear must never be discharged.
  • Comminuted food waste goes at ≥ 3 nm outside special areas and ≥ 12 nm inside, en route.
  • Annex V has no certificate; compliance rests on placards, the Garbage Management Plan and the Garbage Record Book.
  • The GRB has Part I (general garbage) and Part II (cargo residues), and is kept two years after the last entry.
  • Mixed garbage follows the strictest applicable rule.
  • PSC compares GRB entries, receipts and actual onboard storage, and may test crew knowledge.

Frequently asked questions

Can food waste be thrown overboard under MARPOL Annex V?

Yes, but only within limits. Outside special areas, comminuted food waste can go at 3 nm or more from land, and uncomminuted food waste at 12 nm or more, with the ship en route. Inside special areas, only comminuted food waste is allowed, at 12 nm or more. It must not be mixed with plastics or other garbage.

Which ships need a Garbage Record Book?

Originally, ships of 400 GT and above, ships certified to carry 15 or more persons on international voyages, and fixed or floating platforms. Amendments in force from 1 May 2024 extended the requirement to ships of 100 GT and above. Check your flag state's instructions for how this is applied to your ship.

Is there an Annex V certificate like the IOPP certificate?

No. Annex V does not create a statutory certificate. Compliance is shown through placards, the Garbage Management Plan, the Garbage Record Book and the receipts from port reception facilities.

How long must the Garbage Record Book be kept on board?

For two years after the date of the last entry. Keep the reception facility receipts with it so the entries can be verified during inspections.

Can we burn plastics in the ship's incinerator?

Annex V itself does not regulate incineration. Shipboard incineration is governed by MARPOL Annex VI and the incinerator's type approval, which restrict what can be burned, including certain plastics such as PVC except in approved incinerators. Follow the incinerator manual and your Garbage Management Plan, and remember the resulting ash can never be discharged to sea.

Test yourself

What is the basic principle of the revised MARPOL Annex V?

Since 1 January 2013, Annex V starts from a general prohibition with limited exceptions. Reference: MARPOL Annex V, Regulation 3.

Outside special areas, what is the minimum distance from nearest land for discharging comminuted food waste while en route?

Comminuted food waste passing a 25 mm screen may be discharged at 3 nm or more outside special areas. Reference: MARPOL Annex V, Regulation 4.

How should used cooking oil be disposed of under Annex V?

Cooking oil is a separate garbage category and its discharge into the sea is prohibited. Reference: MARPOL Annex V, Regulations 1 and 3.

Which document does MARPOL Annex V require for compliance?

Annex V issues no certificate. It requires placards, a Garbage Management Plan and a Garbage Record Book according to ship size and persons carried. Reference: MARPOL Annex V, Regulation 10.

Inside a special area, when may non-HME cargo residues in hold wash water be discharged?

Discharge of residues in wash water within special areas is allowed only when all the listed conditions are met. Reference: MARPOL Annex V, Regulation 6.
