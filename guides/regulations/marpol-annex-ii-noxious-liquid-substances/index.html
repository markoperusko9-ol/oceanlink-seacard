MARPOL Annex II – Noxious Liquid Substances: A Seafarer's Guide

Quick answer

MARPOL Annex II regulates pollution from noxious liquid substances (NLS) carried in bulk, mainly chemicals and vegetable oils on chemical tankers. It sorts cargoes into Categories X, Y, Z and Other Substances, sets strict conditions for stripping, prewashing and discharging tank residues, and requires an approved P&A Manual, a Cargo Record Book and an NLS emergency plan on board. Port state control checks these records against what the ship actually did.

What Annex II is and when it came into force

Annex II is the part of MARPOL that deals with noxious liquid substances carried in bulk. In practice that means most chemical tanker cargoes, many vegetable and animal oils, and some products carried by offshore supply vessels and product tankers. Oil cargoes fall under Annex I. Packaged dangerous goods fall under Annex III and the IMDG Code.

Key dates you can quote in an oral exam:

  • 2 October 1983: MARPOL 73/78 entered into force, with implementation of Annex II deferred.
  • 6 April 1987: the original Annex II took effect. It used Categories A, B, C and D.
  • 1 January 2007: the revised Annex II entered into force. It introduced the current X, Y, Z and OS system and tighter stripping requirements.

Annex II works hand in hand with the IBC Code. The IBC Code sets the construction and equipment standards for chemical tankers and lists products with their ship type and pollution category. Annex II sets the operational discharge rules. The IBC Code is mandatory under both SOLAS Chapter VII and MARPOL Annex II.

Amendments continue to be adopted. Always work from the current consolidated edition and your ship's approved manuals.

Pollution categories X, Y, Z and OS

Every NLS is placed in a category (Regulation 6). The assessment is based on hazard profiles developed by GESAMP, the joint group of experts on marine environmental protection.

Category Hazard to marine resources or human health Consequence for discharge
X Major hazard Discharge prohibited. Mandatory prewash with residues to a reception facility
Y Hazard, or harm to amenities and legitimate uses of the sea Limited quality and quantity of discharge
Z Minor hazard Less stringent restrictions
OS (Other Substances) Considered no harm when discharged from tank cleaning or deballasting Not subject to Annex II requirements

Where to find the category:

  • IBC Code chapter 17 lists most products. Chapter 18 lists products outside the scope of the Code.
  • The annual MEPC.2/Circular lists provisionally assessed products and those covered by tripartite agreements.

If a cargo has not been categorised, it cannot be carried until a provisional assessment is agreed between the Administrations concerned. Many people are surprised that vegetable oils are Annex II cargoes, mostly Category Y.

How the Annex is structured

The regulations you will be asked about most often:

Regulation Subject
1–5 Definitions, application, exceptions, exemptions, equivalents
6 Categorisation and listing of NLS
7–10 Surveys and certification, including the NLS Certificate
11–12 Design and construction. Pumping, piping, unloading arrangements and slop tanks (stripping)
13 Control of discharges of residues
14 Procedures and Arrangements (P&A) Manual
15 Cargo Record Book
16 Measures of control, including surveyors at unloading ports
17 Shipboard marine pollution emergency plan for NLS
18 Reception facilities and cargo unloading terminal arrangements

The appendices contain:

  • the categorisation guidelines
  • the forms of the Cargo Record Book and NLS Certificate
  • the standard format of the P&A Manual
  • the stripping (water test) assessment
  • the prewash procedures
  • the ventilation procedures

Stripping, prewash, ventilation and discharge at sea

Stripping. Ships built on or after 1 January 2007 must be able to strip each tank carrying Category X, Y or Z so that no more than 75 litres of residue remains in the tank and its associated piping. This is proven by a water test witnessed during survey. Older ships have higher limits set out in Regulation 12.

Discharge conditions at sea (Category X, Y and Z residue/water mixtures). Where discharge is allowed, all of the following must be met:

  • The ship is en route at at least 7 knots if self-propelled, or 4 knots if not.
  • Discharge is below the waterline, through the underwater outlet, at no more than its maximum design rate.
  • The ship is at least 12 nautical miles from the nearest land.
  • The water depth is at least 25 metres.

Category X. A prewash is required before the ship leaves the unloading port. The residues go to a reception facility until the concentration in the effluent is 0.1% by weight or less, or the Annex's prewash procedure has been followed. Water put into the tank after that may be discharged at sea under the conditions above.

Category Y and Z. A prewash with residues to shore is required when:

  • the tank was not unloaded in line with the P&A Manual, for example after a stripping failure, or
  • the cargo is a solidifying or high-viscosity Category Y product.

Amendments in force since 1 January 2021 also require a prewash for certain high-viscosity or solidifying persistent floating products unloaded in specified European areas: North West European waters, the Baltic Sea area, Western European waters and the Norwegian Sea.

Ventilation. Residues of substances with a vapour pressure above 5 kPa at 20 °C may be removed by ventilation, following the procedures in the Annex and the P&A Manual.

Antarctic area. Any discharge of NLS or mixtures containing NLS is prohibited.

Charterers, terminals, regional agreements and your company SMS often impose stricter rules than the Annex. The strictest applicable rule wins.

Records and certificates on board

Certificate.

  • Chemical tankers certified under the IBC Code carry the International Certificate of Fitness for the Carriage of Dangerous Chemicals in Bulk. This is accepted as showing Annex II compliance.
  • Other ships carrying NLS in bulk carry the International Pollution Prevention Certificate for the Carriage of Noxious Liquid Substances in Bulk (NLS Certificate).
  • The certificate lists which products the ship may carry. Validity is up to five years, with annual and intermediate surveys.

P&A Manual (Regulation 14). This manual is approved by the Administration and is ship-specific. It covers:

  • unloading and stripping
  • prewash and tank washing
  • slop handling
  • ventilation
  • ballasting
  • discharge

It follows a standard format so that surveyors anywhere can read it.

Cargo Record Book (Regulation 15). The Cargo Record Book follows the standard form in the Annex. Entries are made tank by tank for:

  • loading and internal transfers
  • unloading
  • mandatory prewash
  • tank cleaning
  • discharge of washings to sea or shore
  • ballasting
  • accidental discharges
  • surveyor control

Each entry is signed by the officer in charge of the operation, and each completed page by the master. The book is kept on board, readily available for inspection, for three years after the last entry.

SMPEP (Regulation 17). Ships of 150 GT and above certified to carry NLS must have an approved NLS emergency plan. It is commonly combined with the Annex I SOPEP as a single Shipboard Marine Pollution Emergency Plan.

Check your flag's requirements and your company's procedures for electronic record books, the record retention location and the endorsement format.

Worked scenario: Category X discharge port

You are chief officer on a chemical tanker discharging a Category X product at a port of a MARPOL Party. Typical sequence:

  1. Before arrival. Re-read the P&A Manual sections for this tank. Confirm reception facility arrangements with the agent and terminal. Confirm whether a surveyor will attend.
  2. Unloading. Discharge and strip in line with the P&A Manual. Note any pump problems at once, because a stripping failure changes your obligations.
  3. Prewash. Carry out the prewash using the Annex procedure (or by measuring concentration if your P&A Manual allows). Washings go ashore.
  4. Surveyor. The surveyor appointed by the port's government verifies the prewash and endorses the Cargo Record Book. Some exemptions also need a surveyor's endorsement, for example when reloading the same or a compatible product.
  5. Record. Make the CRB entries for unloading, prewash and the transfer of washings to shore. Obtain the reception facility receipt.
  6. At sea. If water is later introduced into the tank and you intend to discharge it at sea, confirm that speed, distance, depth and underwater outlet conditions are all met. Log the start and stop positions, then make the CRB entry.

The common failure point is the gap between what was done and what was written. Times, positions and quantities must match the deck log, the receipts and the surveyor's endorsement.

What port state control checks, and oral exam questions

PSC inspectors commonly check:

  • that a valid Certificate of Fitness or NLS Certificate is on board, with the cargo on board listed on it
  • that the approved P&A Manual is on board, matches the ship, and the officers can explain it
  • that Cargo Record Book entries are complete, signed, consistent with the cargo documents and logs, and carry surveyor endorsements where needed
  • that the SMPEP is approved, its contact lists are current, and the crew know their roles
  • the condition of the underwater discharge outlet, stripping system and slop arrangements
  • whether officers can state the categories and discharge conditions without searching for them

Record discrepancies and crew who cannot explain their own procedures are frequent sources of deficiencies. They can also widen an inspection into a detailed one.

Typical oral exam questions:

  • Name the four categories and what each means for discharge.
  • State the at-sea discharge conditions.
  • When is a prewash mandatory for Category Y?
  • What is the P&A Manual and who approves it?
  • How long is the Cargo Record Book kept, and who signs it?
  • Which certificate shows Annex II compliance on an IBC chemical tanker?
Key points
  • Annex II covers noxious liquid substances in bulk; the revised version with Categories X, Y, Z and OS has applied since 1 January 2007.
  • Category X residues must be prewashed to a reception facility; Y and Z face lesser but still strict limits.
  • At-sea discharge needs at least 7 knots (self-propelled), at least 12 nm from land, at least 25 m depth, and an underwater outlet.
  • Ships built from 2007 must strip each tank and its piping to 75 litres or less.
  • Ships certified to carry NLS need an approved P&A Manual and a Cargo Record Book (kept three years); those of 150 GT and above also need an approved NLS emergency plan.
  • IBC chemical tankers show compliance through their Certificate of Fitness; other NLS carriers hold an NLS Certificate.
  • Charterer, terminal, regional and company rules can be stricter; follow the strictest one.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between MARPOL Annex I and Annex II?

Annex I covers oil, including crude, products and machinery space oily residues. Annex II covers noxious liquid substances in bulk, mostly chemicals and vegetable oils. Each has its own certificates, record books and discharge rules. A ship carrying both types of cargo must comply with both annexes.

Are vegetable oils covered by MARPOL Annex II?

Yes. Most vegetable oils are listed as Category Y and are generally carried on chemical tankers certified for them. Check the product's entry in the IBC Code and your Certificate of Fitness before loading.

How long must the Annex II Cargo Record Book be kept?

It must be kept on board and readily available for inspection for three years after the last entry. Entries are signed by the officer in charge of each operation, and each completed page by the master. Some flags permit electronic record books under specific conditions, so check your flag's requirements.

Can tank washings from chemical cargoes be pumped overboard?

Washings containing Category Y or Z residues, and post-prewash washings from Category X tanks, may be discharged at sea only if every condition is met. The ship must be en route at the required speed, at least 12 nm from land, in at least 25 m of water, and discharging through the underwater outlet. Discharge is never permitted in the Antarctic area. Many companies and charterers restrict or forbid it entirely.

What if a cargo is not listed in the IBC Code?

Check the current MEPC.2/Circular, which lists provisionally assessed products and tripartite agreements. A product that has not been categorised may not be carried until the Administrations concerned agree a provisional assessment. Refer it to your company and charterer before accepting the cargo.

Test yourself

Under the revised MARPOL Annex II, which category is regarded as a major hazard, with its residues subject to a mandatory prewash to a reception facility?

Category X is a major hazard; its discharge is prohibited and a prewash to shore is required before the ship leaves the unloading port. Reference: MARPOL Annex II, Regulations 6 and 13.

Which set of conditions applies to at-sea discharge of NLS residue/water mixtures from a self-propelled ship?

The ship must be en route at 7 knots or more (4 if not self-propelled), discharging through the underwater outlet, at least 12 nm from land and in at least 25 m of water. Reference: MARPOL Annex II, Regulation 13.

Who endorses the Cargo Record Book after a mandatory Category X prewash at the unloading port?

The prewash must be verified by a surveyor acting for the receiving Party, who endorses the Cargo Record Book. Reference: MARPOL Annex II, Regulation 16.

Which approved, ship-specific document sets out unloading, stripping, prewash, tank washing and discharge procedures for NLS?

The P&A Manual, approved by the Administration in a standard format, describes the ship's equipment and procedures for complying with Annex II. Reference: MARPOL Annex II, Regulation 14 and Appendix IV.

On an IBC-certified chemical tanker, which certificate demonstrates compliance with MARPOL Annex II?

Chemical tankers surveyed and certified under the IBC Code hold the Certificate of Fitness, which is accepted in place of a separate NLS Certificate. Reference: MARPOL Annex II, Regulations 7 and 9; IBC Code.
