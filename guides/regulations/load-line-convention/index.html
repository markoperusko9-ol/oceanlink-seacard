The Load Line Convention: What Every Seafarer Needs to Know

Quick answer

The International Convention on Load Lines, 1966 sets the minimum freeboard a ship must keep, the load line mark on her side, and the weathertight and watertight fittings that make that freeboard safe. It was adopted in 1966, entered into force on 21 July 1968, and was modified by the 1988 Protocol. On board it means a valid International Load Line Certificate, a record of conditions of assignment, correctly painted marks, maintained closing appliances, and loading that never submerges the mark for the season and zone.

What the Load Line Convention is and when it came in

The International Convention on Load Lines, 1966 (ICLL) is the IMO instrument that limits how deeply a ship may be loaded. It does this in two ways:

  • Freeboard: a minimum distance between the waterline and the freeboard deck. This guarantees reserve buoyancy and keeps the deck reasonably dry.
  • Conditions of assignment: requirements for the hull openings, closures and crew protection on which that freeboard depends.

Key dates you can rely on:

  • Adopted 5 April 1966; entered into force 21 July 1968.
  • 1988 Protocol adopted 11 November 1988; entered into force 3 February 2000. It brought load line surveys into the Harmonized System of Survey and Certification (HSSC), alongside SOLAS and MARPOL. It also amended the technical regulations.
  • The technical annex was substantially revised by amendments adopted in 2003 under resolution MSC.143(77).

The 1988 Protocol only binds states that have accepted it. Most major flags have done so, but check your flag's position if a certificate or survey regime looks unfamiliar.

Which ships it applies to, and how it is structured

The Convention applies to ships on international voyages flying the flag of a party. Under the Articles, it does not apply to:

  • ships of war
  • new ships of less than 24 m in length
  • existing ships of less than 150 gross tonnage
  • pleasure yachts not engaged in trade
  • fishing vessels

These ships are often still covered by national load line rules. That depends on the flag, so do not assume a coaster or fishing vessel has no freeboard rules.

Ship types for freeboard

  • Type A ships are designed to carry only liquid cargoes in bulk. Their exposed deck has high integrity and their loaded cargo tanks have low permeability, so they may be assigned less freeboard.
  • Type B covers all other ships.
  • Type B ships can get reduced freeboards (B-60, B-100) if they meet additional damage stability and other requirements. Bulk carriers commonly fall into this category.

The load line mark and lines on the ship's side

The mark is cut or centre-punched into the shell plating and then painted. It sits at amidships on both sides.

  • Deck line: shows the position of the freeboard deck.
  • Load line mark: a ring with a horizontal line through it. The upper edge of the line passes through the centre of the ring. The distance from the deck line to that upper edge is the summer freeboard.
  • Assigning authority letters: either side of the ring, identifying the authority that assigned the freeboard. This is usually a classification society acting as recognised organisation for the flag.

The lines forward of the ring are:

Line Meaning Relation to Summer
S Summer Level with the centre of the ring
T Tropical Summer draught plus 1/48 of summer draught
W Winter Summer draught minus 1/48 of summer draught
WNA Winter North Atlantic 50 mm below W; only ships not exceeding 100 m in length
F Fresh water Above S by the fresh water allowance
TF Tropical fresh Above T by the fresh water allowance

Ships assigned timber freeboards carry a second set of lines aft of the ring: LS, LT, LW, LWNA, LF and LTF. They apply only when a timber deck cargo is carried in accordance with the timber requirements.

On deck, the bosun's job is simple: keep the mark and the draught marks clean and visible. A faded or painted-over mark is an easy PSC deficiency.

Seasonal zones and loading: worked scenarios

Annex II divides the world into permanent zones (always summer or always tropical) and seasonal zones and areas (winter, summer or tropical according to the date). The load line chart is reproduced in ocean passage publications and in most loading software.

The basic rule is that the line for the season and zone must not be submerged:

  • when the ship puts to sea,
  • during the voyage, and
  • on arrival.

Scenario 1: dock water allowance

The ship is loading at a river berth where the dock water density is 1.010 t/m³. At summer draught, displacement is 40,000 t and TPC is 50.

  1. FWA (mm) = Δ ÷ (4 × TPC) = 40,000 ÷ 200 = 200 mm.
  2. DWA = FWA × (1025 − 1010) ÷ 25 = 200 × 15 ÷ 25 = 120 mm.
  3. At the berth, the summer line may be submerged by up to 120 mm, read as the mean of port and starboard at amidships.
  4. In full salt water the ship will rise to her summer mark.

The Convention also covers a ship that departs from a port on a river or inland waters. Article 12 permits deeper loading equal to the weight of fuel and other materials consumed between the berth and the sea. The calculation must be documented and follow your company SMS.

Scenario 2: changing zones

The ship sails from a port in a summer zone. Her route takes her into a winter seasonal zone during its winter period.

  1. She must arrive at the zone boundary no deeper than her winter mark.
  2. The chief officer therefore loads to the more restrictive condition.
  3. That means summer draught at the load port only if bunkers and water burned before the boundary will bring her up to the winter line.
  4. If not, she loads less.

Do not rely on the loading computer to catch a zone change. The passage plan and the cargo plan must be checked together.

Conditions of assignment: what it means in daily work

The freeboard is only valid if the closures and protection it assumes are actually in place. Annex I covers:

  • hatchways and hatch covers
  • doors in superstructures
  • ventilators and air pipes
  • sidescuttles and deadlights
  • freeing ports
  • guard rails and bulwarks
  • means of safe passage for crew

The Convention sets minimum heights for some of these. For example, hatch coamings in position 1 are 600 mm and in position 2 are 450 mm, and guard rails or bulwarks are at least 1 m. Your own ship's figures are fixed at assignment. The record of conditions of assignment issued by the assigning authority is the document that tells you what each closure is and where it is.

In practice, crew keep the freeboard valid by:

  • maintaining hatch cover rubber seals, compression bars, cleats and drain channels, and hose-testing or ultrasonic-testing per the SMS
  • keeping weathertight doors closed at sea, with dogs, gaskets and hinges free and greased
  • keeping air pipe heads and ventilator closures free. Check the floats or balls in automatic air pipe heads for wear and seizing
  • never wiring or lashing freeing port flaps shut
  • keeping deadlights available and fitted in heavy weather
  • keeping guard rails, lifelines and catwalks intact. Any temporary removal goes through the permit system and is restored before sea

Reporting any wastage or repair affecting these items to the chief officer is not optional. An unapproved alteration can invalidate the certificate.

Certificates, surveys and records on board

Documents

  • International Load Line Certificate: issued after initial survey, valid for a maximum of five years.
  • International Load Line Exemption Certificate: issued only where the flag grants an exemption. Examples are ships with novel features or a ship making a single exempted voyage.
  • Record of conditions of assignment: kept on board with the certificate.
  • Stability and loading information approved for the master. Annex I requires the master to be given enough information to load and ballast safely.

Survey cycle under HSSC

  • Initial survey before the certificate is first issued.
  • Annual surveys within three months before or after each anniversary date. These are endorsed on the certificate. The surveyor checks that the hull, closures and fittings have not been altered and are in good condition.
  • Renewal survey at intervals not exceeding five years.

When a certificate stops being valid

The certificate ceases to be valid if:

  • alterations affect the freeboard,
  • the fittings and appliances are not maintained, or
  • annual surveys are not carried out and endorsed.

Logbook entries

Many administrations and most company SMSs require departure draughts, freeboard and water density to be entered in the official or deck log. The exact format is flag- and company-specific, so follow your own.

What port state control checks

The Convention gives port states the right to verify that:

  1. a valid certificate is on board,
  2. the ship is not loaded beyond the limits the certificate allows,
  3. the load line position corresponds with the certificate, and
  4. there has been no material alteration making the ship unfit to go to sea without danger.

On a typical PSC visit, expect the inspector to:

  • check the certificate dates and the annual survey endorsements
  • look at the draught and mark from the quay or launch, and ask what the dock water density is
  • walk the main deck checking hatch cover condition and securing, ventilator and air pipe closures, door gaskets and dogs, freeing ports, and guard rails
  • ask a crew member to demonstrate closing a ventilator or weathertight door

Overloading is a classic detainable deficiency. So are wasted or holed air pipes and ventilators, and hatch covers that cannot be made weathertight.

Oral exam: questions the examiner likes

Q: What does the ring and bar actually measure? The summer freeboard. It runs from the upper edge of the deck line to the upper edge of the horizontal line through the ring, at amidships.

Q: How do you derive the tropical and winter marks? Summer draught plus or minus 1/48 of the summer draught. Tropical is higher (deeper loading allowed); winter is lower.

Q: Who gets a WNA mark? Ships not exceeding 100 m in length. It is 50 mm below the winter line.

Q: You are loading in dock water. How deep can you go? Calculate the DWA from the FWA and the measured density. Read the mean draught or freeboard on both sides amidships, and do not exceed the appropriate mark plus the DWA.

Q: Name five items the surveyor checks at an annual load line survey. Any five of:

  • hatch covers and securing
  • weathertight doors
  • ventilators and their closures
  • air pipes and their heads
  • sidescuttles and deadlights
  • freeing ports
  • guard rails and bulwarks
  • the marks themselves

Q: What invalidates the certificate? Any of these:

  • material alterations affecting freeboard
  • closures not maintained
  • annual survey not endorsed
  • the five-year period exceeded
Key points
  • The ICLL 1966 entered into force on 21 July 1968; the 1988 Protocol (in force 3 February 2000) brought it into HSSC.
  • The summer freeboard is measured at amidships from the deck line to the upper edge of the line through the ring.
  • Tropical and winter lines are 1/48 of summer draught above and below summer; WNA is 50 mm below W, for ships of 100 m or less.
  • The appropriate seasonal line must not be submerged on departure, during the voyage or on arrival; plan zone changes with consumption.
  • Freeboard depends on the conditions of assignment, so hatch covers, doors, vents, air pipes, freeing ports and guard rails must be maintained.
  • The certificate is valid for up to five years, with annual surveys within three months either side of each anniversary date.
  • PSC checks the certificate, the draught against the marks, and the condition of closures; overloading is detainable.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Load Line Convention in simple terms?

It is the IMO convention that sets the minimum freeboard a ship must keep and the marks showing the maximum draught by season and water density. It also requires the hull openings and crew protection that make that freeboard safe to be kept in good order.

How long is an International Load Line Certificate valid?

The flag Administration specifies the period, which may not exceed five years, and validity is subject to annual surveys endorsed on the certificate. Each annual survey is carried out within three months before or after the anniversary date. Missing an endorsement means the certificate is no longer valid.

How do I calculate fresh water allowance and dock water allowance?

FWA in millimetres is the summer displacement in tonnes divided by four times the TPC. DWA is FWA multiplied by (1025 minus dock water density in kg/m³), divided by 25. The result is how far the appropriate mark may be submerged at the berth.

Does the Load Line Convention apply to small ships and fishing vessels?

The Convention excludes fishing vessels, pleasure yachts not engaged in trade, new ships under 24 m and existing ships under 150 GT. Many flag states apply national load line rules to these vessels, so check your administration's requirements.

What is the record of conditions of assignment?

It is the document issued with the load line assignment that lists the closures, coamings, freeing ports, guard rails and similar items on which the freeboard depends. Surveyors and PSC use it to verify that nothing has been altered. Keep it with the certificate.

Test yourself

Where is the summer freeboard measured?

The summer freeboard is measured vertically at amidships from the upper edge of the deck line to the upper edge of the load line through the ring (ICLL 1966, Annex I, Chapter I).

How is the tropical load line positioned relative to the summer line?

The tropical draught is the summer draught plus 1/48 of the summer draught, so the T line is above S (ICLL 1966, Annex I, Chapter III).

Which ships are assigned a Winter North Atlantic (WNA) line?

The WNA line, 50 mm below the winter line, applies only to ships not exceeding 100 m in length (ICLL 1966, Annex I, Chapter III).

Within what window must the annual load line survey be carried out under HSSC?

Annual surveys are carried out within three months before or after each anniversary date of the certificate (1988 Protocol to ICLL 1966, Article 14).

A ship is loading at a river berth in dock water. Which statement is correct?

In water less dense than seawater the ship floats deeper, so the appropriate mark may be submerged by an allowance proportional to the density difference, derived from the FWA (ICLL 1966, Article 12).
