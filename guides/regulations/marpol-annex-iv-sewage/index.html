MARPOL Annex IV – Sewage: A Practical Guide for Seafarers

Quick answer

MARPOL Annex IV regulates how ships treat, store and discharge sewage. It applies to ships on international voyages of 400 GT and above, and to smaller ships certified to carry more than 15 persons. These ships must hold an International Sewage Pollution Prevention (ISPP) Certificate, carry approved treatment or holding equipment and a standard shore connection, and discharge only under the conditions in Regulation 11, with tighter rules for passenger ships in the Baltic Sea special area.

What Annex IV is and when it came into force

Annex IV is the part of MARPOL that deals with sewage. It was adopted together with the main convention, but it was the last of the original annexes to gain enough ratifications. It entered into force internationally on 27 September 2003.

A revised Annex IV was adopted by resolution MEPC.115(51) and entered into force on 1 August 2005. This is the text you work to today, as later amended. The amendments added the Baltic Sea special area and the requirement for port reception facilities serving passenger ships in that area.

Annex IV is optional for states that join MARPOL, so not every MARPOL party has accepted it. Most major flag and port states have. If your ship trades to a port state that is not party to Annex IV, local law still applies there, so always check port information.

Who it applies to. Annex IV covers ships engaged in international voyages that are:

  • 400 GT and above, or
  • less than 400 GT but certified to carry more than 15 persons.

The 15-person threshold counts crew and passengers together. This is why small passenger craft and many offshore vessels fall within it.

Structure of the Annex and key definitions

The revised Annex IV is short. The chapters run roughly as follows:

Chapter Content
1 – General Definitions, application, exceptions
2 – Surveys and certification Surveys, issue and form of the ISPP Certificate, validity
3 – Equipment and control of discharge Sewage systems, standard discharge connection, discharge rules
4 – Reception facilities Port reception facilities, including for passenger ships in special areas
5 – Port State Control Control of operational requirements
6 – Verification of compliance Link to the IMO Member State Audit Scheme (Regulation 14)

Chapter 6 was added by later amendments that are common to all the MARPOL annexes. The appendix contains the model ISPP Certificate.

What counts as "sewage". Under Annex IV, sewage means:

  • drainage and other wastes from toilets, urinals and WC scuppers
  • drainage from medical premises, such as the dispensary and sick bay, through wash basins, wash tubs and scuppers in those spaces
  • drainage from spaces containing living animals, which matters on livestock carriers
  • other waste waters when mixed with any of the above

Grey water from showers, galley sinks and laundries is not sewage under Annex IV unless it is mixed with sewage. National or regional rules may still regulate grey water. Food waste is a garbage matter under Annex V.

"Nearest land" is measured from the baseline used to establish the territorial sea, in the same way as in Annex I. There are special provisions off parts of the Australian coast.

Equipment required and the discharge rules

Sewage system (Regulation 9). A ship covered by Annex IV must carry one of the following:

  • an approved sewage treatment plant (STP), type-approved by the Administration to the applicable IMO standard
  • a comminuting and disinfecting system, approved by the Administration, with temporary storage for when the ship is within 3 nm of nearest land
  • a holding tank with capacity to the satisfaction of the Administration. The capacity takes account of the ship's operation, the number of persons on board and other factors. The tank must have a means of indicating visually how much it holds.

The IMO effluent standards for STPs have been revised over the years. Older plants, plants installed from 2010 and plants installed from 2016 were approved under different guidelines. The plant's type-approval certificate tells you which standard it meets. Test parameters include thermotolerant coliforms, suspended solids, BOD, COD, pH and residual chlorine.

Standard discharge connection (Regulation 10). The ship must have a pipeline to a standard flange so that any shore or barge reception facility can connect. The dimensions are specified in the Annex.

Discharge (Regulation 11): the core rules outside special areas

Condition of sewage Where/how discharge is permitted
Comminuted and disinfected by an approved system More than 3 nm from nearest land
Not comminuted or disinfected (e.g. raw from holding tank) More than 12 nm from nearest land, ship en route at not less than 4 knots, at a moderate rate approved by the Administration
Treated in an approved STP Anywhere, provided the effluent shows no visible floating solids and does not discolour the surrounding water

The approved discharge rate for holding tanks is based on an IMO standard that takes account of speed and ship dimensions. Your ship should have this rate documented.

When sewage is mixed with wastes or waste water covered by other MARPOL annexes, the requirements of those annexes must be met in addition to Annex IV.

Exceptions (Regulation 3). The discharge rules do not apply to discharge needed to secure the safety of the ship and those on board, or to save life at sea. Nor do they apply to discharge resulting from damage to the ship or its equipment, provided all reasonable precautions were taken before and after the damage.

Baltic Sea special area. The Baltic Sea is currently the only Annex IV special area. In it, passenger ships may discharge sewage only through an approved STP that also meets nitrogen and phosphorus removal standards. Otherwise they must use port reception facilities. These requirements were phased in between 2019 and 2023, depending on whether the ship is new or existing and on its route. Other ship types in the Baltic follow the general rules above.

Certificates and records on board

ISPP Certificate. The International Sewage Pollution Prevention Certificate is issued after an initial survey. It is issued by the Administration or by a recognised organisation on its behalf. Its validity may not exceed five years. Annex IV itself provides for:

  • an initial survey before the ship enters service or before the certificate is first issued
  • a renewal survey at intervals specified by the Administration, not exceeding five years
  • an additional survey, general or partial, after repairs or renewals

Unlike some other annexes, Annex IV does not itself require annual or intermediate surveys. However, flag or class may examine the system during other surveys. The ship must be kept in compliance at all times.

Supporting documents usually kept with the certificate include:

  • the STP type-approval certificate
  • the approved discharge rate documentation for holding tank discharge, where relevant
  • the maker's manual

Record keeping. Annex IV does not prescribe a mandatory sewage record book, unlike the Oil Record Book under Annex I. In practice, most company SMS procedures require a sewage or STP log. This typically records:

  • discharge times
  • positions and distances off
  • speed
  • tank levels
  • use of shore facilities

Some port and coastal states also require their own records. Follow your SMS and any local requirement. Do not assume that no record is needed.

What Annex IV means in daily work

Engine department (normally the officer assigned by the chief engineer):

  • Keep the STP biologically healthy. Check the aeration blowers, the air lift or sludge return, and the condition of the activated sludge.
  • Maintain disinfection dosing and, where fitted, dechlorination. Test pH and residual chlorine as the manual requires.
  • Test high-level alarms on holding and treatment tanks.
  • Desludge as scheduled, either ashore or within the permitted discharge conditions.
  • Do not use bypass lines to dump untreated sewage in prohibited areas. Inspectors look for these.

Deck department:

  • The OOW confirms distance from nearest land and speed before a holding tank discharge is started. This is usually coordinated with the engine room per the SMS.
  • Passage plans should flag no-discharge areas, special areas and local restrictions.

Everyone:

  • Do not flush wipes, rags, sanitary items, oil or chemicals. These kill the biology of an STP and block vacuum toilet systems.
  • Sewage tanks and STP compartments can contain hydrogen sulphide and be oxygen deficient. Treat any entry as an enclosed space entry under permit, with gas testing.

Worked scenario: holding tank discharge

A 9,000 GT general cargo ship has a holding tank only, with no approved STP. She is 14 nm off the coast, making 9 knots, outside any special area, and the tank is at 80%.

  1. Check distance. 14 nm is more than 12 nm from nearest land, so the distance condition is met. Where the baseline and the visible coast differ, measure from the baseline.
  2. Check speed. At 9 knots en route, the 4-knot condition is met.
  3. Check rate. Discharge at the flag-approved rate. Never dump the tank all at once.
  4. Check local rules. Confirm the area is not subject to a stricter national or regional no-discharge rule.
  5. Record it. Enter start and stop positions, times, speed and quantity per SMS.

Now suppose she slows to 3 knots while waiting for a berth. The speed condition is no longer met. Stop the discharge and hold the sewage until conditions are met again, or land it to a reception facility.

What port state control typically checks

PSC officers usually work through the following:

  • ISPP Certificate: valid, with correct ship particulars, and matching the equipment actually fitted.
  • STP: running and in good condition, including blowers, dosing and alarms. Where the ship is discharging, the effluent should be clear, with no visible solids or discoloration.
  • Holding tank: visual level indication working, and documentation of the approved discharge rate.
  • Standard discharge connection: fitted and in usable condition.
  • Pipework: no unauthorised bypasses or blank flanges hiding illegal discharges.
  • Crew familiarity: whether the responsible engineer can explain how the plant works and the discharge rules. Under Regulation 13, PSC may inspect operational requirements where there are clear grounds to believe the crew are not familiar with essential procedures.
  • Records: whatever the SMS requires, checked for consistency with positions in the logbook.

Oral exam corner – questions examiners like:

  • "Define sewage under Annex IV. Is shower water sewage?" Give the four categories. Grey water is not sewage unless mixed with sewage.
  • "You have a comminuting and disinfecting unit. Where can you discharge?" Beyond 3 nm from nearest land.
  • "What is the only Annex IV special area, and who does it affect?" The Baltic Sea, and it affects passenger ships.
  • "What surveys does Annex IV require?" Initial, renewal and additional.
  • "When can you lawfully discharge untreated sewage near land?" Only under the safety-of-life or damage exceptions in Regulation 3.
Key points
  • Annex IV entered into force on 27 September 2003; the revised text in use today entered into force on 1 August 2005.
  • It applies to ships on international voyages of 400 GT and above, or under 400 GT certified to carry more than 15 persons.
  • Ships need an approved STP, a comminuting and disinfecting system, or a holding tank, plus a standard shore discharge connection.
  • Comminuted and disinfected sewage may go overboard beyond 3 nm; untreated sewage only beyond 12 nm, at 4 knots or more and at an approved rate.
  • The ISPP Certificate is valid for no more than five years, backed by initial, renewal and additional surveys.
  • The Baltic Sea is the only special area, with strict nitrogen and phosphorus rules for passenger ships.
  • There is no mandatory sewage record book in Annex IV, but company SMS and local rules usually require one.

Frequently asked questions

Does MARPOL Annex IV apply to my ship?

It applies if your ship is engaged in international voyages and is either 400 GT or above, or under 400 GT but certified to carry more than 15 persons. If your flag state is not party to Annex IV, port states that are party will still apply its requirements to your ship under the no-more-favourable-treatment principle. Expect equivalent compliance to be checked in their ports. Check the ISPP Certificate on board: if one has been issued, the ship is being treated as covered.

Is grey water covered by MARPOL Annex IV?

No. Grey water from showers, sinks, galleys and laundries is not sewage under Annex IV unless it is mixed with sewage. Some national and regional rules do control grey water discharge, so check local requirements before discharging near a coast.

Can we discharge treated sewage inside 3 nm?

Under the general rules, effluent from an approved sewage treatment plant may be discharged without a distance limit. The conditions are that it produces no visible floating solids and does not discolour the water. Local port or coastal state rules may be stricter, and Baltic special area rules apply to passenger ships there.

How long is an ISPP Certificate valid?

It is valid for a period set by the Administration, not exceeding five years. It is renewed after a renewal survey. An additional survey is needed after important repairs or renewals to the sewage system.

Is a sewage record book mandatory under MARPOL?

Annex IV does not prescribe a mandatory sewage record book in the way Annex I requires an Oil Record Book. Most company SMS procedures and some port states require sewage discharge records anyway. Keep whatever your SMS and local law demand, and make sure entries match the deck log positions.

Test yourself

A ship has an approved comminuting and disinfecting system but no STP. What is the minimum distance from nearest land for discharge?

Comminuted and disinfected sewage may be discharged more than 3 nm from nearest land. The 12 nm limit applies to untreated sewage (MARPOL Annex IV, Regulation 11).

Which conditions apply to discharging untreated sewage from a holding tank outside a special area?

Untreated sewage requires distance, speed and approved rate conditions together (MARPOL Annex IV, Regulation 11).

Which certificate shows compliance with MARPOL Annex IV?

The International Sewage Pollution Prevention Certificate is issued under Annex IV. The IOPP and IAPP Certificates belong to Annexes I and VI (MARPOL Annex IV, Regulation 5).

Under Annex IV, how is grey water from crew showers treated?

The definition of sewage covers other waste waters only when they are mixed with toilet, medical or animal space drainage (MARPOL Annex IV, Regulation 1).

Which is currently the only special area under MARPOL Annex IV?

The Baltic Sea was designated the Annex IV special area, with requirements for passenger ships. The other seas are special areas under other annexes, not Annex IV (MEPC.200(62)).
