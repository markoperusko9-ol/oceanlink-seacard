MARPOL Annex VI – Air Pollution: What It Means On Board

Quick answer

MARPOL Annex VI is the IMO regime that limits air emissions from ships, covering sulphur in fuel, NOx from diesel engines, ozone-depleting substances, incineration, cargo vapours and energy efficiency. It entered into force on 19 May 2005, and a revised version took effect on 1 July 2010. On board, it shows up as the IAPP and IEE certificates, bunker delivery notes and samples, fuel changeover logs, engine NOx files, the ODS record book and the SEEMP, and port state control checks all of these.

What Annex VI is and when it came in

Annex VI was added to MARPOL by the 1997 Protocol. It entered into force on 19 May 2005. IMO then adopted a substantially revised Annex VI in 2008, which entered into force on 1 July 2010. That revision brought in the progressive sulphur limits and the NOx tier system you work with today.

The energy efficiency chapter came into force on 1 January 2013. It introduced the EEDI for new ships and the SEEMP for all ships of 400 GT and above. Later amendments added:

  • fuel consumption data collection (DCS), with data collected from 2019
  • the EEXI and the Carbon Intensity Indicator (CII), effective from 1 January 2023

Annex VI is still being amended regularly, more than most other instruments. Treat any printed summary, including this one, as a starting point. Check the current consolidated text, flag circulars and your company SMS for the latest dates.

Structure at a glance

Annex VI is set out in chapters. The regulations you meet most often at sea are these:

Chapter / Regulation Subject Who mostly deals with it
Ch. 2 (Regs 5–11) Surveys, certificates (IAPP), PSC control Master, Chief Engineer
Reg 12 Ozone-depleting substances Engineers (refrigeration, fire systems)
Reg 13 NOx from diesel engines over 130 kW Engineers
Reg 14 Sulphur oxides and particulate matter Engineers, OOW for ECA logging
Reg 15 Volatile organic compounds (tankers) Chief Officer, Pumpman
Reg 16 Shipboard incineration Engineers
Reg 18 Fuel oil availability and quality, BDNs, samples Chief Engineer
Ch. 4 Energy efficiency (EEDI, EEXI, SEEMP, DCS, CII) Master, Chief Engineer, company

There are also appendices. They cover certificate forms, the BDN contents, the fuel sampling procedure and the criteria for designating Emission Control Areas. The NOx Technical Code 2008 is a mandatory companion document that governs engine certification.

Sulphur limits, ECAs and fuel changeover

Two sulphur limits to know:

  • Outside ECAs: 0.50% m/m globally since 1 January 2020.
  • Inside SOx ECAs: 0.10% m/m since 1 January 2015.

Since 1 March 2020, a ship without an approved exhaust gas cleaning system (scrubber) may not even carry non-compliant fuel for use on board. A ship with an approved scrubber, accepted by the flag as an equivalent under Reg 4, may burn higher-sulphur fuel. Some ports and coastal states restrict discharge of open-loop washwater under their own laws, so check before arrival.

Current SOx ECAs:

  • Baltic Sea
  • North Sea
  • North American
  • United States Caribbean
  • Mediterranean Sea, from 1 May 2025

Further ECAs have been adopted, so always check the current list in your passage plan.

Regional law can be stricter than Annex VI. Examples include the EU requirements for ships at berth and California's rules. These are separate obligations on top of MARPOL.

Worked scenario: entering an ECA from deep sea on VLSFO

  1. Passage planning. Mark the ECA boundary on ECDIS or the paper chart, with a wheel-over-style alert well before it.
  2. Calculate timing. The Chief Engineer works out how long the changeover takes. This depends on the volume of service tank, settling tank and piping to be flushed, consumption, and temperature limits for the fuel pumps.
  3. Follow the written procedure. Annex VI requires a ship using separate fuels to carry a written changeover procedure. Follow it, and watch viscosity and temperature, because thermal shock to fuel pumps is the classic risk.
  4. Complete before the line. Changeover must be complete, with compliant fuel at the engines, before the ship enters the ECA.
  5. Log it. Record the following in the logbook required by your flag. Many companies also keep a dedicated fuel changeover log.
    • date, time and position when changeover was completed
    • volume of compliant fuel in each tank
  6. Leaving the ECA. Do not start changing back until after exit. Make the same entries.

The common deficiency is not the fuel itself. It is the missing or inconsistent record: a position that does not match the bridge log, or tank volumes that do not add up.

Bunkering under Reg 18: BDN, samples and FONAR

Regulation 18 puts duties on both the supplier and the ship.

  • Bunker Delivery Note (BDN). The supplier must provide one with the minimum content set out in the Annex, including the sulphur content and a declaration of compliance. Keep it on board and readily available for inspection for three years after delivery.
  • MARPOL delivered sample. A sealed, representative sample is taken during delivery and signed by the supplier's representative and the master or officer in charge. Keep it under the ship's control until the fuel is substantially consumed, and in any case not less than 12 months from delivery.
  • In-use and on-board samples. Amendments introduced verification of fuel in use and fuel on board. Ships must have designated sampling points fitted, with dates depending on construction and survey cycle. Know where yours are, because PSC may ask for a sample to be drawn from them.
  • Fuel Oil Non-Availability Report (FONAR). If compliant fuel genuinely cannot be obtained despite best efforts, the ship records what was attempted and notifies the flag and the competent authority at the next port. A FONAR is evidence for the authority to consider. It is not a licence to burn non-compliant fuel, and how it is handled varies by port state.

On deck, the officer in charge of bunkering should make sure the sampling is actually witnessed. Do not simply sign a pre-filled label.

NOx, ozone-depleting substances, incineration and VOCs

NOx (Reg 13)

NOx limits apply to marine diesel engines over 130 kW output. They are set in three tiers by date of construction and rated speed:

  • Tier I: the original limit.
  • Tier II: applies to ships constructed on or after 1 January 2011.
  • Tier III: applies only while the ship is operating in a NOx ECA, if it was built on or after that ECA's relevant date. That is 2016 for the North American and US Caribbean ECAs, and 2021 for the Baltic and North Sea.

Each engine has:

  • an EIAPP certificate
  • a Technical File
  • where the parameter check method is used, a Record Book of Engine Parameters

If you replace components such as injectors, nozzles or turbocharger parts, the part numbers must match those approved in the Technical File. Record the change. An unapproved spare can put the engine outside its certification.

Ozone-depleting substances (Reg 12)

  • Deliberate emissions of ODS are prohibited. This includes venting during maintenance.
  • New installations containing ODS are banned. HCFCs such as R22 were permitted in new installations only up to 1 January 2020.
  • Ships with rechargeable systems containing ODS keep an ODS Record Book. Log top-ups, repairs, discharges and landing ashore.

Incineration (Reg 16)

  • Shipboard incinerators installed on or after 1 January 2000 must be type-approved.
  • Burning is prohibited for the following, among others:
    • Annex I, II and III cargo residues
    • PCBs
    • garbage with more than traces of heavy metals
    • refined petroleum products containing halogen compounds
    • EGCS residues
  • PVC may be burned only in incinerators approved for it.
  • Sludge generated on board may be burned in boilers or power plant, but not in ports, harbours or estuaries.
  • The operating manual must be available, and operators must be trained in its use.

VOCs (Reg 15)

  • Tankers calling at ports that require vapour emission control must have approved collection systems.
  • Crude oil tankers must carry and follow an approved VOC Management Plan.

Energy efficiency: SEEMP, DCS, EEXI and CII

Chapter 4 is mostly managed by the company, but the data comes from the ship.

  • SEEMP. This is a ship-specific plan required for ships of 400 GT and above.
    • Part II covers the fuel data collection methodology.
    • Part III covers the CII implementation plan, for ships in its scope.
  • DCS. Ships of 5,000 GT and above collect annual fuel consumption, distance and hours data and report it to the flag. The flag issues a Statement of Compliance, which must be kept on board.
  • EEXI. This is a one-off technical index for existing ships of certain types. It was verified at the first survey after 1 January 2023. Many ships comply through an engine power limitation, which is sealed and documented. Overriding it outside the permitted conditions, such as safety or saving life, is a serious matter and must be recorded.
  • CII. This is an annual operational rating from A to E for in-scope ships of 5,000 GT and above. A ship rated D for three consecutive years, or E in a single year, needs a corrective action plan in its SEEMP.

For crew, this means accurate noon reports, fuel figures and distance logged. Errors there flow directly into the ship's official rating.

Certificates, records and what PSC typically checks

Certificates and documents you should be able to produce:

  • IAPP Certificate with its Supplement. Required for ships of 400 GT and above, valid up to five years, and endorsed at annual and intermediate surveys.
  • IEE Certificate, for ships of 400 GT and above.
  • EIAPP certificates, Technical Files and Record Books of Engine Parameters.
  • BDNs covering the past three years, plus retained MARPOL samples.
  • Fuel changeover procedure and logbook entries.
  • ODS Record Book, where applicable.
  • Incinerator type approval and manual.
  • SEEMP, and the DCS Statement of Compliance where applicable.
  • EGCS approval documents and records, if fitted.
  • VOC Management Plan, on crude carriers.

Typical PSC focus:

  • Comparing BDN sulphur figures against the fuel in use.
  • Taking an in-use sample for analysis.
  • Checking that changeover positions match the bridge log.
  • Asking the duty engineer to explain the changeover. Crew unfamiliarity is itself a finding.
  • Checking engine parts against the Technical File.
  • Looking at ODS records against refrigerant top-ups.

Inspectors also look for consistency across documents. Detention policy and penalties vary by port state and regional MoU.

Oral exam: questions examiners like

  • Q: You find the BDN sulphur figure is 0.48% but your lab result says 0.54%. What do you do? Do not use the fuel in question. Inform the flag Administration, the port state authority and the supplier, following company procedure and IMO guidance on non-compliant fuel. Document everything. Keep the MARPOL delivered sample, because it is the statutory sample that authorities use for verification. Commercial dispute samples are handled separately.
  • Q: When must changeover be complete when entering an ECA? Before entry. Explain the logbook entries required.
  • Q: Difference between EIAPP and IAPP? EIAPP certifies an individual engine's NOx compliance. IAPP certifies the ship as a whole under Annex VI.
  • Q: What can you not burn in the incinerator? List the Reg 16 prohibited items.
  • Q: What is a FONAR and what does it not do? It records best efforts to obtain compliant fuel. It does not exempt the ship.
  • Q: Who needs a VOC Management Plan? Crude oil tankers.
Key points
  • Annex VI entered into force on 19 May 2005; the revised Annex took effect on 1 July 2010 and is still amended frequently.
  • Fuel sulphur limit is 0.50% m/m globally and 0.10% m/m in SOx ECAs, unless an approved scrubber is fitted.
  • Changeover must be complete before entering an ECA, with date, time, position and tank volumes logged.
  • Keep BDNs for three years; keep the MARPOL sample until the fuel is substantially consumed, and for at least 12 months.
  • Engines over 130 kW need an EIAPP certificate and Technical File; replacement parts must match the approved ones.
  • Ships of 400 GT and above carry the IAPP and IEE certificates and a SEEMP.
  • PSC checks consistency between BDNs, samples, engine logs, bridge logs and crew knowledge.

Frequently asked questions

What is the sulphur limit under MARPOL Annex VI?

Since 1 January 2020 the global limit is 0.50% m/m. Inside SOx Emission Control Areas it is 0.10% m/m. Ships with an approved exhaust gas cleaning system may use higher-sulphur fuel as an equivalent, subject to flag acceptance and local discharge rules.

How long must a bunker delivery note be kept on board?

For three years after delivery, readily available for inspection. The associated MARPOL delivered sample is kept under the ship's control until the fuel is substantially consumed, and for not less than 12 months.

What is the difference between the IAPP and EIAPP certificates?

The IAPP Certificate is issued to the ship and covers its overall compliance with Annex VI. The EIAPP Certificate is issued for each individual diesel engine over 130 kW and covers its NOx emission compliance under the NOx Technical Code.

Does a FONAR allow a ship to burn non-compliant fuel?

No. A Fuel Oil Non-Availability Report documents the efforts made to buy compliant fuel and must be sent to the flag and the port authority. The port state decides what action to take, and practice varies between administrations.

Which ships need an ODS record book?

Ships that have rechargeable systems containing ozone-depleting substances, such as older refrigeration or air-conditioning plants using HCFCs. The book records top-ups, repairs, discharges and delivery of ODS ashore.

Test yourself

What is the maximum sulphur content of fuel used outside ECAs on a ship without a scrubber since 1 January 2020?

The global cap fell from 3.50% to 0.50% m/m on 1 January 2020; 0.10% applies inside SOx ECAs. MARPOL Annex VI, Regulation 14.

For how long must a bunker delivery note be retained on board?

BDNs must be kept on board and available for three years; 12 months is the minimum for the retained sample, not the BDN. MARPOL Annex VI, Regulation 18.

Which document certifies that an individual diesel engine meets its NOx limit?

The EIAPP Certificate is issued per engine under the NOx Technical Code; the IAPP covers the ship as a whole. MARPOL Annex VI, Regulation 13 and NOx Technical Code 2008.

How long must the MARPOL delivered fuel sample be retained under the ship's control?

The sample is kept until the fuel is substantially consumed, with a minimum of 12 months. MARPOL Annex VI, Regulation 18.

When do Tier III NOx limits apply to a ship's diesel engines?

Tier III is an ECA-only standard tied to the ship's construction date and the ECA's date; Tier II applies elsewhere. MARPOL Annex VI, Regulation 13.
