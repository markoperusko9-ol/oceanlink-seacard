INDoS Number (India): A Practical Guide for Seafarers

Quick answer

The INDoS number is the unique identification number given to a seafarer in the Indian National Database of Seafarers, maintained by the Directorate General of Shipping (DGS). It is not a certificate. It is the key that links your CDC, STCW course certificates and Certificate of Competency in the DGS records. The number itself stays with you for your whole career, but the records attached to it must be kept accurate and current, or you can be stopped at joining.

What an INDoS number actually is

INDoS stands for Indian National Database of Seafarers. When you enter the Indian maritime system, DG Shipping allots you a unique alphanumeric number. From then on, your training and certification records are recorded against that number.

Think of it as a register entry, not a qualification:

  • It does not certify that you are competent to do anything.
  • It does let DGS-approved institutes, the DGS itself, recruitment agencies, employers and foreign administrations confirm that your certificates are genuine.
  • Your INDoS number is printed on, or recorded alongside, your Indian documents such as the CDC and course certificates.

If someone at a manning office asks for your INDoS, they want to look you up in that database. They are not asking for a separate certificate.

Where the requirement comes from

No international convention names INDoS. It is India's own administrative system. Behind it sits a real STCW obligation.

  • STCW Regulation I/2 requires each Party to keep a register, or registers, of the certificates and endorsements it issues. It must also make information on their status available to other Parties and companies that ask to verify a certificate. India meets this obligation through the DGS database, with INDoS as the identifier.
  • STCW Code Section A-I/2 sets out what that register should contain and the verification arrangements.
  • Domestic basis: INDoS is run under Indian merchant shipping legislation and DGS orders, notices and circulars. Do not rely on old printouts or forwarded messages for the detail. Check the current DGS notices, because procedures and portals have changed over the years.

MLC 2006 does not specifically require INDoS. MLC does require that seafarers be trained or certified for their duties. In practice, the documents that prove this for Indian seafarers are verified through the DGS records.

Who needs one, and who doesn't

You will need an INDoS number if you are an Indian seafarer who wants to:

  • attend DGS-approved STCW courses, including pre-sea training, basic safety, refreshers and modular courses;
  • obtain an Indian CDC (Continuous Discharge Certificate);
  • sit Indian CoC examinations or have an Indian CoC issued or revalidated;
  • be recruited through Indian licensed manning agencies, which are expected to verify your documents.

Not relevant to you if:

  • You are a Filipino or other non-Indian seafarer certified by your own administration. In the Philippines, MARINA keeps its own records of certificates. INDoS has nothing to do with your MARINA documents.

Grey area: Some foreign nationals attend DGS-approved institutes in India. Whether and how they are entered in the database depends on current DGS rules. Ask the institute before enrolling, not after.

How you get an INDoS number: step by step

The exact route depends on current DGS procedure, but the general flow is:

  1. Choose a DGS-approved institute. Most first-timers get their INDoS number when they enrol for their first approved course, such as pre-sea training or STCW basic safety training. Courses at unapproved centres will not appear against your INDoS. They are of little use for Indian certification.
  2. Submit your details. You submit these through the institute or the DGS online portal, depending on current practice. Typically this means identity details matching your passport, a photograph and a signature. DGS may ask for other documents, such as educational or medical records, depending on the requirement at the time.
  3. Check every character. Name spelling, date of birth, place of birth and passport number must match your passport exactly.
  4. Receive the number. It is allotted by DGS, not invented by the institute. Note it down and keep a copy of any confirmation.
  5. Later courses get linked. Each subsequent DGS-approved course should be uploaded by the institute against your INDoS.

One seafarer should have one INDoS number. If you apply again because you lost the first number, you can create a duplicate record. Untangling duplicates is slow and can hold up a CDC or CoC application.

Validity: the number lasts, the records expire

This is where many people get confused.

Item Does it expire?
INDoS number itself No. It is a career-long identifier.
CDC Yes. It has a validity period set by DGS.
Basic training (PST, FPFF) and PSCRB and AFF Evidence of maintained competence is required every five years under STCW Regulations VI/1, VI/2 and VI/3.
CoC Yes. Revalidation is under STCW Regulation I/11.
Medical fitness certificate Yes. The period depends on the administration and your age.

When people say their "INDoS has a problem", they nearly always mean one of these things:

  • a certificate linked to the number has expired;
  • a certificate was never uploaded;
  • the personal details on the record do not match the passport.

The exact revalidation process for each certificate depends on current DGS rules. For a seafarer on a foreign flag, the flag administration may also have its own requirements for endorsements.

Why crew get stopped at joining: worked scenarios

Scenario 1 – Name mismatch. A 3rd Engineer's passport shows his full name. His INDoS record has an initial instead of his middle name. The flag state endorsement application is held because the CoC verification does not match the passport. Fix: get the record corrected through the DGS procedure before the joining date. This cannot be sorted at the gangway.

Scenario 2 – Course not uploaded. An AB completes his refresher, but the institute has not uploaded it. The manning agent's online check shows the old, expired certificate. Fix: chase the institute and check the online record yourself after every course. Keep the physical certificate as well.

Scenario 3 – Duplicate records. A cadet applied twice after losing his paperwork. His sea service sits under one number and his courses under the other. The CoC exam application stalls. Fix: apply to DGS to merge or cancel the duplicate. Expect this to take time.

Scenario 4 – Unapproved course. A rating did a cheap "STCW course" at a centre that was not DGS-approved. It does not appear against his INDoS at all. Fix: there isn't one. You have to redo the course at an approved institute.

Other common stops:

  • CDC expired or close to expiry;
  • medical certificate not valid for the length of the contract;
  • photo or signature on the record unclear or outdated.

Oral exam corner

Examiners may not ask about INDoS by name. Certificate verification and the administration's register do come up, especially at management level.

  • Q: Which STCW regulation requires an administration to keep a register of certificates? A: Regulation I/2, supported by Section A-I/2 of the Code.
  • Q: Does the INDoS number prove competence? A: No. It identifies the seafarer in the national database. Competence is shown by the certificates recorded against it.
  • Q: A PSC officer doubts a crew member's certificate. How can it be checked? A: Through the issuing administration's verification arrangements, which STCW requires Parties to provide. For Indian certificates, that means the DGS records.
  • Q: What must a seafarer do every five years to keep basic training valid? A: Provide evidence of maintained competence, normally through refresher training, as required under Chapter VI of STCW.

Tips for keeping your record clean

  • Match your passport. Every new document should use exactly the same spelling of your name and the same date of birth.
  • Check online after every course. Confirm the course appears against your INDoS before you leave the institute's town if you can.
  • Track expiry dates. Keep a simple list of your CDC, CoC, STCW refreshers, medical, passport and visas. Start renewals well before you expect to go on leave.
  • Update personal details. If your passport is renewed or your address changes, update the record through the current DGS procedure.
  • Never take a second number. Recover the existing one instead.
  • Use approved institutes only. Confirm approval on the DGS list before paying anything.
  • Keep both paper and digital copies. Database checks are the norm, but originals still matter for some checks and for disputes.
Key points
  • INDoS is India's national seafarer database; your INDoS number is a unique ID, not a certificate of competence.
  • It supports India's obligation under STCW Regulation I/2 to keep a register of certificates and allow verification.
  • Indian seafarers need it for DGS-approved courses, the CDC and CoC; Filipino seafarers are covered by MARINA records instead.
  • The number does not expire, but the certificates linked to it (CDC, CoC, STCW refreshers, medical) do.
  • Most joining delays come from name or date mismatches, courses not uploaded, duplicate numbers or unapproved courses.
  • Check your online record after every course and keep details matched to your passport.

Frequently asked questions

Does my INDoS number expire?

No. The INDoS number is a career-long identifier. What expires are the certificates and documents linked to it, such as your CDC, CoC, STCW refreshers and medical. Keeping those current is what keeps your record usable.

I lost my INDoS number. Should I apply for a new one?

No. Applying again can create a duplicate record that splits your courses and sea service across two numbers. Recover the existing number through the institute where you did your first course or through the DGS online system and its current procedure.

My course certificate isn't showing on my INDoS record. What do I do?

Contact the DGS-approved institute that ran the course, because uploading the record is normally their responsibility. Keep your physical certificate as proof meanwhile. If the institute was not DGS-approved, the course will not appear and will generally not be accepted for Indian certification.

Do Filipino seafarers need an INDoS number?

No. INDoS is India's system for seafarers certified by the Indian administration. Philippine-certified seafarers have their certificates recorded and verified through MARINA. The exception may be someone training at an Indian institute, who should confirm the current rules with that institute.

How do I correct a spelling mistake in my INDoS record?

Apply for correction through the procedure currently prescribed by DG Shipping. Supporting documents, usually your passport, will be required. Do this well before any joining date, because a mismatch can hold up flag endorsements and document checks.

Test yourself

What is an INDoS number?

INDoS is the Indian National Database of Seafarers. The number identifies the seafarer and links their records; it is not a certificate. Reference: DGS administration supporting STCW Regulation I/2.

Which STCW regulation requires each Party to keep a register of certificates and endorsements it issues?

Regulation I/2 covers certificates and endorsements, including keeping a register and providing verification. I/11 deals with revalidation and VI/1 with basic safety training.

A seafarer's INDoS record shows an expired refresher he has actually renewed. What is the most likely cause?

The number does not expire; missing records usually mean the course was not uploaded by the approved institute. Reference: DGS record-keeping supporting STCW Regulation I/2.

How often must evidence of maintained competence in basic training such as PST and FPFF be provided?

Evidence of maintained competence in basic training is required every five years. Reference: STCW Regulation VI/1 and Section A-VI/1.

What is the correct action for a seafarer who has forgotten his INDoS number?

A second application risks a duplicate record that splits courses and sea service. The existing number should be recovered instead. Reference: DGS INDoS procedures under STCW Regulation I/2 obligations.
