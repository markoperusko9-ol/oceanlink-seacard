Crowd Management Training (Passenger Ships): Who Needs It, What It Covers and How to Keep It Valid

Quick answer

Crowd management training is an STCW requirement under Regulation V/2 and Section A-V/2 of the Code. It applies to masters, officers, ratings and any other crew named on the muster list to assist passengers in an emergency on seagoing passenger ships. It must be refreshed, or competence otherwise proven, at intervals not exceeding five years. How that is done and what document is accepted depends on the flag administration.

What crowd management training is and where it comes from

Crowd management training prepares crew to move a large number of frightened, untrained people to their muster and embarkation stations safely. The hard part is keeping them calm while you do it.

It sits in STCW Chapter V, which contains special training requirements for personnel on certain types of ship. The relevant instruments are:

  • STCW Regulation V/2 sets the mandatory minimum training for masters, officers, ratings and other personnel on passenger ships.
  • STCW Code, Section A-V/2 sets out the competences and minimum standards for each passenger ship training element.

Regulation V/2 contains several separate passenger ship elements. Candidates often confuse them:

Element Broadly who it is for
Passenger ship emergency familiarisation Everyone serving on board
Safety training for personnel providing direct service to passengers Crew working in passenger spaces
Crowd management Officers, ratings and other crew designated on the muster list to assist passengers in emergencies
Crisis management and human behaviour Master, chief engineer, chief mate, second engineer and anyone with responsibility for passenger safety in emergencies
Passenger safety, cargo safety and hull integrity Master, chief engineer, chief mate, second engineer and ro-ro passenger ship personnel with immediate responsibility for embarking/disembarking passengers, cargo operations or closing hull openings

Crowd management is a separate requirement. It does not replace your STCW basic training under Chapter VI, and basic training does not replace it.

Who needs it

The regulation applies to personnel serving on seagoing passenger ships engaged on international voyages. Each administration decides whether, and how far, it applies to passenger ships on domestic voyages. Domestic ferry crew should therefore check their own flag's rules rather than assume.

Under Regulation V/2, crowd management is required for:

  • masters, officers and ratings qualified under STCW Chapters II, III and VII (masters, deck and engine officers including ETOs, and deck, engine and electro-technical ratings, including those certified under the alternative certification of Chapter VII), and
  • other personnel designated on the muster list to assist passengers in emergency situations.

In practice, on a cruise ship or ro-pax this covers far more than the deck and engine departments. Hotel staff are commonly included if the muster list gives them a role such as:

  • stairway or corridor guide
  • assembly station staff
  • cabin search party
  • embarkation station assistant

The trigger is your role on the muster list, not your job title. A cook who is a stairway guide needs it.

A ship only counts as a passenger ship if it carries more than twelve passengers. Most private yachts are therefore outside this requirement. Yachts certified to carry more passengers can be different, so check the flag's requirements for that vessel.

What the course covers

Section A-V/2 sets the competence. Approved courses build around the same core areas.

1. Knowledge of the ship's emergency arrangements - Life-saving appliance and control plans, the muster list and emergency instructions - Emergency exits and evacuation routes - Restrictions on using lifts during an emergency - Equipment and means used to aid evacuation

2. Assisting passengers to muster and embarkation stations - Giving clear, reassuring orders - Controlling passengers in corridors, staircases and passageways - Keeping escape routes clear of obstructions - Methods for evacuating disabled persons and those needing special assistance - Searching accommodation spaces so that no one is left behind

3. Mustering procedures - Why keeping order matters - Techniques for reducing and avoiding panic - Using passenger lists for counts where appropriate - Checking that passengers are suitably clothed and have donned lifejackets correctly

Good courses include practical exercises, such as role-play with confused or uncooperative "passengers" and smoke-filled or blocked routes. They also cover non-verbal communication, because many passengers will not share your language.

Course length, format and whether it is combined with crisis management and human behaviour all vary by administration and approved training provider. Check the approved course list of the flag you will serve under.

Worked scenario: the blocked stairway

You are designated stairway guide on Deck 5 aft. The general emergency alarm sounds, followed by an announcement that passengers should proceed to assembly stations. This is how a trained crew member works it.

  1. Go to your post immediately. Take whatever the muster list assigns you, such as a torch, a radio, or a hi-vis vest or cap. You cannot control a crowd from behind it.
  2. Stop the lift habit. Passengers will crowd the lift lobby. Direct them firmly to the stairs and explain briefly why. Lift restrictions are part of the competence.
  3. Use short, clear commands. "This way, walk, hold the handrail" works. Long explanations do not. Point and use your whole arm. Body language carries further than words in a noisy stairwell.
  4. Deal with the obstruction. Suppose a passenger has stopped on the landing with luggage. Separate the person from the bag, keep the flow moving, and report the obstruction by radio.
  5. Identify people needing assistance. An elderly passenger with a walking frame needs a different plan. Follow the ship's procedure for persons requiring special assistance. Do not let them block the main flow, and do not abandon them.
  6. Report. Your area is clear only when the cabin search for your section is confirmed. Report to the bridge or emergency control station by the method on your instructions.

The point the examiner looks for is that you kept routes open, kept people moving and reported. Lone heroics are not the aim.

Validity, refresher training and documents

Regulation V/2 requires personnel trained in crowd management to do one of two things at intervals not exceeding five years:

  • undertake appropriate refresher training, or
  • provide evidence of having achieved the required standard of competence within the previous five years.

The same five-year rule applies to crisis management and human behaviour, and to the ro-ro passenger safety, cargo safety and hull integrity training.

What differs between administrations:

  • The form of the document. Some flags or maritime administrations issue a formal certificate of proficiency. Others accept a certificate from an approved training provider.
  • How the five years is counted. Some print an expiry date. Others expect you or the company to track it from the issue date.
  • What counts as "evidence of competence". Most seafarers simply do the refresher course. Acceptance of shipboard experience or assessment in lieu is entirely up to the administration.
  • Recognition of foreign training. A certificate from a provider approved by your national administration is usually accepted by other flags, but not automatically. Some flags have their own recognition processes.

Company SMS requirements can be stricter than the flag's. Cruise operators in particular often run their own crowd-control training and drills on top of the STCW certificate.

Finally, regulation requires the administration to ensure documentary evidence is issued to everyone found qualified. If you do not hold the document, you are not qualified for the purpose of joining, however good your experience.

Why crew get stopped at joining

These are the common reasons a crowd management certificate holds up a sign-on or gets flagged at a company audit or port state control inspection:

  • Expired. It is more than five years old with no refresher. This is the most common problem.
  • Wrong provider. The course was run by a centre not approved by an administration the flag recognises.
  • Wrong course. The seafarer holds only passenger ship familiarisation or "safety training for personnel providing direct service". These are not crowd management.
  • Muster role changed. A crew member moves from a role that did not need it to one that does, such as stairway guide or embarkation station duty, without the training.
  • Senior officers missing the companion course. A chief mate or second engineer holds crowd management but not crisis management and human behaviour, which their rank also requires.
  • Ro-pax gap. A ferry officer with cargo or hull-opening duties lacks the passenger safety, cargo safety and hull integrity training.
  • Document problems. Names do not match the passport or CDC, a copy is offered where the original is required, or the certificate is in a language the flag will not accept without translation.

Tips for keeping it in date

  • Put the five-year date in your phone and your document wallet, and book the refresher during leave well before it lapses.
  • Refresh crowd management and crisis management together where your provider runs them back to back.
  • Before signing a contract with a new company or flag, ask the manning agent exactly which passenger ship elements that ship requires.
  • Keep originals with you and a scanned set accessible.
  • Read the muster list on joining. If your role there needs training you do not have, tell the master or staff captain immediately.

Oral exam style questions

Examiners, and masters doing a joining interview, commonly ask:

  • "Where is crowd management training required?" STCW Regulation V/2, with the standard of competence in Section A-V/2 of the Code.
  • "Who on board needs it?" Officers and ratings qualified under Chapters II, III and VII, plus anyone designated on the muster list to assist passengers in an emergency.
  • "How often must it be refreshed?" At intervals not exceeding five years, by refresher training or evidence of competence. The exact method is set by the administration.
  • "Why not use the lifts?" Lifts can fail or take people to a fire-affected deck, and they create bottlenecks. Restrictions on their use form part of the evacuation procedures.
  • "How do you reduce panic?" Visible presence and calm, clear, repeated instructions. Give people a simple task, such as putting on their lifejacket, keep groups moving, and stop rumours with accurate information from the bridge.
  • "What is the difference between crowd management and crisis management?" Crowd management is hands-on control of passengers. Crisis management and human behaviour is about leading, organising resources and decision-making under stress. It is aimed at senior officers and those responsible for passenger safety.
Key points
  • Crowd management training is required by STCW Regulation V/2, with the competence set in Section A-V/2 of the STCW Code.
  • It applies on seagoing passenger ships on international voyages; the administration decides the position for domestic voyages.
  • It is required for officers and ratings under STCW Chapters II, III and VII, and for anyone the muster list designates to assist passengers.
  • Your muster list role, not your department, decides whether you need it.
  • It must be refreshed, or competence otherwise proven, at intervals not exceeding five years.
  • The document format, provider approval and recognition of foreign certificates vary by administration.
  • The biggest joining problems are lapsed refreshers, unapproved providers and confusing it with familiarisation training.

Frequently asked questions

Do hotel staff on cruise ships need crowd management training?

Yes, if the muster list designates them to assist passengers in an emergency, for example as stairway guides, assembly station staff or cabin searchers. Many cruise ships give most hotel staff such roles, so in practice large numbers of them need it. Hotel staff without such a role still need passenger ship emergency familiarisation, and those working in passenger spaces need the safety training for direct service personnel.

How long is a crowd management certificate valid?

STCW requires refresher training, or evidence of having achieved the required standard of competence, at intervals not exceeding five years. Some administrations print an expiry date on the document and others expect it to be tracked from the issue date. Check what your flag and company require.

Is crowd management the same as crisis management and human behaviour training?

No. They are separate elements under STCW Regulation V/2. Crowd management covers the practical control and evacuation of passengers. Crisis management and human behaviour is required for the master, chief engineer, chief mate, second engineer and anyone responsible for passenger safety, and covers leadership and decision-making in emergencies.

Do I need crowd management training on a cargo ship or tanker?

No. The requirement applies to passenger ships, meaning ships carrying more than twelve passengers. If you move from cargo ships to a cruise ship, ferry or ro-pax, you will need it if your rank or muster list role falls within the regulation.

Will a crowd management certificate from my home country be accepted by another flag?

Usually, if the course was approved by an administration that meets STCW requirements, but acceptance is not automatic. Some flags have their own recognition process or require specific documentation. Confirm with your manning agent or the flag administration before joining.

Test yourself

Which STCW regulation contains the requirement for crowd management training on passenger ships?

Passenger ship training, including crowd management, is in Regulation V/2. VI/1 covers basic safety training, V/1-1 covers oil and chemical tankers, and VI/6 covers security training. Reference: STCW Regulation V/2.

What mainly decides whether a hotel department crew member needs crowd management training?

Regulation V/2 links the requirement for non-watchkeeping personnel to their designation on the muster list to assist passengers. Reference: STCW Regulation V/2 and Section A-V/2.

At what interval must crowd management competence be refreshed or otherwise demonstrated?

Regulation V/2 requires refresher training or evidence of competence at intervals not exceeding five years. The administration sets the details of how this is done. Reference: STCW Regulation V/2.

Which of these is part of the crowd management competence in the STCW Code?

Assisting passengers to muster stations includes methods for evacuating disabled persons and those needing special assistance. Reference: STCW Code, Section A-V/2.

A second engineer joining a cruise ship holds a valid crowd management certificate only. What else does Regulation V/2 require of that rank?

The master, chief engineer, chief mate and second engineer must also complete crisis management and human behaviour training. Reference: STCW Regulation V/2 and Section A-V/2.
