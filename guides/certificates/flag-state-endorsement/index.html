Flag State Endorsement: What It Is, Who Needs It and How to Keep It Valid

Quick answer

A flag state endorsement is the document a ship's flag Administration issues to recognise a certificate of competency issued by another country. STCW Regulation I/10 requires it. Without it, or without valid proof that you have applied for it, you cannot lawfully serve in an officer or radio capacity on that flag's ships. It is specific to one flag, can never outlast the CoC it is based on, and is a common cause of refused joinings.

What a flag state endorsement actually is

Your Certificate of Competency (CoC) is issued by your own Administration, for example DG Shipping in India or MARINA in the Philippines. On its own, it lets you serve on ships flying that country's flag. Most Indian and Filipino officers sail on Panama, Liberia, Marshall Islands, Singapore, Malta, Bahamas and other flags. Each of those flags must formally accept your CoC before you can serve on its ships.

That acceptance is the endorsement attesting recognition, which most people call the flag state endorsement or flag endorsement. It is a separate document issued by the flag. It refers to your national CoC by number and states the capacity you may serve in on that flag's ships.

Key points to understand:

  • It is not a new certificate of competency. It only recognises the one you already hold.
  • It is flag-specific. A Liberian endorsement is no use on a Marshall Islands ship.
  • It is not a course. In most cases it is an application process. Some Administrations add their own requirements for certain ranks.
  • It must be read together with your national CoC. One is worthless on board without the other.

The legal basis: STCW and ISM

The requirement comes from the STCW Convention:

  • Regulation I/10 (Recognition of certificates)
    • An Administration that recognises another Party's certificate must endorse it to show that recognition.
    • Before doing so, it must satisfy itself that the issuing Party meets STCW standards. This can include evaluating that Party's training and certification system.
    • Certificates issued by a non-Party cannot be recognised.
    • The endorsement must not allow you to serve beyond what your CoC supports.
  • Regulation I/10 (interim service)
    • A flag may allow you to serve for up to three months while your endorsement is pending.
    • This is at the flag's discretion ("if circumstances require"), and the CoC held must be valid and appropriate for the capacity.
    • Documentary proof that you have applied for the endorsement must be readily available.
  • Regulation I/2 (Certificates and endorsements)
    • Endorsements expire when the underlying certificate expires or is withdrawn, suspended or cancelled.
    • In any case they cannot run beyond five years from issue.
    • Certificates and endorsements are generally to be kept in original form on board the ship where you are serving.
  • STCW Code Section A-I/2 sets out the form of certificates and endorsements.
  • Regulation I/4 (Control procedures) allows port State control officers to check that each seafarer holds the required certificate, a valid dispensation, or proof of an endorsement application.

The ISM Code (section 6.2) also places a duty on the company. It must ensure each ship is manned with qualified, certificated seafarers in line with national and international requirements. This is why crewing departments chase your flag paperwork so hard.

Who needs one

As a rule, you need a flag endorsement if all of the following apply:

  1. You hold an STCW certificate issued by one country.
  2. You will serve on a ship flying a different country's flag.
  3. You will serve in a capacity that requires that certificate.

In practice that covers:

  • Deck officers from OOW to Master.
  • Engineer officers from OOW to Chief Engineer.
  • ETOs holding an STCW CoC.
  • GMDSS radio operators, usually the GOC held by deck officers. Some flags endorse it separately and some include it in the deck endorsement.
  • Tanker and gas qualifications (STCW V/1-1, V/1-2). For masters and officers these certificates of proficiency must also be recognised by the flag under Regulation I/10. Some flags record them on the officer endorsement, others issue a separate document. For ratings, treatment varies by flag.

Some cases depend on the flag, so check that flag's requirements:

  • Ratings holding certificates of proficiency (watch ratings, able seafarer). Many flags do not issue endorsements for these. Some issue their own documents or seaman's books.
  • Other certificates of proficiency (for example ship security officer). Treatment varies between flags.

If you are joining a ship that flies the same flag as your CoC, for example an Indian CoC holder on an Indian-flag ship, no flag endorsement is needed.

How the process usually works

Procedures differ between flags. Most open registries work through online portals or authorised offices, and the company or manning agent normally submits the application. A typical sequence:

  1. The company confirms the vessel and flag. The endorsement is applied for against a specific flag and capacity.
  2. Documents are collected. Usually these are:
    • your national CoC
    • GMDSS certificate if applicable
    • passport
    • medical fitness certificate
    • relevant certificates of proficiency
    • photographs
  3. Verification. The flag checks that your CoC is genuine with the issuing Administration. Many Administrations run online verification for this. Delays often start here.
  4. Interim document. Many flags issue proof of application, commonly called a CRA (Certificate of Receipt of Application). It lets you serve for no more than three months.
  5. Full endorsement issued. The original is sent to you or the ship. Some flags now issue electronically verifiable documents.

Some Administrations add extra steps, particularly for senior ranks. These can include a test of national maritime law, or issuing endorsements only where the flag has a recognition undertaking with your issuing country.

Fees, processing times and document lists are set by each flag and change over time. Check current requirements with the flag or your company rather than relying on what a colleague did three years ago.

Validity and revalidation

Remember one rule: the endorsement can never outlive the CoC it recognises.

Event Effect on flag endorsement
CoC reaches expiry date Endorsement ceases to be valid
CoC revalidated (new certificate issued) Old endorsement refers to old CoC, so apply for a new one
Promoted, new CoC for higher capacity New endorsement needed for the new capacity
CoC suspended or withdrawn Endorsement falls with it
Five years from endorsement issue Endorsement expires even if CoC is still valid
Ship changes flag You need the new flag's endorsement

Many flags simply set the endorsement expiry to match the CoC expiry. Some set a shorter period, so read the date on the document itself.

Revalidating your national CoC under Regulation I/11 does not carry over to the flag document. When your Administration issues a fresh CoC with a new number or date, the flag endorsement linked to the old certificate is dead.

Why crew get stopped at joining

These are the cases that turn up again and again at the gangway, at the airport check-in desk with the agent, or in PSC findings:

  • CRA beyond three months. The full endorsement never arrived and the interim period ran out mid-contract. You are now serving without valid documentation.
  • Endorsement tied to the old CoC. You revalidated at home but nobody applied to the flag for a new endorsement.
  • Wrong flag. You transferred to a sister ship under a different flag, or the ship reflagged during your contract.
  • Capacity mismatch. You were promoted on board or on joining, but the endorsement still shows the lower rank. Or the endorsement limits you to a capacity below the one on the crew list.
  • Limitations carried over. Restrictions on your CoC, such as tonnage, propulsion power, near-coastal or equipment limitations, appear on the endorsement too. The ship may not fit them.
  • GMDSS not covered. The deck endorsement exists but the GOC endorsement does not.
  • Tanker qualification not recognised by that flag. The national tanker certificate is valid, but the flag's recognition of it, on the officer endorsement or a separate document, is missing.
  • Copy only. You have a scan but the flag expects the original on board.
  • Personal details mismatch. The name spelling or date of birth differs between passport, CoC and endorsement.

Worked scenario. A 2nd Officer with an Indian CoC joins a Panama-flag bulk carrier on a CRA dated 20 days before joining. At about the two-month mark the company tells him the vessel will be sold and reflagged to Liberia at the next port. He now needs two things:

  • Liberian recognition (at least a valid interim document) before the flag changes.
  • His full Panama endorsement, or another plan, if the reflagging is delayed past the three-month CRA limit.

The master should raise both issues with crewing immediately, not on arrival.

Oral exam corner and keeping it in date

Examiners like this topic because it tests whether you understand certification as a system. Typical questions:

  • "Under which regulation does a flag recognise a foreign CoC?" The answer is STCW Regulation I/10, with endorsements dealt with in Regulation I/2.
  • "Can you sail on a receipt of application?" Yes, if the flag permits it, for no more than three months, with the proof readily available on board.
  • "What happens to the endorsement when you revalidate?" It lapses with the old CoC, so a new one is needed.
  • "As Master, what do you check when a new officer joins?"
    • CoC and endorsement are originals, match each other and are in date.
    • Capacity and limitations fit the ship and the safe manning document.
    • The GMDSS endorsement is in place.
    • Any CRA is still within its period.

Practical tips for keeping it valid:

  • Keep a personal list of expiry dates for:
    • CoC
    • each flag endorsement
    • GOC
    • medical
    • tanker documents
  • When you revalidate your CoC, tell your company the same week so new flag applications go in early.
  • Before signing on, ask which flag the ship flies, and whether a reflagging or transfer is planned.
  • Check the validity or expiry stated on every CRA you receive. STCW caps interim service at three months, and the flag decides how the period is counted. Chase the full endorsement well before it runs out.
  • Make sure your name is spelled identically across passport, CDC, CoC and all flag documents.
  • Keep scans for your own records, but carry the originals unless the flag clearly states that electronic versions are acceptable.
Key points
  • A flag state endorsement is the flag's recognition of your foreign CoC under STCW Regulation I/10. It is not a new CoC.
  • It is valid only for one flag and only for the capacity stated, which cannot exceed what your CoC supports.
  • Proof of application (often called a CRA) allows service for no more than three months, at the flag's discretion.
  • The endorsement expires with the underlying CoC and in any case cannot exceed five years.
  • Revalidating or upgrading your CoC means applying for a new flag endorsement.
  • Officers' tanker and gas certificates must also be recognised by the flag, either on the endorsement or as a separate document, and GMDSS treatment varies by flag.
  • Fees, processing times and extra requirements are set by each flag, so always check current rules.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a flag endorsement if my ship flies my own country's flag?

No. Your national CoC is valid on ships of the country that issued it. The endorsement is only needed when the ship's flag is different from the Administration that issued your certificate.

How long can I sail on a CRA or receipt of application?

STCW allows a flag to permit service for up to three months while the endorsement application is processed. A flag may apply stricter conditions. You must keep the documentary proof of application readily available on board.

My CoC was revalidated. Is my old flag endorsement still valid?

Generally no. The endorsement recognises a specific certificate, so when that certificate is replaced the endorsement linked to it lapses. Ask your company to apply for a new endorsement against the revalidated CoC before your next joining.

Do ratings need a flag state endorsement?

It depends on the flag. Many flags do not endorse ratings' certificates of proficiency. Some issue their own seaman's book or other documents, so check with your manning agent for the specific flag.

Does a flag endorsement cover my GMDSS certificate as well?

Not automatically. Some flags endorse the GOC within the deck officer's endorsement and others issue a separate one. Check that radio operator recognition is shown before joining.

Test yourself

Which STCW regulation requires an Administration to endorse a foreign certificate it recognises?

Regulation I/10 covers recognition of certificates and the endorsement attesting recognition. I/11 covers revalidation and I/14 covers company responsibilities (STCW Convention, Regulation I/10).

What is the maximum period a flag may allow an officer to serve while an endorsement application is pending?

STCW allows interim service of no more than three months, with documentary proof of application readily available (STCW Convention, Regulation I/10).

An officer revalidates his national CoC and receives a new certificate. What happens to his existing flag endorsement?

An endorsement refers to a specific certificate. It expires when that certificate expires or is withdrawn, suspended or cancelled, and in practice a new endorsement is needed against the revalidated CoC (STCW Convention, Regulation I/2).

A 3rd Officer's CoC is issued at OOW level. What capacity can a flag endorsement grant?

Recognition cannot allow service beyond the capacity, function and level of the certificate being recognised (STCW Convention, Regulation I/10).

Your Panama-flag ship is reflagged to Liberia mid-contract. What is required for the officers?

Endorsements are flag-specific, so the new flag must recognise each officer's certificate (STCW Convention, Regulation I/10; ISM Code, section 6.2).
