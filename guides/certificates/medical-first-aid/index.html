Medical First Aid for Seafarers: STCW VI/4 Requirements, Course and Validity

Quick answer

Medical First Aid is the STCW Regulation VI/4 qualification for seafarers designated to provide medical first aid on board. It sits above Elementary First Aid (part of Basic Safety Training) and below Medical Care. Administrations commonly require it for deck officer certificates of competency, while requirements for engineer officers and ratings vary by administration. STCW does not set a five-year refresher for it the way it does for parts of Basic Safety Training, but some flag states and companies do, so check before you join.

What Medical First Aid is and where it fits

There are three levels of medical training under STCW. Know which one your next job needs:

Level STCW reference Who it is for
Elementary First Aid (EFA) Section A-VI/1, part of Basic Safety Training Every seafarer with designated safety or pollution-prevention duties
Medical First Aid (MFA) Regulation VI/4, Section A-VI/4 paras 1–3 Seafarers designated to provide medical first aid on board
Medical Care Regulation VI/4, Section A-VI/4 paras 4–6 Seafarers designated to take charge of medical care on board

EFA teaches you to keep a casualty alive until someone better trained arrives. MFA makes you that better-trained person. You are expected to give immediate, effective first aid with the ship's medical chest and radio medical advice, with no doctor on board.

Medical Care goes further. It covers ongoing treatment, medicines, record keeping and running the ship's hospital, usually for the master or the officer responsible for medical care.

Which rules require it

STCW. Regulation VI/4 requires seafarers designated to provide medical first aid to meet the standard of competence in Section A-VI/4, paragraphs 1 to 3. Table A-VI/4-1 sets out the detail. The operational-level deck standard in Table A-II/1 also includes applying medical first aid on board. Administrations therefore commonly make MFA a prerequisite for the OOW (deck) certificate of competency.

MLC, 2006. Regulation 4.1 and Standard A4.1 cover medical care on board. In broad terms:

  • Ships carrying 100 or more persons and ordinarily engaged on international voyages of more than three days must carry a qualified medical doctor.
  • A ship without a doctor must have at least one seafarer either in charge of medical care or competent to give medical first aid as part of their regular duties.
  • Those people must have training that meets STCW.

This is why port state control and MLC inspections look at who on board holds MFA or Medical Care certificates.

National and company rules. Your administration decides exactly which CoC levels, and which engineering or rating grades, must hold MFA. Some companies also list it in their training matrix for ranks the flag state does not strictly require. Check both your administration's current notice and the company matrix. Don't rely on what a colleague from another flag tells you.

What the course covers

Training follows Table A-VI/4-1. Most approved courses are built on the IMO model course for Medical First Aid. Course length and assessment format are set by your administration and the training centre. Expect theory plus practical assessment on:

  • Using the first-aid kit and the ship's medical chest.
  • Body structure and function, enough to examine a casualty and describe findings.
  • Toxicological hazards on board, including use of the Medical First Aid Guide for Use in Accidents Involving Dangerous Goods (MFAG) or its national equivalent.
  • Examining a casualty or patient.
  • Spinal injuries.
  • Burns, scalds and the effects of heat and cold.
  • Fractures, dislocations and muscular injuries.
  • Medical care of rescued persons.
  • Radio medical advice. How to get it and what information to have ready.
  • Pharmacology and sterilisation at a basic level.
  • Cardiac arrest, drowning and asphyxia, including CPR.

You need to apply all this with the references actually found on board. Those include the International Medical Guide for Ships (or the national equivalent your flag requires), the medical section of the International Code of Signals, and MFAG.

Worked scenario: engine room burn at sea

A motorman is scalded on the forearm and chest by a leaking steam line. The ship is three days from port. You hold MFA and are first on scene.

  1. Scene safety first. The leak must be isolated before anyone goes in. Wear the right PPE. Don't create a second casualty.
  2. Remove the casualty from danger and check the primary survey: airway, breathing, circulation.
  3. Cool the burn with clean, cool running water for a prolonged period. Current first-aid guidance commonly gives about 20 minutes. Watch for hypothermia, especially with large burn areas.
  4. Remove rings, watches and loose clothing near the burn before swelling starts. Leave anything stuck to the skin.
  5. Cover loosely with a sterile non-adherent dressing or cling film. Do not apply creams or burst blisters.
  6. Assess the size and depth of the burn using the guidance in the ship's medical guide. Look for signs of shock or airway burns.
  7. Get radio medical advice through a Telemedical Assistance Service (TMAS), normally via the MRCC or the company's procedure. Have ready:
    • vital signs
    • burn area and depth
    • time of injury
    • medicines available
    • ship's position and ETA
  8. Record everything in the medical log and casualty notes. Inform the master, who decides on diversion or evacuation.

This sequence of scene safety, assessment, treatment, radio advice and documentation is what examiners and assessors want to see.

Oral exam style questions

In CoC orals the medical questions are usually practical. Typical ones:

  • Which publication do you use for a casualty exposed to a dangerous cargo? MFAG, used with the cargo's emergency information, and the IMDG Code for packaged goods.
  • What information do you send when requesting radio medical advice? Patient details, symptoms and vital signs, history, medicines already given, medical chest contents, and ship position, course, speed and nearest port.
  • How do you treat a suspected spinal injury on deck? Keep the casualty still and immobilise the head and neck. Only move them if there is immediate danger, and then use a proper extrication method.
  • Who is responsible for medical care on your ship and where is that recorded? Know your ship's SMS answer, not a textbook one.
  • What is the difference between EFA, MFA and Medical Care? See the table above.

Examiners dislike candidates who recite a course syllabus but cannot say where the medical chest list or the medical log is kept on their last ship.

Validity and revalidation

This is where seafarers most often get confused.

What STCW says. The Manila amendments brought in refresher training at intervals not exceeding five years for:

  • Personal Survival Techniques and Fire Prevention and Fire Fighting (the BST elements in Section A-VI/1 para 2.1.1–2.1.2; Elementary First Aid and PSSR have no STCW refresher)
  • PSCRB
  • Fast Rescue Boats
  • Advanced Fire Fighting

Regulation VI/4 does not contain an equivalent refresher requirement for Medical First Aid or Medical Care.

What happens in practice:

  • Many administrations issue MFA certificates with no expiry date.
  • Some administrations require a refresher or update course for MFA and/or Medical Care at set intervals. This is particularly common for those in charge of medical care.
  • Some companies, charterers and vetting regimes treat an old MFA certificate as out of date. They ask for a refresher even where the flag does not.
  • Your CoC revalidation process may check that the MFA certificate is recorded correctly in the national system.

The only safe answer to "does my MFA expire?" is this: read your administration's current rules and your company's training matrix. Don't trust an interval quoted by a crewing agent from memory.

Why crew get stopped at joining, and how to avoid it

Common problems found during document checks by the manning office, the ship, or a flag state endorsement application:

  • Holding EFA but not MFA. The rank requires MFA and the seafarer assumed BST covered it.
  • Holding MFA when the company wants Medical Care. This happens to masters and chief officers, and on some ships the officer designated for medical care.
  • Course not approved by the administration, or the training centre's approval had lapsed when the course was done.
  • Certificate not traceable in the national online verification system, or details that do not match the passport and CDC.
  • Flag or company refresher requirement not met. The certificate is valid under the issuing administration but fails the requirements of the ship's flag or the company.
  • Fraudulent or doubtful certificates. These lead to rejection and can lead to action by the administration. Only use approved institutes.

Tips for keeping it in date:

  • Keep a scanned copy of every certificate. Note any expiry or refresher date in a calendar with a three-month reminder.
  • Before accepting a contract, ask for the company's training matrix for your rank and the ship's flag.
  • If you are moving up to chief officer or master, plan Medical Care training before your next contract, not during leave week.
  • Check online verification yourself after every new certificate is issued.
  • When applying for a flag state endorsement, read that flag's list of required certificates. Some flags ask for things your home administration does not.
Key points
  • Medical First Aid is required by STCW Regulation VI/4 for seafarers designated to provide medical first aid.
  • The standard of competence is in STCW Code Section A-VI/4, paragraphs 1–3 and Table A-VI/4-1.
  • EFA (part of BST) is lower than MFA, and MFA is lower than Medical Care; they are not interchangeable.
  • MLC 2006 Standard A4.1 requires ships without a doctor to have trained crew for medical care or medical first aid.
  • STCW sets no five-year refresher for MFA, but some flag states and companies impose one.
  • Common joining refusals come from wrong level, unapproved institutes, or certificates not verifiable in the national system.

Frequently asked questions

Is Medical First Aid the same as Elementary First Aid?

No. Elementary First Aid is part of STCW Basic Safety Training and is for all seafarers with safety duties. Medical First Aid is a separate, more advanced course under STCW Regulation VI/4 for those designated to provide first aid on board. Holding EFA does not satisfy a requirement for MFA.

Does the Medical First Aid certificate expire?

STCW does not set a mandatory five-year refresher for Medical First Aid as it does for the PST and FPFF elements of Basic Safety Training, PSCRB, Fast Rescue Boats and AFF. However, some administrations and many companies require a refresher or update. Check your administration's current rules and your company's training matrix.

Who needs Medical First Aid on a merchant ship?

Anyone designated to provide medical first aid on board. Administrations commonly require it for deck officers' certificates of competency, and often for other grades. Exactly which ranks need it depends on your administration and your company's training matrix.

Do I need Medical Care instead of Medical First Aid as chief officer or master?

If you are designated to take charge of medical care on board, STCW Regulation VI/4 requires the Medical Care standard, not just MFA. Masters and chief officers are commonly expected to hold it. Whether your administration makes it mandatory for a particular CoC grade varies, so check national rules and your company's requirements.

Can I do Medical First Aid in a different country from my CoC administration?

Sometimes. It depends on whether your administration accepts training from that country's approved institutes. Many administrations accept only courses they have approved, or those approved by administrations they recognise. Confirm acceptance before paying for the course to avoid problems with CoC applications and joining checks.

Test yourself

Which STCW regulation covers mandatory minimum requirements for Medical First Aid?

Regulation VI/4 covers medical first aid and medical care. VI/1 is basic safety training and VI/3 is advanced fire fighting (STCW Convention, Regulation VI/4).

Under STCW, what is the refresher requirement for Medical First Aid?

The five-year refresher introduced by the Manila amendments applies to the PST and FPFF elements of BST, and to PSCRB, FRB and AFF. Regulation VI/4 contains no equivalent interval (STCW Code, Sections A-VI/1 to A-VI/3; STCW Convention, Regulation VI/4).

Elementary First Aid forms part of which STCW training?

EFA is one of the Basic Safety Training elements in Section A-VI/1. It is distinct from Medical First Aid (STCW Code, Section A-VI/1).

Under MLC 2006, what must a ship not carrying a doctor have?

The standard requires at least one seafarer in charge of medical care or competent in medical first aid, trained to STCW standards (MLC 2006, Standard A4.1).

Which publication do you consult for a crew member exposed to a dangerous cargo?

Table A-VI/4-1 specifically lists toxicological hazards, including use of MFAG or its national equivalent (STCW Code, Table A-VI/4-1).
