GMDSS Restricted Operator's Certificate (ROC)

Quick answer

The GMDSS Restricted Operator's Certificate (ROC) qualifies you to operate GMDSS radio equipment only on ships sailing exclusively within Sea Area A1, which means VHF coast station coverage. It is issued under STCW Regulation IV/2 and the ITU Radio Regulations. Deep-sea officers need the General Operator's Certificate (GOC) instead, and the most common joining problem is an ROC holder posted to a ship that trades beyond A1.

What the ROC is and where it comes from

The ROC is one of the two GMDSS operator certificates used on SOLAS ships. The other is the General Operator's Certificate (GOC). Both are defined in the ITU Radio Regulations, the international treaty governing radio use, and both are tied into STCW.

Three instruments matter:

  • SOLAS Chapter IV, Regulation 16. Every ship must carry personnel qualified for distress and safety radiocommunications, to the satisfaction of the Administration. These must be holders of certificates specified in the Radio Regulations.
  • STCW Regulation IV/2 and Section A-IV/2 of the Code. Anyone in charge of or performing radio duties on a ship required to participate in GMDSS must hold an appropriate GMDSS certificate, issued or recognised by the Administration. The Code sets out the required knowledge and competence.
  • ITU Radio Regulations (Article 47, operators' certificates). This defines the GOC and ROC and what each authorises.

In short, the ROC is a limited-area licence. It covers the same distress and safety principles as the GOC, but only for the equipment and procedures used in Sea Area A1.

Sea Area A1 and why the restriction matters

SOLAS Chapter IV defines four sea areas:

Sea area Broadly means
A1 Within radiotelephone coverage of at least one VHF coast station with continuous DSC alerting, as declared by a Contracting Government
A2 Within MF coast station coverage with continuous DSC alerting, excluding A1
A3 Within coverage of a recognised mobile satellite service, excluding A1 and A2
A4 Everywhere else (the polar regions)

The extent of A1 is not a fixed distance. It depends on the coast station coverage each government declares, so check the national listings in your admiralty list of radio signals or equivalent publication.

The practical point: an ROC holder is a recognised GMDSS operator only while the ship stays inside A1. If the voyage takes the ship into A2, A3 or A4, the ROC does not satisfy SOLAS IV/16 for that ship. You then need a GOC, regardless of rank or experience.

This is why the ROC is mostly found on domestic and coastal tonnage, harbour and port craft, short-sea ferries operating within VHF coverage, and some offshore and inshore work. It is not the certificate for a foreign-going officer on a bulk carrier, tanker or container ship.

Who needs it (and who needs the GOC instead)

  • Deck officers on ships trading only in A1. Here the ROC is typically the GMDSS qualification required for the watchkeeping officers. The exact manning requirement depends on the flag state and the ship's Safe Manning Document.
  • Masters and officers on near-coastal or domestic licences. Many administrations pair near-coastal deck certificates with the ROC. Whether the ROC is mandatory for your licence is set nationally.
  • Deep-sea deck officers. In practice, officers on foreign-going ships are normally certificated with the GOC, and most national CoC routes for unlimited trade require it. An ROC alone will not get you onto a foreign-going ship's GMDSS roster.
  • Ratings and engineers. These are not normally required to hold any GMDSS certificate. A company may choose to put a certificated person in another rank on the radio roster, but that is a company or flag decision.

Do not confuse the ROC with the Short Range Certificate (SRC). The SRC is a VHF/DSC licence aimed at leisure and non-SOLAS craft. Crewing offices sometimes mix them up, and a flag state will not accept an SRC where a GMDSS ROC is required.

What the course and exam cover

Course structure, hours, practical requirements and exam format are set by each administration. IMO publishes a model course that many training centres follow. Expect the content to be built around the A1 toolkit:

  • GMDSS principles: the sea areas, the functional requirements (alerting, SAR coordination, on-scene communications, locating, maritime safety information, general and bridge-to-bridge communications) and the role of rescue coordination centres.
  • VHF DSC: MMSI and identities, call categories (distress, urgency, safety, routine), sending and acknowledging a distress alert, relays, and the Channel 70 watch.
  • VHF radiotelephony: Channel 16 distress, urgency and safety traffic, and Channel 13 bridge-to-bridge. Also MAYDAY, PAN PAN and SÉCURITÉ procedures, the phonetic alphabet and standard phrases.
  • Maritime safety information: NAVTEX operation, message types and station selection.
  • Locating and alerting devices: EPIRB, SART or AIS-SART, and portable survival craft VHF. Covers how they activate, testing and maintenance basics.
  • False alerts: how they happen, how to cancel them and why it matters.
  • Practical operation on simulators or real equipment. Most administrations assess this, not just the theory.

The content follows the competence tables in STCW Section A-IV/2, restricted to A1 operation.

Worked scenario: false DSC distress alert on VHF

You are OOW on a coastal ferry in A1. A cadet cleaning the console accidentally triggers a VHF DSC distress alert. This is a classic exam question and a real-life event.

  1. Stop the transmission. Reset or switch off the DSC unit if it is still repeating, following the manufacturer's procedure. If the equipment has a DSC distress cancellation (self-cancel) function, use it, then follow up by voice.
  2. Tune to Channel 16.
  3. Broadcast a cancellation by voice to all stations. Give the ship's name, call sign and MMSI, and say you are cancelling the distress alert sent at a stated date and time. The usual layout is "All stations" said three times, then "this is" with your identity, then the position and the cancellation.
  4. Keep listening on Channel 16 and respond to any coast station or RCC that calls you.
  5. Inform the Master and record the event in the GMDSS radio log.
  6. Follow company procedure for reporting, and look at why it happened, such as an unguarded button or poor familiarisation.

Examiners also expect you to know the opposite case. Never ignore a false alert you have sent by mistake, and never simply switch off and say nothing. An uncancelled false alert can trigger a full search and rescue response.

Validity, revalidation and endorsements

This is where administrations differ most, so confirm with your own maritime authority.

  • STCW revalidation (Regulation I/11). Radio operators holding certificates under STCW chapters other than Chapter VI must revalidate at intervals not exceeding five years to continue sea service. They must show continued professional competence, usually by approved sea service or a refresher or assessment.
  • How the five-year rule is applied varies.
    • Some administrations issue the ROC itself with an expiry date.
    • Others treat the underlying radio certificate as permanent but put a time limit on the STCW endorsement attached to it.
    • Read both documents carefully.
  • Revalidation route. The amount of qualifying sea service, and whether a refresher course or exam can replace it, is set nationally. Do not rely on what a colleague from another flag tells you.
  • Flag state endorsement (Regulation I/10). Serving on a ship under a different flag from your issuing country normally requires that flag to recognise your GMDSS certificate by endorsement. While the endorsement is processed, many flags accept documentary proof that an application has been submitted, for a limited period. The flag decides whether it accepts this and for how long.

Why crew get stopped at joining

Real reasons ROC holders are held back by the crewing office, the Master or a port state inspector:

  • Wrong certificate for the trade. The ship's trading pattern or certificates show operation beyond A1, so an ROC does not count. Check the Record of Equipment attached to the safety radio certificate. It shows the sea areas the ship is equipped for.
  • Expired certificate or endorsement. The licence may look valid while the STCW endorsement or revalidation date has passed.
  • No flag state endorsement, or an expired proof of application, for a foreign-flag ship.
  • SRC presented instead of ROC, or a certificate that does not show it was issued in line with STCW IV/2.
  • Name or document mismatch between the GMDSS certificate, passport, CDC and CoC. This is often caused by spelling, an added middle name or a changed surname.
  • Safe Manning Document requires GOC holders. The ship is manned for a wider area than the officer's certificate covers.

Check these points before you sign the contract, not at the gangway.

Oral exam style questions and tips for staying in date

Questions examiners like:

  • Define Sea Area A1 and explain who decides its extent.
  • What can an ROC holder legally do, and when does it stop being valid on board?
  • Walk me through sending a VHF DSC distress alert and the voice follow-up.
  • How do you cancel a false alert?
  • What is your action on receiving a DSC distress alert from another ship in A1?
  • Which channels are used for DSC, distress voice and bridge-to-bridge traffic?
  • How do you test an EPIRB and a SART without causing a false alert?

Keeping your ROC in date:

  • Keep a scan of the certificate, every endorsement and every flag endorsement on your phone and in cloud storage, with expiry dates in your calendar.
  • Start revalidation well before expiry. Processing times vary and some administrations are slow.
  • Get your GMDSS sea service and radio duties recorded properly in your CDC or testimonials, since that evidence supports revalidation.
  • If you plan to move to foreign-going ships, plan the step up to the GOC early. It is a separate course and exam, not an upgrade stamp.
Key points
  • The ROC authorises GMDSS radio operation only on ships trading exclusively in Sea Area A1 (VHF DSC coverage).
  • It is required under SOLAS IV/16 and STCW Regulation IV/2, and defined in the ITU Radio Regulations.
  • Foreign-going deck officers need the GOC; an ROC alone does not cover A2, A3 or A4.
  • STCW Regulation I/11 requires revalidation at intervals not exceeding five years; the exact route depends on the administration.
  • Serving under another flag normally needs that flag's endorsement of your GMDSS certificate.
  • Check the ship's Record of Equipment and Safe Manning Document before joining to confirm the ROC is acceptable.
  • An SRC is not a GMDSS ROC and will not satisfy flag or port state requirements.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between GMDSS ROC and GOC?

The ROC covers GMDSS operation only in Sea Area A1, essentially VHF DSC and its associated equipment. The GOC covers all sea areas, A1 to A4, including MF/HF and satellite systems. Deep-sea officers need the GOC.

Can I sail on a foreign-going ship with only a GMDSS ROC?

Not as a recognised GMDSS operator if the ship trades beyond A1, which foreign-going ships almost always do. You could serve in a role that has no radio duties, but deck officer posts on such ships normally require the GOC. Check the flag's requirements and the Safe Manning Document.

How long is the GMDSS ROC valid?

STCW requires revalidation at intervals not exceeding five years for continued sea service. Some administrations put an expiry on the ROC itself, while others time-limit the STCW endorsement attached to it. Check both documents and your own administration's rules.

Is the Short Range Certificate (SRC) the same as the ROC?

No. The SRC is a VHF licence mainly for leisure and non-SOLAS craft. The ROC is a GMDSS certificate under STCW and the Radio Regulations, so a flag state requiring a GMDSS operator will not accept an SRC in its place.

Do I need a flag state endorsement for my ROC?

If the ship's flag differs from the country that issued your certificate, the flag normally requires an endorsement recognising it under STCW Regulation I/10. Many flags accept proof of application for a limited period while the endorsement is processed. The flag decides the details.

Test yourself

A GMDSS ROC holder is recognised as a GMDSS operator on a ship operating in which sea area(s)?

The ROC is limited to operation in Sea Area A1; wider areas need the GOC. Ref: ITU Radio Regulations Article 47; SOLAS Chapter IV.

Which STCW regulation sets the mandatory requirements for GMDSS radio operator certification?

STCW Regulation IV/2, with Section A-IV/2 of the Code, covers GMDSS radio operators.

You accidentally transmit a VHF DSC distress alert. What is the correct action after stopping the transmission?

IMO guidance on false distress alerts requires cancellation, including a voice broadcast on Channel 16 to all stations, then logging and informing the Master.

Which document best shows the sea areas a ship's GMDSS installation is equipped for?

The Record of Equipment attached to the SOLAS safety radio certificate shows the sea areas the ship's radio installation is equipped for. Ref: SOLAS Chapter I and Chapter IV.
