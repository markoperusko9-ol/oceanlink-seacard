Certificate of Competency (CoC) – Chief Mate and Master

Quick answer

The Chief Mate and Master CoC is the management-level deck certificate required by STCW Regulation II/2 for anyone serving as chief mate or master on a seagoing ship of 500 GT or more. You qualify by building approved sea service on an OICNW certificate, completing approved management-level training, and passing your administration's examinations. The certificate must be revalidated under STCW Regulation I/11, normally at intervals of no more than five years.

What the certificate is and where it comes from

The Chief Mate and Master CoC is the deck management-level certificate. STCW Regulation II/2 sets the requirement. Section A-II/2 of the STCW Code sets the standard of competence.

The Regulation splits the certificate by tonnage:

  • 3,000 GT or more: often called the "unlimited" certificate.
  • 500 GT up to 3,000 GT: a lower-tonnage certificate with a reduced syllabus in some areas.
  • Below 500 GT: covered separately, under Regulation II/3. It is not part of II/2.

The CoC itself is issued by your national administration, for example DG Shipping in India or MARINA in the Philippines. The Convention sets the floor. Each administration adds its own exam structure, course list and application process on top.

The Maritime Labour Convention, 2006 (Regulation 1.3) also requires seafarers to be trained or certified for their duties. Port State Control inspectors check certificates against both instruments.

Who needs it and the sea-service route

You need this CoC to sign on as chief mate (chief officer) or master on a seagoing ship of 500 GT or more. Under STCW Regulation II/2, the candidate must first meet the requirements for an OICNW certificate on ships of 500 GT or more. Sea service is then built on top of that.

Certificate Approved sea service as OICNW (STCW minimum)
Chief mate, 3,000 GT or more Not less than 12 months
Master, 3,000 GT or more Not less than 36 months. This can be reduced to not less than 24 months if at least 12 months were served as chief mate
Chief mate, 500–3,000 GT Meet the OICNW (500 GT+) requirements
Master, 500–3,000 GT Not less than 36 months. This can be reduced to not less than 24 months if at least 12 months were served as chief mate

Treat these as the international minimum. Many administrations add conditions on how sea service is counted and evidenced. Examples include testimonials, discharge book entries, records of watchkeeping service, and limits on how standby or yard time is counted.

Administrations often split the route into two steps. You sit chief mate first, then master after further service. Check your administration's current notices before planning your leave around an exam.

What the training and exams cover

Section A-II/2 organises the management-level standard into three functions.

1. Navigation at the management level - Voyage planning and conducting navigation in all conditions, including restricted waters, ice and traffic separation schemes - Position fixing, and determining and allowing for compass errors - Establishing watchkeeping arrangements and procedures - Use of ECDIS and associated navigation systems to assist command decision-making - Weather and oceanographic forecasting - Responding to navigational emergencies - Ship handling and manoeuvring in all conditions - Coordinating search and rescue operations

2. Cargo handling and stowage at the management level - Planning safe loading, stowage, securing and discharge, including dangerous cargoes - Assessing reported defects and damage to cargo spaces, hatch covers and ballast tanks

3. Controlling the operation of the ship and care for persons on board - Trim, stability and stress - Monitoring compliance with legislative requirements - Maintaining the safety and security of the ship, crew and passengers, and the condition of life-saving and fire-fighting appliances - Emergency and damage control plans - Leadership and managerial skills - Organising medical care on board

In practice, administrations require a set of approved courses before you can sit the exams. For the officer designated to take charge of medical care, this usually includes Medical Care on board (STCW A-VI/4). The course list, any written papers and the format of the oral or practical exam are all set nationally. Do not rely on a colleague's list from a different country or a different year.

Oral exam: what examiners actually probe

Management-level orals test judgement, not recitation. Expect the examiner to hand you a situation and keep adding problems.

Scenario 1: Damage stability. "You are chief mate. A ballast tank is breached after contact with a quay and you have a list. Walk me through it."

A strong answer works through these steps:

  1. Inform the master, and check if anyone is injured.
  2. Sound all tanks and identify the flooded compartment.
  3. Assess the effect on GM, including free surface.
  4. Look at the trim, stability and strength position.
  5. Consider counter-ballasting, and the risk it carries.
  6. Notify the company DPA and the class/flag route through the SMS.
  7. Record everything.

Scenario 2: Master's authority. "The charterer pressures you to sail before the weather window you planned. What do you do?"

The examiner wants to hear that the master's overriding authority for safety comes from the ISM Code and SOLAS. You should also explain how you document the decision.

Scenario 3: Watchkeeping arrangements. "You are entering a busy strait at night with a newly joined third officer. How do you set up the bridge team?"

Cover these points:

  • Your standing and night orders
  • Manning levels
  • When the master is called
  • Lookout
  • Use of the pilot
  • Fatigue and rest-hour compliance

Examiner's tip: answer in sequence and say why at each step. If you do not know something, say how you would find out: the SMS, the trim and stability booklet, the cargo securing manual or the passage plan. Do not guess.

Validity and revalidation

STCW Regulation I/11 requires masters and officers to revalidate at intervals not exceeding five years. At each revalidation you must show two things:

  • Medical fitness: a valid certificate to the STCW Regulation I/9 standard.
  • Continued professional competence: proven in one of the ways set out in Section A-I/11.

Section A-I/11 offers several ways to prove continued competence:

  • Approved sea service performing functions appropriate to the certificate, of at least one year in total during the preceding five years
  • Functions the administration considers equivalent
  • Passing an approved test, or completing approved course(s)
  • Approved seagoing service of not less than three months in total in a supernumerary capacity, or in a lower officer rank than that for which the certificate is held, immediately before taking up the rank

Which of these options your administration accepts, and what evidence it wants, varies. Some also attach conditions to revalidation without sea service, such as refresher courses or an oral exam.

Your Basic Training and Advanced Fire Fighting / PSCRB certificates also need refreshing. Under Chapter VI of STCW, you must provide evidence every five years that you maintain the required standard. A management-level CoC does not cover an expired PST or FPFF.

Why officers get stopped at joining

Most refusals at the gangway or the manning office are document problems, not competence problems. The common ones:

  • No flag state endorsement. Serving on a foreign flag requires that flag's recognition endorsement under STCW Regulation I/10. Regulation I/10 lets a flag allow service for up to three months on proof that an endorsement application has been submitted, but only if that flag permits it. Do not assume it does.
  • Wrong capacity or limitation. A 500–3,000 GT certificate on a 30,000 GT bulker will be refused. So will a chief mate CoC presented for a master's berth, or a near-coastal limitation on an ocean-going ship.
  • Missing tanker endorsement. A chief mate or master on an oil, chemical or gas tanker needs the relevant advanced tanker training and endorsement under STCW Regulation V/1-1 or V/1-2. The CoC alone is not enough.
  • GMDSS. Masters and deck officers on GMDSS ships need the appropriate radio operator's certificate (STCW Regulation IV/2). An expired GOC or its endorsement is a frequent miss.
  • Expired medical or Chapter VI refreshers. These are separate documents with separate expiry dates.
  • Originals not available. STCW Regulation I/2 requires the original certificate to be available on board. Scans are not enough.
  • Name or date mismatches between the CoC, passport and seaman's book.
  • Type-specific ECDIS or other company and flag requirements. These can be raised by the company under its SMS and STCW Regulation I/14, by the flag, or by the charterer's vetting criteria.

Keeping it in date: practical habits

  • Keep one expiry list covering:
    • CoC
    • Flag endorsements
    • Medical
    • GOC
    • Tanker endorsements
    • BST/AFF/PSCRB refreshers
    • Passport
    • Visas
  • Start revalidation well before expiry. Processing times vary by administration and can be long. Some only accept applications within a set window before expiry.
  • Collect sea-service evidence as you go. Get signed testimonials and discharge book entries before you sign off, not months later from the office.
  • Ask the manning agent early which flag endorsements the next ship needs, and apply before joining.
  • Carry originals plus clear copies in a waterproof folder. Keep verified scans for your own records.
  • Check online verification. Many administrations offer certificate verification. Confirm your record shows correctly, because companies and PSC use it.
Key points
  • STCW Regulation II/2 requires the CoC for chief mates and masters on seagoing ships of 500 GT or more, with separate standards above and below 3,000 GT.
  • On 3,000 GT+ ships, chief mate needs at least 12 months' approved OICNW service; master needs 36 months, reducible to 24 with 12 months as chief mate.
  • Section A-II/2 covers management-level navigation, cargo handling and stowage, and control of ship operations and care for persons on board.
  • Revalidation is under STCW Regulation I/11 at intervals not exceeding five years, proving medical fitness and continued competence.
  • Serving on a foreign flag needs that flag's endorsement under Regulation I/10; any interim service on proof of application depends on the flag.
  • Tanker endorsements, GMDSS certificates and Chapter VI refreshers are separate documents and a common cause of joining refusals.
  • Exam formats, approved course lists and evidence requirements are set by each administration, so check current national notices.

Frequently asked questions

How many months of sea service do I need for a chief mate CoC?

Under STCW Regulation II/2, a chief mate certificate for ships of 3,000 GT or more requires at least 12 months of approved sea service as OICNW. For ships of 500–3,000 GT, the candidate must meet the OICNW requirements for ships of 500 GT or more. Your administration may add conditions on how that service is counted and evidenced.

Can I sit for master directly without sailing as chief mate?

STCW allows certification as master with 36 months of approved OICNW service. That period can be reduced to 24 months if at least 12 months were served as chief mate. Many administrations, however, structure the route so you hold the chief mate certificate first, so check your national rules.

What happens if my CoC expires while I am on board?

You are no longer certificated for the capacity once the certificate expires, and PSC can detain the ship or require changes to manning. Inform the master and company immediately. Revalidation is handled by your administration, and some will not process it remotely, so plan renewal before signing on.

Do I need a separate endorsement to work on a Panama or Marshall Islands ship?

Yes. Serving on a ship flagged by a state other than the one that issued your CoC requires that flag's recognition endorsement under STCW Regulation I/10. Some flags allow up to three months' service on proof that the application was submitted, but this is the flag's decision.

Is my chief mate CoC valid on an oil tanker?

The CoC alone is not enough. A master or chief mate on an oil, chemical or gas tanker also needs the relevant advanced tanker training and endorsement under STCW Regulation V/1-1 or V/1-2, as does any other person with immediate responsibility for cargo operations. The ship's flag and the company may also have their own tanker experience requirements.

Test yourself

Under STCW Regulation II/2, what is the minimum approved sea service as OICNW for a chief mate certificate on ships of 3,000 GT or more?

Regulation II/2 requires not less than 12 months' approved sea service as OICNW for chief mate on ships of 3,000 GT or more. Reference: STCW Regulation II/2.

An officer has 26 months as OICNW, including 12 months as chief mate, on ships over 3,000 GT. Under STCW, can they be certificated as master?

The 36-month requirement may be reduced to not less than 24 months if at least 12 months were served as chief mate. Reference: STCW Regulation II/2.

How often must a master or chief mate CoC be revalidated under STCW?

Regulation I/11 requires revalidation at intervals not exceeding five years, proving medical fitness and continued professional competence. Reference: STCW Regulation I/11 and Section A-I/11.

A chief mate is joining a Liberian-flag ship, holds an Indian CoC, and has only applied for the Liberian endorsement. Which statement is correct?

Regulation I/10 allows a flag to permit service for up to three months on documentary proof that an endorsement application has been submitted. It is not automatic. Reference: STCW Regulation I/10.

Besides the II/2 CoC, what does a chief mate with responsibility for cargo on an oil tanker need?

Masters, chief mates and others with immediate responsibility for cargo operations on oil tankers need the advanced training endorsement, and basic training alone is insufficient. Reference: STCW Regulation V/1-1.
