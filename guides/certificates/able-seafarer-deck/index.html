Able Seafarer Deck: STCW II/5 Certificate of Proficiency Explained

Quick answer

Able Seafarer Deck is a certificate of proficiency issued under STCW Regulation II/5. It proves a deck rating can work at support level beyond basic watchkeeping. To qualify you must be at least 18, already meet the Rating Forming Part of a Navigational Watch (II/4) requirements, have the required approved sea service and meet the competence standard in STCW Code Section A-II/5. STCW sets no fixed expiry for it, so validity and any renewal depend on your issuing administration. Your supporting documents, such as the medical certificate and Basic Training refreshers, still have to be kept in date.

What the Able Seafarer Deck certificate is

The Able Seafarer Deck certificate was introduced into STCW by the 2010 Manila amendments, which entered into force on 1 January 2012. Before that, able seaman certification was mostly governed by national rules (and, for some countries, ILO Convention No. 74 of 1946), so standards differed from one country to the next. Now there is an international minimum standard, set out in two places:

  • STCW Regulation II/5 gives the mandatory minimum requirements for certification of ratings as able seafarer deck.
  • STCW Code Section A-II/5 sets the standard of competence: what you must know and do, and how it is assessed.

It is a certificate of proficiency (CoP), not a certificate of competency. It is issued by your national maritime administration, or by a body that administration has authorised, after you complete approved training and assessment.

Keep the job title and the certificate separate in your mind. A company can sign you on as "AB", but the safe manning document and port State control will look for the STCW qualification behind that title. The certificate shows you have been assessed as competent at support level in navigation, cargo handling, ship operation and maintenance.

Who needs it and how it fits with the watchkeeping rating

Deck ratings normally progress like this:

Step STCW basis Typical role
Basic Training Reg VI/1 Everyone with designated safety duties
Rating Forming Part of a Navigational Watch (RFPNW) Reg II/4 OS / watchkeeping rating
Able Seafarer Deck Reg II/5 AB, and the usual route towards bosun

Able Seafarer Deck builds on RFPNW. It does not replace it. Regulation II/5 requires you to meet the II/4 requirements first.

Who actually needs it on a given ship depends on two things:

  • the safe manning document, which the flag State issues for that ship;
  • the company's manning policy.

Most ocean-going manning scales call for a number of able seafarers on deck. If you are signed on as AB, expect to show the II/5 certificate.

Cadets and officers do not normally need it. The deck officer certificate of competency covers far more ground.

Eligibility: age, sea service and training

Under Regulation II/5, every candidate must:

  1. Be not less than 18 years of age.
  2. Meet the requirements for RFPNW under Regulation II/4.
  3. Have approved seagoing service in the deck department, completed while already qualified as RFPNW, of either:
    • not less than 18 months, or
    • not less than 12 months plus approved training.
  4. Meet the standard of competence in Section A-II/5.

You also need, as with any seafarer:

  • a valid medical fitness certificate under Regulation I/9;
  • valid Basic Training under Regulation VI/1.

Points that catch people out:

  • Sea service before RFPNW does not count. Months sailed before you held RFPNW do not count towards the II/5 service.
  • Each administration sets the details. It decides how sea service must be documented (discharge book entries, sea service testimonials, a record book), what the "approved training" consists of, and how assessment is run. Check the current rules with your own administration or an approved training institute before you apply.
  • Older national AB certificates. STCW allowed transitional arrangements for seafarers who already held these. Those arrangements ended on 1 January 2017. If you hold an old national AB certificate and have never been issued a II/5 CoP, ask your administration about your status.

What you are assessed on (Section A-II/5)

Section A-II/5 covers work at support level across four functions. In plain terms you must show you can:

Navigation - Contribute to a safe navigational watch. This includes steering and lookout, and goes deeper than at RFPNW level, for example communicating clearly with the OOW and understanding watch handover. - Contribute to berthing, anchoring and mooring operations, including work with tugs and towing.

Cargo handling and stowage - Contribute to handling cargo and stores safely.

Controlling the operation of the ship and care for persons on board - Operate deck equipment and machinery safely, for example winches, windlass, cranes and hatch covers. - Apply occupational health and safety precautions, including enclosed space entry and permits. - Take precautions to prevent marine pollution. - Operate survival craft and rescue boats. Many administrations expect a Proficiency in Survival Craft and Rescue Boats certificate here, so check what yours requires.

Maintenance and repair - Contribute to maintenance and repair. This covers ropes and wires, splicing, painting, lubrication, use of hand and power tools, and the safe use of the materials involved.

Competence is shown by an approved mix of training, practical demonstration, in-service experience and examination. Exactly how the evidence is gathered differs between administrations.

Validity and revalidation: it depends on the administration

This is where crew get the most confused, so here are the facts in order.

  • STCW does not require the II/5 CoP itself to be revalidated. The five-yearly revalidation rule in Regulation I/11 applies to certificates of competency for masters, officers and radio operators, and to certain CoPs for masters and officers such as tanker and Polar Code certificates. It does not apply to rating CoPs like Able Seafarer Deck.
  • Your administration can still set an expiry. Many issue the II/5 CoP with no expiry date. Others print a validity period or tie the certificate to other documents. Read what is printed on your own certificate. Do not go by what a shipmate's certificate from another country says.
  • Your supporting documents do expire. In practice these are what keep you sailing:
    • Medical certificate (Reg I/9): valid for a maximum of two years for seafarers aged 18 and over. The issuing administration or doctor may set a shorter period.
    • Basic Training (Reg VI/1): you must show you have maintained competence in Personal Survival Techniques and Fire Prevention and Fire Fighting every five years. This is usually done through refresher training. Elementary First Aid and PSSR do not carry this five-yearly requirement under STCW.
    • Other certificates you hold, such as PSCRB, tanker training or security, each follow their own rules.

Bottom line: an Able Seafarer Deck CoP without an expiry date is useless at the gangway if your medical or Basic Training refreshers have lapsed.

Why ABs get stopped at joining: worked scenarios

Scenario 1: "You're signed as AB but only hold RFPNW." Ravi has sailed as OS for two years and has been promoted on paper. He never applied for II/5. At the document check, the master or manning agent sees only the II/4 certificate. Fix: apply for II/5 as soon as your service qualifies. Do not accept an AB position without the CoP.

Scenario 2: Basic Training refresher lapsed. Jun's AB certificate has no expiry date, but his PST and FPFF refreshers ran out last month. He cannot be counted as meeting VI/1. Fix: track refresher dates, not just the AB certificate.

Scenario 3: Name mismatch. The passport shows a middle name and the CoP does not. The difference is enough for a document check, port State control or an online verification to flag it. Fix: get documents corrected or reissued before you are deployed.

Other common stops: - Certificate does not verify. The issuing administration's online register does not show it, or the number does not match. Fraudulent certificates are a known problem, so verification is taken seriously. - Only photocopies or scans carried. Carry originals unless the flag State and company tell you otherwise in writing. - Flag State requirements. Some flags have their own rules for documenting ratings' qualifications. Ask the manning agent well before deployment. - Ship-type extras missing. For example, tanker training for a tanker, or the security certificate needed for your designated duties.

Oral and assessment-style questions you should be ready for

Assessors and masters often check an AB's practical knowledge with questions like these:

  • "You're on lookout and see a red light fine on the starboard bow. What do you do?" Report it at once to the OOW with its bearing. Do not wait to be sure what it is.
  • "The helm order is 'port twenty'. What do you do and say?" Repeat the order, apply the helm, then report when it is on, for example "twenty port". Follow your ship's helm order procedure.
  • "Where is the snap-back zone during mooring, and how do you know?" Explain that the whole mooring deck can be at risk if a line parts. Painted zones are only a guide. Stay out of the bight and away from lines under tension.
  • "What must be in place before you enter a ballast tank?" A permit, ventilation, atmosphere testing, an attendant at the entrance, communications and rescue arrangements, all following the company SMS.
  • "How do you inspect a mooring rope before use?" Look for abrasion, cuts, glazing or fusing on synthetic ropes, broken strands, and damage at the eye or splice. Report anything doubtful.

Answer the way you actually work. Short, safe and procedure-based beats a long textbook recital.

Tips for keeping your AB qualification usable

  • Keep a document calendar. List the medical, PST and FPFF refreshers, PSCRB, security, any tanker or other ship-type certificates, passport, seaman's book and visas, each with its expiry. Start renewals well before the date, because course and appointment slots fill up.
  • Verify your own certificate. Check it on your administration's online register, if one exists, before every joining.
  • Carry originals in a waterproof folder, plus clean scans you can send quickly.
  • Keep sea service records tidy. Discharge book entries and testimonials are needed for II/5 now and for bosun roles or officer training later.
  • Match names and dates of birth across passport, seaman's book, INDoS or national records, and every certificate.
  • Read your certificate. Know whether your administration gave it an expiry date. Do not assume.
  • Ask before you fly. Confirm flag-specific and ship-type requirements with the manning agent before deployment, not at the gangway.
Key points
  • Able Seafarer Deck is a certificate of proficiency under STCW Regulation II/5, with the competence standard in Section A-II/5.
  • You must be at least 18 and already meet the RFPNW requirements of Regulation II/4.
  • Sea service needed is 18 months, or 12 months plus approved training, served while qualified as RFPNW.
  • STCW sets no five-yearly revalidation for the II/5 CoP, but your administration may print an expiry, so check your certificate.
  • Your medical certificate (maximum two years) and PST/FPFF refreshers (every five years) must be in date to sail.
  • Common reasons for joining refusals include holding only RFPNW, lapsed refreshers, name mismatches or certificates that will not verify.

Frequently asked questions

Is Able Seafarer Deck the same as RFPNW?

No. RFPNW (Regulation II/4) is the watchkeeping rating qualification. Able Seafarer Deck (Regulation II/5) is the next level and needs RFPNW first. Being signed on as AB normally means you are expected to hold the II/5 certificate.

How much sea service do I need for Able Seafarer Deck?

Under Regulation II/5 you need at least 18 months of approved deck seagoing service, or at least 12 months plus approved training. The service must be completed while you are already qualified as RFPNW. How it must be documented is set by your administration.

Does the Able Seafarer Deck certificate expire?

STCW does not require rating certificates of proficiency like II/5 to be revalidated every five years. Some administrations still print a validity period, so check your own certificate. Your medical and Basic Training refreshers do expire and must be kept current.

Do I need PSCRB to get Able Seafarer Deck?

Section A-II/5 includes operating survival craft and rescue boats. Many administrations therefore expect a PSCRB certificate as evidence of that competence. Requirements differ, so confirm with your national administration or an approved training institute.

Can I sail as AB on a foreign-flagged ship with my national II/5 certificate?

Generally your II/5 CoP is the qualification the manning scale relies on. Some flag States have their own documentation requirements for ratings, however. Confirm the flag's rules with your manning agent before deployment.

Test yourself

Which STCW regulation covers certification of ratings as able seafarer deck?

Able Seafarer Deck is covered by Regulation II/5. II/4 is RFPNW, VI/1 is Basic Training and II/1 is the OOW certificate of competency (STCW Regulation II/5).

What is the minimum age for an Able Seafarer Deck certificate under STCW?

Regulation II/5 requires candidates to be not less than 18 years of age (STCW Regulation II/5).

Which sea service counts towards the Able Seafarer Deck requirement?

The 18 months, or 12 months plus approved training, must be deck service completed while qualified as RFPNW (STCW Regulation II/5).

An AB's II/5 certificate has no expiry date. What can still stop him signing on?

Regulation I/11 revalidation does not apply to rating CoPs. Expired Basic Training refreshers or an expired medical certificate will still stop him (STCW Regulations VI/1 and I/9).

What is the maximum validity of a seafarer medical certificate for someone aged 18 or over under STCW?

Under Regulation I/9 the medical certificate is valid for a maximum of two years for seafarers aged 18 and over, or one year if under 18 (STCW Regulation I/9).
