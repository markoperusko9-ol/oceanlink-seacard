Certificate of Competency (CoC) – Officer in Charge of a Navigational Watch

Quick answer

The Officer in Charge of a Navigational Watch (OOW) CoC is the first deck officer certificate under STCW Regulation II/1. You need it to keep a bridge watch on seagoing ships of 500 GT or more. It is issued by your national maritime administration after approved sea service, training and examination. It must be revalidated at intervals of no more than five years, and on a foreign-flag ship it needs a flag state endorsement.

What the OOW CoC is and who needs it

The OOW certificate is the operational-level deck licence under STCW. It allows you to be the officer in charge of a navigational watch on seagoing ships of 500 GT or more. Different administrations give it different local names:

  • India: Second Mate (Foreign Going), issued after DG Shipping / MMD examinations.
  • Philippines: MARINA issues it as Officer in Charge of a Navigational Watch.

The rank on board and the certificate are separate things. Any officer standing a navigational watch in charge needs at least this CoC. That is typically a 3rd or 2nd Officer, and a 4th Officer where the company assigns independent watches.

Two parties are responsible for checking it:

  • The company. Under STCW Regulation I/14 it must ensure every seafarer it assigns holds the appropriate certificate.
  • Port State Control. It can check certificates under Regulation I/4.

Ships of less than 500 GT fall under a separate regulation, II/3, which has specific provisions for ships engaged on near-coastal voyages. Many administrations issue restricted certificates under it, limited by tonnage or trading area. Read the limitations printed on any CoC before assuming it covers a particular ship.

Legal basis: STCW, the Code and MLC 2006

The certificate rests on several instruments.

  • STCW Regulation II/1 sets the minimum requirements for OOW certification on ships of 500 GT or more.
  • STCW Code Section A-II/1 contains the competence tables. They are grouped under three functions at operational level:
    • navigation
    • cargo handling and stowage
    • controlling the operation of the ship and care for persons on board
  • Regulation I/2 covers issue and endorsement of certificates. It also requires originals to be available on board the ship you are serving in.
  • Regulation I/9 covers medical standards.
  • Regulation I/11 covers revalidation.
  • Regulation I/10 covers recognition of another country's certificate by a flag state.
  • Section A-VIII/2 sets out the watchkeeping principles the certificate entitles you to apply.
  • MLC 2006:
    • Regulation 1.3 requires seafarers to be trained or certified as competent for their duties.
    • Regulation 1.2 requires a valid medical certificate.
    • Regulation 2.3 deals with hours of work and rest, alongside STCW Section A-VIII/1.

Requirements to obtain it

Regulation II/1 sets the international minimum. Your administration adds its own detail on syllabus, exam format and documents.

Requirement STCW minimum
Age Not less than 18
Sea service (structured route) At least 12 months as part of an approved training programme, with onboard training documented in an approved training record book
Sea service (alternative route) At least 36 months of approved seagoing service
Supervised bridge watchkeeping At least 6 months within the required service, under the master or a qualified officer
Radio Meet the Chapter IV requirements for GMDSS radio duties (normally a GOC)
Competence Approved education and training plus assessment against Section A-II/1
Safety training Standards in A-VI/1 para 2, A-VI/2 paras 1–4, A-VI/3 paras 1–4 and A-VI/4 paras 1–3
Medical Fitness meeting Regulation I/9

In practice the safety training row means:

  • Basic Training
  • PSCRB
  • Advanced Fire Fighting
  • Medical First Aid

What the course and exams cover (paraphrasing A-II/1):

  • passage planning and position fixing
  • watchkeeping and COLREG
  • radar/ARPA and ECDIS
  • bridge resource management
  • compasses and steering control
  • meteorology
  • emergency and distress response
  • ship manoeuvring
  • cargo handling and monitoring
  • stability and construction basics
  • pollution prevention
  • fire and LSA
  • medical first aid
  • legislative requirements
  • leadership and teamwork

Administrations also differ on:

  • whether separate ECDIS or radar course certificates must be presented
  • the number and type of written and oral exams
  • how sea service is verified

Check your own administration's current notices. Do not rely on what a batch-mate did several years ago.

Validity and revalidation

Administrations issue the CoC with a validity not exceeding five years. Under Regulation I/11, at intervals of no more than five years you must show:

  • continued medical fitness, and
  • continued professional competence as set out in Section A-I/11.

Section A-I/11 gives several ways to prove continued competence:

  • at least 12 months in total of approved seagoing service performing the functions of the certificate during the preceding five years
  • performing functions the administration considers equivalent
  • passing an approved test
  • completing approved course(s)
  • at least three months of approved seagoing service in a supernumerary capacity, or in a lower officer rank, immediately before taking up the rank the certificate allows

Which of these routes your administration accepts, and the paperwork it wants, is a national decision.

Separate items also have their own refresher or revalidation cycles:

  • Basic Training, PSCRB and Advanced Fire Fighting refreshers
  • GMDSS endorsement
  • tanker endorsements

A valid CoC does not help if your firefighting refresher has lapsed. Track every expiry date, not just the CoC.

Worked scenario. Ravi's OOW CoC expires in four months. In the last five years he has only 8 months of sea service as OOW, because he spent two years ashore.

  • He does not meet the 12-month service route.
  • His options depend on what DG Shipping (or his administration) accepts. These might be an approved test or course, or sailing supernumerary or in a lower officer rank for three months before stepping back up.
  • He should apply early. Revalidation processing, medical renewal and any required refreshers all take time, and a lapsed certificate can cost him a joining date.

Serving on a foreign flag: endorsements

Your national CoC is not enough by itself on a ship flying another flag. Under Regulation I/10 that flag state must recognise your certificate and issue its own endorsement. Points to know:

  • The flag state can only recognise certificates from administrations it has assessed and accepted.
  • The endorsement cannot outlast your national CoC. Once the CoC expires, the endorsement goes with it.
  • Temporary service: many flag states issue a receipt or confirmation that your endorsement application has been submitted (often called a CRA). I/10 allows the flag to let you serve on that basis for up to three months, but whether it does is the flag state's decision.
  • GMDSS radio endorsements follow the radio regulations and can be handled separately. Check that the endorsement covers the GOC as well as the deck CoC.

If you move to a ship under a different flag, you will need an endorsement from that flag. Do not assume your old Panama or Liberia endorsement covers a Marshall Islands ship.

Why officers get stopped at joining

These are the problems that most often delay a joining:

  • Expired or soon-to-expire CoC. Some companies will not send you if expiry falls during the contract, even though the law only requires validity while you serve.
  • No flag endorsement or CRA for the actual flag of the ship, or one issued for the wrong capacity.
  • Limitations on the CoC. Examples include near-coastal, tonnage or trading-area limits. Some administrations have printed limitations relating to ECDIS where training was not evidenced.
  • Expired medical certificate, or a medical carrying restrictions such as a requirement for spectacles that you are not carrying.
  • Lapsed safety refreshers. Basic Training, PSCRB or AFF may have expired while the CoC is still valid.
  • GMDSS GOC or its endorsement expired.
  • Missing tanker or gas endorsements for the ship type.
  • ECDIS type-specific familiarisation not evidenced, where the company SMS requires it before you take a watch.
  • Name or passport mismatch between the CoC, passport, seaman's book and visa.
  • Only copies carried. Originals must be available on board.
  • Certificate cannot be verified online. Many administrations run verification systems, and unverifiable documents raise fraud concerns.

Oral exam style: questions examiners like

Q: What does your OOW certificate allow you to do? It allows you to be in charge of a navigational watch on seagoing ships within the limits printed on it. The master can still give standing and night orders, and you call the master in line with them.

Q: Your relief comes to the bridge and appears unfit, perhaps tired or smelling of alcohol. What do you do? Do not hand over. Under A-VIII/2 the watch is not handed over if the relieving officer is not capable of carrying out the duties effectively. Inform the master.

Q: When should you not hand over the watch, apart from fitness? While a manoeuvre or other action to avoid a hazard is in progress. Wait until that action is completed.

Q: How do you revalidate? Give the I/11 / A-I/11 routes, then explain how your own administration applies them.

Q: You are joining a foreign-flag ship. What extra document do you need? The flag state endorsement under I/10, or documentary proof that you have applied for it if the flag allows.

Examiners want regulation-backed answers, then the practical action. "I would inform the master and log it" beats a recited paragraph.

Keeping it in date: practical tips

  • Keep a single expiry list covering:
    • CoC
    • flag endorsements
    • GOC and its endorsement
    • medical
    • Basic Training, PSCRB, AFF and MFA
    • tanker endorsements
    • passport
    • visas
  • Set reminders well ahead of each expiry.
  • Collect sea service testimonials and discharge entries before you sign off. Chasing a signature from a previous master later is painful.
  • Count your OOW service against the rolling five-year window. Do this especially if you take long shore breaks or sail in non-watchkeeping roles.
  • Book refreshers in the same leave as the CoC revalidation so the dates line up.
  • Carry originals, keep scanned copies, and check your certificate shows on your administration's verification portal.
  • Before joining, ask the manning agent for the ship's flag and type. Confirm your endorsements match both.
Key points
  • The OOW CoC is issued under STCW Regulation II/1 for watchkeeping on ships of 500 GT or more.
  • STCW minimums: age 18, 12 months structured or 36 months alternative sea service, and 6 months supervised bridge watchkeeping.
  • GMDSS (normally GOC), Basic Training, PSCRB, AFF and Medical First Aid are prerequisites.
  • Revalidate at intervals not exceeding five years under Regulation I/11; accepted routes and paperwork depend on your administration.
  • Foreign-flag service needs a flag state endorsement under Regulation I/10, which cannot outlast the CoC.
  • Joining is often delayed by lapsed endorsements, refreshers or medicals even when the CoC itself is valid.
  • Originals must be available on board; copies alone are not enough.

Frequently asked questions

Is Second Mate (FG) the same as the OOW CoC?

Yes. In India the Second Mate (Foreign Going) certificate is the national form of the STCW Regulation II/1 OOW certificate. Other administrations use different titles for the same STCW level. Always check any limitations printed on the certificate itself.

How long is the OOW CoC valid?

Administrations issue it for no more than five years. Under STCW Regulation I/11 you must revalidate it at intervals not exceeding five years by showing medical fitness and continued professional competence. The exact process is set by your administration.

How much sea time do I need to revalidate my CoC?

One route under Section A-I/11 is at least 12 months in total of approved seagoing service in the preceding five years, performing the functions of the certificate. If you lack this, there are alternatives such as an approved test, approved courses, or three months supernumerary or in a lower officer rank. Your administration decides which of these it accepts.

Can I join a foreign-flag ship before the flag endorsement is issued?

Often yes, if the flag state allows it. Regulation I/10 lets a flag permit service for up to three months with documentary proof that the endorsement application has been submitted. Whether this is allowed, and in what form, depends on the flag state.

Does my CoC cover ships under 500 GT or near-coastal trade?

An unrestricted II/1 certificate generally covers smaller ships as well. Restricted certificates issued under Regulation II/3, or certificates with tonnage or area limitations, only cover what is printed on them. Read the capacity and limitations on both your CoC and your flag endorsement.

Test yourself

What is the minimum age for an OOW certificate under STCW Regulation II/1?

Regulation II/1 requires candidates to be not less than 18 years of age. STCW Regulation II/1.

How often must an OOW revalidate their certificate to keep serving at sea?

Regulation I/11 requires revalidation of medical fitness and professional competence at intervals not exceeding five years. STCW Regulation I/11.

Which of these satisfies the sea-service route for revalidation under Section A-I/11?

Section A-I/11 accepts at least 12 months in total of approved seagoing service in the preceding five years. STCW Code, Section A-I/11.

An Indian OOW is joining a Liberian-flag ship. What additional certification is required under STCW?

Regulation I/10 requires the flag state to recognise and endorse a certificate issued by another party. STCW Regulation I/10.

Within the required sea service for the OOW CoC, how long must supervised bridge watchkeeping be?

Regulation II/1 requires at least six months of bridge watchkeeping under the supervision of the master or a qualified officer. STCW Regulation II/1.
