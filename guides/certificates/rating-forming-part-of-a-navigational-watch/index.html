Rating Forming Part of a Navigational Watch (STCW II/4): What It Is and How to Keep It Valid

Quick answer

The 'Rating Forming Part of a Navigational Watch' qualification is the STCW Regulation II/4 certificate of proficiency. Any deck rating (other than a rating under training) who steers, keeps lookout or otherwise forms part of the bridge watch on a seagoing ship of 500 GT or more must hold it. You need to be at least 16, complete approved sea service or special training, and meet the competences in STCW Code Table A-II/4. Whether the document carries an expiry date depends on the issuing administration, but your Basic Safety Training refreshers and your medical always expire, and lapsed ones stop you at joining.

What the II/4 certificate is and who needs it

Regulation II/4 of the STCW Convention sets the minimum requirements for ratings who form part of a navigational watch. In practice that means the OS or AB who:

  • takes the wheel,
  • keeps lookout on the bridge or bridge wing, or
  • forms part of the bridge team during the watch.

The regulation applies on seagoing ships of 500 GT or more. Two groups are excluded:

  • Ratings under training. A trainee rating standing a watch under supervision does not need it yet. Deck cadets are trainee officers covered by their own approved training programme.
  • Ratings whose duties are unskilled. A rating who never forms part of the watch is not covered.

On ships under 500 GT, the requirement comes from the flag's national rules, not from II/4 itself.

Since the 2010 Manila amendments, STCW calls the document a certificate of proficiency (CoP). Administrations still title it in different ways, for example 'Navigational Watch Rating', 'Watchkeeping Certificate (Deck)' or 'CoP – Rating forming part of a navigational watch'. The title matters less than the reference printed on it. It should show STCW Regulation II/4.

II/4 is also the foundation for Able Seafarer Deck (Regulation II/5). You must already meet II/4 before you can qualify at II/5. Many ships' minimum safe manning documents call for II/5 ABs, so II/4 alone may not get you an AB billet.

Requirements to qualify

Under Regulation II/4, a candidate must:

  1. Be not less than 16 years of age.
  2. Have completed one of the following:
    • approved seagoing service including not less than six months of training and experience, or
    • special training, either pre-sea or on board, which includes approved seagoing service of not less than two months.
  3. Meet the standard of competence in STCW Code Section A-II/4.

The sea service must relate to navigational watchkeeping duties. It must be done under the direct supervision of the master, the officer of the watch or a qualified rating. Months spent chipping and painting with no bridge time do not meet the intent, and a careful assessor will ask about it.

You also need:

  • a valid medical fitness certificate under Regulation I/9, including the eyesight standard for deck watchkeepers;
  • Basic Safety Training under Section A-VI/1;
  • security training under Section A-VI/6. This is Ship Security Awareness, or Designated Security Duties if you will keep gangway or security watches.

How this works in India and the Philippines. The route usually runs through an approved pre-sea rating course and onboard service, with the certificate issued by DG Shipping or MARINA respectively. Both administrations revise their schemes from time to time. Check the current circular or memorandum before you pay for any course, and only use institutes the administration has approved.

What the competence standard covers

Table A-II/4 places the qualification at the support level under the function Navigation. It has four competences:

Competence What you must be able to do
Steer the ship and comply with helm orders, also in English Use the magnetic and gyro compass. Follow and repeat back helm orders. Change over between autopilot and hand steering.
Keep a proper lookout by sight and hearing Detect sound signals, lights and other objects. Report their approximate bearing in degrees or points.
Contribute to monitoring and controlling a safe watch Use shipboard terms and definitions for the watch. Communicate with the OOW. Hand over and take over the watch correctly.
Operate emergency equipment and apply emergency procedures Know your emergency duties and alarm signals. Know the pyrotechnic distress signals, the satellite EPIRB and the SART. Avoid false distress alerts and know what to do if one is sent.

The English-language requirement for helm orders is real. Pilots in most ports give orders in English, and the helmsman must understand them and repeat them back accurately. The IMO Standard Marine Communication Phrases (SMCP) give the standard wording, and learning them is time well spent.

Validity and revalidation: what depends on the administration

STCW's revalidation rule, Regulation I/11, applies to masters, officers and radio operators (including masters' and officers' continued competence for tankers).

It does not set a five-year revalidation cycle for the II/4 rating certificate. Many administrations therefore issue II/4 without an expiry date. Do not assume yours is one of them. Some administrations print an expiry, attach conditions, or link validity to your continuous discharge certificate. Read the face of your certificate and the issuing administration's rules.

Other documents must stay valid for you to sail as a watch rating, even if the II/4 itself never expires:

  • Medical certificate. Under STCW and MLC 2006 its validity is normally capped at two years, and less for seafarers under 18. Some flags set shorter periods.
  • Basic Safety Training. You must show evidence of maintaining competence in Personal Survival Techniques and Fire Prevention and Fire Fighting every five years. That means a refresher, done in the way your administration specifies.
  • Passport and seaman's book (CDC).
  • Flag-state documents, where the flag requires them. STCW's recognition-endorsement process in Regulation I/10 is aimed at officers' certificates of competency. Some flags still ask for their own seaman's book or a form of acceptance for ratings. Your manning agent should confirm this before you fly.

Why ratings get stopped at joining

These are the problems that most often cause a sign-on refusal, a delay, or a port state control deficiency:

  • Expired BST refreshers. The II/4 is fine, but the PST or FPFF evidence is more than five years old.
  • Medical expired, or expires mid-contract without a plan for renewal.
  • Name or date-of-birth mismatch between the certificate, passport and CDC. A missing middle name or a swapped surname order is enough to cause trouble.
  • Certificate cannot be verified. PSC officers and companies increasingly check online registers. Certificates from unapproved or fraudulent institutes get caught, and holding one can cost you your career.
  • Wrong level for the billet. The safe manning document calls for an AB at II/5 and you only hold II/4.
  • Copies only. Under STCW, the original certificate must be carried on board when you serve.
  • Security training missing for a rating assigned to gangway or security duties.
  • Under-age issues. Seafarers under 18 have night-work restrictions under MLC 2006. A 17-year-old may hold II/4 but may not be rostered on some watches.

At port state control, the inspector may ask the lookout or helmsman to show the certificate, or to change over steering modes. Being unable to do a basic task can lead to a competence-related deficiency against the ship.

Worked scenario: night lookout report

It is the 0000–0400 watch in open water and you are lookout on the starboard wing. You see a white masthead light with a green sidelight below it, roughly 30° on the starboard bow.

  1. Report immediately, even if you think the OOW has already seen it. For example: 'Light, white over green, about three points on the starboard bow.'
  2. Wait for acknowledgement. If the OOW does not respond, report again. Do not assume.
  3. Keep watching it. Report any change: a second masthead light opening, a different sidelight showing, or a change in bearing.
  4. Do not leave the lookout post unless you are properly relieved.

The point of the scenario is this: under Section A-VIII/2, the lookout's job is to look out and report. Doing other tasks while on lookout takes your attention off that job. Under COLREG Rule 5, keeping a proper lookout is an obligation of the ship, and you are the person carrying it out.

Oral and practical check: what assessors ask

Expect these questions from an assessor, from the master when you join, or from a PSC officer:

  • 'Starboard twenty.' What do you do? Repeat the order back, apply the wheel, then report 'wheel starboard twenty' when the rudder indicator confirms it.
  • What does 'Midships' mean, and what does 'Steady' mean? 'Midships' means rudder amidships. 'Steady' means reduce the swing as rapidly as possible. 'Steady as she goes' means steer a steady course on the compass heading at the time of the order; repeat it back with the heading, e.g. 'Steady as she goes, course 095'.
  • How do you change from autopilot to hand steering? Show the selector, and know the bridge's own procedure.
  • Where is emergency steering, and how do you communicate from the steering flat?
  • What are the general alarm and the abandon ship signal on this ship?
  • What do you do if you see someone fall overboard? Shout 'Man overboard' and say which side. Throw a lifebuoy with light and smoke. Keep pointing at the person. Raise the alarm.
  • What are the hours of rest limits? At least 10 hours in any 24 hours and 77 hours in any 7 days, under Regulation VIII/1 and MLC 2006. Flags may allow limited exceptions within the Convention.

Practise saying helm and lookout reports out loud in English. Hesitating over wording is the most common reason a capable helmsman gets marked down.

Tips for keeping your documents in date

  • Keep one list of expiry dates covering your medical, BST refresher, passport, CDC, visas and any flag documents. Plan renewals for your leave, not your last week before joining.
  • Book BST refreshers early. Places fill up, and some administrations only accept refreshers from specific approved centres.
  • Get your sea service signed properly in your CDC and record book. This includes discharge entries, rank and GT. You will need it for II/5 and for any later move towards officer certification.
  • Check your name on every new certificate before you leave the institute.
  • Verify your certificates online where your administration offers it. Keep scanned copies in the cloud, but carry the originals.
  • Ask your manning agent what the flag of your next ship requires for ratings. Do this before travel, not at the gangway.
Key points
  • The II/4 certificate is the STCW certificate of proficiency for ratings who steer or keep lookout on seagoing ships of 500 GT or more.
  • Minimum age is 16, with either six months' approved watchkeeping-related service or special training plus at least two months at sea.
  • The four competences are steering (with helm orders in English), lookout, contributing to a safe watch, and emergency procedures.
  • STCW does not set a five-year revalidation for II/4, but some administrations print an expiry, so check your own certificate.
  • Your medical and five-yearly BST refresher evidence must be valid, or the II/4 alone will not get you aboard.
  • II/4 is the prerequisite for Able Seafarer Deck (II/5), which many safe manning documents require for AB billets.
  • Carry originals, make sure names match across all documents, and only use administration-approved training institutes.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Rating Forming Part of a Navigational Watch certificate expire?

STCW Regulation I/11 does not apply a five-year revalidation to the II/4 rating certificate, so many administrations issue it without an expiry date. Some administrations do set an expiry or conditions, so read your certificate and your administration's rules. Your medical and Basic Safety Training refresher evidence do expire, and both are needed to sail.

What is the difference between II/4 and Able Seafarer Deck (II/5)?

II/4 qualifies you to form part of a navigational watch at support level. For II/5 Able Seafarer Deck you must already meet II/4, be at least 18, and have either at least 18 months' approved deck seagoing service, or at least 12 months' approved seagoing service plus approved training. II/5 also adds wider deck competences such as mooring, cargo work and maintenance. Many ships' safe manning documents specify II/5 for AB positions.

Can a deck cadet keep lookout without a II/4 certificate?

Cadets are trainee officers, not ratings, so II/4 does not apply to them. A cadet may keep lookout under supervision as part of approved onboard training, but must not be counted as the qualified watch rating for safe manning purposes.

Do I need a flag state endorsement for my II/4 certificate?

STCW's recognition-endorsement process under Regulation I/10 is aimed at masters', officers' and radio operators' certificates of competency, not ratings' certificates. Some flags still require their own seaman's book or other documents for ratings. Confirm the requirements for your next ship's flag with your manning agent before you travel.

Is the II/4 certificate valid on ships under 500 GT?

Regulation II/4 itself applies to seagoing ships of 500 GT or more. On smaller ships, the watch rating requirements come from the flag's national rules. Many flags accept or require II/4, but check with the administration concerned.

Test yourself

Which STCW regulation sets the requirements for ratings forming part of a navigational watch?

Regulation II/4 covers navigational watch ratings. II/1 covers OOW officers, II/5 covers Able Seafarer Deck and VI/1 covers basic safety training. Reference: STCW 1978 as amended, Regulation II/4.

Under the special training route in Regulation II/4, what is the minimum approved seagoing service required?

Special training, pre-sea or on board, must include approved seagoing service of not less than two months. Six months applies to the service-only route. Reference: STCW Regulation II/4.

Above what size does Regulation II/4 apply to watch ratings on seagoing ships?

II/4 applies to ratings forming part of a navigational watch on seagoing ships of 500 GT or more. Smaller ships fall under national requirements. Reference: STCW Regulation II/4.

Which statement about II/4 validity is correct?

Regulation I/11 revalidation covers masters, officers, radio operators and masters' and officers' tanker competence, not II/4. Administrations may still impose an expiry. Reference: STCW Regulation I/11; STCW Code Section A-VI/1.

The pilot orders 'Port ten'. What should the helmsman do first?

Helm orders are repeated back before execution so that errors are caught, and then confirmed once the rudder indicator shows the angle ordered. Reference: STCW Code Table A-II/4; IMO SMCP, Resolution A.918(22).
