Basic Training for Liquefied Gas Tanker Cargo Operations: A Seafarer's Guide

Quick answer

Basic Training for Liquefied Gas Tanker Cargo Operations is the STCW qualification required under Regulation V/1-2 for officers and ratings who have specific cargo duties on LNG, LPG and other liquefied gas carriers. You must already hold STCW basic safety training. You then qualify either through an approved course or through approved gas tanker sea service, depending on what your administration accepts. Your administration decides the format, validity and revalidation rules, so check with them and with your flag before you sign a gas carrier contract.

What this certificate is

This is the entry-level gas tanker qualification under STCW. It shows you have the minimum knowledge to work safely around liquefied gas cargo. That means you understand the hazards, the cargo systems in outline and your part in an emergency.

It applies to ships carrying liquefied gases in bulk under the IGC Code, such as:

  • LNG carriers
  • LPG carriers
  • ethylene and ammonia carriers
  • other gas carriers

It does not cover oil or chemical tankers. Those need their own tanker training under Regulation V/1-1.

Administrations issue it in different forms:

  • a stand-alone certificate of proficiency
  • an endorsement on another document

Some administrations use other names, such as dangerous cargo endorsement (DCE). Older certificates may be titled tanker familiarisation. Check which level your document represents, basic or advanced. Also check whether it meets the current STCW standard.

Where the requirement comes from

The requirement sits in the STCW Convention and Code:

  • STCW Regulation V/1-2 sets the minimum training and qualification requirements for masters, officers and ratings on liquefied gas tankers.
  • STCW Code, Section A-V/1-2 gives the detail. Table A-V/1-2-1 lists the competences for basic training. Table A-V/1-2-2 covers the advanced level.
  • Regulation I/11 and Section A-I/11 cover continued professional competence for masters and officers serving on tankers.
  • Regulation I/14 makes the company responsible for ship-specific familiarisation. The certificate does not replace your familiarisation on joining.

Under Regulation V/1-2, a candidate must first have completed STCW basic training (Section A-VI/1). They must then meet the basic gas tanker standard of competence by one of two routes:

Route What it involves
Sea service At least three months of approved seagoing service on liquefied gas tankers
Course An approved basic training course for liquefied gas tanker cargo operations

In practice, many administrations and most gas operators expect the shore course even where the sea-service route exists on paper. Do not assume your gas carrier time alone will be converted. Ask your administration what it actually accepts.

The MLC 2006 does not set this qualification. It is an STCW matter.

Who needs it, and when you need the advanced level

Basic training is required for officers and ratings who are assigned specific duties and responsibilities related to cargo or cargo equipment on a liquefied gas tanker. On a typical gas carrier that includes:

  • deck officers
  • engineers
  • the ETO
  • cargo engineers
  • ABs and pumpmen/cargo ratings
  • motormen and fitters who work on cargo machinery

Crew without cargo duties, such as catering staff, may not strictly need it under STCW. However, many gas operators require it of every crew member as a company policy. At joining, you will be checked against the company SMS and the manning agency's checklist.

Advanced training (Table A-V/1-2-2) is required for:

  • masters
  • chief engineer officers
  • chief mates
  • second engineer officers
  • anyone with immediate responsibility for loading, discharging, care in transit, handling, tank cleaning or other cargo-related operations

The advanced level builds on the basic one. Candidates need:

  • the basic qualification
  • qualifying gas tanker experience, either sea service or supervised onboard training with a set number of loading and unloading operations
  • an approved advanced course

Worked scenario: Ramon is an AB holding Basic Training for Oil and Chemical Tanker Cargo Operations. He is offered a contract on an LPG carrier. His oil/chem certificate does not satisfy Regulation V/1-2. He needs gas tanker basic training before joining. He must also check that it is issued or recognised as his administration and the flag require. His oil/chem qualification stays valid for oil and chemical tankers. It simply does not transfer to gas.

What the course covers

Table A-V/1-2-1 groups the competences under six headings. An approved course will cover at least the following.

Contribute to safe cargo operation

  • types of gas carrier and cargo containment systems
  • general arrangement
  • cargo piping, valves, pumps and compressors
  • loading, discharging and care in transit
  • the emergency shutdown (ESD) system
  • drying, inerting, purging, gassing-up and gas-freeing
  • basic physics:
    • pressure and temperature
    • the vapour pressure/temperature relationship
    • why liquefied gases boil off

Take precautions to prevent hazards

The main hazards are:

  • flammability and explosion
  • toxicity and reactivity
  • corrosion
  • extremely low temperatures
  • pressure
  • electrostatic charge
  • vapour clouds

The controls taught include:

  • inerting and atmosphere control
  • gas detection
  • ventilation and segregation
  • inhibition of certain cargoes
  • cargo compatibility

You will also learn to read a safety data sheet (SDS/MSDS).

Occupational health and safety

  • gas measuring instruments
  • enclosed space entry
  • precautions for hot and cold work
  • electrical safety
  • PPE
  • first aid for cargo exposure, including cold burns

Fire-fighting on a gas carrier

  • tanker fire-fighting organisation and action to be taken
  • special hazards of liquefied gas cargoes
  • fire-fighting agents for gas fires
  • fixed dry chemical powder system operations
  • basic knowledge of spill containment in relation to fire-fighting

Respond to emergencies

  • emergency procedures, including ESD activation and your muster role

Prevent pollution

  • basic measures in the event of spillage, leakage or venting of cargo

The administration and the approved training provider set the course duration, format and assessment. Check those details with them, not with a crewmate's old certificate.

Validity and revalidation

This is where practice varies most. STCW allows each administration to issue and manage these qualifications in its own way.

For masters and officers, Regulation I/11 requires you to establish continued professional competence for tankers at intervals not exceeding five years. Section A-I/11 allows two ways to do this:

  • at least three months in total of approved tanker service in the preceding five years, in duties appropriate to the tanker qualification held, or
  • an approved relevant training course

Many administrations therefore print a five-year expiry on officers' tanker endorsements and tie it to the CoC revalidation cycle.

For ratings, administrations differ:

  • Some put an expiry date on the certificate of proficiency.
  • Some issue it without one.
  • Some link revalidation to gas tanker sea service.

Read what is printed on your document and confirm the rule with the issuing administration.

Three practical points:

  • If you have been off gas carriers for years, expect the company to ask for refresher training even if the certificate shows no expiry.
  • Revalidating your CoC does not automatically revalidate your tanker endorsement unless your administration handles them together.
  • Your basic safety training (A-VI/1) must also be in date. An expired PST or FPFF can stop you joining even when the gas certificate is valid.

Common reasons crew get stopped at joining

Manning agents and masters see the same problems at joining, and port state control inspectors pick them up later on board:

  • Wrong tanker type. The seafarer holds oil/chem training only and is joining a gas carrier.
  • Basic held, advanced needed. The seafarer has been promoted to chief mate or second engineer, or appointed cargo engineer, without the advanced qualification.
  • Course certificate only. Some administrations require the training institute's course certificate to be converted into an administration-issued certificate of proficiency or endorsement. Turning up with the course certificate alone is not enough.
  • Flag requirements not met. Officers' CoCs need a flag state endorsement. Some flags also want to see the tanker qualification reflected or recognised. Check the flag's requirements, not just your home administration's.
  • Expired or lapsed. This applies to the gas qualification itself or to the underlying basic safety training.
  • Online verification fails. The certificate number does not show on the administration's or institute's verification system, or the institute was not approved when the course was done.
  • Name or date mismatch. The certificate does not match the passport or seaman's book exactly.
  • Originals left at home. Many companies and flags want originals, or certified copies, on board.

Oral exam style questions

Surveyors and company interviewers like to test whether the knowledge stuck. Typical questions:

  • What does Regulation V/1-2 require before basic gas training? Completed STCW basic training under A-VI/1.
  • Why do liquefied gases boil off in transit? Heat leaks into the tank through the insulation. The cargo stays at, or close to, its boiling point for the tank pressure, so heat input produces vapour.
  • LPG leak on deck: where does the vapour go? Propane and butane vapours are heavier than air. They collect at low points and can drift towards ignition sources. Cold LNG vapour also initially hugs the deck until it warms.
  • What happens to skin or ordinary steel in contact with LNG? LNG is at about minus 162°C at atmospheric pressure. It causes cold burns on skin. It can make ordinary hull steel brittle and cause it to crack. That is why drip trays and water curtains are used.
  • What is ESD and who can activate it? The emergency shutdown system stops cargo transfer and closes the shutdown valves. Know your ship's activation points and procedure.
  • Why inert before gassing-up? To remove oxygen so that a flammable mixture never forms when cargo vapour is introduced.

Tips for keeping it in date

  • Keep a document tracker. Record the expiry dates of the gas qualification, basic safety training, CoC, medical and endorsements together. Look at it before every contract, not at the airport.
  • Log your gas tanker sea service carefully. Get it signed and stamped in your seaman's book or CDC. Officers can use three months' tanker service in five years to revalidate, but only if the service is properly documented.
  • Plan the advanced course early if you are aiming for promotion. You need qualifying gas tanker experience first, so build it in while serving at basic level.
  • Use approved providers only. Check the institute's approval with your administration before paying.
  • Verify online after issue, and save a screenshot of the verification page.
  • Check the flag's requirements whenever you change company or ship registry.
Key points
  • Required under STCW Regulation V/1-2 and Section A-V/1-2 (Table A-V/1-2-1) for crew with cargo duties on liquefied gas tankers.
  • STCW basic safety training (A-VI/1) must be completed first.
  • Oil and chemical tanker training does not cover gas carriers. STCW treats gas tanker basic training (V/1-2) as meeting IGF Code basic training (V/3), but not the reverse.
  • Masters, chief engineers, chief mates, second engineers and anyone with immediate cargo responsibility need the advanced level.
  • Officers revalidate tanker competence within five years by three months' tanker service or an approved course; rules for ratings vary by administration.
  • Most joining refusals come from wrong tanker type, missing advanced level, unconverted course certificates or lapsed underlying certificates.

Frequently asked questions

Is basic oil and chemical tanker training enough to join an LPG or LNG carrier?

No. Oil and chemical tanker training falls under STCW Regulation V/1-1. Gas carriers need the separate liquefied gas tanker training under Regulation V/1-2. You need the gas qualification before joining in a role with cargo duties.

Can I get gas tanker basic training through sea service instead of a course?

STCW allows at least three months of approved seagoing service on liquefied gas tankers as an alternative to the course, provided you meet the standard of competence. Many administrations and operators still require the approved shore course. Confirm with your administration what it accepts before relying on sea service.

Does the gas tanker basic training certificate expire?

It depends on the issuing administration. Officers must establish continued tanker competence at intervals not exceeding five years under Regulation I/11. Some administrations print expiry dates on ratings' certificates and some do not. Check your document and your administration's rules.

What is the difference between gas tanker basic training and IGF Code basic training?

Gas tanker training under Regulation V/1-2 is for ships carrying liquefied gas as cargo under the IGC Code. IGF Code training under Regulation V/3 is for ships using gas or other low-flashpoint fuels, such as an LNG-fuelled container ship. Under Regulation V/3, holders of V/1-2 gas tanker training are considered to meet the IGF Code basic training requirement. IGF Code training does not qualify you for cargo duties on a gas tanker, so confirm how your administration documents this.

Do I need advanced gas tanker training as a second engineer on an LNG carrier?

Yes. STCW requires the advanced level for masters, chief engineer officers, chief mates, second engineer officers and anyone with immediate responsibility for cargo operations. You need the basic qualification, qualifying gas tanker experience and the approved advanced course.

Test yourself

Which STCW regulation sets the training requirements for liquefied gas tanker crews?

Regulation V/1-2 covers liquefied gas tankers. V/1-1 covers oil and chemical tankers, V/2 covers passenger ships and V/3 covers IGF Code ships (STCW Regulation V/1-2).

What must a candidate have completed before gas tanker basic training can be issued?

Regulation V/1-2 requires completed STCW basic training (A-VI/1). Oil/chem training, a CoC or AFF are not prerequisites (STCW Regulation V/1-2).

A container ship burns LNG as fuel but carries no gas cargo. Which training applies to its crew?

Ships using gas as fuel fall under the IGF Code, so crew need IGF Code training under Regulation V/3. Holders of V/1-2 gas tanker qualifications are deemed to meet the IGF basic requirement (STCW Regulation V/3).

How can an officer establish continued professional competence for tankers?

Section A-I/11 requires tanker service appropriate to the endorsement, or an approved relevant course, at intervals not exceeding five years (STCW Regulation I/11, Section A-I/11).

Who on a gas carrier must hold the advanced, not just basic, gas tanker qualification?

The advanced level is required for masters, chief engineers, chief mates, second engineers and anyone with immediate cargo responsibility (STCW Regulation V/1-2).
