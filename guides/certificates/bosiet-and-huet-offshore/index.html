BOSIET and HUET for Seafarers: What They Are and When You Need Them

Quick answer

BOSIET (Basic Offshore Safety Induction and Emergency Training) is an OPITO industry standard, not an STCW requirement. It includes HUET (Helicopter Underwater Escape Training), and you need it when an offshore operator or charterer requires it, usually because you will fly to an installation or vessel by helicopter. It is generally kept valid by completing the OPITO FOET refresher, but the client decides which version and which equivalent courses it will accept.

What BOSIET and HUET actually are

BOSIET stands for Basic Offshore Safety Induction and Emergency Training. It is a training standard published by OPITO, the oil and gas industry's skills body. It is not published by IMO or a flag administration. Training centres must be OPITO-approved to issue it.

HUET is Helicopter Underwater Escape Training. You practise escaping from a helicopter simulator that ditches in a pool and capsizes. HUET is a module inside BOSIET. It is also sold as a stand-alone course by many centres, including non-OPITO versions.

The common variants are:

  • BOSIET with CA-EBS: the full course, including training on the Compressed Air Emergency Breathing System worn on many offshore helicopter flights. Most current BOSIET courses include it, but older certificates may not.
  • T-BOSIET (Tropical BOSIET): a version for warm-water regions. Clients typically accept it only for work in those regions.
  • FOET (Further Offshore Emergency Training): the refresher that keeps BOSIET valid. T-FOET is the equivalent for T-BOSIET.

OPITO revises its standards from time to time. Course content and the exact rules on variants depend on the edition in force when you train, so check the current standard with your training centre.

Is it an STCW or flag requirement?

No. Neither STCW nor MLC 2006 requires BOSIET or HUET. Your STCW Basic Training (PST, FPFF, EFA, PSSR) is a separate, statutory requirement under STCW. The two do not replace each other:

  • STCW Basic Training does not satisfy an offshore operator who asks for BOSIET.
  • BOSIET does not replace STCW Basic Training on your seafarer documents. A port State control officer or flag inspector will still want the STCW certificates.

The requirement for BOSIET comes from:

  • the offshore operator or charterer, through its contract and site safety rules;
  • industry guidance in particular offshore regions;
  • in some sectors, the national offshore regime, which may require its own course or a recognised equivalent. Norway is a well-known example where a national basic safety course applies.

For personnel on mobile offshore units, IMO has published non-mandatory recommendations on training and certification (Resolution A.1079(28)). Flag states may apply these in their own way.

The practical rule: the client's joining instructions decide what you need. Ask early.

Who needs it

You will usually be asked for BOSIET if you will:

  • crew-change by helicopter to an FPSO, drillship, semi-submersible, jack-up, accommodation unit or platform;
  • work on a vessel that sends crew to or from installations by helicopter, which some DSVs, construction vessels and accommodation vessels do;
  • be listed as offshore personnel on board (POB) under an operator's system, even if you are marine crew.

You may not need it on an OSV, AHTS or PSV that crew-changes in port and never puts crew on a helicopter. Even so, some charterers require it for all marine crew, so a certificate already in hand makes you easier to place.

A typical example is a 2nd Officer moving from tankers to a DP construction vessel. He holds full STCW documentation. The charterer's pre-joining matrix lists BOSIET with CA-EBS, an offshore medical and a client induction. None of those is on his CDC or CoC. If he books the courses only after selection, the joining date slips.

What the course covers

BOSIET is a mix of classroom work and practical sessions. Under the OPITO standard, the main elements are:

Module What you do
Safety induction Offshore hazards, safety management, permit to work, emergency response and alarms on installations
Helicopter safety and escape (HUET) Pre-flight briefing, survival suits, CA-EBS use, simulated ditching and capsize, escape through exits
Sea survival and first aid Lifejackets, survival craft, entering the water, group survival, basic first aid and hypothermia
Fire-fighting and self-rescue Portable extinguishers, escape through smoke, use of escape sets

The HUET pool sessions are the part people worry about. The escape drill is taught roughly as follows:

  1. Fasten the harness. Note your nearest exit and a fixed reference point.
  2. On the ditching command, take the brace position. If CA-EBS is used, deploy it and breathe from it before your head goes under.
  3. Keep one hand on the reference point. Wait until the simulator has inverted and stopped moving.
  4. Jettison or push out the window or exit, keeping hold of the frame.
  5. Release the harness only then, and pull yourself out hand over hand.
  6. Surface, inflate your lifejacket and get clear.

The usual mistake is releasing the harness too early. You end up disoriented and floating away from the exit.

Centres normally ask for a medical fitness declaration, and some ask for a medical certificate, before pool work. Some regions also expect your shoulder width to be measured and recorded for helicopter seating. Ask the centre whether this is done during the course.

Validity and revalidation

An OPITO BOSIET or FOET certificate is valid for four years. To stay current, complete FOET before the certificate expires. FOET is shorter than BOSIET and refreshes the HUET, sea survival and fire-fighting elements.

Things that vary, and that you should check rather than assume:

  • Lapsed certificates. Whether you can still take FOET after expiry, or must repeat the full BOSIET, depends on the OPITO rules in force. Ask the centre before you book.
  • Client rules. Some operators will not let you travel if the certificate expires during your hitch, even though it was valid on the day you flew out.
  • Equivalents. Non-OPITO courses, such as national courses, other industry standards and stand-alone HUET, are accepted or refused by each client and regime. There is no universal equivalence table.
  • T-BOSIET. Expect it to be accepted only in the regions it was designed for. Do not assume it will get you onto a North Sea flight.

OPITO-approved centres register certificates on OPITO's central records, so clients can verify them online. Many clients verify certificates on the OPITO register and may refuse a certificate they cannot find there, however genuine the paper looks.

Why crew get stopped at joining

These are the problems that most often stop crew at the heliport or at the agent's office:

  • Expired, or expiring mid-hitch. This is the most common one. Check the expiry against your sign-off date, not your joining date.
  • No CA-EBS. The client needs BOSIET with CA-EBS, but you hold an older certificate or HUET without EBS.
  • Wrong variant. You hold T-BOSIET, but the field is in a cold-water region.
  • Not on the register. The certificate came from a non-approved provider, or the centre has not yet uploaded the record.
  • Name mismatch. The certificate name differs from your passport, for example through spelling, name order or a missing middle name. Names are easily reordered or misspelt, especially where naming conventions differ from the centre's country, so check the certificate against your passport before you leave the centre.
  • Missing companion documents. BOSIET is in date, but there is no offshore medical (such as the OEUK medical in the UK sector), no shoulder measurement or no client-specific induction.
  • National regime not met. Some sectors require their own course or a recognised equivalent, and BOSIET alone may not be enough.
  • Stand-alone HUET offered when BOSIET is required. HUET is part of BOSIET, but it does not replace the full course.

Keeping it in date, and exam-style questions

Practical tips

  • Put the BOSIET expiry in the same tracker as your CoC, medical, passport and visas. Plan FOET well ahead of expiry, timed around your contract cycle.
  • Book FOET while you are at home, not in the gap between flights to a new contract.
  • Make sure the centre spells your name exactly as on your passport. Ask them to confirm the register upload before you leave.
  • Keep a scan of the certificate and a screenshot of the register entry on your phone.
  • If you are moving into offshore work, ask the manning agent for the client's full training matrix before you pay for anything.

Questions an examiner or client marine superintendent might ask

  • Is BOSIET an STCW certificate? No. It is an OPITO industry standard. STCW Basic Training is a separate statutory requirement.
  • When do you release your harness in a capsized helicopter? Only after the aircraft has inverted and stopped moving, with your exit open and a hand on your reference point.
  • When do you deploy CA-EBS? Before your head goes under, as briefed for that aircraft and system.
  • How do you keep BOSIET valid? Complete FOET before the four-year certificate expires, and check client rules if it runs out during a hitch.
  • Your BOSIET is valid, so can you fly? Only if every other client requirement is also met: offshore medical, inductions and any national requirements.
Key points
  • BOSIET is an OPITO industry standard, not an STCW or MLC requirement.
  • HUET is a module within BOSIET, and stand-alone HUET does not replace the full course.
  • OPITO BOSIET and FOET certificates are valid for four years, and FOET is the refresher.
  • The client or national offshore regime decides what is required and which equivalents are accepted.
  • Check for CA-EBS, the right variant (T-BOSIET vs BOSIET) and expiry against your sign-off date.
  • Your certificate must appear on OPITO's register with your name exactly as on your passport.
  • BOSIET does not replace STCW Basic Training, and STCW Basic Training does not replace BOSIET.

Frequently asked questions

Does my STCW Basic Safety Training count as BOSIET?

No. STCW Basic Training is the statutory requirement for seafarers, while BOSIET is an OPITO offshore industry standard that includes helicopter escape. Offshore clients asking for BOSIET will not accept STCW certificates instead, and you still need the STCW certificates for your seafarer documents.

How long is BOSIET valid and how do I renew it?

An OPITO BOSIET or FOET certificate is valid for four years. You renew it by completing FOET (Further Offshore Emergency Training) before expiry. If it has already lapsed, ask an OPITO-approved centre whether you can still take FOET or must repeat the full BOSIET.

Do I need BOSIET to work on an OSV or AHTS?

Not always. If the vessel crew-changes in port and you never fly by helicopter, it may not be required. Many charterers still list it for all marine crew, so check the client's training matrix with your manning agent.

What is the difference between BOSIET and T-BOSIET?

T-BOSIET is a tropical version designed for warm-water regions, while BOSIET is the full standard used in cold-water areas such as the North Sea. Clients generally accept T-BOSIET only in the regions it was designed for. Confirm with the operator before booking.

Is HUET alone enough to fly offshore?

Sometimes, but often not. Some operators or regions accept a stand-alone HUET, often with CA-EBS, for certain visits. Many require full BOSIET or a specified national course. The client's requirements decide, so do not assume HUET alone will be accepted.

Test yourself

Which body publishes the BOSIET training standard?

BOSIET is an OPITO industry standard delivered by OPITO-approved centres, not an IMO or ILO instrument (OPITO BOSIET standard).

During a HUET capsize drill, when should you release your seat harness?

Releasing early leaves you disoriented and away from the exit, so you wait for motion to stop and keep hold of a reference point (OPITO BOSIET, HUET module).

A seafarer holds full STCW Basic Training. An offshore client asks for BOSIET. What is the correct position?

STCW Basic Training (STCW Code, Section A-VI/1) and OPITO BOSIET are separate, and neither replaces the other.

How is an OPITO BOSIET normally kept valid?

FOET (Further Offshore Emergency Training) is the OPITO refresher that renews BOSIET for another four years (OPITO FOET standard).

Your BOSIET expires halfway through a planned four-week offshore hitch. What should you expect?

Some operators require validity for the whole hitch, and only FOET at an approved centre renews it (client joining rules; OPITO FOET standard).
