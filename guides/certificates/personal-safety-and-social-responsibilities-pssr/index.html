Personal Safety and Social Responsibilities (PSSR): What Every Seafarer Needs to Know

Quick answer

PSSR is one of the four elements of STCW basic training under Regulation VI/1 and Section A-VI/1. Anyone with designated safety or pollution-prevention duties must complete it before taking up shipboard duties. STCW does not require a five-year refresher for PSSR, unlike PST and FPFF, but how your administration formats and dates the certificate decides what the joining-port document check will accept.

What PSSR is and where it comes from

PSSR is the short course that teaches you how to live and work safely on a ship. It also covers how to get on with the people you sail with.

It is one of the four elements of STCW basic training:

  • Personal Survival Techniques (PST)
  • Fire Prevention and Fire Fighting (FPFF)
  • Elementary First Aid (EFA)
  • Personal Safety and Social Responsibilities (PSSR)

The legal basis is STCW Regulation VI/1. The detailed minimum standard is in Section A-VI/1 of the STCW Code, with the PSSR competences set out in Table A-VI/1-4. Guidance sits in Section B-VI/1. IMO has also published a model course for PSSR, which many training centres use as the basis for their syllabus. Course length and format are set by the approving administration, not by the Convention.

PSSR also ties in with the MLC 2006, Regulation 1.3. In paraphrase, that regulation says seafarers must not be allowed to work on a ship unless they have completed training for personal safety on board. Port State Control inspectors can therefore look at it from both the STCW and the MLC side.

Who needs it

STCW draws a line between two things:

  • Safety familiarisation. Every person employed or engaged on a seagoing ship, other than passengers, must receive familiarisation before being assigned to shipboard duties.
  • Basic training, including PSSR. Seafarers employed or engaged in any capacity as part of the ship's complement with designated safety or pollution-prevention duties must hold basic training before being assigned to shipboard duties.

On a merchant ship, almost every crew member has a muster list duty. In practice that means everyone from cadet and messman to Master and Chief Engineer needs PSSR.

Some people sit in grey areas, for example:

  • small craft
  • domestic or near-coastal trades
  • riding squads
  • some offshore personnel
  • supernumeraries

For these, the requirement depends on your flag administration and the company's SMS. Do not assume. Ask the company or manning agent in writing before you travel.

Cadets: most administrations and companies require basic training, PSSR included, before your first sea service. Indian and Filipino cadet programmes normally build it into pre-sea training. If yours does not, complete it separately at an approved centre before joining.

What the course covers

Table A-VI/1-4 lists the competences. In paraphrase, you must show you can:

Competence What it means on board
Comply with emergency procedures Know the types of emergency, your muster list duty, alarm signals and escape routes
Take precautions to prevent pollution of the marine environment Understand the basics of shipboard pollution and the procedures to prevent it
Observe safe working practices Know shipboard hazards, the use of PPE, and why permits and procedures exist
Contribute to effective communications on board Make sure messages are understood, including across languages and ranks
Contribute to effective human relationships on board Work in a multinational crew, respect others, and know your rights and obligations
Understand and take necessary actions to control fatigue Recognise fatigue in yourself and others, and know why rest hours matter

A typical course is mostly classroom work, assessed by written and oral questioning. Centres approved under different administrations will vary in detail. The competences above are the common minimum.

Validity and revalidation: the part people get wrong

Since the 2010 Manila amendments, STCW requires seafarers with basic training to provide evidence every five years that they maintain competence. That five-year requirement applies to PST and FPFF only, which is why refresher courses exist for those two.

STCW does not require a refresher for PSSR or EFA. An original PSSR certificate from an approved centre generally stays valid under the Convention.

The catch is in how administrations issue the paperwork:

  • Separate course certificates. Some administrations, India's DG Shipping for example, issue a separate certificate for each element. Each is recorded against your INDoS number. Your PSSR certificate stands on its own, with no expiry.
  • One combined certificate. Other administrations issue a single basic training certificate of proficiency covering all four elements. That certificate carries the expiry date driven by PST and FPFF. Once it lapses, your whole basic training is out of date on paper, even though PSSR itself has no refresher.
  • Company or charterer rules. Some companies and charterers set their own policies above the STCW minimum.

Check what your own administration's document says. If it shows an expiry date, that date governs at the gangway.

Why crew get stopped at joining because of PSSR

These are the usual problems found at the manning office, the airport or the gangway:

  • Missing certificate. Someone has PST, FPFF and EFA but never did PSSR, often because they assumed a refresher "covered everything". Refreshers cover PST and FPFF only.
  • Not verifiable online. The certificate does not appear in the administration's database, such as INDoS in India or the MARINA records in the Philippines. Sometimes the centre never uploaded it. Sometimes it is not genuine.
  • Centre not approved. The certificate came from a centre that was not approved by an administration for that course, or whose approval had lapsed.
  • Name or date-of-birth mismatch. The certificate details differ from your passport or CDC, such as a missing middle name, swapped surname, or a typo.
  • Lapsed combined certificate. You hold a combined basic training certificate that has expired because the PST and FPFF refreshers were not done.
  • Damaged or photocopied originals. You have a poor copy where the company or flag requires originals or certified copies.
  • Wrong document type. Offshore safety tickets such as BOSIET are not a substitute for STCW basic training, and the reverse is also true. Each sector checks its own.

If the certificate cannot be verified, the agent will usually not let you fly. Sort it out at the training centre and the administration before you are booked.

Worked scenario: an AB rejoining after a long break

An AB has been ashore for six years and is offered a contract. He holds PSSR and EFA from 2015, PST and FPFF from 2015, and his CDC.

Step 1: check each element. PST and FPFF are past five years, so refreshers are needed. PSSR and EFA need no STCW refresher.

Step 2: check the format. If his administration issued separate certificates, he only needs the PST and FPFF refreshers. If it issued one combined certificate of proficiency, he may need a new or revalidated combined certificate once the refreshers are done.

Step 3: verify online. He confirms that all four certificates appear correctly against his records, for example on INDoS. He fixes any spelling errors through the issuing centre.

Step 4: check the company's requirements. The company's SMS or charterer may require extra courses or a fresh PSSR. He asks the agent in writing.

Step 5: carry the documents. He packs the originals plus scanned copies, and confirms the joining list matches his passport name exactly.

Oral exam style: what an examiner might ask

PSSR topics can come up indirectly in CoC oral examinations, for example for OOW. Whether there is an oral exam, and its format, depends on the administration. Expect questions like:

  • "Where is basic training required in STCW?" Regulation VI/1, with the standard in Section A-VI/1. PSSR's competences are in Table A-VI/1-4.
  • "Which basic training elements need refreshing every five years?" PST and FPFF. Then mention that administrations may combine the elements into one dated certificate.
  • "What's the difference between familiarisation and basic training?" Familiarisation applies to everyone employed on board before they take up duties. Basic training applies to those with designated safety or pollution-prevention duties.
  • "Give me an example of how fatigue affects safety on your watch." Use a real example: slowed reactions, a missed alarm, poor lookout. Link it to rest-hour records.
  • "How do you make sure an order is understood in a mixed-nationality crew?" Closed-loop communication: the receiver repeats the order back, and the giver confirms it.

Examiners want practical answers that show you live these competences, not textbook recitations.

Keeping your PSSR and basic training in order

  • Track the PST and FPFF expiry dates. On many administrations' documents, those dates decide whether your basic training is acceptable.
  • Verify online after every course. Do not leave the centre until you know the certificate has been uploaded.
  • Match names exactly across your passport, CDC and certificates. Fix errors early, because corrections take time.
  • Keep scans in cloud storage and keep the originals in a waterproof folder.
  • Use only centres approved by your administration for the specific course. Check the approval list yourself.
  • Ask before you sign. Before signing a contract, get written confirmation of any company or charterer requirements beyond STCW.
Key points
  • PSSR is one of the four STCW basic training elements, under Regulation VI/1 and Section A-VI/1.
  • Its competences are listed in Table A-VI/1-4 of the STCW Code.
  • Anyone with designated safety or pollution-prevention duties needs it before taking up shipboard duties.
  • STCW's five-year refresher applies to PST and FPFF, not to PSSR or EFA.
  • Some administrations issue one combined basic training certificate with an expiry date, so check your own document.
  • Most joining-day problems come from unverifiable certificates, name mismatches or lapsed combined certificates.
  • MLC 2006 Regulation 1.3 also requires personal safety training before working on board.

Frequently asked questions

Does the PSSR certificate expire?

Under STCW there is no five-year refresher requirement for PSSR; only PST and FPFF must be refreshed. However, if your administration issues one combined basic training certificate, that document carries an expiry date tied to PST and FPFF. Check what your own certificate says.

Is PSSR the same as STCW Basic Safety Training?

No. PSSR is one of the four parts of basic training, alongside PST, FPFF and EFA. You need all four to meet Section A-VI/1.

Do I need to redo PSSR when I do my BST refresher?

STCW does not require it, because the refresher covers PST and FPFF. Some companies, charterers or administrations may have their own policies. Confirm with your company or manning agent.

Can I do PSSR at any training centre?

It must be done at a centre approved by an administration for that course. Otherwise it may not be accepted or verifiable at joining. Indian seafarers should make sure it appears against their INDoS number, and Filipino seafarers should check MARINA recognition.

Does my company's familiarisation on joining replace PSSR?

No. Shipboard familiarisation is a separate requirement for everyone employed on board. It covers that particular ship. PSSR is formal basic training required for anyone with designated safety or pollution-prevention duties.

Test yourself

Where in the STCW Code are the minimum competences for PSSR set out?

PSSR is part of basic training under Section A-VI/1, with competences in Table A-VI/1-4. A-VI/2 is survival craft and A-VI/6 is security.

Which basic training elements does STCW require you to refresh every five years?

Since the Manila amendments, Regulation VI/1 and Section A-VI/1 require evidence of maintained competence in PST and FPFF every five years.

Which of these is NOT a PSSR competence in Table A-VI/1-4?

Launching survival craft belongs to the PSCRB proficiency under Section A-VI/2, not PSSR.

As an STCW minimum, a person employed on board with no designated safety or pollution-prevention duties must receive:

Section A-VI/1 requires familiarisation for everyone employed on board. Basic training is required for those with designated safety or pollution-prevention duties.

A rating holds a separate PSSR certificate issued in 2014. The manning agent says it has 'expired' under STCW. What is correct?

STCW Code Section A-VI/1 requires five-yearly evidence of maintained competence only for PST and FPFF. Any further requirement comes from the administration's certificate format or company policy.
