Fire Prevention and Fire Fighting (FPFF): What It Is, Who Needs It and How to Keep It Valid

Quick answer

FPFF is one of the four STCW Basic Training modules. It is required under STCW Regulation VI/1, and its standard is set out in Section A-VI/1 and Table A-VI/1-2 of the STCW Code. Anyone with designated safety or pollution-prevention duties must hold it, and you must show evidence of maintained competence every five years, normally through a refresher course. Expired, unrecognised or mismatched FPFF certificates can stop crew from joining.

What FPFF is and where it sits in STCW

Fire Prevention and Fire Fighting (FPFF) is the fire module of STCW Basic Training. The other three modules are Personal Survival Techniques (PST), Elementary First Aid (EFA) and Personal Safety and Social Responsibilities (PSSR).

The legal chain works like this:

  • STCW Regulation VI/1 sets the mandatory minimum requirements for safety familiarisation, basic training and instruction for all seafarers.
  • STCW Code Section A-VI/1 sets out who must undergo basic training and the revalidation requirement.
  • Table A-VI/1-2 is the actual standard of competence for FPFF. It lists the competences, the knowledge required, and how competence is demonstrated and assessed.

FPFF is the entry-level fire qualification. It is not the same as Advanced Fire Fighting (AFF), which falls under Regulation VI/3 and Section A-VI/3. AFF is required for seafarers designated to control fire-fighting operations, which in practice means officers. You normally need FPFF before AFF. Whether an in-date AFF or AFF refresher also satisfies FPFF revalidation depends on the administration. Some accept it, and others require both to be refreshed separately.

Some administrations issue one combined Basic Training certificate. Others issue a separate certificate of proficiency for each module. Either format is acceptable as long as it shows the STCW reference and is issued or recognised by the right administration.

Who needs it

Under Section A-VI/1, basic training is required before a seafarer is assigned shipboard duties if they are employed or engaged on the business of the ship as part of the complement with designated safety or pollution-prevention duties. Crew without such duties still need safety familiarisation, but not necessarily the full basic training.

In practice:

  • Deck and engine officers, cadets and ratings: always required. Your muster list fire party, boundary cooling and BA duties are designated safety duties.
  • Galley and catering crew (cooks, stewards, messmen): almost always required on cargo ships. They appear on the muster list, and the galley is a classic fire source.
  • Passenger ship hotel staff: requirements depend on whether they have designated safety duties. These ships also have their own additional training requirements. Follow the flag state's rules and the company SMS.
  • Cadets: you need valid basic training, including FPFF, before your first ship. Your training institute normally arranges this.

The Convention sets the minimum. Most manning agencies and companies require every crew member to hold full basic training regardless of rank. Expect that as standard.

What the course covers

Table A-VI/1-2 groups FPFF into two competences: minimising the risk of fire and keeping ready to respond, and fighting and extinguishing fires.

Theory you will be taught and assessed on:

  • Shipboard fire-fighting organisation, the location of fire-fighting appliances and emergency escape routes
  • The elements of fire and explosion (fire triangle), ignition sources, flammable materials and how fire spreads
  • The need for constant vigilance, and the actions to take on board when a fire is detected
  • Fire and smoke detection and automatic alarm systems
  • Classification of fires and the right extinguishing agent for each
  • Fixed installations, portable equipment, fire-fighter's outfits and personal equipment
  • Fire-fighting methods and procedures, including the use of breathing apparatus

Practical exercises (the part you cannot fake):

  • Using the various types of portable extinguisher
  • Using self-contained breathing apparatus (SCBA)
  • Extinguishing small fires, and larger fires with water using jet and spray nozzles
  • Extinguishing fires with foam, powder or other suitable chemical agents
  • Entering and passing through a compartment filled with high-expansion foam, using a lifeline but without breathing apparatus
  • Fighting fire in smoke-filled enclosed spaces wearing SCBA
  • Extinguishing fire with water fog or another agent in an accommodation room or simulated engine room with fire and heavy smoke
  • Extinguishing oil fire with a fog applicator and spray nozzles, dry powder or foam applicators
  • Carrying out a rescue from a smoke-filled space wearing breathing apparatus

Course length, class size and fees are set by the approving administration and the training centre. Check the current approved syllabus with an approved institute.

One trap to watch: fire classification letters are not universal. The European/ISO system and the US (NFPA) system use the letters differently, especially for electrical and cooking-oil fires. Learn the system on your extinguisher labels on board, not the one you remember from a different school.

Validity and refresher training

Section A-VI/1 requires seafarers qualified in basic training to provide evidence every five years that they have maintained the required standard of competence. This applies to the competences in the PST and FPFF tables.

The revalidation duty came in with the 2010 Manila amendments. The transitional period ended on 1 January 2017. An old 'lifetime' FPFF certificate issued before then is not enough on its own today.

How the five-yearly evidence is provided depends on the administration:

  • Most administrations require a shore-based refresher course with practical fire-fighting exercises at an approved centre. A new certificate is issued with a fresh validity date.
  • The Code allows administrations to accept onboard training and experience for some elements. Whether your flag or issuing administration does this, and for which elements, is their decision. Do not assume your drill record counts unless the administration says so in writing.
  • Some administrations print an expiry date on the certificate. Others print only an issue date and expect you to count five years yourself. Either way, the five-year clock applies.

EFA and PSSR are not covered by the Convention's five-yearly revalidation requirement. Some administrations and companies still set their own validity periods for them, so check your own documents.

AFF has its own separate five-yearly requirement under Section A-VI/3. Officers normally have two fire certificates to track, unless their administration accepts an AFF refresher as also covering FPFF.

Why crew get stopped at joining

Document checks happen at the manning agency, at the company, at immigration and sometimes on board during Port State Control. These are the FPFF problems that most often cause an offsigner to wait for a relief that never arrives:

  1. Expired, or expiring mid-contract. Many companies will not send you if FPFF expires during the planned contract. That is company policy, not Convention text, but it is common. Count your contract length plus a margin.
  2. Pre-2017 certificate never refreshed. A seafarer returns after years ashore holding only the original basic training certificate. It no longer meets the requirement without a refresher.
  3. Not verifiable. The certificate is not found in the issuing administration's online database. For Indian-issued certificates, details are tied to your INDoS record, so a mismatch there will show up.
  4. Unapproved or fraudulent institute. Certificates from centres not approved by the administration get rejected. Fake certificates can end careers. Only train at centres listed by your administration.
  5. Name or date-of-birth mismatch with your passport or seaman's book. One spelling difference can be enough for a rejection.
  6. Wrong certificate presented. AFF is offered in place of FPFF, unless the administration and company accept AFF as covering FPFF. Or a company in-house fire course is offered in place of the STCW certificate.
  7. Flag state acceptance. Some flags or charterers have specific expectations about which administrations' certificates of proficiency they accept. When joining a new flag, check with the company in advance.
  8. Only a photocopy or scan carried. Many joining checks require originals.

Worked scenario: planning your refresher

Situation. An AB's FPFF certificate was issued in April 2021, so the five-year point is April 2026. He is offered a contract starting in July 2024, about 21 months before that date. The contract may be extended.

Step by step:

  1. Work out the expiry. Find the date printed on the certificate, or issue date plus five years if no expiry is printed. Note PST and AFF dates too if held, since they usually sit close together.
  2. Check the company rule. Ask the crewing office how much remaining validity they require at joining. Do not guess.
  3. Add the extension risk. If the contract plus any likely extension runs past the expiry, refresh before joining. Doing it on leave is far easier than mid-contract.
  4. Book at an approved centre. Combining the PST and FPFF refreshers in one visit is common and saves leave days.
  5. Update records. Make sure the new certificate appears on the administration's verification system before you travel. Send the manning agent a scan, and carry the original.
  6. Set a reminder. A calendar alert before the next expiry stops this becoming a joining-day emergency.

Oral exam and on-board questions to be ready for

Examiners, masters and PSC officers often test basic fire knowledge out loud. Typical questions and the points a good answer covers:

  • 'Where is your muster station for fire, and what is your duty?' Know it from the muster list and your familiarisation, not from guessing.
  • 'Explain the fire triangle and how each extinguishing agent works.' Water cools, foam smothers and cools, CO2 smothers by displacing oxygen, and dry powder chemically interrupts combustion.
  • 'Why should you not use a water jet on an oil fire or live electrical equipment?' On oil, the jet spreads the burning fuel. On live equipment, there is a shock risk.
  • 'What do you do when you discover a fire?' Raise the alarm, inform the bridge, attempt to fight it only if safe and small, close doors, and report to your station.
  • 'Walk me through donning and testing SCBA.' Cover visual check, cylinder pressure, face-mask seal, low-pressure warning, and entry and exit procedure with a partner and a means of communication.
  • 'Why is boundary cooling important?' It stops heat spreading by conduction through steel bulkheads and decks to adjacent spaces.

If you cannot answer these confidently, a PSC officer may decide crew are not familiar with their duties. That can lead to a deficiency or a repeated fire drill, regardless of what your certificate says.

Key points
  • FPFF is the fire module of STCW Basic Training, required under Regulation VI/1 with the standard in Table A-VI/1-2.
  • Anyone with designated safety or pollution-prevention duties needs it, and most companies require it for all crew.
  • Evidence of maintained competence is required every five years, usually via an approved refresher course.
  • Whether onboard drills count towards revalidation is the administration's decision, not yours or the company's.
  • FPFF and Advanced Fire Fighting are separate certificates, each with its own five-yearly requirement in STCW; whether an AFF refresher also covers FPFF depends on the administration.
  • Check that your certificate is verifiable online, matches your passport exactly, and will not expire mid-contract.
  • Refresh PST and FPFF together on leave to avoid joining-day problems.

Frequently asked questions

Is FPFF the same as Advanced Fire Fighting (AFF)?

No. FPFF is basic training for all crew with safety duties, under STCW Regulation VI/1. AFF is a higher-level course under Regulation VI/3 for those designated to control fire-fighting operations, normally officers. Officers usually need both; whether refreshing AFF also covers FPFF depends on the administration.

How long is an FPFF certificate valid?

STCW requires evidence of maintained competence every five years. In practice the certificate is treated as valid for five years from issue or last refresher. Some administrations print an expiry date and others do not, but the five-year requirement applies either way. Companies may want extra remaining validity at joining.

Can my onboard fire drills count instead of a refresher course?

The STCW Code allows administrations to accept onboard training and experience for some elements of the requirement. Whether your issuing administration does so, and for which elements, varies. Most seafarers still complete a shore-based refresher with practical fire-fighting. Confirm with your administration before relying on drill records.

Do cooks and stewards need FPFF?

If they have designated safety duties on the muster list, which is normal on cargo ships, the Convention requires basic training including FPFF. Most companies require it for all crew anyway. Passenger ship hotel staff can be treated differently depending on their duties, the flag state and the company SMS.

My FPFF is from before 2017 and has no expiry date. Is it still valid?

On its own, generally not for sea service today. The 2010 Manila amendments introduced the five-yearly evidence requirement, with the transitional period ending on 1 January 2017. You will normally need a refresher course at an approved centre before joining.

Test yourself

Which part of the STCW Code contains the standard of competence for Fire Prevention and Fire Fighting?

Table A-VI/1-2 is the FPFF standard. A-VI/1-1 is PST, A-VI/3 is Advanced Fire Fighting and A-II/1 is the OOW deck standard (STCW Code, Part A).

How often must a seafarer provide evidence of maintained competence in FPFF under STCW?

Section A-VI/1 requires evidence every five years. This was introduced by the 2010 Manila amendments and replaced the old 'lifetime' view of basic training.

Under the Convention, which basic training elements carry the five-yearly evidence requirement?

Section A-VI/1 applies the five-yearly requirement to the competences in Tables A-VI/1-1 (PST) and A-VI/1-2 (FPFF). Individual administrations or companies may add their own rules for the other modules.

In the FPFF practical, how do trainees pass through a compartment filled with high-expansion foam?

Table A-VI/1-2 includes entering and passing through a high-expansion foam compartment with a lifeline but without breathing apparatus.

Who decides whether onboard training and experience can count towards FPFF revalidation?

Section A-VI/1 leaves acceptance of onboard training and experience for some elements to the administration. Neither the company, the master nor PSC can grant it.
