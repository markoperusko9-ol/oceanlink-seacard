Dynamic Positioning (DP) Operator Certificate: What It Is and How to Keep It Valid

Quick answer

A DP certificate qualifies a deck officer to operate a vessel's dynamic positioning system. It is not an STCW certificate of competency. It is issued under an industry training scheme, most widely the Nautical Institute's, following IMO and STCW Part B guidance. Charterers and the company decide what grade you need, and most joining problems come from an expired certificate, the wrong grade or an unverified DP logbook.

What a DP certificate actually is

Dynamic positioning is the computer-controlled use of a vessel's own thrusters and propellers to hold position or heading, or to follow a track, without anchors or moorings. The DP operator (DPO) watches and controls that system from the DP desk.

The DP operator certificate shows you have done structured shore training, gained supervised DP watchkeeping time at sea and been signed off as competent. It sits alongside your Certificate of Competency. It does not replace it.

The most widely recognised scheme is run by The Nautical Institute (NI). Other training and certification schemes exist, including ones run by classification societies and some national bodies. Which scheme your company or charterer accepts depends on their requirements. Confirm this before you spend money on a course.

Which regulation requires it? (Short answer: none directly)

This trips up many candidates in orals and interviews.

  • STCW does not make a DP certificate mandatory. No regulation in STCW Chapter V requires one. STCW Code Part B, Section B-V/f gives guidance on training and experience for personnel operating DP systems. Part B is recommendatory, not mandatory.
  • IMO MSC/Circ.738 (as revised) gives guidelines for DP operator training.
  • IMO MSC.1/Circ.1580 gives guidelines for vessels and units with DP systems and defines the DP equipment classes. It applies to vessels and units built on or after 9 June 2017. MSC/Circ.645 remains the reference for many older DP vessels.
  • Industry guidance drives the real-world requirement. IMCA M 117 on training and experience of key DP personnel is the main example. Oil majors and charterers write it into their vetting and contract terms.

In practice, the requirement comes from the charterer, the company SMS and the client's vetting, not the flag. Some administrations or coastal states may have their own rules for certain operations or national waters. Check with the flag and company rather than assuming.

Who needs it

You will normally need DP certification, or be working towards it, if you hold or will hold the DP desk on:

  • Platform supply vessels and anchor handlers working at installations
  • Diving support, ROV support and construction vessels
  • Drillships and DP semi-submersibles
  • Shuttle tankers loading offshore
  • Cable-lay, pipe-lay and wind farm installation vessels
  • Some cruise ships and research vessels fitted with DP

Deck officers are the usual DPOs, from junior officers up to master. The DP scheme sets its own entry requirements. Under the NI scheme these are linked to holding or working towards a deck CoC. Check the current scheme rules for your situation.

Engineers and ETOs on DP vessels are not usually required to hold a DPO certificate. They are expected to understand the power plant, redundancy concept and FMEA, and many companies send them on DP maintenance or awareness courses.

Charterers commonly expect a higher grade of certificate and more DP experience for senior DPOs and masters on DP2 and DP3 vessels. The exact experience expected is set by the client. It is not fixed in any convention.

How the certification route works (NI scheme outline)

The NI scheme follows a staged route. The broad sequence is:

  1. DP Induction (basic) course at an accredited training centre. This covers principles, system components, position reference systems, sensors, thrusters, power, alarms and basic operations.
  2. Seagoing familiarisation. You record supervised DP time in the scheme's DP logbook.
  3. DP Simulator (advanced) course. This covers failure scenarios, operational planning, ASOG/WSOG use, emergency responses and loss-of-position handling.
  4. Further supervised DP watchkeeping on a DP vessel, recorded and countersigned.
  5. Statement of suitability from the master. You then apply to the scheme with your logbook and evidence.

The scheme issues certificates at different grades. The Unlimited grade is for experience on vessels of higher DP class. A Limited grade reflects experience gained on DP1 vessels. The minimum sea-time days, assessment steps and logbook format have changed over the years. Always check the current scheme rules before you plan your sea time. Do not rely on what a colleague did ten years ago.

Worked scenario. A third officer finishes the Induction course and joins a DP1 PSV. She logs her DP watches carefully, completes the simulator course on leave and returns to the same vessel to finish her watchkeeping time. Her experience is all on a DP1 vessel, so she should expect a Limited certificate. If she wants Unlimited, she needs to plan qualifying time on a DP2 or DP3 vessel.

Validity and revalidation

A DP operator certificate is time-limited. Under the NI scheme it has a fixed validity period, historically five years. Revalidation normally depends on showing recent DP sea time within that period. If you lack the sea time, there is usually an alternative route, such as additional training or assessment.

Keep these points in mind:

  • The exact revalidation conditions are set by the issuing scheme, not by STCW or the flag. They have changed in past scheme revisions.
  • Certificates from other schemes have their own validity rules.
  • Your company or charterer may set stricter recency requirements than the scheme. They may expect recent experience on the same DP class or equipment type even when your certificate is valid.
  • Your STCW CoC revalidation and your DP certificate revalidation run on separate clocks. Track both.

Common reasons crew get stopped at joining

These are the problems that most often come up at the crewing office, the joining port or a client audit:

  • Expired certificate. It ran out during the contract or leave, and nobody checked before the flight was booked.
  • Wrong grade. A Limited certificate holder is booked as DPO on a DP2 or DP3 vessel where the client requires Unlimited.
  • Only a course certificate. The Induction or Simulator course certificate is not a DPO certificate. Crewing sometimes confuses them, but client auditors do not.
  • Unverifiable DP logbook. Entries are missing the master's signature or the vessel stamp, or record time on a vessel or mode that does not count under the scheme.
  • Insufficient recent experience. The certificate is valid, but the client's matrix requires recent DP hours that you cannot show.
  • Doubtful documents. Issuing schemes and clients increasingly verify certificates online. A certificate that cannot be verified will stop you, whatever the reason.
  • Missing vessel-specific familiarisation. Some clients want evidence of familiarisation on the specific DP system make or vessel before you take the desk alone.

Oral exam and interview questions you should be ready for

Expect questions like these from a DP master, superintendent or examiner:

  • "Explain DP equipment classes 1, 2 and 3."
    • Class 1: loss of position may occur after a single fault.
    • Class 2: a single fault in an active component or system should not cause loss of position.
    • Class 3: a single fault should not cause loss of position, and the single fault includes loss of a whole compartment by fire or flooding.
  • "Is your DP certificate an STCW certificate?" No. It is an industry scheme qualification. STCW Part B gives only guidance.
  • "What is the FMEA and why do you read it?" It analyses how the DP system responds to failures and shows whether the redundancy concept holds. You need it to understand your worst-case failure.
  • "What is an ASOG/WSOG?" It is the activity- or well-specific operating guideline. It sets defined limits and the required action at each status level. Many operators use a colour system such as green, advisory, yellow and red.
  • "Name your position references and their weaknesses."
    • DGNSS: signal loss or interference.
    • Taut wire: angle limits.
    • Hydroacoustic: noise and aeration.
    • Laser and radar targets: line-of-sight and reflector problems.
  • "What do you do on a drive-off?" Follow your ASOG and vessel procedures. Identify and isolate the cause, for example by deselecting the faulty thruster or reference. Take manual or joystick control if the procedure requires it, inform the installation or dive control, and log events.

If you cannot explain your vessel's worst-case failure and your references' weaknesses, you are not ready for the desk.

Tips for keeping your DP certificate in date

  • Log DP time as you go. Get every page signed and stamped before the master signs off or you leave the vessel.
  • Keep scans of your logbook, certificates and course documents in cloud storage and on your phone.
  • Diary your expiry date and start the revalidation process well before it, so you have time to collect missing signatures.
  • Check the current scheme rules at each revalidation. Do not assume they are unchanged.
  • Ask crewing which grade and recency the client requires before you accept a DP vessel assignment.
  • Keep your offshore safety training in date as well, for example BOSIET/HUET where the client requires it. A valid DP certificate will not get you to the installation without it.
  • Read the FMEA and DP operations manual on every new vessel, even if your certificate is current.
Key points
  • A DP operator certificate is an industry qualification, not an STCW certificate of competency.
  • STCW Code Part B, Section B-V/f and IMO MSC/Circ.738 give guidance only. Charterers and the company SMS set the real requirement.
  • The usual route is Induction course, supervised DP sea time, Simulator course, more DP watchkeeping and a master's statement of suitability.
  • Grades matter: experience only on DP1 vessels generally leads to a Limited certificate.
  • Certificates are time-limited, and revalidation depends on recent DP sea time under the issuing scheme's current rules.
  • Unsigned logbooks, the wrong grade and expired certificates are the most common reasons crew are stopped at joining.
  • Know your vessel's DP class, FMEA, worst-case failure and position reference weaknesses before taking the desk.

Frequently asked questions

Is the DP certificate mandatory under STCW?

No. STCW does not make DP certification mandatory. Part B of the STCW Code contains only recommendatory guidance on DP training. The requirement comes from charterers, clients and company procedures, and occasionally from national rules for specific operations.

Does the DP Induction course certificate let me work as a DPO?

No. The Induction course is only the first stage. You still need supervised DP sea time, the Simulator course, further watchkeeping and final certification by the scheme. Clients check for the operator certificate itself, not course certificates.

What is the difference between a Limited and Unlimited DP certificate?

Under the NI scheme, the grade reflects the DP class of the vessels on which you gained your experience. Experience only on DP1 vessels generally leads to a Limited certificate. Many clients require Unlimited for DPOs on DP2 and DP3 vessels. Check the current scheme rules for how to upgrade.

Can engineers get a DP certificate?

The DPO certificate is aimed at deck officers who operate the DP desk. Scheme entry requirements are linked to a deck CoC route. Engineers and ETOs on DP vessels usually attend DP maintenance or awareness training instead. Check with the training provider and the scheme before enrolling.

How long is a DP certificate valid and how do I revalidate it?

It is valid for a fixed period set by the issuing scheme. The NI certificate has historically been valid for five years. Revalidation generally requires evidence of recent DP sea time or an alternative training or assessment route. Check the scheme's current rules, as they have been revised before.

Test yourself

Which instrument gives STCW guidance on training and experience for DP operators?

DP guidance is in Part B of the STCW Code, Section B-V/f, which is recommendatory. Regulation V/1-1 covers tanker training, and A-II/1 covers OOW competence. Reference: STCW Code, Section B-V/f.

On a DP class 2 vessel, what should a single fault in an active component cause?

Class 2 requires that a single fault in an active component or system does not cause loss of position. Class 1 accepts loss of position after a single fault. Reference: IMO MSC.1/Circ.1580.

What mainly determines the DP certificate grade and experience a DPO needs for a particular job?

DP certification is not mandated by STCW. Clients and companies, guided by industry documents such as IMCA M 117, set grade and experience requirements. Reference: STCW Code, Section B-V/f (guidance only).

What does a DP class 3 single-fault criterion add compared with class 2?

Class 3 extends the single fault to include loss of any one compartment due to fire or flooding. Reference: IMO MSC.1/Circ.1580.

A DPO arrives on board with a valid Simulator course certificate but no DP operator certificate. What is the correct position?

The Simulator course is one stage of the scheme. Certification also requires supervised watchkeeping and final issue by the scheme. Reference: IMO MSC/Circ.738 (as revised), DP operator training guidelines.
