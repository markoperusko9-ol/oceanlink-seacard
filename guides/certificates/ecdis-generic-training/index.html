ECDIS Generic Training: What It Is, Who Needs It and How to Keep It Valid

Quick answer

ECDIS generic training is the approved course that gives deck officers the ECDIS competence required by the STCW Code for officers serving on ships fitted with ECDIS. It is usually based on IMO Model Course 1.27, but it does not replace type-specific familiarisation on the actual make and model on board. How long it stays valid, and whether a missing certificate limits your CoC, depends on your administration.

What ECDIS generic training actually is

ECDIS generic training teaches you how electronic chart display and information systems work in general, whatever the manufacturer. It covers the chart data, the standards behind the display, safety settings, alarms, route planning and monitoring, and the limits of the system.

The IMO guidance syllabus is IMO Model Course 1.27, Operational Use of ECDIS. Most maritime administrations approve courses built on that model course, normally delivered at an approved training institute with a simulator.

Keep these two things separate:

  • Generic training covers ECDIS principles and is the competence your CoC relies on.
  • Type-specific familiarisation covers the particular manufacturer and software version fitted on your ship. It is the company's responsibility, and the two are not interchangeable.

Having one without the other is a frequent ECDIS gap raised during inspections.

Which regulations require it

There is no stand-alone STCW 'ECDIS certificate' regulation. ECDIS competence was written into the deck competence tables by the 2010 Manila amendments to STCW:

  • STCW Code, Table A-II/1 (officer in charge of a navigational watch) includes using ECDIS to maintain safe navigation.
  • STCW Code, Table A-II/2 (chief mate and master) includes maintaining safe navigation through ECDIS and associated systems to support command decision-making.

The Manila amendments entered into force on 1 January 2012, with a transitional period that ended on 31 December 2016. After that, officers serving on ECDIS-fitted ships were expected to hold the competence.

The carriage requirement sits elsewhere. SOLAS Chapter V, Regulation 19 made ECDIS mandatory on most SOLAS ships, phased in between 2012 and 2018 by ship type, size and build date. The equipment itself must meet IMO performance standards for ECDIS.

Type-specific familiarisation is required under two instruments:

  • STCW Regulation I/14 and Section A-I/14 make the company responsible for familiarising seafarers with the equipment on board.
  • ISM Code Section 6 covers the company's duty to ensure personnel are properly familiarised.

MLC 2006 does not set ECDIS training requirements.

Who needs it

In practice the following need it:

  • Masters, chief officers, 2nd and 3rd officers serving on ships fitted with ECDIS, which today means most ocean-going SOLAS tonnage.
  • Candidates for OICNW and Chief Mate/Master CoCs. Many administrations require proof of approved ECDIS training before issuing or revalidating a deck CoC, or before issuing it without limitation.
  • Officers moving from coastal or non-SOLAS craft into ships with ECDIS.

The following generally do not need it:

  • Engine officers, ETOs and ratings. Ratings forming part of a navigational watch are not required to hold ECDIS competence, although company procedures may brief them on bridge equipment.

The point at which deck cadets take the course varies. Some administrations fold ECDIS into the CoC preparatory course, and others require it as a separate approved course. Check with your national administration and your training institute rather than assuming.

What the course covers

Syllabus content follows the model course. A good course will have you doing most of this hands-on in a simulator:

  • Chart data: the difference between ENCs (vector) and RNCs (raster), cells, usage bands and scale, licensing and permits, and the chart catalogue.
  • Standards and display: how the S-57 data standard and the S-52 presentation library control what you see. You also learn the display base, the standard display and 'all other information'.
  • Safety settings: safety depth, safety contour, shallow and deep contours, look-ahead or safety frame, and cross-track limits.
  • Route planning: building a route and running a route check against safety settings. You learn not to just click past the warnings.
  • Route monitoring: alarms, indications, overscale and underscale, chart priority, and own-ship symbol settings.
  • Sensors: position, heading and speed inputs, datum, sensor failure, and how errors propagate.
  • Overlays: radar and AIS overlays, and target information.
  • Updates: manual and automatic updates, and checking that updates have actually been applied.
  • Records: voyage logging and playback.
  • Limitations: over-reliance, alarm fatigue, and back-up arrangements if ECDIS fails.
  • Legal aspects: carriage requirements, RCDS mode, and the officer's responsibilities.

Course length, assessment method and class sizes are set by the approved syllabus of the administration concerned. Confirm these with the institute.

Validity and revalidation

This is where administrations differ, so do not rely on what a colleague from another flag tells you.

  • Under STCW itself, ECDIS competence is part of your deck CoC. It is kept current through CoC revalidation under STCW Regulation I/11, which requires medical fitness and evidence of continued professional competence, for example approved sea service or an approved alternative such as a refresher course or test (Section A-I/11). STCW does not set a separate expiry date for a generic ECDIS course certificate.
  • Some administrations require evidence of ECDIS training at CoC revalidation. Some also want an updated or refresher course, particularly where an older course did not meet the revised model course.
  • Many administrations place a limitation on the CoC if approved ECDIS training has not been completed, meaning the certificate is not valid for service on ships fitted with ECDIS. Removing it means doing the approved course and applying to the administration.
  • Flag state endorsements are issued on the basis of your national CoC. If your CoC carries an ECDIS limitation, the endorsement will normally reflect it.
  • Companies and charterers may set their own refresher intervals or require a recent course. These are company requirements, not STCW ones.

Rule of thumb: read your CoC carefully, including any limitations box, and check your own administration's current circulars.

Why officers get stopped at joining

These are the usual problems found at the manning office, at the gangway or during the first port state control inspection:

  • ECDIS limitation on the CoC. Your CoC says not valid on ECDIS-fitted ships, and the ship has ECDIS.
  • Course not approved. The course was done at an institute not approved by your administration, or the certificate cannot be verified.
  • Generic certificate only. There is no evidence of type-specific familiarisation for the make and model fitted. Inspectors often ask for this.
  • Outdated course. The old course does not meet your administration's current requirement for revalidation.
  • Name or document mismatch. The name on the course certificate differs from the passport or CDC.
  • Original missing. The original certificate was left at home, and only a scan is carried where the company or flag wants the original.

Worked scenario. A 2nd officer with valid generic training joins a bulk carrier fitted with a make he has never used. During a PSC inspection two days later, the inspector asks him to show the safety contour setting and to explain why an alarm was acknowledged. He cannot find the menu. There is no type-specific training certificate and no signed company familiarisation record.

The ship risks a deficiency even though his generic training is perfectly valid. The fix would have been:

  1. Type-specific training or a manufacturer-approved course before joining, where the company requires it.
  2. A documented onboard familiarisation with the master or navigating officer, signed and filed according to the SMS.
  3. A personal walkthrough of the settings menu, alarm list and route check before taking the first watch.

Oral exam: questions examiners like to ask

Expect questions like these at your CoC oral or from a PSC inspector on the bridge:

  • What is the difference between safety depth and safety contour? Safety depth controls how spot soundings are highlighted. Safety contour is the contour line ECDIS uses to separate safe from unsafe water and to trigger alarms.
  • You set a safety contour of 13 m and no 13 m contour exists in the ENC. What happens? ECDIS uses the next deeper contour available in the data.
  • What is the display base? It is the minimum information that cannot be removed from the screen.
  • What happens when you use raster charts? ECDIS runs in RCDS mode. Several automatic functions, such as grounding alarms based on chart depth data, are not available. An appropriate folio of up-to-date paper charts is required where the administration permits RCDS use.
  • How do you know your ENCs are up to date? Check the update status, the date of the latest update applied, and the licence and permit validity.
  • What is your back-up if ECDIS fails? It depends on the ship's approved back-up arrangement, for example a second ECDIS or paper charts. You must know what your ship has and what the SMS says to do.
  • Why is overscale dangerous? Zooming beyond the compilation scale gives a false impression of accuracy and can hide the true reliability of the data.

Tips for keeping it in date

  • Check your CoC limitations box every time you revalidate. Do not assume a limitation was removed automatically.
  • Use only approved institutes. Ask for the approval reference and check it against your administration's list. In India, for example, ensure the course is DGS-approved and recorded against your INDoS. Filipino officers should confirm MARINA approval.
  • Keep a type-specific record for each make you sail on. Store manufacturer course certificates and the signed onboard familiarisation forms with your documents.
  • Carry originals and copies. Keep a scanned set in cloud storage in case documents go missing.
  • Watch your administration's circulars for changes to ECDIS revalidation rules.
  • Ask before you sign on. Ask the manning agent which ECDIS make and software version is fitted, then request type-specific training early.
  • Practise on watch. Run a route check, review alarm settings and verify sensor inputs as part of your handover. Competence fades faster than certificates expire.
Key points
  • ECDIS competence for deck officers is in STCW Code Tables A-II/1 and A-II/2, introduced by the 2010 Manila amendments.
  • Generic courses are usually based on IMO Model Course 1.27 and must be approved by your administration.
  • Generic training does not replace type-specific familiarisation, which is a company duty under STCW Regulation I/14 and the ISM Code.
  • Many administrations put an 'ECDIS limitation' on CoCs where approved training has not been completed.
  • STCW sets no separate expiry for the generic course, but some administrations and companies require refreshers. Check yours.
  • At PSC, expect to demonstrate safety settings and alarms, not just show a certificate.

Frequently asked questions

Is ECDIS generic training mandatory for all deck officers?

It is required for deck officers serving on ships fitted with ECDIS, because the competence is part of STCW Tables A-II/1 and A-II/2. Most administrations require proof of approved training for an unrestricted deck CoC. Without it, many administrations restrict the CoC so it is not valid on ECDIS-fitted ships.

Does the ECDIS generic certificate expire?

STCW does not set a stand-alone expiry date for the generic course. The competence is maintained through CoC revalidation under Regulation I/11. Some administrations and companies require refresher or updated training, so check your own administration's rules and your company's requirements.

If I have the generic course, do I still need type-specific ECDIS training?

Yes. Generic training covers ECDIS principles, but you must also be familiar with the specific make and model on your ship. The company must ensure this under STCW Regulation I/14 and the ISM Code. It may be a manufacturer course, an approved computer-based course or a documented onboard familiarisation, depending on company and flag requirements.

How do I remove the ECDIS limitation from my CoC?

Complete an ECDIS course approved by the administration that issued your CoC. Then apply to that administration to reissue the CoC without the limitation. The exact documents and process vary by administration, so follow their current guidance.

Will my flag state accept an ECDIS course done in another country?

Flag state endorsements are normally based on your national CoC, so what matters most is whether your national administration recognises the course. Some flags may ask for extra evidence. Confirm with the manning agent and the flag administration before joining.

Test yourself

Where in STCW is ECDIS competence for an officer in charge of a navigational watch set out?

The OICNW competence table, STCW Code Table A-II/1, includes use of ECDIS. SOLAS V/19 covers carriage, Regulation I/11 covers revalidation, and ISM Section 6 covers personnel familiarisation.

An officer holds a valid generic ECDIS certificate and joins a ship with a make he has never used. What is still required?

Familiarisation with the specific shipboard equipment is a company responsibility under STCW Regulation I/14 and the ISM Code. Generic training alone is not enough.

You set a safety contour of 13 m but the ENC has no 13 m contour. What does ECDIS do?

Under the ECDIS performance standards and the S-52 presentation rules, ECDIS defaults to the next deeper available contour. Safety depth is a separate setting that highlights soundings.

What is true about ECDIS operating with raster charts (RNCs)?

Raster data is not vectorised, so automatic depth-based alarms are not available. The IMO ECDIS performance standards require an appropriate folio of up-to-date paper charts when operating in RCDS mode.

Which statement about the ECDIS display base is correct?

The display base is permanently shown and cannot be removed, as defined in the IMO ECDIS performance standards. The standard display and 'all other information' add to it.
