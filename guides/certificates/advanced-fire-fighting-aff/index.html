Advanced Fire Fighting (AFF): who needs it, what it covers and how to keep it valid

Quick answer

Advanced Fire Fighting (AFF) is the STCW training required under Regulation VI/3 for seafarers designated to control fire-fighting operations on board. In practice it is required for officer certificates of competency. Like basic fire fighting, it needs evidence of maintained competence every five years. Exactly how you prove that, and who checks it, depends on your administration and your company.

What AFF is and where the requirement comes from

Advanced Fire Fighting is the step above the fire-fighting module of your Basic Safety Training. Basic fire fighting (STCW Section A-VI/1) teaches you to be a useful member of a fire party. AFF (STCW Regulation VI/3 and Section A-VI/3) teaches you to run the fire party. That covers organisation, tactics and command.

The legal chain looks like this:

  • STCW Regulation VI/3 sets the mandatory minimum requirement for AFF training. It applies to seafarers designated to control fire-fighting operations.
  • STCW Code, Section A-VI/3 sets out the standard of competence, the competence table, and the requirement to show maintained competence every five years.
  • Officer CoC regulations, such as Regulation II/1 (OICNW) and Regulation III/1 (OICEW), require candidates to meet the AFF standard in Section A-VI/3. That is why every deck and engineer officer route includes it.
  • MLC 2006, Regulation 1.3 requires seafarers to be trained or certified as competent for their duties. A port State inspector can use it as a lever when certification is missing.
  • SOLAS Chapter II-2 sets the shipboard side: fire-fighting equipment, fire control plans, on-board training and drills. Your AFF knowledge is applied against those requirements.

The IMO publishes a model course for AFF. Approved training centres build their syllabus around it. Course length, fees and the exact assessment format are set by each administration and centre, so check with an approved provider in your country.

Who needs AFF

Officers: If you are working towards or already hold a deck or engineer officer CoC, you need AFF. Most administrations will not let you sit the CoC exam, or will not issue the CoC, without valid AFF documentation. Check your own administration's checklist, because the timing of when it must be held differs.

Ratings: STCW does not require AFF for ratings as such. However, the requirement attaches to the role of controlling fire-fighting, not to rank. If your company's muster list or SMS designates a bosun, pumpman or senior rating as a team leader, the company may require AFF. Some charterers also ask for it on certain trades. Read your company's training matrix.

Prerequisite: You will normally need valid basic fire-fighting training (FPFF under A-VI/1) before you are accepted on an AFF course. Centres check this at enrolment.

Do not confuse AFF with other courses:

Course STCW reference Purpose
Fire Prevention and Fire Fighting (FPFF) A-VI/1 Basic member of a fire party
Advanced Fire Fighting (AFF) A-VI/3 Controlling fire-fighting operations
Tanker / gas cargo courses Chapter V Cargo-specific hazards; separate requirement

A tanker course covers cargo fire hazards, but it is not a substitute for AFF.

What the course covers

The competence table in Section A-VI/3 is built around four areas:

  1. Control fire-fighting operations aboard ships. This covers procedures at sea and in port, the use of water and its effect on stability, communications and coordination, ventilation control (including smoke extraction), and control of fuel and electrical systems. It also covers hazards such as dry distillation, chemical reactions and boiler uptake fires, plus fires involving dangerous goods.
  2. Organise and train fire parties. This covers contingency planning, the composition and allocation of personnel to fire parties, and running realistic training.
  3. Inspect and service fire detection and extinguishing systems and equipment. This includes fixed systems, portable equipment, breathing apparatus and alarms. It also covers knowledge of statutory and classification survey requirements.
  4. Investigate and compile reports on incidents involving fire. You learn to assess the cause of a fire and how to report it.

Expect classroom theory plus practical exercises in a fire-ground or simulator. Practical work usually includes leading a team wearing breathing apparatus in smoke-filled spaces, and running a fire from a command position with communications to the bridge or fire control station. Assessment methods vary by centre and administration.

The point of the course is command. On a real ship you will be the one deciding when to withdraw a team, when to close down a space, and when to recommend a fixed-system release to the Master.

Worked scenario: engine room fire on your watch

You are 2nd Engineer. At 0230 a fuel oil leak on an auxiliary engine ignites. This is how AFF thinking applies, step by step. Always follow your ship's own fire plan and SMS.

  1. Raise the alarm and inform the bridge. Give the location, what is burning, and whether anyone is missing.
  2. Isolate the fuel. Use the quick-closing valves and stop fuel pumps from outside the space if needed. Without this, extinguishing is a waste of effort.
  3. Muster and account for people. No headcount, no decisions about fixed systems.
  4. Assess the fire. Can it be fought locally with portable equipment or a local application system? Or is it beyond that?
  5. If you attack with a BA team: brief the team, set a BA control point with entry and exit times, maintain communications, and set boundary cooling on adjacent spaces.
  6. If it is beyond control: withdraw the team. Close openings, ventilation and fire dampers, and stop the machinery as per the fixed-system procedure. Carry out a full headcount. Then recommend fixed-system release to the Master, who decides under the ship's procedures.
  7. After the fire is out: keep boundary cooling going and do not rush re-entry. Monitor for re-ignition. Re-enter only under a controlled procedure with atmosphere testing.
  8. Report. Record times, actions and the likely cause. That record feeds the incident report and the company investigation.

Examiners love step 6. Releasing a fixed gas system with a person unaccounted for, or with openings still open, is the classic failure.

Oral exam style questions

These topics come up in CoC orals because they test AFF knowledge directly:

  • "You have flooded the hold with water to fight a fire. What are you worried about?" Stability. Free surface effect, added weight high up, and possible list. Know your ship's drainage arrangements and monitor the list.
  • "Why boundary cooling?" Heat passes through steel bulkheads and decks and can ignite material in adjacent spaces. Cool and monitor all six sides where possible.
  • "What is your first action in a galley fire?" Isolate power and fuel to the equipment and smother the fire. Never put water on burning cooking oil. Know the location of the hood or duct fire system and the duct damper.
  • "How do you control a BA team?" Use a BA control board or log with entry times and pressures, calculate turnaround and exit times, keep communications, and have a standby team ready.
  • "Who decides fixed-system release?" It is the Master's decision under the fire plan. You must know the preconditions: headcount complete, space sealed, ventilation and fuel stopped.
  • "What surveys apply to the fixed CO2 system?" Periodic inspection, testing and servicing are required under SOLAS Chapter II-2 (Regulation 14) and the FSS Code. The intervals are given in IMO guidance (MSC.1/Circ.1318) and in the ship's planned maintenance system. Statutory surveys are carried out by the flag Administration or by a recognised organisation acting on its behalf.

Validity and revalidation

Under Section A-VI/3, seafarers qualified in advanced fire fighting must provide evidence of maintaining the required standard of competence every five years. In practice this means a refresher or revalidation course at an approved centre. Most administrations require an approved refresher course, so check whether yours accepts any alternative evidence. Which method is accepted is set by your administration.

Points that vary and that you must check yourself:

  • Whether the five years runs from the original course date or from the date printed as an expiry.
  • Whether the refresher must be done in your own country or can be done abroad at a centre approved by your administration.
  • Whether the flag State of the ship you are joining recognises your certificate. This usually follows from your CoC endorsement, but confirm it.
  • Whether a lapsed certificate can be refreshed or requires the full course again.

Your CoC revalidation under Regulation I/11 is a separate process. Many administrations expect your safety training, including AFF, to be in date when you revalidate your CoC, so plan both together.

Why crew get stopped at joining, and how to avoid it

Common reasons a manning agent, company or port State inspector stops you:

  • Expired certificate, or one that expires during the contract. Many companies will not sign you on if a certificate lapses mid-contract.
  • Basic FPFF expired. Your AFF is fine, but the basic fire-fighting refresher was missed.
  • Unapproved training centre. The certificate was issued by a centre not approved by the administration, or the certificate number cannot be verified on the administration's online system.
  • Name or date-of-birth mismatch with your passport, seaman's book or CoC.
  • Wrong certificate presented. For example, a tanker fire course or a company in-house drill record offered in place of AFF.
  • No evidence of maintained competence. An old AFF certificate without a refresher in the last five years is not accepted. This includes certificates issued before the Manila amendments transitional period ended on 1 January 2017.
  • Flag issue. The ship's flag does not accept the document without endorsement or recognition.

Tips for keeping it in date:

  • Keep a list of every certificate with its expiry date. Set reminders 9–12 months ahead.
  • Book refreshers during leave. Do not leave them to the week before joining.
  • Do FPFF and AFF refreshers together where your centre allows it.
  • Check the certificate details the day you receive it.
  • Verify the course appears on your administration's online record, where one exists, before you leave the centre's city.
  • Carry originals plus scanned copies, and send scans to the agent early so problems surface before travel.
Key points
  • AFF is required by STCW Regulation VI/3 for those designated to control fire-fighting operations; the standard is in Section A-VI/3.
  • Officer CoCs (e.g. Regulations II/1 and III/1) require the AFF standard, so all deck and engineer officers need it.
  • Ratings need it only if their company role or SMS makes them responsible for controlling fire-fighting, or the company or charterer requires it.
  • The course covers command, organising fire parties, inspection of systems and equipment, and fire investigation and reporting.
  • Evidence of maintained competence is required every five years; most administrations require an approved refresher course.
  • Most joining refusals come from expiry, an unapproved centre, unverifiable records, or name mismatches.

Frequently asked questions

Is Advanced Fire Fighting compulsory for ratings?

Not under STCW as a general rating requirement. The requirement attaches to people designated to control fire-fighting operations. Some companies designate senior ratings such as bosuns as team leaders and then require AFF, so check your company's training matrix.

How long is the AFF certificate valid?

STCW requires evidence of maintained competence every five years. How that evidence is provided, and whether expiry runs from the course date, depends on your administration. Most seafarers meet it with an approved refresher course.

Can I do AFF before Basic Safety Training?

Normally no. Approved centres usually require valid basic fire-fighting (FPFF) training before enrolment, because AFF builds on that foundation. Check the entry requirements with your training centre.

Does my tanker course cover advanced fire fighting?

No. Tanker and gas cargo courses under STCW Chapter V include cargo-related fire hazards, but they are separate requirements. You still need AFF under Regulation VI/3.

My AFF expired while I was on board. What happens?

It depends on the flag, your administration and company policy. Inform the company immediately and do not wait for an inspector to find it. Many companies arrange a refresher at the next opportunity, but you may be refused for your next contract until it is renewed.

Test yourself

Which STCW regulation sets the minimum requirement for advanced fire-fighting training?

Regulation VI/3 covers advanced fire fighting. VI/1 is basic safety, VI/2 is survival craft and V/1-1 covers tanker cargo training. Reference: STCW Regulation VI/3.

How often must AFF holders provide evidence of maintained competence under STCW?

Section A-VI/3 requires evidence of maintained competence every five years. The method of proof is set by the administration. Reference: STCW Code, Section A-VI/3.

Before recommending release of a fixed gas system into an engine room, what must be confirmed first?

A full headcount and closing of openings, ventilation and fuel supply are preconditions for release under the ship's fire plan. Reference: SOLAS Chapter II-2 and the ship's fire control procedures.

Why is stability a concern when fighting a fire with large quantities of water?

Water trapped in compartments adds weight, often high up, and creates free surface effect that reduces effective GM. Reference: STCW Code, Table A-VI/3 (effect of water on ship stability).

A rating holds a valid chemical tanker basic course. Does this replace AFF if he is designated as fire team leader?

Chapter V cargo training does not substitute for advanced fire-fighting competence under Regulation VI/3. Reference: STCW Regulation VI/3 and Chapter V.
