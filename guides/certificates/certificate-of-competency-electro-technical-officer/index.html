Certificate of Competency – Electro-Technical Officer (ETO)

Quick answer

The Electro-Technical Officer CoC is the STCW certificate for anyone serving as ETO on a seagoing ship with main propulsion machinery of 750 kW or more. It is issued under STCW Regulation III/6 against the competence standard in Section A-III/6 of the STCW Code. Like other STCW certificates, its validity runs no longer than five years and it must be revalidated under Regulation I/11, with the exact process set by your administration.

What the ETO CoC is and where it comes from

Before the 2010 Manila Amendments to STCW, ship's electricians and electrical officers were certificated in very different ways. Some held national certificates and some held nothing beyond a trade qualification. The Manila Amendments created a dedicated STCW grade:

  • STCW Regulation III/6 sets the mandatory minimum requirements for certification of electro-technical officers.
  • STCW Code Section A-III/6 sets the standard of competence: what you must know, understand and be able to demonstrate.
  • Regulation III/7 is a separate grade, the Electro-Technical Rating (ETR). Do not confuse the two. The ETR is a rating certificate, not an officer's CoC.

The certificate covers three functions at the operational level:

  1. Electrical, electronic and control engineering
  2. Maintenance and repair
  3. Controlling the operation of the ship and care for persons on board

An ETO CoC is an officer's certificate, but not a watchkeeping certificate. It does not by itself qualify you to be in charge of an engineering watch. That needs a Regulation III/1 CoC.

Who needs it, and who doesn't

Regulation III/6 applies to anyone serving as an electro-technical officer on a seagoing ship powered by main propulsion machinery of 750 kW propulsion power or more.

STCW does not oblige every ship to carry an ETO. Whether your ship must carry one is decided by the flag state through the Minimum Safe Manning Document (SOLAS Chapter V, Regulation 14). Many large container ships, LNG carriers, cruise ships, offshore units and DP vessels carry an ETO. Some bulk carriers and tankers do not, and the engineers cover electrical work instead.

In practice:

  • If you sign on as ETO on a ship of 750 kW or more, you need a valid III/6 CoC, or an equivalent the flag accepts.
  • If the company carries an "electrician" as a rating, the flag may require the III/7 ETR certificate or a national qualification instead. Check the crew list and the safe manning document.
  • Older electrical officers who served before the Manila Amendments were covered by transitional arrangements. Those windows have long closed, and anyone without a III/6 CoC today must go through the normal route of their administration.

Requirements and what the training covers

Regulation III/6 sets the international minimums. Your administration (for example DG Shipping in India or MARINA in the Philippines) decides the approved courses, exam format, sea-time documentation and fees. The convention minimums are:

  • Age: at least 18.
  • Workshop training and sea service: a combined period of workshop skills training and approved seagoing service. On the approved-programme route this is at least 12 months, including at least 6 months at sea, documented in an approved training record book. The regulation also allows a longer alternative route based mainly on engine-department sea service. Your administration will tell you which route it accepts and how to prove it.
  • Education and training meeting the Section A-III/6 standard, followed by assessment (written and/or oral, depending on the administration).
  • Safety training to the standards of:
    • Section A-VI/1 (basic training)
    • A-VI/2 paragraphs 1–4 (survival craft and rescue boats)
    • A-VI/3 paragraphs 1–4 (advanced fire fighting)
    • A-VI/4 paragraphs 1–3 (medical first aid)
  • Medical fitness under Regulation I/9.

Typical syllabus areas under A-III/6 include:

  • Generators and distribution systems, including paralleling and load sharing
  • Electric motors and starters
  • Power electronics
  • Automation and control systems, sensors and alarm systems
  • Bridge navigation equipment and ship communication systems
  • Electrical safety and high-voltage installations
  • Fault-finding and maintenance planning
  • Workshop practice
  • English
  • Leadership and teamwork
  • Pollution prevention, fire fighting, life-saving appliances and medical first aid

High voltage is part of the ETO standard. Even so, many companies and some administrations also ask for a separate high-voltage course certificate, so check before you join.

Validity and revalidation

Every STCW CoC carries an expiry date not exceeding five years. Revalidation is governed by Regulation I/11 and Section A-I/11. To revalidate, you show:

  1. Continued medical fitness under Regulation I/9.
  2. Continued professional competence by one of the routes in A-I/11:
    • approved seagoing service performing ETO functions for at least one year in total during the preceding five years; or
    • functions the administration considers equivalent; or
    • passing an approved test; or
    • completing approved course(s); or
    • approved seagoing service of at least three months in a supernumerary capacity or in a lower officer rank immediately before taking up the ETO rank.

The safety certificates are revalidated separately. Since the Manila Amendments, Regulations VI/1, VI/2 and VI/3 require evidence every five years that you maintain the standard of competence for basic training, survival craft and advanced fire fighting. Many administrations do this through refresher courses.

What depends on your administration:

  • how early you can apply
  • whether the new certificate runs from the old expiry date or from the issue date
  • whether online or in-person application is required
  • which refresher courses are accepted
  • whether a short gap in sea service can be covered by a test or course

Common reasons ETOs get stopped at joining

Crewing departments, masters and port state control officers check the same things every time. These are the usual failures:

Problem Why it stops you
Flag state endorsement missing or expired A foreign CoC needs the flag's endorsement under Regulation I/10. The endorsement can expire before your CoC does.
No proof of endorsement application Regulation I/10 lets a flag allow service for up to three months only if documentary proof that the application has been submitted is on board.
Safety refresher lapsed A valid CoC does not cover expired BST, PSCRB or AFF.
Medical certificate expiring mid-contract or carrying restrictions Flags and companies often refuse sign-on if the medical will lapse soon.
Name or date-of-birth mismatch Differences between the CoC, passport and seaman's book trigger verification failures.
Certificate not on the issuing administration's verification database The company cannot confirm authenticity, so you wait ashore.
Ship-type training missing Examples are high-voltage, tanker basic training (where your duties involve cargo equipment), IGF or Polar Code courses, depending on duties and company SMS.
Wrong certificate for the billet An ETR (III/7) holder booked against an ETO position on the safe manning document.

Most of these are found at the airport or at the gangway. Every one of them can be prevented with a document check before travel.

Worked scenarios

Scenario 1: revalidation with enough sea time

Ravi's ETO CoC expires in five months. In the last five years he has done three contracts as ETO, totalling about 16 months on articles.

  1. He collects his discharge entries and sea-service testimonials.
  2. He checks his medical validity.
  3. He confirms his BST, PSCRB and AFF refreshers are valid.
  4. He applies to his administration within its stated revalidation window.
  5. Because he has more than one year of ETO service in the preceding five years, the sea-service route applies.
  6. Then he applies for new flag endorsements, because endorsements tied to the old CoC do not carry over automatically.

Scenario 2: short on sea time

Maria spent four years ashore in a port electrical workshop and has only about seven months as ETO in the last five years. She cannot use the one-year sea-service route. Her options under A-I/11 depend on what her administration offers:

  • an approved test or course; or
  • functions accepted as equivalent; or
  • about three months as supernumerary or in a lower officer rank before taking up the ETO post.

She should ask the administration in writing which of these it accepts, before the CoC expires.

Scenario 3: flag change at short notice

Joseph is transferred to a sister ship under a different flag. He holds a valid CoC, but there is no endorsement from the new flag yet. The company may lodge an endorsement application so he can serve for up to three months, if the new flag permits this under Regulation I/10 and the proof of application travels with him.

Oral exam: questions examiners like to ask ETOs

Expect practical, "what would you do" questions. Answer from first principles and link back to safety:

  • Why are most ship distribution systems insulated (unearthed)? A single earth fault does not trip essential services, so supply continuity is maintained. However, the fault must be found and cleared promptly, because a second earth fault on another phase can cause a short circuit.
  • How do you trace an earth fault indicated on the switchboard? Isolate circuits one at a time, with permission and the effect on essential services considered, while watching the earth indication. Then confirm with an insulation resistance test.
  • What conditions must be met before closing a generator breaker onto live busbars? Voltage, frequency and phase sequence must match, and the incoming set must be in phase at the moment of closing.
  • What is the purpose of a reverse power trip? It protects against a generator motoring, which could damage the prime mover and overload the remaining sets.
  • What is preferential tripping? Non-essential loads are shed automatically on overload, so the essential services stay on line.
  • Walk me through safe isolation on a high-voltage panel. Work under the permit and the company procedure:
    1. Isolate.
    2. Lock and tag.
    3. Prove dead with a tested instrument.
    4. Apply earths.
    5. Issue the safety document before work starts.
  • What would you check after a blackout and recovery? Check:
    • that the cause is identified
    • that steering gear and essential pumps have restarted
    • alarm system status
    • the emergency generator's return to standby
    • that the event is recorded

Examiners want to see that you know your limits: when to stop the job and inform the chief engineer.

Key points
  • The ETO CoC is issued under STCW Regulation III/6, with the competence standard in Section A-III/6.
  • It applies to anyone serving as ETO on ships with main propulsion machinery of 750 kW or more.
  • Whether a ship must carry an ETO is set by the flag's safe manning document, not by STCW itself.
  • An ETO CoC is not an engineering watchkeeping certificate; that needs Regulation III/1.
  • Validity does not exceed five years; revalidation needs medical fitness plus proof of continued competence under Regulation I/11.
  • Flag endorsements and safety refreshers expire independently of the CoC, so check every date before travel.
  • Course content, exam format and revalidation details are set by your own administration.

Frequently asked questions

Is the ETO certificate the same as the Electro-Technical Rating certificate?

No. The ETO CoC is an officer's certificate under STCW Regulation III/6. The Electro-Technical Rating (ETR) is a rating certificate under Regulation III/7. They are not interchangeable on a crew list or safe manning document.

Can I keep an engineering watch with an ETO CoC?

Not on the strength of the ETO CoC alone. Being in charge of an engineering watch requires a Regulation III/1 certificate or higher. Some ETOs hold both, but they are separate certificates with separate requirements.

How much sea time do I need to revalidate my ETO CoC?

The main route under Section A-I/11 is at least one year in total of approved seagoing service performing ETO functions during the preceding five years. If you lack this, A-I/11 allows alternatives such as an approved test, approved courses or a short period of supernumerary service. Your administration decides which of these it offers and how you apply.

Do I need a separate high-voltage course if I hold an ETO CoC?

High-voltage installations are part of the Section A-III/6 competence standard. Even so, many companies and some administrations ask for a separate high-voltage course certificate. Check your company's requirements and your administration's rules before joining a ship with HV plant.

My CoC is valid but the flag endorsement has expired. Can I join?

Normally no. You need a valid endorsement from the ship's flag, or documentary proof that an application has been submitted. Under Regulation I/10 that proof allows service for up to three months, but only where the flag permits it. Sort this out with the company before you travel.

Test yourself

Which STCW regulation sets the mandatory minimum requirements for certification of electro-technical officers?

Regulation III/6 covers ETOs. III/1 is the engineering watch officer, III/7 is the electro-technical rating and VI/1 is basic safety training (STCW Regulation III/6).

To revalidate an ETO CoC by the sea-service route, how much approved service is normally needed?

Section A-I/11 requires at least one year in total of approved seagoing service performing functions appropriate to the certificate during the preceding five years. It does not need to be continuous (STCW Code, Section A-I/11).

Regulation III/6 applies to ETOs serving on seagoing ships with main propulsion machinery of what power?

The ETO grade applies to ships of 750 kW propulsion power or more. The 3,000 kW figure is the threshold for some engineer grades, and high-voltage plant is not the trigger (STCW Regulation III/6).

You hold a valid foreign CoC and the company has applied for the new flag's endorsement. What does Regulation I/10 allow?

A flag may permit service for a period not exceeding three months where documentary proof of the endorsement application is readily available (STCW Regulation I/10).

An ETO with only a III/6 CoC is asked to take sole charge of the engine room watch. What is correct?

The ETO CoC covers electrical, electronic and control engineering functions, not engineering watchkeeping, which is certificated under Regulation III/1 (STCW Regulations III/1 and III/6).
