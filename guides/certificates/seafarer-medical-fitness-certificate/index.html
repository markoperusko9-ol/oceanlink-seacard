Seafarer Medical Fitness Certificate: What It Is, Validity and Why Crew Get Stopped

Quick answer

Every seafarer, officer, rating, cook or cadet, must hold a valid medical fitness certificate issued by a doctor recognised by the administration. This is required by STCW Regulation I/9 and MLC 2006 Regulation 1.2. The certificate is valid for a maximum of two years (one year if you are under 18), and the colour vision part is valid for up to six years. Which doctors are acceptable and how the certificate is endorsed depend on the flag state and your national administration.

What the medical fitness certificate is

The medical fitness certificate is a doctor's statement that you are fit to do the duties you will perform at sea. You need one from the day you first go on board as a cadet or trainee, and you keep renewing it until the day you retire.

It is not the same as a company pre-employment medical (often called a PEME). A PEME is a manning agent, owner or P&I requirement and can be stricter. The statutory certificate is the one port state control, the flag and your CoC revalidation depend on. Many seafarers hold both, sometimes from the same clinic on the same day. Even then they remain different documents with different purposes.

The certificate typically confirms:

  • That your hearing is satisfactory
  • That your unaided and aided eyesight meet the standard
  • That your colour vision meets the standard, where relevant to your duties
  • Whether you are fit for lookout duties (deck)
  • That you have no condition likely to be made worse by service at sea, make you unfit, or endanger others on board
  • Any limitations on your fitness, such as wearing glasses or a restriction on trading area or duties
  • The date of the examination and the expiry date

The legal basis: STCW I/9 and MLC Regulation 1.2

Two instruments require the certificate, and they work together.

Instrument What it covers
STCW Regulation I/9 and Section A-I/9 Medical standards for seafarers certified under STCW, including the in-service eyesight standards in Table A-I/9. Also covers validity, review/appeal processes (per national provisions) and recognition of medical practitioners.
STCW Section B-I/9 Non-mandatory guidance on medical examinations and fitness standards
MLC 2006 Regulation 1.2, Standard A1.2, Guideline B1.2 No seafarer may work on a ship unless certified medically fit. This applies to all seafarers covered by MLC, including catering and hotel staff.
ILO/IMO Guidelines on the Medical Examinations of Seafarers Joint guidance for administrations and doctors on how to examine and what to assess

The certificate must also be valid when you revalidate your CoC. STCW Regulation I/11 makes meeting the medical fitness standards of Regulation I/9 a condition of revalidation.

The detailed fitness criteria are set by each administration. These include blood pressure limits, acceptable medications, diabetes, epilepsy, mental health and BMI-related assessments. The conventions set the framework and minimum eyesight standards. National medical standards fill in the rest, so a condition acceptable to one administration may carry a limitation under another.

Who needs it and who can issue it

Who needs it. Every seafarer serving on a seagoing ship covered by STCW and MLC needs one, whatever the rank or department. That includes masters, engineers, ETOs, ratings, cooks, stewards and cadets. Under STCW, a candidate for certification must also be at least 16 years of age.

Who can issue it. Only a duly qualified medical practitioner recognised by the administration can issue it. That doctor must have full professional independence in making the fitness judgement. In practice:

  • India: Examinations are done by doctors approved by the Directorate General of Shipping. Check the current approved list before you book.
  • Philippines: Examinations are done at clinics accredited under the national system (the Department of Health together with MARINA requirements). Check accreditation is current.
  • Other administrations: Many publish their own lists of approved doctors. Some recognise certificates issued under another country's system and some do not.

The flag-state question. Some flags accept your national STCW medical. Others require an examination by a doctor they have approved, or their own format. This varies by flag and changes over time. Confirm with your manning agent or the flag administration before you join, not at the airport.

The examination: what the doctor checks

The exact test list depends on the national standard and the clinic. Expect most of the following:

  1. History and declaration. You complete a medical history form. Declare everything honestly, including medication and past surgery. A false declaration can invalidate the certificate and create serious problems if something happens on board.
  2. Eyesight. Distance and near vision are tested, aided and unaided. Deck personnel with lookout duties face stricter standards than engine personnel under Table A-I/9.
  3. Colour vision. This is tested by a method recognised by the administration. Deck officers and lookouts are the most affected.
  4. Hearing. This is tested by audiometry or another accepted method.
  5. General physical examination. This covers heart, lungs, blood pressure and mobility. Mobility matters because you must be able to perform emergency duties: climbing ladders, wearing breathing apparatus, donning an immersion suit and boarding survival craft.
  6. Laboratory tests. Urine and blood tests are common, and chest X-ray or ECG may be included depending on age and national rules.
  7. Drug and alcohol screening. This is often required by the company or the flag rather than the convention itself.

If you are found unfit or given a limitation, you have a right to a review. MLC Standard A1.2 gives you the opportunity of a further examination by another independent medical practitioner or an independent medical referee. STCW Section A-I/9 requires the administration to provide a review process in line with its national appeal provisions. Ask the clinic or your administration how to apply. Do not simply go to another clinic hoping for a different answer.

Validity, expiry at sea and renewal

The conventions set maximum periods. An administration or doctor can issue a shorter one, for example to monitor a medical condition.

Item Maximum validity
Medical certificate, seafarer 18 or over 2 years
Medical certificate, seafarer under 18 1 year
Colour vision part 6 years

Expiry during a voyage. If the certificate expires during a voyage, it stays in force until the next port of call where a recognised medical practitioner is available. This extension cannot exceed three months.

Urgent cases. In urgent cases, the administration may allow a seafarer to work without a valid certificate until the next port of call with a recognised practitioner. Again the limit is three months, and the seafarer must hold an expired certificate of recent date.

Both provisions are safety valves, not a planning tool. Most companies will not send you on board with a certificate expiring during the contract. Many require it to remain valid for the whole contract plus a margin. That margin is company policy, not convention law, so check your SMS or ask your manning office.

Language. For ships ordinarily on international voyages, the certificate must be provided in English.

Why crew get stopped at joining

Common reasons a seafarer is held at the manning office, the airport or the gangway:

  • The certificate expires during the contract. It is legal on the joining date but fails company policy.
  • The doctor is not recognised by the flag. The certificate is valid nationally but not for this flag, or the clinic's approval had lapsed.
  • Restrictions conflict with the job. A "not fit for lookout" note on an OS or AB, a trading-area restriction, or a requirement to wear glasses with no glasses in your bag.
  • Colour vision is missing or expired within the six-year window.
  • Details do not match. The name or date of birth differs from your passport or CDC.
  • The PEME fails even though the statutory certificate is valid. Common causes are uncontrolled blood pressure, high blood sugar, or a positive drug or alcohol test.
  • The only copy is a phone photo. Carry the original. Port state control officers may ask to see it under MLC inspections.

Worked scenario. An AB has a medical issued 23 months ago and a nine-month contract offered next week. The certificate is valid on the joining date, but it expires about a month after joining.

The extension rule does not help him, because the vessel calls at ports with recognised doctors. The company will almost certainly refuse him. The correct action is to renew now, before joining, with a doctor recognised for that flag. He should also confirm whether the company needs a separate PEME.

Oral exam: what the examiner may ask

Examiners for operational and management level CoCs often test the regulatory side. Be ready for these:

  • Q: Which regulations require a seafarer's medical certificate? A: STCW Regulation I/9 with Section A-I/9, and MLC 2006 Regulation 1.2 with Standard A1.2.
  • Q: What is the maximum validity? A: Two years, or one year if under 18. The colour vision part is valid for up to six years.
  • Q: Your AB's medical expires mid-ocean. What happens? A: It continues in force until the next port with a recognised doctor, up to three months. Arrange an examination there and record it.
  • Q: A crew member is declared unfit. What are their rights? A: Under MLC Standard A1.2 they can have a further examination by an independent medical practitioner or referee. STCW Section A-I/9 requires the administration to provide a review process under its national appeal provisions.
  • Q: As master, what do you check on joining crew? A: Validity through the contract and any limitations against their duties, such as lookout or glasses. Also check the issuing doctor is recognised by the flag and the details match the passport and CDC.

Keeping it in date: practical tips

  • Track two dates. Note the medical expiry and the colour vision expiry separately.
  • Renew early. Do it at home between contracts, not in the last week before flying.
  • Check the approved list. Confirm the doctor is approved by your administration, and recognised by the flag of your next ship, before booking.
  • Carry spare glasses if your certificate requires correction.
  • Manage long-term conditions such as blood pressure and diabetes with your own doctor well before the exam. Bring your medication records.
  • Keep copies. Carry the original and a certified copy, and lodge a scan with your manning agent.
  • Remember CoC revalidation. It needs a valid medical, so align renewals where you can.
Key points
  • STCW Regulation I/9 and MLC Regulation 1.2 require every seafarer to hold a valid medical fitness certificate.
  • Maximum validity is two years (one year if under 18); the colour vision part can be valid for up to six years.
  • Only a doctor recognised by the administration can issue it, and flag acceptance varies, so check before joining.
  • A certificate expiring mid-voyage stays valid to the next port with a recognised doctor, for no more than three months.
  • A company PEME is separate from the statutory certificate and may have stricter standards.
  • Seafarers declared unfit or given a limitation can seek review: MLC provides an independent practitioner or referee, and STCW requires a national appeal process.
  • Most joining refusals come from expiry during the contract, unrecognised doctors, duty restrictions or mismatched details.

Frequently asked questions

How long is a seafarer medical certificate valid?

The maximum is two years for seafarers aged 18 and over, and one year for those under 18. The colour vision part can be valid for up to six years. A doctor or administration may issue a shorter validity, for example to monitor a medical condition.

Can I join a ship if my medical expires during the contract?

Legally, a certificate that expires at sea stays valid until the next port with a recognised doctor, for up to three months. However, most companies will not sign you on if it expires during the contract, and many require an extra margin. Renew before joining.

Is the PEME the same as the STCW medical certificate?

No. The statutory medical certificate is required by STCW and MLC and is issued by a doctor recognised by the administration. A PEME is a company, manning agent or P&I requirement and may include extra tests or stricter limits.

Will a foreign flag accept my Indian or Philippine medical certificate?

It depends on the flag. Some accept certificates issued under another STCW Party's national system, while others require examination by a doctor they have approved. Confirm with your manning agent or the flag administration before you travel.

What can I do if a doctor declares me unfit for sea service?

MLC 2006 gives you the right to a further examination by an independent medical practitioner or referee, and STCW requires your administration to provide a review or appeal process. Ask the issuing clinic or your national administration how to request a review. Bring your medical records and any specialist reports.
