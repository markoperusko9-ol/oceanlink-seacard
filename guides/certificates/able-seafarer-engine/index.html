Able Seafarer Engine: STCW III/5 Certificate Guide

Quick answer

Able Seafarer Engine is the STCW certificate of proficiency under Regulation III/5 for engine ratings on seagoing ships with 750 kW or more of main propulsion power. To get it you must be at least 18, medically fit and already hold Rating Forming Part of an Engineering Watch (III/4). You then need either 12 months' approved engine-department sea service, or 6 months plus approved training. Whether the certificate carries an expiry date depends on the issuing administration. Your medical expires, and you must show evidence of maintaining basic safety training standards (personal survival techniques and fire fighting refreshers) at least every five years; letting either lapse can stop you joining.

What the Able Seafarer Engine certificate is

Able Seafarer Engine (often written AB Engine or ASE) is the senior engine-rating qualification in STCW. It was added by the 2010 Manila amendments. Before that, the Convention only recognised the watch rating, Rating Forming Part of an Engineering Watch (RFPEW).

Key facts:

  • Legal basis: STCW Regulation III/5. The standard of competence is in STCW Code Section A-III/5.
  • Type of document: It is a certificate of proficiency, not a certificate of competency. It is issued by, or on behalf of, the administration under Regulation I/2.
  • Scope: It applies to ratings serving as able seafarer engine on seagoing ships with main propulsion machinery of 750 kW or more.
  • Position in the ladder: It sits above RFPEW (Regulation III/4) and below the officer CoC (Regulation III/1).

Job titles such as motorman, oiler and fitter are company and flag titles, not STCW terms. Which certificate a post requires depends on:

  • the ship's Minimum Safe Manning Document;
  • the flag state;
  • the company's crewing matrix.

On many ships the senior engine ratings are expected to hold III/5. Do not assume this; check the manning document and your contract.

Eligibility: who can apply

Regulation III/5 sets the minimum requirements. Your administration may add more of its own.

Requirement What STCW asks
Age Not less than 18 years
Medical Meet the medical fitness standards of Regulation I/9 and Section A-I/9
Prior qualification Be qualified as RFPEW (Regulation III/4)
Sea service While qualified as RFPEW, approved engine-department seagoing service of either 12 months, or 6 months plus approved training
Competence Meet the standard in Section A-III/5

Two points catch people out:

  1. The sea service clock starts after you are RFPEW-qualified. Engine-room time served before your III/4 certificate does not count towards III/5 under the Convention wording.
  2. "Approved" means approved by your administration. Your sea service must be:
    • documented in the form they accept (CDC entries, sea service testimonials signed by Master or Chief Engineer, and so on);
    • on ships that qualify under their rules.

Basic safety training under Regulation VI/1 is a separate requirement for anyone with safety or pollution-prevention duties. You will need it valid in any case.

What the course and assessment cover

Section A-III/5 sets the competences at support level. In paraphrase, they cover four areas.

Marine engineering

  • Contributing to a watch in a manned engine room, or as designated duty in a periodically unmanned engine room.
  • Contributing to monitoring and controlling an engine-room watch.
  • Contributing to fuelling and oil transfer operations.
  • Contributing to bilge and ballast operations.
  • Contributing to the operation of equipment and machinery.

Electrical, electronic and control engineering

  • Contributing to the safe operation of electrical equipment.

Maintenance and repair

  • Contributing to shipboard maintenance and repair.
  • Contributing to the maintenance and repair of machinery and equipment.
  • In practice this means safe use of hand tools, machine tools and measuring instruments, and an understanding of materials and safe working practices in the workshop.

Controlling the operation of the ship and care for persons on board

  • Contributing to the handling of stores.
  • Applying precautions to prevent pollution of the marine environment.
  • Applying occupational health and safety procedures.

How competence is shown: typically through a combination of:

  • approved in-service experience;
  • workshop skills training;
  • practical demonstration;
  • assessment.

If you take the 6-month route, the approved training course covers this ground. Its length, format, assessment method and whether an oral is required all depend on the administration and the approved training provider. Check with your national maritime administration before you book anything.

Validity and revalidation

This is where seafarers get conflicting answers. The honest position is:

  • STCW does not set a fixed revalidation cycle for III/5. The five-yearly revalidation in Regulation I/11 is written mainly around certificates of competency and certain specialised proficiencies.
  • The administration decides the format. Some issue the III/5 certificate without an expiry date. Others print a validity period or link it to other documents. Read what is printed on your own certificate and follow your administration's current circulars.
  • The supporting documents still need to be kept current:
    • your medical fitness certificate expires;
    • you must show evidence of maintaining basic safety training standards (personal survival techniques and fire fighting refreshers) at least every five years under Section A-VI/1;
    • other proficiencies needed for the ship type, such as tanker endorsements, may have their own validity periods. Check your administration's requirements.

In practice, an "unlimited" III/5 is worthless at the gangway if your BST refresher or medical has lapsed.

Worked scenario: wiper to Able Seafarer Engine

Ravi joins as a wiper with BST and a medical, but no watchkeeping certificate. A typical path, subject to his administration's exact rules, looks like this:

  1. Get RFPEW (III/4). He completes the required engine-room service and training, which depends on the route his administration offers. His administration then issues the III/4 certificate of proficiency.
  2. Start counting III/5 service from that date. He serves as an engine rating on ships of 750 kW or more. He collects a signed sea service testimonial at every sign-off, not months later by email.
  3. Choose the route:
    • Route A: 12 months' approved sea service after RFPEW, no additional course;
    • Route B: 6 months' sea service plus an approved training course.
  4. Apply to the administration with:
    • CDC or seaman's book;
    • sea service testimonials;
    • RFPEW certificate;
    • valid medical;
    • BST;
    • course certificate, if Route B;
    • any national identity numbers required (in India, for example, the INDoS number).
  5. Receive the III/5 certificate of proficiency. The certificate issued by the administration is the qualification. The training centre's course completion certificate on its own is not.

Common error: Ravi counts his wiper days from before RFPEW, applies at six months, and is refused for insufficient qualifying service.

Why crew get stopped at joining

Agents, Masters and Port State Control officers regularly find these problems:

  • Wrong level of certificate. The crew list or manning document requires III/5, but the rating holds only III/4.
  • Course certificate instead of CoP. The rating produces a training centre certificate but not the administration-issued certificate of proficiency.
  • Expired supporting documents. Medical or BST refresher has lapsed, or will lapse during the contract and the company's policy does not allow that.
  • Unverifiable certificate. Many administrations offer online verification. If the number does not appear, expect the joining to stop until it is resolved.
  • Name or date of birth mismatch between the passport, CDC and certificate.
  • Flag-specific requirements not checked. Some flags or companies require additional documentation for ratings. Ask the manning office in writing before you travel.
  • Damaged or illegible originals. Carry the originals in a waterproof wallet, with scans stored separately.

A fraudulent or purchased certificate is a career-ending risk. It can also be criminal, and verification checks are routine.

Oral exam style: questions a Chief Engineer or assessor may ask

Expect practical questions rather than regulation numbers.

  • "What do you check on a routine engine-room round?"
    • Levels: sumps, header tanks, day and settling tanks.
    • Temperatures and pressures against normal readings.
    • Leaks, unusual noise or vibration, bilge levels.
    • Fire hazards such as oil-soaked lagging.
    • Report anything abnormal to the duty engineer at once.
  • "What is your role during bunkering?"
    • Follow the bunker plan and checklist.
    • Monitor soundings and ullages as directed.
    • Watch for leaks at flanges and vents.
    • Know where the SOPEP equipment is.
    • Know the agreed stop signal.
  • "Before working on an electric motor, what must happen?"
    • Isolation under the permit-to-work and lock-out tag-out procedure.
    • Verification that the equipment is dead.
    • Never rely only on someone else's word that it is switched off.
  • "What would make you stop a job?"
    • Any unsafe condition, missing permit, failed gas test or change in conditions. Stop-work authority applies to you.
  • "How do you prevent pollution from the bilges?"
    • Never bypass the oily water separator.
    • Record operations as instructed.
    • Report any irregular overboard connection.

Keeping your Able Seafarer Engine profile in date

  • Keep a document expiry list for your medical, BST refreshers, security training, ship-type courses, passport and visas. Plan renewals during leave, not in the week before joining.
  • Get testimonials at every sign-off, signed and stamped by the Master or Chief Engineer, with:
    • ship name and IMO number;
    • propulsion power;
    • rank;
    • dates.
  • Check the propulsion power. Service on ships below 750 kW may not count towards, or require, III/5. Confirm with your administration before relying on it.
  • Verify your own certificate online where your administration offers it, and fix any data errors early.
  • Read administration circulars. Validity rules for ratings' certificates can change, especially when STCW is amended.
  • Plan upward. III/5 service and workshop skills are a good base for an engine officer route, but III/1 is a separate qualification with its own training and assessment requirements.
Key points
  • Able Seafarer Engine is an STCW certificate of proficiency under Regulation III/5, with competence standards in Section A-III/5.
  • It applies to ratings on seagoing ships with main propulsion machinery of 750 kW or more.
  • Minimum entry: age 18, medically fit and already qualified as RFPEW (III/4).
  • Sea service counts only after you hold RFPEW: 12 months, or 6 months plus approved training.
  • Whether the certificate expires is set by your administration; your medical expires and BST elements need refreshing every five years.
  • A training centre course certificate is not the administration-issued certificate of proficiency.
  • Collect signed sea service testimonials at every sign-off.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between RFPEW and Able Seafarer Engine?

RFPEW (STCW Regulation III/4) qualifies you to form part of an engineering watch. Able Seafarer Engine (III/5) is the senior rating level and adds maintenance, repair, fuelling support and stores handling competences. You must hold RFPEW before you can qualify for III/5.

How much sea time do I need for Able Seafarer Engine?

STCW requires approved engine-department sea service while qualified as RFPEW. That is either 12 months, or 6 months plus approved training. Your administration decides what service counts as approved and how it must be documented.

Does the Able Seafarer Engine certificate expire?

It depends on the issuing administration. Some issue it without an expiry date and others set a validity period. Regardless, your medical certificate expires and you must show evidence of maintaining basic safety training standards (personal survival techniques and fire fighting refreshers) at least every five years; letting either lapse can stop you joining.

Do motormen and oilers need Able Seafarer Engine?

Motorman and oiler are company job titles, not STCW terms. Whether the post requires III/5 depends on the ship's Minimum Safe Manning Document, the flag and the company's crewing requirements. Check before accepting the contract.

Is the certificate from my training centre enough to join?

Usually not. The qualification is the certificate of proficiency issued by, or on behalf of, your maritime administration. A course completion certificate is normally one of the documents you submit to obtain it.

Test yourself

Which STCW regulation covers Able Seafarer Engine?

Able Seafarer Engine is Regulation III/5. III/4 is RFPEW, II/5 is Able Seafarer Deck and III/6 is ETO. Reference: STCW Convention, Regulation III/5.

Which qualification must you already hold before qualifying as Able Seafarer Engine?

Regulation III/5 requires the candidate to be qualified as RFPEW under Regulation III/4. Reference: STCW Regulation III/5.

Without additional approved training, how much approved engine-department sea service is needed while qualified as RFPEW?

The requirement is 12 months, or 6 months if combined with approved training. Reference: STCW Regulation III/5.

Regulation III/5 applies to ratings on seagoing ships with main propulsion machinery of at least:

The threshold is 750 kW propulsion power. The 3,000 kW figure relates to officer CoC limits, not to III/5. Reference: STCW Regulation III/5.

What is the minimum age for an Able Seafarer Engine certificate under STCW?

Regulation III/5 requires the candidate to be not less than 18 years of age. Reference: STCW Regulation III/5.
