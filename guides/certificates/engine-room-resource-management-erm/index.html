Engine Room Resource Management (ERM): what it is, who needs it and how to keep it valid

Quick answer

Engine Room Resource Management (ERM) is a competence required of engineer officers under the STCW Code since the 2010 Manila amendments. It covers using people, equipment and information so that one person's mistake does not become a casualty. Most administrations and companies expect documentary proof, usually an approved course certificate. Whether that certificate expires depends on your administration and your company, not on STCW itself.

What ERM actually is

ERM is the engine-room counterpart of Bridge Resource Management. It is not about thermodynamics or plant knowledge. It is about how the watch, the department and the ship's resources are organised and used, especially when something goes wrong.

The idea comes from accident investigation. Many engine-room casualties had no single technical cause. They followed a chain of small failures, such as:

  • an alarm acknowledged and not investigated
  • a junior who saw a problem but did not speak up
  • a handover that missed a valve left in an unusual position
  • a tired engineer working alone on a job that needed two people
  • a second engineer and a chief engineer each assuming the other had informed the bridge

ERM training teaches you to recognise that chain and break it early.

Which regulations require it

ERM became a formal STCW requirement with the 2010 Manila amendments. Those amendments entered into force on 1 January 2012, with a transitional period that ended on 1 January 2017.

  • Operational level (STCW Code, Table A-III/1): engine-room resource management knowledge forms part of the competence for maintaining a safe engineering watch. The same table also requires leadership and teamworking skills.
  • Management level (STCW Code, Table A-III/2): leadership and managerial skills are required, and these build on ERM principles.
  • Watchkeeping (STCW Code, Section A-VIII/2): the principles for keeping an engineering watch include engine-room resource management. Section B-VIII/2 gives further guidance.

The principles named in the Code include:

  • allocation, assignment and prioritisation of resources
  • effective communication
  • assertiveness and leadership
  • obtaining and maintaining situational awareness
  • taking account of team experience

An important point many people miss: STCW does not create a separate "ERM certificate" in the way it creates Basic Safety Training. ERM is a competence you must show to obtain your Certificate of Competency. Each administration decides how you prove it. Many approve a standalone ERM course. Some combine it with leadership and teamwork or leadership and managerial skills courses. Others build it into the CoC programme or simulator assessment.

Under the ISM Code, the company must make sure personnel are properly trained. That is why many SMS crew matrices list ERM as a separate document, even where the flag does not.

If you obtained your CoC before the Manila amendments, you may have been required to do an ERM or leadership course to revalidate or upgrade after 2017. How your administration handled that transition is something to confirm with them directly.

What the course covers

Course length, format and simulator use depend on the approving administration and the training centre. Most approved courses cover:

  • Human error and the error chain: how incidents build up, illustrated with real casualty case studies.
  • Situational awareness: knowing plant status, trends and the ship's situation (manoeuvring, pilotage, narrow waters).
  • Communication: closed-loop orders, clear handovers, toolbox talks and communication with the bridge.
  • Assertiveness and challenge: how a junior raises a concern, and how a senior listens without losing authority.
  • Leadership and team management: briefing, delegation and allocating people according to their experience.
  • Workload, fatigue and stress: rest hours, recognising overload and asking for help early.
  • Decision-making under pressure: prioritising during alarms, blackouts and fires.
  • Simulator exercises (where provided): for example a blackout while manoeuvring, a purifier fire or a lube oil low-pressure trip, with debriefs on team behaviour.

The assessment usually looks at behaviour in the exercises, not just a written test.

Worked scenario: blackout during port approach

The vessel is approaching the pilot station on stand-by. The 3rd engineer is on watch in the ECR and the motorman is on the floor plates. One generator trips and the standby fails to take load, causing a blackout.

Poor ERM:

  • The 3/E runs to the generator himself and leaves the ECR unmanned.
  • Nobody informs the bridge.
  • The motorman, unsure what to do, starts resetting breakers.
  • The 2/E arrives and starts a second recovery without asking what has already been done.

Good ERM:

  1. The 3/E informs the bridge immediately, using the ship's agreed procedure, and calls the 2/E or C/E.
  2. The 3/E stays at the ECR as the information point and sends the motorman to a defined task, such as checking a specific generator.
  3. The 3/E follows the blackout recovery procedure in the SMS and uses closed-loop orders: "Start No. 2 generator" – "Starting No. 2 generator" – "No. 2 running."
  4. When the senior engineer arrives, there is a short, structured status briefing before the senior takes control.
  5. After restoration, a debrief identifies why the standby failed. The cause is recorded and the defect is raised.

The technical recovery is identical in both cases. The difference is organisation, communication and situational awareness, and that difference is what ERM trains.

Oral exam: questions examiners like

Expect ERM to come up in management-level orals and in company interviews. Typical questions:

  • "What would you do if your chief engineer gave an order you believed was unsafe?" Raise the concern clearly and respectfully, giving the reason. If the danger remains, escalate as your SMS allows. Never stay silent.
  • "How do you hand over a watch?" Cover plant status, running and standby machinery, defects, work in progress, isolations or permits in force, the bridge's intentions, and anything abnormal. Make sure the relief is fit and has fully understood before you leave.
  • "When must the chief engineer be called?" Refer to the standing orders and your company procedures. Typical triggers include serious machinery failure, fire, flooding, any doubt about safety, and when you are unsure.
  • "Give an example of an error chain." Use a real incident you know, and say where it could have been broken.
  • "How do you manage a new junior engineer?" Familiarise them, match tasks to their experience, supervise and check their understanding.

Examiners want to hear judgement and clear reasoning, not memorised definitions.

Why crew get stopped at joining, and how to avoid it

Common problems at document check or flag endorsement stage:

  • Course not approved by the right administration. A certificate from a centre that the issuing or flag administration does not recognise may be rejected.
  • Pre-Manila certificate. An older ERM certificate issued before the 2010 amendments may not include the leadership and teamworking elements now required. Some companies insist on a newer course.
  • Company or charterer requirements. Tanker and gas operators often set their own rules, such as a refresher interval or a simulator-based course. Your CoC may be valid while you still fail the company matrix.
  • Name or number mismatch. The name or passport number on the certificate differs from your passport or CDC.
  • No original or verifiable copy. Many companies check certificates through the training centre's or administration's online verification. If that check fails, you can be held at the office.
  • Promoted without the management-level element. You were promoted to 2/E, but the leadership and managerial skills requirement was not completed.

Tips for keeping it in date:

  • Find out from your administration whether your ERM certificate carries an expiry date. Many do not, but company refresher rules may still apply.
  • Read your company's training matrix before each contract, not at the airport.
  • Keep scanned copies with your CoC and check that verification works online.
  • When upgrading your CoC, confirm which leadership or ERM course the next level requires.
  • If you change to a company with a different flag, check whether that flag's endorsement process accepts your ERM evidence.
Key points
  • ERM is an STCW competence for engineer officers, introduced by the 2010 Manila amendments.
  • STCW Code Tables A-III/1 and A-III/2 and Section A-VIII/2 are the key references.
  • STCW does not create a standalone ERM certificate; each administration decides how you prove it, usually through an approved course.
  • Expiry and refresher rules depend on the administration and the company, not on STCW itself.
  • Core principles: resource allocation, communication, assertiveness and leadership, situational awareness, and taking account of team experience.
  • Crew are most often stopped over unapproved providers, pre-Manila certificates or company matrix requirements.
  • Check your company training matrix and online certificate verification before every contract.

Frequently asked questions

Is ERM mandatory under STCW?

Yes, ERM knowledge is a required competence for engineer officers under the STCW Code, mainly in Table A-III/1 and in the watchkeeping principles of Section A-VIII/2. STCW does not prescribe a standalone certificate. Your administration decides what evidence it accepts, which in most cases is an approved course certificate.

Does the ERM certificate expire?

STCW does not set a revalidation period for ERM in the way it does for the CoC or basic safety refreshers. Many administrations issue ERM certificates with no expiry date. However, some companies and charterers require a refresher at set intervals. Check both your administration's rules and your company matrix.

Is ERM the same as BRM?

The principles are similar: teamwork, communication, situational awareness and challenge. BRM is built around the bridge team and navigation, while ERM is built around the engine department and machinery operations. Some centres run combined courses. Whether a combined course satisfies your requirement depends on your administration's approval.

Do I need a new ERM course when I upgrade to second or chief engineer?

Management level brings its own requirement for leadership and managerial skills under Table A-III/2. Many administrations deliver this as a separate course from operational-level ERM. Confirm with your administration which course the management-level CoC requires before you apply.

My ERM certificate is from before 2012. Is it still accepted?

It may or may not be, depending on how your administration handled the Manila transitional arrangements and on your company's policy. Older courses often lack the leadership and teamworking content added in 2010. Many companies now ask for a newer course, so check before you join.

Test yourself

Which STCW Code table contains the operational-level engineering competence that includes engine-room resource management knowledge?

ERM knowledge is part of maintaining a safe engineering watch at operational level. Reference: STCW Code, Table A-III/1.

Which set of amendments made ERM and leadership a formal STCW requirement for engineers?

The 2010 Manila amendments added ERM and leadership competences. They entered into force on 1 January 2012, with transition to 2017. Reference: STCW Convention as amended 2010.

During a blackout on stand-by, what should the watchkeeping engineer do first, under good ERM practice?

Early communication with the bridge and seniors, plus coordinated tasking, keeps situational awareness intact. Reference: STCW Code, Section A-VIII/2 engineering watch principles.

Which statement about ERM certificate validity is correct?

STCW sets the competence, not a fixed ERM revalidation period. Administrations and company SMS set any expiry or refresher. Reference: STCW Code, Table A-III/1; ISM Code.

Which of the following is listed among the ERM principles in the STCW Code?

The Code lists assertiveness and leadership alongside communication, situational awareness, resource allocation and team experience. Reference: STCW Code, Table A-III/1.
