Certificate of Competency – Second Engineer and Chief Engineer

Quick answer

The Second Engineer and Chief Engineer CoC is the management-level engineering certificate under STCW Chapter III. Regulation III/2 covers ships of 3,000 kW propulsion power or more, and Regulation III/3 covers ships between 750 kW and 3,000 kW. You need it to sail as second or chief, and it must be revalidated at intervals of no more than five years. On a foreign flag it also needs a flag state endorsement, and the exact exam and paperwork route depends on the administration that issues it.

What this certificate is and where it comes from

This is the management-level engineering Certificate of Competency. Your watchkeeping CoC (STCW Regulation III/1) says you can stand an engineering watch. The management-level CoC says you can run the engine department.

There are two power bands in STCW Chapter III:

  • Regulation III/2: chief engineer and second engineer on ships with main propulsion machinery of 3,000 kW or more. The competence standard is in STCW Code Section A-III/2.
  • Regulation III/3: chief and second engineer on ships between 750 kW and 3,000 kW. The standard is in Section A-III/3.

Second and chief are assessed against the same management-level competence table. The difference between them is mainly the sea service you need before you can be certified.

MLC 2006 Regulation 1.3 adds a general requirement. Seafarers must be trained or certified as competent for their duties, and STCW certification is the usual way that is met.

Administrations name these certificates differently. In India they are commonly called MEO Class II (second) and Class I (chief). Other administrations have their own titles. Always go by what the capacity and limitations on the certificate actually say, not by the name.

Who needs it and the sea-service route

You need this CoC to sign on as second engineer or chief engineer on a seagoing ship within the power band it covers. Each grade has its own entry conditions under Regulation III/2.

Second engineer - Already hold the requirements for officer in charge of an engineering watch (III/1). - Have at least 12 months of approved seagoing service as assistant engineer officer or engineer officer. - Complete approved education and training and meet the Section A-III/2 standard.

Chief engineer - Meet the requirements for second engineer. - Have at least 36 months of approved seagoing service, of which at least 12 months must be in a position of responsibility while qualified to serve as second.

The III/3 (750–3,000 kW) route has its own service requirements. Check the regulation and your administration's rules before you plan a career path around smaller tonnage.

Your administration decides several things on top of STCW:

  • what counts as "approved" service;
  • how service is verified (discharge book, company letters, online records);
  • whether time on certain ship types counts.

Get your service verified before you book a course. That is where most applications stall.

What the course and examination cover

Section A-III/2 groups management-level competence under four functions:

Function Typical content
Marine engineering Planning and scheduling operations, managing propulsion plant and auxiliaries, fuel, lubrication and ballast operations
Electrical, electronic and control engineering Managing electrical and control equipment, fault diagnosis
Maintenance and repair Safe working practices, maintenance planning, detecting and identifying machinery malfunctions
Controlling ship operation and care for persons on board Stability and trim, pollution prevention, damage control, legislative compliance, leadership and managerial skills

The 2010 Manila amendments added leadership and managerial skills at management level. They also introduced high-voltage competence (systems above 1,000 V). Many administrations meet these through separate approved courses, such as engine room resource management or high-voltage training at management level. Others build them into the main course.

The structure of the exam varies by administration. Typically you can expect:

  • a mandatory approved preparatory course;
  • written papers (for example general engineering knowledge, motor or steam engineering, electrotechnology, naval architecture, safety and legislation);
  • an oral examination by a surveyor or examiner;
  • sometimes a practical or simulator assessment.

Course length, fees, the number of attempts allowed and the sequence of papers are all set by your administration. Get them from the official notices, not from a training institute's advert.

Validity, revalidation and the documents around it

Under Regulation I/11, every officer serving at sea, or returning to sea, must revalidate at intervals of no more than five years. To revalidate you must:

  1. Meet the medical fitness standards of Regulation I/9.
  2. Prove continued professional competence under Section A-I/11 by one of these routes:
    • (a) at least 12 months of approved seagoing service performing functions appropriate to the certificate held, in total during the preceding five years; or
    • (b) having performed functions considered equivalent to the required seagoing service; or
    • (c) passing an approved test or completing approved course(s); or
    • (d) at least 3 months of approved seagoing service as a supernumerary, or in a lower officer rank, immediately before taking up the rank the certificate entitles you to.

Some administrations also accept 3 months' service in the last 6 months. Check yours.

Around the CoC you also need several other documents in date:

  • Basic safety refreshers. Proof of continued competence in PST and FPFF (Section A-VI/1), PSCRB (Section A-VI/2) and AFF (Section A-VI/3), at intervals of no more than five years.
  • Medical certificate. STCW and MLC cap it at two years for adults, and it can be shorter if the doctor limits it.
  • Flag state endorsement (Regulation I/10) when the ship's flag is not your issuing administration. The endorsement cannot outlast the CoC it recognises. Regulation I/10 allows the flag administration to permit service for up to three months while you carry documentary proof that the endorsement application has been submitted. Check that the flag applies this.
  • Tanker endorsements. Advanced oil, chemical or liquefied gas tanker training under Regulations V/1-1 and V/1-2 applies to chief and second engineers, as well as to the master and chief mate.

Some administrations reissue the CoC itself at revalidation. Others issue a separate revalidation endorsement. Know which yours does.

Why second and chief engineers get stopped at joining

Most refusals at joining are paperwork problems, not competence problems. These are the usual causes:

  • Power limitation. You hold a III/3 (under 3,000 kW) certificate but the ship is above 3,000 kW.
  • Other limitations. The CoC is restricted to motor ships, near-coastal voyages or a particular propulsion type that does not match the ship.
  • Capacity mismatch. You hold a second engineer CoC but are being signed on as chief. Safe manning and port state control will not accept it.
  • Flag endorsement missing or expired. Regulation I/10 allows the flag administration to permit service for up to three months while you carry documentary proof that the endorsement application has been submitted. Check that the flag applies this. Without the endorsement, and without a flag concession of this kind, you cannot sail.
  • Missing tanker endorsement for the cargo type, or the wrong one (oil and chemical versus gas).
  • Refresher certificates lapsed. PST, FPFF, AFF or PSCRB out of date, even though the CoC itself is valid.
  • Medical expiring mid-contract, or a medical carrying restrictions the company did not notice.
  • Online verification fails. The certificate number or name does not match the administration's database, often because of name spelling differences between passport, CDC and CoC.
  • CoC expiring during the contract, where the company's policy (or the charterer's) does not accept that risk.

Worked scenario: planning a revalidation around a contract

A second engineer holds a III/2 CoC that expires in four months. He has been offered a six-month contract on a foreign-flag product tanker.

  1. Check sea service. Add up time in the previous five years served performing functions appropriate to his certificate (check whether his administration accepts service in a lower rank). If it is 12 months or more in total, he qualifies for revalidation on service alone.
  2. Check the refreshers. If his FPFF refresher expires in eight months, renew it now. The CoC and the refreshers go together at the gangway.
  3. Check the medical. It must cover the full contract, not just the joining date.
  4. Revalidate before joining. He applies to his administration with the service proof and receives the revalidated CoC.
  5. Re-endorse. The old flag endorsement was tied to the old CoC, so he applies for a new one. If the endorsement has not arrived, he checks whether the flag permits service on proof of application. If it does, he carries the application receipt, keeping in mind the three-month limit.
  6. Tanker endorsement. He confirms that his advanced oil tanker training endorsement is valid and matches the ship type.

If he joins without doing this, he risks the CoC expiring at sea. That leaves the ship short of a certificated second engineer under its safe manning document, which is a problem for port state control and for the master.

Oral exam: what examiners typically probe

Examiners at management level want decision-making and responsibility, not textbook recitation. Expect questions like these:

  • "You have taken over as chief. What do you check in the first 24 hours?"
  • "Scavenge fire on the main engine at sea. Walk me through your actions and your report to the master."
  • "How do you verify a bunker delivery and protect the ship if the sample is disputed?"
  • "The oily water separator alarm keeps tripping. What are your options, and what can you never do?"
  • "Under the ISM Code, how do you handle a non-conformity found in the engine department?"
  • "Blackout manoeuvring in a channel. Sequence of recovery?"
  • "How do you plan a high-voltage isolation, and who signs what?"

Tips from the other side of the table:

  • Answer in sequence: immediate safety, then containment, then reporting, then record-keeping.
  • Name the relevant document: Oil Record Book, permit to work, PMS, SMS procedure.
  • Say "I would consult the manufacturer's instructions and company SMS" when it is genuinely true. Do not use it as a dodge.
  • If you don't know, say so and say how you would find out. Guessing a figure is worse.
Key points
  • STCW Regulation III/2 covers chief and second engineers on ships of 3,000 kW or more; III/3 covers 750–3,000 kW.
  • Second engineer needs at least 12 months of approved engineer service; chief needs 36 months, including 12 months while qualified as second.
  • Revalidate at intervals of no more than five years, usually with 12 months of service in the last five years.
  • Keep PST, FPFF, AFF and PSCRB refreshers and the medical in date alongside the CoC.
  • On a foreign flag you need a flag state endorsement; the flag may permit up to three months' service on proof of application, so check that it does.
  • Chief and second engineers on tankers need the matching advanced tanker endorsement.
  • Exam format, fees and course length are set by your administration; check its official notices.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sail as chief engineer with a second engineer CoC?

Not as the certificated chief engineer required by the safe manning document. You need a CoC endorsed for chief engineer within the correct power band. Under STCW Article VIII, a flag state may not grant a dispensation for a chief engineer except in circumstances of force majeure, and then only for the shortest possible period. It is never a company decision.

How long is a Second or Chief Engineer CoC valid?

Under STCW Regulation I/11 it must be revalidated at intervals of no more than five years. The expiry date printed on your certificate or revalidation endorsement is what counts. Any flag state endorsement expires no later than the CoC it is based on.

What if I don't have 12 months of sea service in the last five years?

Section A-I/11 offers three alternatives. You can show you have performed functions considered equivalent. You can pass an approved test or complete approved course(s). Or you can complete at least 3 months of approved service as a supernumerary or in a lower officer rank immediately before taking up the rank. Some administrations also accept 3 months' service in the last 6 months, so check your administration's official guidance before planning.

Does my III/3 (under 3,000 kW) CoC let me serve on a bigger ship?

No. The power limitation is printed on the certificate and checked at joining and by port state control. Moving up to III/2 needs whatever additional service, training and examination your administration requires.

Do I need a separate high-voltage course for the management-level CoC?

STCW includes management-level competence for systems above 1,000 V. Many administrations require a separate approved high-voltage course as evidence of it, while others cover it inside the main programme. Check your administration's list of mandatory courses for the exam.

Test yourself

Which STCW regulation covers certification of chief and second engineers on ships of 3,000 kW propulsion power or more?

Regulation III/2 covers chief and second engineers on ships of 3,000 kW or more. III/1 is the watchkeeping level, III/3 covers 750–3,000 kW and III/6 is the ETO (STCW Chapter III).

What is the minimum total approved seagoing service in the preceding five years that, on its own, satisfies the continued-competence requirement for revalidation under Section A-I/11?

Section A-I/11 requires at least 12 months of approved seagoing service, performing functions appropriate to the certificate held, in total during the preceding five years (STCW Code, Section A-I/11).

You join a foreign-flag ship with proof that your flag state endorsement application has been submitted. How long may you serve on that basis?

Regulation I/10 allows service for up to three months while carrying documentary proof that the endorsement application has been submitted, if the flag administration permits it (STCW Regulation I/10).

What is the maximum validity of a seafarer medical certificate for an adult under STCW?

STCW Regulation I/9 and MLC Standard A1.2 cap the medical certificate at two years for seafarers aged 18 and over.

On an oil tanker, which officers must hold advanced oil tanker cargo operations training?

Regulation V/1-1 applies the advanced training requirement to masters, chief engineers, chief mates, second engineers and others with immediate responsibility for cargo operations (STCW Regulation V/1-1).
