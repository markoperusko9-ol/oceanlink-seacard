STCW Basic Safety Training: What It Covers, Who Needs It and How to Keep It Valid

Quick answer

STCW Basic Safety Training is required under STCW Regulation VI/1 and Section A-VI/1 before you take up any shipboard duty with safety or pollution-prevention responsibilities. It has four elements: Personal Survival Techniques, Fire Prevention and Fire Fighting, Elementary First Aid, and Personal Safety and Social Responsibilities. You must show every five years that you have kept up your competence in PST and FPFF. Your flag state and administration decide how that is evidenced and certified.

What STCW Basic Safety Training actually is

Basic Safety Training (BST, also called Basic Training or STCW Basic) is the entry-level safety qualification required for seafarers with designated safety or pollution-prevention duties on seagoing merchant ships. In practice that means almost all crew. It is not one skill. It is four separate competences, each with its own table of required knowledge in the STCW Code:

Element STCW Code table What it is about
Personal Survival Techniques (PST) Table A-VI/1-1 Surviving at sea if the ship is abandoned
Fire Prevention and Fire Fighting (FPFF) Table A-VI/1-2 Preventing fires and fighting them as part of a team
Elementary First Aid (EFA) Table A-VI/1-3 Immediate action when someone is injured or taken ill
Personal Safety and Social Responsibilities (PSSR) Table A-VI/1-4 Safe working, pollution prevention, shipboard communication and relationships

Some administrations issue one combined Basic Training certificate. Others issue four separate certificates of proficiency. Both meet STCW as long as each competence has been covered by approved training, assessed to the Section A-VI/1 standard, and certified by or under the authority of the Administration.

Ship Security Awareness, and Designated Security Duties where it applies, is a different requirement under Section A-VI/6. Training centres often sell it in the same package, but it is not part of Basic Training.

Where the requirement comes from

  • STCW Convention, Regulation VI/1: sets the mandatory minimum requirements for safety familiarisation, basic training and instruction for all seafarers.
  • STCW Code, Section A-VI/1: gives the detailed standard of competence for the four elements. It also contains the five-yearly requirement to show you have maintained competence in PST and FPFF.
  • STCW Regulation I/2: covers the issue of certificates and documentary evidence, including certificates of proficiency.
  • MLC 2006, Regulation 1.3: says seafarers must not work on a ship unless they are trained or certified as competent for their duties. It also requires them to have completed training for personal safety on board.

Keep two ideas separate. Safety familiarisation is given on board to every person employed or engaged on the ship, before they are assigned duties. It applies even to people with no designated safety role. Basic Training is a shore-based, certificated course. It is required for seafarers who form part of the ship's complement with designated safety or pollution-prevention duties.

Who needs it

In practice, nearly everyone serving on a seagoing merchant ship needs BST:

  • All deck and engine officers, from cadet to Master and Chief Engineer.
  • All ratings: OS, AB, bosun, wiper, oiler, motorman, fitter, pumpman.
  • Catering crew (cook, steward, messman). They have muster list duties like everyone else.
  • Cadets and trainees, before they join for their first sea service.

STCW lets administrations make certain exemptions, for example by size of ship or nature of the voyage. Passenger ships also have extra rules for some personnel. Do not assume an exemption applies to you. Whether one exists is a decision for the flag state, not for the crewing agent or the individual seafarer.

The offshore sector often requires BOSIET/HUET on top of STCW BST, depending on the operator and region. These are industry requirements that sit alongside STCW. They do not replace it.

What the course covers

The detailed syllabus is set by Section A-VI/1. Course length and structure are set by each administration and approved training institute, so check with them rather than relying on a figure from another country.

PST - Types of emergency and the alarm signals. - Life-saving appliances and where they are stowed. - Donning a lifejacket and immersion suit. - Jumping safely into the water. - Righting a capsized liferaft. - Boarding survival craft. - Initial actions after boarding. - Survival priorities: protection, location, water, food.

FPFF - Fire chemistry and sources of ignition. - Fire detection and fixed systems. - Fire-fighting equipment and extinguisher selection. - Shipboard fire organisation. - Practical fire-fighting with breathing apparatus. - Escape through smoke-filled spaces.

EFA - Assessing a casualty. - Basic life support (CPR). - Control of bleeding. - Management of shock, burns and electric shock. - Rescue and transport of a casualty.

PSSR - Emergency procedures and following safe working practices. - Preventing marine pollution. - Effective communication on board. - Human relationships on board. - Understanding and controlling fatigue.

PST and FPFF have substantial practical assessment, in the pool and in the fire ground. They are also the two basic training elements for which STCW requires five-yearly evidence of maintained competence.

Validity and refresher training: the part that catches people out

Section A-VI/1 requires seafarers qualified in basic training to provide evidence every five years that they have maintained competence in PST and FPFF. This came in with the 2010 Manila Amendments. Since the transitional period ended on 1 January 2017, a PST or FPFF certificate from years ago with no refresher behind it will not be accepted.

How this works depends on the administration:

  • Format of evidence. Many administrations require an approved refresher (updating) course and issue a new certificate with an expiry date. Others issue certificates without printed expiry but still expect five-yearly evidence.
  • Onboard training and experience. The Code allows administrations to accept onboard training and experience for some elements of maintaining competence. Not all do, and the elements accepted vary.
  • EFA and PSSR. STCW does not set a five-yearly refresher for these two elements. Some administrations, companies or charterers still ask for recent certificates, so check your contract and company SMS.

Worked check before signing a contract

  1. List the issue and expiry dates of your PST and FPFF certificates, or of your combined BT certificate.
  2. Compare them with the end date of the contract plus a margin for extensions, not just the joining date. Many companies will not send you if a certificate expires mid-contract.
  3. Confirm the certificate is verifiable in your administration's online system, where one exists (for example the DG Shipping database for Indian seafarers, linked to your INDoS number).
  4. Check that the flag state of the new ship accepts your certificate. If you need a flag endorsement recognising your CoC, many flags also ask for your BST documentation as part of the application, so check that flag's requirements.

Common reasons crew are stopped at joining

Most refusals at the crewing office, the airport or the gangway come down to a handful of avoidable problems:

  • PST or FPFF expired, or expiring during the contract. This is the commonest one by far.
  • Old certificates with no refresher, issued before the Manila Amendments took full effect.
  • Certificate not traceable in the administration's database. It may come from an unapproved institute, or be a fake or a typing error.
  • Name or date of birth mismatch between certificate, passport, seaman's book and CoC.
  • Missing elements. You hold PST and FPFF but cannot produce EFA or PSSR, or the reverse.
  • Security training confused with BST. You hold Ship Security Awareness but think it covers basic training.
  • Only scanned copies. Some companies, flags and port State control officers will want originals.

Port State Control officers can check certificates under STCW Article X and Regulation I/4. A crew member without valid BST is a deficiency against the ship, not just against the individual. That is why masters and companies are strict about it.

Oral exam style questions

Examiners and company interviewers often test BST knowledge, especially for junior officers and ratings. Be ready for questions like these:

  • "Name the four elements of basic training and where they are found." PST, FPFF, EFA and PSSR, in Section A-VI/1, Tables A-VI/1-1 to 1-4.
  • "What is the difference between familiarisation and basic training?" Familiarisation is shipboard, for everyone, before duties are assigned. BT is certificated shore training for those with designated safety or pollution-prevention duties.
  • "Which elements need revalidating and how often?" PST and FPFF, evidence every five years. The method depends on the administration.
  • "You find you are on board but your FPFF has expired. What do you do?" Tell the Master at once and inform the company. Do not hide it, because PSC could find it. Arrange the refresher at the earliest opportunity as directed by the company and administration.
  • "Is Ship Security Awareness part of BST?" No. It is a separate requirement under Section A-VI/6.

Tips for keeping BST in date

  • Track expiry dates for every certificate in one place, and set a reminder at least six months ahead.
  • Do refreshers while on leave, not in the rush before a joining date. Approved course slots fill up.
  • Use only approved institutes and verify approval on your administration's website before paying.
  • Check spelling and date of birth on the certificate before leaving the training centre.
  • Carry originals plus clear copies, and keep scans in your phone and email.
  • Ask the company what it and the flag state accept before you book. Some will arrange or require specific refreshers.
  • Keep onboard drill records honest and signed. Where your administration accepts onboard experience, these records are your evidence.
Key points
  • Basic Training is required by STCW Regulation VI/1, with the standard in Section A-VI/1.
  • It has four elements: PST, FPFF, EFA and PSSR.
  • PST and FPFF need evidence of maintained competence every five years.
  • STCW sets no five-yearly refresher for EFA and PSSR, but some administrations and companies ask for one.
  • Safety familiarisation on board is separate and applies to everyone on the ship.
  • Ship Security Awareness is a separate Section A-VI/6 requirement, not part of BST.
  • Check expiry against the end of your contract, not just the joining date.

Frequently asked questions

How long is STCW Basic Safety Training valid?

STCW requires evidence every five years that you have maintained competence in PST and FPFF. Many administrations meet this by issuing refresher certificates with a five-year validity. The exact certification method depends on your administration, so check its current rules.

Do I need to repeat Elementary First Aid and PSSR?

STCW itself does not set a five-yearly refresher for EFA or PSSR. However, some administrations, companies or charterers may want recent certificates. Check with your company and flag before you join.

Is my old STCW 95 basic safety certificate still valid?

Not on its own for PST and FPFF. Since the Manila Amendments transitional period ended on 1 January 2017, you need evidence of maintained competence in those two elements within the last five years. In most cases that means an approved refresher course.

Can I do the refresher course in a different country from the one that issued my CoC?

Sometimes, but acceptance is not automatic. Your administration or the ship's flag state may only accept courses from institutes it approves or recognises. Confirm this with your administration and company before booking.

Does Ship Security Awareness count as part of Basic Safety Training?

No. Security training is a separate requirement under STCW Section A-VI/6. Training centres often sell it in the same package, but you need both on their own merits.

Test yourself

Which four elements make up STCW basic training?

Basic training comprises Personal Survival Techniques, Fire Prevention and Fire Fighting, Elementary First Aid, and Personal Safety and Social Responsibilities. Ref: STCW Code, Section A-VI/1, Tables A-VI/1-1 to A-VI/1-4.

Under STCW, which elements of basic training require evidence of maintained competence every five years?

The five-yearly requirement in Section A-VI/1 applies to PST and FPFF. Ref: STCW Code, Section A-VI/1.

Who must receive safety familiarisation on board before being assigned duties?

Safety familiarisation applies to everyone employed or engaged on the ship, not only those with designated safety duties. Ref: STCW Regulation VI/1 and Section A-VI/1.

Where is Ship Security Awareness training required?

Security-related training and instruction is in Section A-VI/6 and is separate from basic training. Ref: STCW Code, Section A-VI/6.

What does MLC 2006 Regulation 1.3 require regarding safety training?

Regulation 1.3 says seafarers must not work on board unless trained or qualified for their duties. It also requires them to have completed training for personal safety on board. Ref: MLC 2006, Regulation 1.3.
