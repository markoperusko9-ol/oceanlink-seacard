Proficiency in Fast Rescue Boats (PFRB): Who Needs It and How to Keep It Valid

Quick answer

Proficiency in Fast Rescue Boats (PFRB) is the STCW certificate of proficiency, issued to the standard in Regulation VI/2, for seafarers who launch, handle and recover a fast rescue boat. SOLAS (e.g. III/26) and the company SMS require designated FRB crews to hold it. You must already hold Proficiency in Survival Craft and Rescue Boats (PSCRB). Many administrations require refresher training or evidence of maintained competence, typically every five years, so check your administration's rules.

What the certificate is and where it comes from

Proficiency in Fast Rescue Boats, usually shortened to PFRB or FRB, is a certificate of proficiency (CoP) under the STCW Convention. It is a CoP, not a certificate of competency. It sits on top of your PSCRB.

The legal basis:

  • STCW Regulation VI/2 covers survival craft, rescue boats and fast rescue boats. Its second part sets out what a seafarer needs to be issued a fast rescue boat certificate.
  • STCW Code Section A-VI/2 contains the fast rescue boat standard of competence. Table A-VI/2-2 sets out the competences, the knowledge required and how competence is assessed.
  • SOLAS Chapter III decides which ships carry fast rescue boats. Regulations 19 and 26 set the drill requirements.
  • The LSA Code, Chapter V sets the design and performance standard for the boats themselves.

To be issued the certificate, Regulation VI/2 expects you to:

  • already hold a certificate of proficiency in survival craft and rescue boats other than fast rescue boats (PSCRB),
  • complete an approved training course, and
  • meet the standard in Section A-VI/2.

If your PSCRB is not in order, your FRB position is weak. Most administrations will not issue an FRB certificate without a valid PSCRB.

Who actually needs it

STCW sets the standard for the certificate. SOLAS (e.g. III/26) and the company SMS require designated FRB crews to hold it. Whether you personally need it depends on three things:

  • the ship,
  • the muster list,
  • the company SMS.

Ships where you will most often meet a fast rescue boat:

  • Ro-ro passenger ships. SOLAS Chapter III, Regulation 26 requires them to carry at least one fast rescue boat. It also requires crews for each fast rescue boat to be trained and drilled regularly, with at least two such crews per boat.
  • Offshore support, standby and supply vessels. These often carry FRBs or daughter craft under flag, coastal state or client requirements.
  • Other ships that carry an FRB as their rescue boat by design or owner's choice. This includes some cruise ships, large tankers and specialist vessels.

Ranks usually designated:

  • officers who command the boat, often a navigating officer as coxswain,
  • an engineer or motorman who knows the outboard or inboard engine,
  • ABs and bosuns detailed as boat crew.

In practice, manning agents on ro-pax and offshore tonnage often ask for PFRB as a joining requirement even before you see the muster list. This is a company or charterer requirement, not an STCW one, so it varies.

What the course covers

The course follows the competences in Table A-VI/2-2. Course length, structure and approved venues are set by each administration. Check with your national authority or an approved institute rather than relying on another country's format.

The five competences are:

  1. Understand construction, maintenance, repair and outfitting of FRBs. This includes:
    • rigid and inflated hulls,
    • buoyancy chambers,
    • the equipment inventory,
    • routine checks and minor repairs.
  2. Take charge of the launching appliance and arrangement as commonly fitted during launching and recovery. This covers launch and recovery in calm and adverse weather. It includes:
    • single-point davits,
    • painters,
    • the wave-force damping / heave-compensation devices fitted to FRB launching appliances,
    • the release gear fitted to the boat.
  3. Take charge of a fast rescue boat as commonly fitted during launching and recovery. This includes:
    • preparing and handling the boat while it is lowered,
    • releasing it,
    • bringing it back alongside and hooking on for recovery.
  4. Take charge of a fast rescue boat after launching. This includes:
    • boat handling at speed and in a seaway,
    • righting a capsized FRB,
    • search patterns,
    • recovering a person from the water,
    • working with a helicopter or other rescue units,
    • communications and navigation equipment.
  5. Operate the FRB engine. This covers:
    • starting and stopping,
    • kill-cord use,
    • daily and weekly checks,
    • simple fault-finding.

Expect real practical work. This normally includes:

  • launching and recovering from a davit,
  • high-speed handling,
  • man-overboard pick-ups,
  • a capsize and righting exercise.

The LSA Code requires FRBs to be either self-righting or capable of being readily righted by their crew. The course is where you learn that it is harder than it looks.

Validity and revalidation

Many administrations require refresher training or evidence of maintained competence for the fast rescue boat certificate, typically every five years. Check your administration's rules rather than assuming a single international format.

How you prove it is decided by the administration, and practices differ:

Point What varies by administration
Expiry date Some print a five-year expiry on the certificate; older or some national certificates do not
Method Refresher course at an approved centre, or approved onboard experience and drills, or a combination
Link to PSCRB Some require PSCRB to be valid or revalidated at the same time; some issue a combined refresher
Verification Online verification through the national system (e.g. India's DG Shipping records linked to INDoS, MARINA in the Philippines)

Check three sources, in this order:

  1. your own administration's current circular or notice,
  2. the flag state of the ship you are joining,
  3. your company's SMS or crewing matrix.

Where they disagree, the strictest one usually decides whether you sign on.

Why crew get stopped at joining

Problems an examiner or crewing superintendent sees again and again:

  • Refresher or revalidation not shown. The original certificate is from years ago, and no refresher or revalidation has been done where the administration or company requires one.
  • PSCRB out of date. Your FRB certificate may look fine, but the prerequisite has lapsed.
  • Certificate cannot be verified. It was issued by an institute not approved for that course, or it is not visible in the national online database.
  • Name or details mismatch. The spelling or date of birth differs between your passport, CDC and certificates. Port State Control and agents will flag it.
  • Expiry during the contract. Many companies will not sign you on if a required certificate expires before the planned sign-off date.
  • Client or charterer extras. Offshore clients may want additional, client-specific boat training on top of STCW PFRB.
  • Scanned copy only. Some ports, flags and companies want originals or properly attested copies on board.

Worked scenario: launching the FRB for a man overboard

This is the kind of sequence an assessor or chief officer wants you to talk through. Your ship's SMS and the boat maker's manual come first. This is a generic outline only.

  1. Bridge actions start. The OOW starts the man-overboard manoeuvre, sounds the alarm and broadcasts as per procedure. The master decides whether the sea state allows a safe launch.
  2. Crew muster. The boat crew dress in:
    • immersion or anti-exposure suits,
    • lifejackets,
    • helmets,
    • with a portable VHF. The coxswain fits the kill cord.
  3. Pre-launch checks:
    • drain plugs in,
    • fuel on,
    • engine tilt and lock correct,
    • painter rigged and led forward,
    • release gear set,
    • heave compensation available.
  4. Swing out and lower with the ship's way reduced. Start the engine before or just as the boat touches the water, as per the SMS.
  5. Release. Release the hook as the boat settles on a crest, then run out along the painter. Slip the painter once clear and under control.
  6. Approach the casualty. Approach heading into wind and sea. Slow down well before you reach them. Take the engine out of gear near the person to keep the propeller away.
  7. Recover the casualty. Lift them as horizontally as practicable where hypothermia is suspected. Report to the bridge.
  8. Return and recovery. Run alongside on the painter, hook on, and hoist using heave compensation. Then debrief and record the event.

Oral exam style questions

Questions examiners and masters commonly put to FRB candidates, with the points they want to hear:

  • "What is the difference between a rescue boat and a fast rescue boat?" An FRB is built for much higher speed under the LSA Code. It needs a trained crew able to handle it at speed and in a seaway. It also needs its own STCW certificate on top of PSCRB.
  • "Why use a painter during launch?" It keeps the boat lying alongside and heading the right way while the ship still has way on. It also reduces the risk of the boat swinging under the falls or being swamped.
  • "What do you check before every launch?"
    • drain plugs,
    • fuel,
    • kill cord,
    • release gear status,
    • personal protective equipment,
    • communications,
    • the weather limits in the SMS.
  • "The boat has capsized. What do you do?" Account for all crew first. Then follow the righting method taught for that boat type, restart the engine only as per the maker's procedure, and report.
  • "Who decides whether to launch?" The master. The coxswain can and should report if conditions are beyond the boat or crew.
Key points
  • PFRB is an STCW certificate of proficiency under Regulation VI/2 and Section A-VI/2, Table A-VI/2-2.
  • You must already hold PSCRB before you can be issued an FRB certificate.
  • SOLAS III/26 requires ro-ro passenger ships to carry at least one FRB with trained crews.
  • Many administrations require refresher training or evidence of maintained competence, typically every five years; check your administration's rules.
  • The commonest joining failures are no refresher or revalidation since the original course, or an expired PSCRB.
  • Make sure every certificate can be verified online and your name matches across passport, CDC and certificates.
  • Offshore and ro-pax operators may add company or client training on top of STCW.

Frequently asked questions

Is PSCRB required before doing the fast rescue boat course?

Yes. STCW Regulation VI/2 requires you to hold a certificate of proficiency in survival craft and rescue boats other than fast rescue boats before being certified for fast rescue boats. Approved institutes normally check this at enrolment.

How long is the Proficiency in Fast Rescue Boats certificate valid?

Many administrations require refresher training or evidence of maintained competence, typically every five years. Administrations differ on three points: whether a printed expiry date appears, whether you need a refresher course, and whether sea service counts. Check the current notice from your national authority.

Do all deck officers need the FRB certificate?

No. STCW sets the certificate standard, and SOLAS and the company SMS require it for those designated to take charge of or crew a fast rescue boat. On ro-pax and offshore vessels, companies often require it of all deck officers and some ratings as a joining condition.

Can I do the FRB refresher in a different country from where I got the original?

Sometimes. It depends on whether your issuing administration accepts that training and whether the flag state of your ship recognises the result. Confirm with your administration and crewing department before booking.

Why was my FRB certificate rejected at joining when it has no expiry date?

Many administrations and companies require refresher training or evidence of maintained competence, typically every five years, even if an older certificate shows no expiry. A certificate more than five years old without revalidation or a refresher is often refused. Check your administration's rules and your company's crewing matrix.

Test yourself

Which STCW regulation covers the fast rescue boat certificate of proficiency?

Regulation VI/2 covers survival craft, rescue boats and fast rescue boats. VI/1 is basic training, VI/3 is advanced fire fighting and V/2 is passenger ships.

What must a seafarer already hold before being certified in fast rescue boats?

STCW Regulation VI/2 makes PSCRB the prerequisite for the fast rescue boat certificate.

Who sets the refresher or revalidation rules you must meet to keep your FRB certificate accepted?

Certificates of proficiency under STCW Regulation VI/2 are issued by the administration, which sets its own refresher or revalidation rules. Flags and companies may add requirements.

Which ship type does SOLAS Chapter III, Regulation 26 specifically require to carry at least one fast rescue boat?

SOLAS III/26 contains the extra requirements for ro-ro passenger ships, including carriage of at least one fast rescue boat.

During an FRB launch in a seaway, what is the main purpose of the painter?

The painter holds the boat in position and heading after release, reducing the risk of swamping or swinging under the falls. This is taught under the launching and recovery competences in Table A-VI/2-2.
