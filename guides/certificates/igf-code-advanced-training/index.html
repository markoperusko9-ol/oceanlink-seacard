IGF Code Advanced Training: A Seafarer's Guide

Quick answer

IGF Code Advanced Training is the STCW Regulation V/3 certificate of proficiency for masters, engineer officers and anyone with immediate responsibility for the care and use of gas or other low-flashpoint fuels. You need IGF basic training first, plus approved advanced training and seagoing service that includes bunkering operations, or an accepted equivalence route. Your administration sets how it is revalidated, so check before your certificate lapses.

What the certificate is and why it exists

The IGF Code (International Code of Safety for Ships using Gases or other Low-flashpoint Fuels) became mandatory through SOLAS Chapter II-1, Part G and entered into force on 1 January 2017. It covers ships that burn fuels with a flashpoint below the normal SOLAS limit for ship fuel. In practice this mostly means LNG-fuelled ships. Methanol and other low-flashpoint fuels are handled through the Code's alternative design route and IMO interim guidelines.

Alongside the Code, IMO amended STCW and added Regulation V/3 and Section A-V/3 of the STCW Code. These set two levels of training:

  • Basic training, for seafarers with safety duties connected to the fuel.
  • Advanced training, for those who actually run and are responsible for the fuel system.

The advanced competence standard is set out in Table A-V/3-2. Successful candidates are issued a certificate of proficiency (CoP) by, or under the authority of, their administration.

One point that catches people out: gas carriers burning their own cargo boil-off fall under the IGC Code, not the IGF Code. On an LNG carrier, your liquefied gas tanker training (STCW V/1-2) is the relevant qualification. The IGF certificates are for LNG-fuelled container ships, car carriers, ferries, bulkers, tankers, OSVs and similar ships.

Who needs it on board

Under STCW V/3, advanced training is required for:

  • Masters
  • Engineer officers
  • All personnel with immediate responsibility for the care and use of fuels and fuel systems on ships subject to the IGF Code

On many ships the last category is wider than seafarers expect. Depending on the company's SMS and how bunkering duties are allocated, it can include:

  • the chief officer, if they run or co-ordinate the bunker transfer
  • the ETO, if they work on gas detection and ESD systems
  • a dedicated gas engineer

The safe approach is to read the ship's manning documents and SMS duty matrix before you sign the contract.

Ratings and deck officers with designated safety duties connected to the fuel normally need basic training, not advanced. However, some owners require advanced training for more ranks than STCW demands, as a company policy. That is their right, and it is a common reason for last-minute certificate requests.

Entry requirements and the equivalence route

Standard route under Regulation V/3. While holding the IGF basic training CoP, the candidate must:

  1. Complete approved advanced training and meet the standard of competence in Section A-V/3.
  2. Complete at least one month of approved seagoing service on ships subject to the IGF Code, including a minimum of three bunkering operations.

Two of those three bunkering operations may be replaced by approved simulator training on bunkering, delivered as part of the advanced course. You therefore still need at least one real bunkering on an IGF ship.

Equivalence route for gas tanker officers. Regulation V/3 allows masters, engineers and others who hold advanced liquefied gas tanker training (V/1-2) to be treated as meeting the advanced IGF competence. This only applies if they also meet the regulation's additional conditions: the bunkering requirement for advanced IGF training, or participation in conducting three cargo operations on board a liquefied gas tanker, plus three months' seagoing service within the previous five years on ships subject to the IGF Code, tankers carrying IGF fuels as cargo, or ships using gases or low-flashpoint fuels as fuel.

Administrations apply this route differently, and the details matter. Do not assume your LNG carrier certificate automatically covers an LNG-fuelled ship. Ask your administration or training centre for written confirmation of what you need.

Keep proof of your bunkering operations. Get the master or chief engineer to sign them off with dates, port and type of transfer: truck-to-ship, ship-to-ship or terminal-to-ship. Your administration may also accept a training record book entry or a company-issued letter, so check which form it wants.

What the advanced course covers

Course length and format are set by the approved training provider and administration, so check locally. Content follows the competences in Table A-V/3-2. Expect to be examined on:

  • Fuel properties. Physical and chemical behaviour of LNG and other low-flashpoint fuels:
    • LNG is carried at around minus 162°C.
    • Methane's flammable range is roughly 5–15% in air.
    • Cold vapour hugs the deck until it warms.
  • Cryogenic hazards. Cold burns, brittle fracture of ordinary steel, and the purpose of drip trays and water curtains at the bunker station.
  • Fuel system design. Tank types, double-walled piping, gas valve units, ventilation of annular spaces, and the difference between gas-safe and ESD-protected machinery spaces.
  • Operating the plant. Gas and dual-fuel engine control, changeover between fuels, and pressure and temperature management in the tank.
  • Bunkering. Planning, compatibility checks, the ship–supplier checklist, safety and security zones, communications, ship-to-ship ESD links, cool-down, transfer, draining, inerting and purging.
  • Safety systems. Gas detection, ESD logic, fire detection and extinguishing at the bunker station and tank connection space, and enclosed space precautions.
  • Emergencies and pollution. Leak response, fire on the bunker manifold, releases to atmosphere, and methane slip awareness.
  • Legislation. Your responsibilities under SOLAS, the IGF Code, STCW and the ship's SMS.

Validity and revalidation

STCW Regulation V/3 requires holders to show continued competence at intervals not exceeding five years. This is done either by refresher training or by evidence of having maintained the required standard.

What counts as evidence is set by your administration. Depending on the flag or issuing authority, this may be:

  • recent sea service on IGF ships or gas carriers
  • an approved refresher course
  • an assessment

How the expiry date is shown also varies. Some administrations print it on the CoP itself. Others tie it to the CoC revalidation cycle.

Item Fixed by STCW Decided by administration
Who must hold it Yes Can add wider rules for its own ships
Bunkering and sea service for first issue Yes Form of evidence accepted
Five-year maximum revalidation interval Yes Exact method and documents
Course duration and fees No Yes, via approved providers

If you sail under a flag different from the country that issued your CoC, the flag state may have its own recognition or endorsement requirements. Confirm these with the manning agent before joining.

Why crew get stopped at joining

These are the problems most often raised by manning agents, masters and port state control:

  1. Basic only, joining in an advanced role. For example, a second engineer arrives holding only IGF basic training.
  2. Gas tanker certificate assumed to cover IGF. The seafarer has no record of meeting the additional V/3 conditions.
  3. Expired or unrevalidated CoP. The five-year interval quietly passed during a long stretch on conventional ships.
  4. Course not approved by the issuing administration. A cheap course from an unapproved centre gives you a certificate, not a valid CoP.
  5. Certificate not in the administration's online verification database. The company cannot verify it, so it is treated as invalid.
  6. Name or date mismatches between the CoP, passport, CDC and CoC.
  7. Company requirement above STCW. The SMS requires advanced training for a deck rank, but the seafarer only read the STCW minimum.

Worked scenario. A third engineer has done the advanced IGF course ashore with simulator bunkering but has never served on an IGF ship. He is offered a berth on an LNG-fuelled container ship.

He meets the course requirement, but not the sea service requirement for the advanced CoP: one month including at least one real bunkering. He should check with his administration whether it will issue the advanced CoP yet. If it will not, the company may be able to place him in a supernumerary or training capacity to build the service, but only if the flag accepts that arrangement. Sort this out before travel, not at the gangway.

Oral exam style questions

Examiners and superintendents like to probe the practical side. Be ready for questions such as:

  • Why must the bunker station have a drip tray and water curtain? LNG spilling onto ordinary hull steel can cause brittle fracture. The water curtain protects the hull plating and the drip tray contains the spill.
  • Gas detected in the annular space of a double-walled pipe. What happens and what do you do? Explain the automatic ESD and shutdown response for your design, the changeover to the other fuel, and investigation under permit.
  • What is the difference between a gas-safe and an ESD-protected machinery space? In a gas-safe space, a single failure cannot release gas into the space. In an ESD-protected space, a gas leak triggers emergency shutdown and isolation of the gas supply and the affected equipment.
  • Name the checks before starting ship-to-ship LNG bunkering. Pre-transfer checklist, agreed communications and ESD link test, safety zone, compatibility of connections, pressure and temperature limits, and emergency release arrangements.
  • Why might methane vapour not rise immediately after a spill? Cold vapour is denser than ambient air until it warms, so it can spread at deck level.

Keeping it in date

  • Track expiry dates alongside your CoC. Use a single spreadsheet or app reminder covering all CoPs.
  • Log every bunkering. Record date, port, method and quantity, with a senior officer's signature. That evidence is valuable for both first issue and revalidation.
  • Ask early. Get your administration's current revalidation guidance well before expiry, because rules are periodically updated.
  • Use approved providers only. Confirm the course appears on your administration's approved list.
  • Check the online record. Make sure your CoP can be verified by the company before signing on.
  • Mixed LNG and IGF careers. If you move between LNG carriers and LNG-fuelled ships, keep both your V/1-2 and V/3 certificates current. Don't rely on one to cover the other.
Key points
  • IGF Code Advanced Training is required under STCW Regulation V/3 and Section A-V/3 (Table A-V/3-2).
  • It applies to masters, engineer officers and anyone with immediate responsibility for fuel care and use on IGF ships.
  • First issue needs IGF basic training, an approved advanced course and one month at sea on an IGF ship including three bunkerings.
  • Up to two of the three bunkerings may be replaced by approved simulator training.
  • Gas carriers burning cargo boil-off fall under the IGC Code, not the IGF Code.
  • Continued competence must be shown within five years; the method depends on your administration.
  • Unapproved courses, lapsed CoPs and unverified gas tanker equivalence are the usual reasons crew are stopped at joining.

Frequently asked questions

Does my LNG tanker advanced certificate cover IGF ships?

Not automatically. STCW V/3 allows holders of advanced liquefied gas tanker training to be treated as meeting the advanced IGF competence, but only if they also meet additional conditions on bunkering or cargo-operation experience and recent sea service. Get written confirmation from your administration.

Can I do all three bunkering operations on a simulator?

No. Two of the three may be replaced by approved simulator training within the advanced course. At least one real bunkering on a ship subject to the IGF Code is still needed.

Does a deck officer need IGF advanced training?

STCW requires it for the master and for anyone with immediate responsibility for the care and use of the fuel. A chief officer who runs bunkering may fall into that group under the company SMS, and some owners require it as policy. Other deck officers with fuel-related safety duties normally need basic training.

How long is the IGF advanced certificate valid?

STCW requires continued competence to be shown at intervals not exceeding five years. How you do that, whether by sea service, refresher course or assessment, is set by your administration.

Is the IGF Code only about LNG?

The Code contains detailed requirements for natural gas. Other low-flashpoint fuels such as methanol are approved through its alternative design route and IMO interim guidelines. The STCW V/3 training requirement applies to ships subject to the IGF Code whatever the low-flashpoint fuel.

Test yourself

Which STCW regulation sets the training requirements for ships subject to the IGF Code?

Regulation V/3 covers personnel on ships subject to the IGF Code. V/1-1 and V/1-2 are tanker training and VI/1 is basic safety training (STCW Regulation V/3).

What seagoing service is required for the first issue of the advanced IGF CoP?

Regulation V/3 requires at least one month of approved seagoing service including at least three bunkering operations, two of which may be replaced by approved simulator training (STCW Regulation V/3).

An LNG carrier burns its own cargo boil-off in the engines. Which code governs that fuel use?

Gas carriers using cargo as fuel are covered by the IGC Code, not the IGF Code (SOLAS Chapter II-1, Part G and IGC Code).

Why is a water curtain used at the LNG bunker station?

Spilled cryogenic LNG can embrittle ordinary hull steel, and the water curtain protects the shell plating. Methane is not usefully dissolved by water (IGF Code, bunkering arrangements).

Who decides the exact method of revalidating the IGF advanced CoP?

STCW sets the maximum five-year interval, and the administration specifies the accepted evidence or refresher training (STCW Regulation V/3).
