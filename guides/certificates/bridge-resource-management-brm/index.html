Bridge Resource Management (BRM): What It Is, Who Needs It and How to Keep It Valid

Quick answer

Bridge Resource Management (BRM) is the knowledge and skill of using every resource on the bridge (people, equipment, information and time) to keep a safe navigational watch. STCW requires it as part of the deck officer's competence for a Certificate of Competency, but it is not a standalone STCW certificate. Whether you need a separate BRM course certificate, and how often you refresh it, depends on your administration and your company or charterer.

What BRM actually is

BRM is the bridge version of what aviation calls crew resource management. The idea is simple. Most groundings and collisions are not caused by a lack of knowledge. They come from a team that stopped working as a team. Examples include a pilot nobody challenged, an OOW fixated on one target, a master who overrode the plan without telling anyone, or a lookout who saw the light but assumed someone else had.

BRM covers how the bridge team:

  • Allocates and prioritises resources: who does what, especially when workload spikes.
  • Communicates: closed-loop orders, clear reports, shared mental model.
  • Uses assertiveness and leadership: juniors speak up, seniors listen and decide.
  • Builds and keeps situational awareness: everyone knows where the ship is, where it is going and what is coming.
  • Accounts for team experience: who is new, who is tired, who is unfamiliar with this equipment.

The resources include people (master, OOW, lookout, helmsman, pilot, engine room). They also include equipment (radar/ARPA, ECDIS, AIS, echo sounder) and external sources such as VTS and tugs.

Where the requirement comes from

BRM sits inside the STCW Convention, not in a separate regulation of its own:

  • STCW Code, Table A-II/1 (officer in charge of a navigational watch): under the competence of maintaining a safe navigational watch, candidates must show knowledge of BRM principles. These are the five areas listed above. The same table also includes the application of leadership and teamworking skills.
  • STCW Code, Table A-II/2 (chief mate and master): the management level carries the use of leadership and managerial skills, which builds on BRM.
  • STCW Code, Section A-VIII/2: the watchkeeping principles. This section expects bridge watch arrangements to take account of BRM principles. It also says a pilot on board does not relieve the master or OOW of their duties for the safety of the ship.
  • Section B-VIII/2 gives non-mandatory guidance on watchkeeping arrangements, including resource management.

These BRM and leadership elements came in with the 2010 Manila amendments. All officers had to meet them once the transitional period ended on 1 January 2017.

Key point: STCW makes BRM a competence you must demonstrate. It does not create a separate STCW certificate of proficiency, as it does for Basic Training or Advanced Fire Fighting. How you prove the competence is set by your administration. Some build it into the approved CoC course and exam. Others require a separately approved BRM, Bridge Team Management or Leadership and Teamwork course before you sit for your CoC. IMO Model Course 1.22 (Ship Simulator and Bridge Teamwork) and Model Course 1.39 (Leadership and Teamwork) are commonly used as a basis. Check your own administration's current requirements.

Who needs it

Person Position under STCW Practical reality
Deck cadet heading for OOW CoC BRM knowledge is part of the A-II/1 competence Usually a course or module before the CoC exam, depending on administration
OOW / 3rd and 2nd Officer Must hold the competence through their CoC Companies often ask for a BRM course certificate in the document set
Chief Officer / Master Management-level leadership competence Many companies and charterers expect a simulator-based BRM/BTM course, sometimes with refreshers
Pilots Covered by national pilotage rules, not STCW Many pilot organisations run their own Bridge Resource Management training
Engineers Engine Room Resource Management (ERM) is the equivalent See the ERM guide

Ratings forming part of a navigational watch are not required to hold a BRM certificate. They are, however, part of the bridge team. Good BRM training stresses that the lookout and helmsman must report and must be listened to.

What the course covers

Course content, duration and format vary by training centre and by what the administration approves. A typical approved course includes:

  1. Accident case studies: real groundings and collisions taken apart to find the human and team failures.
  2. Human factors: fatigue, stress, complacency, error chains, workload and situational awareness.
  3. Communication: closed-loop orders, standard phrases, briefings and debriefings, cultural and language barriers in mixed crews.
  4. Leadership and assertiveness: how a junior challenges a master or pilot respectfully and effectively, and how a senior creates a bridge where that is possible.
  5. Master–pilot exchange: sharing the passage plan, agreeing the pilot's intentions, monitoring the pilot's actions.
  6. Passage planning and monitoring as a team: assigning roles for pilotage, coastal and restricted-visibility phases.
  7. Simulator exercises: full-mission or part-task bridge scenarios with deliberate failures and high workload, followed by a structured debrief.

The debrief is where the learning happens. Expect the instructor to replay your exercise and ask why nobody mentioned the cross-track error for six minutes.

Worked scenario: breaking the error chain

Situation: Night, pilot on board, inbound channel, master on the bridge, 2nd Officer as OOW, AB on the wheel. The pilot orders a course alteration earlier than the planned wheel-over point.

Poor BRM: The OOW notices the ship is heading off the ECDIS track but assumes the pilot knows the local set. The master is on the phone to the agent. Nobody says anything. The ship touches the edge of the channel.

Good BRM, step by step:

  1. Before pilotage: the master briefs the team on roles. The OOW monitors position and the track; the master monitors the pilot and traffic. All agree that any deviation from the plan is called out.
  2. Master–pilot exchange: the pilot's intended track, speed and tug plan are compared with the ship's plan. Any differences are agreed before the critical stretch.
  3. During the alteration: the OOW reports clearly: "Captain, we are turning 4 cables before the planned wheel-over, the cross-track is increasing to starboard."
  4. Response: the master queries the pilot, who either explains (for example, a tidal set) or corrects.
  5. If no satisfactory answer: the master takes action. The pilot's presence does not relieve the master or OOW of responsibility.
  6. Afterwards: a short debrief and entry in the log as per the company SMS.

Validity, refreshers and why people get stopped at joining

Validity: STCW sets no fixed validity for a BRM course certificate. Many are issued without an expiry date. What expires is your CoC, which must be revalidated at intervals not exceeding five years under STCW Regulation I/11. Some administrations, companies and oil-major or charterer vetting regimes expect a BRM refresher at set intervals or on promotion. The interval is set by them, not by STCW, so check your company's matrix.

Common reasons crew are held up at joining:

  • Course not from an approved institute where the administration requires approval, or the certificate cannot be verified online.
  • Wrong course for the rank: an operational-level course presented for a Chief Officer or Master post when the company wants management-level BRM/BTM.
  • Company or charterer matrix not met: for example, a tanker client requiring simulator-based BRM within a set period, which the crewing office missed.
  • Name or document mismatch between the BRM certificate, passport and CDC.
  • Certificate missing from the uploaded document set even though you hold it. Crewing offices and vetting inspectors only see what is on file.
  • Flag state endorsement application stalled because supporting training documents were incomplete.

Tips for keeping it in date:

  • Ask your crewing office for the company's training matrix for your rank and vessel type, in writing, before you sign on.
  • Keep scanned copies of every course certificate with your CoC, CDC and passport.
  • Before promotion, check whether your administration or company wants a management-level course.
  • If your certificate shows a validity date, put it in your calendar with the CoC expiry.

Oral exam: what examiners ask about BRM

Examiners rarely ask you to recite definitions. They ask what you would do. Be ready for:

  • "The pilot is taking the ship into danger. What do you do?" Inform the pilot, then the master. The OOW may take action in an emergency. The pilot's presence does not relieve you of responsibility (A-VIII/2).
  • "What is situational awareness and how do you lose it?" Know where you are, where you are going and what is around you. You lose it through fixation, distraction, fatigue and over-reliance on one sensor.
  • "How do you brief a new lookout?" Cover the duties, what and how to report, and confirm the lookout understood. That is closed-loop communication.
  • "What is an error chain?" A sequence of small failures leading to an accident. Any team member can break it by speaking up.
  • "When would you call the master?" Refer to the master's standing and night orders and the SMS. Calling early is good BRM, not weakness.

Answer with specific actions. Saying "I would maintain good communication" scores nothing. Saying "I would tell the pilot the cross-track error and ask his intention, then inform the master" scores.

Key points
  • BRM is a mandatory STCW competence for deck officers (Table A-II/1), not a standalone STCW certificate of proficiency.
  • Its core elements are resource allocation, communication, assertiveness and leadership, situational awareness and team experience.
  • Whether you need a separate BRM course certificate depends on your administration and company or charterer.
  • STCW sets no validity period for a BRM course; refresher intervals come from administrations, companies or vetting regimes.
  • A pilot on board never relieves the master or OOW of responsibility for the ship's safety.
  • Most joining delays come from unapproved courses, rank mismatch or certificates missing from the document file.

Frequently asked questions

Is BRM mandatory under STCW?

Knowledge of BRM principles is mandatory for officers in charge of a navigational watch under STCW Code Table A-II/1. Leadership competences apply at operational and management level. STCW does not create a separate BRM certificate, so your administration decides how you prove the competence, often through an approved course.

How long is a BRM certificate valid?

STCW sets no validity period for a BRM course certificate, and many are issued without an expiry date. Some administrations, companies and charterers require refreshers at their own intervals. Check your company's training matrix and your administration's rules.

What is the difference between BRM and BTM?

Bridge Team Management (BTM) is a name some administrations and training centres use for a course aimed at management-level officers. It builds on the same BRM principles with more emphasis on leading the bridge team. The exact names and approval rules differ between administrations.

Do engineers need BRM?

No. The engine-room equivalent is Engine Room Resource Management (ERM), which applies the same human-factors principles to engineering watches. Some companies run combined bridge and engine-room courses, but that is a company choice.

Can I do BRM online?

It depends on your administration and company. Many require an approved course with simulator exercises, and online-only certificates may not be accepted at joining or by vetting inspectors. Confirm acceptance with your crewing office before you pay for any course.

Test yourself

Under STCW, how is BRM primarily treated?

Knowledge of BRM principles is part of the competence to maintain a safe navigational watch in STCW Code Table A-II/1, not a separate certificate of proficiency.

A pilot is conning the ship. Who remains responsible for the safety of the ship?

STCW Code Section A-VIII/2 makes clear that the pilot's presence does not relieve the master or OOW of their duties for the ship's safety.

Which of these is one of the BRM principles listed for OOW competence?

STCW Code Table A-II/1 lists situational awareness alongside resource allocation, communication, assertiveness and leadership, and team experience.

What validity period does STCW set for a BRM course certificate?

STCW sets no validity for a BRM course. Refresher intervals come from administrations, companies or vetting regimes, while the CoC itself is revalidated under Regulation I/11.

The OOW sees the ship drifting off track during pilotage. What is the best BRM response?

Assertive closed-loop communication and informing the master are core BRM practice under STCW Code Section A-VIII/2 watchkeeping principles.
