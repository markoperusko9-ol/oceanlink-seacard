Proficiency in Survival Craft and Rescue Boats (PSCRB): A Seafarer's Guide

Quick answer

PSCRB is the STCW certificate of proficiency, required under Regulation VI/2, for anyone designated to launch or take charge of a survival craft or rescue boat (other than a fast rescue boat). The main STCW officer CoCs require the Section A-VI/2 standard, so in practice officers need it, and many companies also expect it from senior ratings named on the muster list. Since the Manila amendments you must show every five years that you have kept up your competence, by a method your administration decides, so check the requirements before your certificate runs out.

What PSCRB is and where it comes from

Proficiency in Survival Craft and Rescue Boats (PSCRB) is a certificate of proficiency issued under the STCW Convention. It is a separate document from a certificate of competency (CoC).

The legal chain works like this:

  • STCW Regulation VI/2 sets the mandatory minimum requirements for issuing certificates of proficiency in survival craft, rescue boats and fast rescue boats.
  • STCW Code Section A-VI/2 and Table A-VI/2-1 set out the standard of competence for survival craft and rescue boats other than fast rescue boats.
  • Section B-VI/2 gives non-mandatory guidance on training.

On some older certificates you will still see the earlier name, "Proficiency in Survival Craft" (PSC). A Proficiency in Fast Rescue Boats certificate is a separate qualification, and you must hold PSCRB before you can get it.

The MLC 2006 also expects seafarers to be trained, certified or otherwise qualified for the duties they are given (Regulation 1.3). It does not create the PSCRB requirement, though. That comes from STCW.

Who needs it

Under Regulation VI/2, every person who is to be designated to launch or take charge of a survival craft or rescue boat must hold the certificate. In practice this covers:

  • Deck and engineer officers. The main STCW officer CoCs (OOW, chief mate/master, engineer officers, ETO) require you to meet the standard in Section A-VI/2, so in practice officers need PSCRB. The administration sets the requirements for some limited or near-coastal certificates.
  • Ratings named on the muster list as boat crew, for example lifeboat or rescue boat crew, coxswain's assistant or engine operator. Whether a given rating must hold it depends on their muster list duties and on the company SMS. Many companies make it a standard joining requirement for the bosun and ABs.
  • Cadets and trainees. Many cadets complete PSCRB as part of their pre-sea or STCW course package.

Eligibility under Regulation VI/2 (paraphrased):

  • You must be at least 18 years of age.
  • You must have one of the following:
    • at least 12 months' approved seagoing service, or
    • an approved training course plus at least 6 months' approved seagoing service.
  • You must meet the competence standard in Section A-VI/2.

Administrations apply the seagoing-service condition to cadets differently. Confirm with your training institute and administration (DG Shipping in India, MARINA in the Philippines) how and when your certificate will be issued.

What the course covers

Table A-VI/2-1 groups the competences under these headings:

  1. Take charge of a survival craft or rescue boat during and after launch.
  2. Operate a survival craft engine.
  3. Manage survivors and the survival craft after abandoning ship.
  4. Use locating devices, including communication and signalling apparatus and pyrotechnics.
  5. Apply first aid to survivors.

In the classroom and at the water's edge, this normally covers:

  • Types of lifeboats and liferafts and how they are built. This includes enclosed, free-fall and davit-launched boats, and throw-over and davit-launched rafts.
  • Launching appliances, davits, winches, brakes and on-load and off-load release gear, including the safety precautions around hooks.
  • Launching and recovery in a seaway, clearing the ship's side, and boarding from the water.
  • Starting and running the lifeboat engine, plus steering, rowing and compass work.
  • Survival craft equipment, sea anchors, rations and water, and thermal protective aids.
  • Righting a capsized liferaft.
  • Leadership in the craft: allocating duties, lookouts, morale and keeping survivors together.
  • Pyrotechnics, EPIRB, SART and two-way VHF.
  • Hypothermia, seasickness and basic first aid in the craft.

The practical elements are assessed. Expect to:

  • launch and recover a boat,
  • act as coxswain giving orders,
  • right a raft in the pool, and
  • demonstrate the equipment.

Your administration approves the course duration and syllabus, so it can vary between countries and centres.

Validity and revalidation

The 2010 Manila amendments to STCW added a requirement for seafarers holding PSCRB to provide evidence every five years that they have maintained the required standard of competence.

The administration decides how you meet this, and practice differs:

Approach What it usually means
Refresher course A shorter approved course at a training centre, with practical assessment
Onboard evidence Some administrations accept documented onboard training and drills toward part of the requirement
Full course again Some centres or flags require this if the certificate has long expired

Points to check with your own administration:

  • Whether your certificate shows an expiry date, or only an issue date with the five-year rule applying.
  • Whether a refresher is accepted once the certificate has already expired, or whether you must redo the full course.
  • Whether a certificate issued by another country's approved centre is accepted for your CoC revalidation.
  • How the requirement links to your CoC revalidation. Many administrations want valid PSCRB evidence before they will revalidate an officer CoC.

Why crew get stopped at joining

Manning agents, company crewing departments and port state control inspectors check STCW proficiency certificates. These are the problems that most often hold people back:

  • Expired, or no refresher evidence. This is the most common one. The certificate is more than five years old and there is no refresher or other accepted revalidation.
  • The certificate cannot be verified. Certificates from centres that are not approved, or that do not appear on the administration's online verification system, are rejected. Fake STCW certificates are a known problem, and companies check.
  • Name or date-of-birth mismatch. The details on the certificate differ from your passport, CDC or INDoS record.
  • Wrong certificate for the ship. The vessel carries a fast rescue boat and you are named as its crew, but you hold only PSCRB without Proficiency in Fast Rescue Boats.
  • Old-format certificate. An old certificate titled 'Proficiency in Survival Craft' (pre-STCW 95 wording), or any PSCRB with no evidence of the five-year update required since the 2010 Manila amendments, may not be accepted.
  • Validity too short for the contract. Some companies will not sign you on if a certificate expires during the planned contract. This is a company policy, not an STCW rule, but it is common.

Worked scenario. A 3rd Officer has a CoC valid for three more years. His PSCRB was issued six years ago and he has never done a refresher. The agent rejects his documents. The CoC is not the problem. The missing PSCRB revalidation evidence is, and under many administrations' rules it will also block his next CoC revalidation. The fix is to book an approved refresher before the next joining date and confirm the new certificate appears on the administration's online verification system.

Oral exam style: what examiners ask

In CoC orals, the examiner often uses PSCRB knowledge to test leadership. Be ready for questions like these:

  • "You are in charge of a lifeboat. Talk me through launching." Cover these points:
    • musters and headcount,
    • lifejackets and immersion suits on,
    • sea painter rigged,
    • gripes and lashings released,
    • plug in,
    • engine started,
    • controlled lowering,
    • release only when waterborne, following the maker's procedure for the hook gear,
    • clearing away using the painter and the ship's way.
  • "What do you check before releasing on-load hooks?" Say that the boat is fully waterborne. Say that the hook indicator or hydrostatic interlock shows the correct state. Say that you follow the manufacturer's instructions and the company SMS. Mention that accidents during drills and hook release are a well-documented cause of injuries.
  • "First actions after getting clear of the ship?" Cover these points:
    • stream the sea anchor,
    • look for survivors and recover them,
    • keep crafts together,
    • post a lookout,
    • check for injuries,
    • issue seasickness tablets early,
    • ration water and food according to the instructions in the craft,
    • activate locating devices as appropriate,
    • keep a log.
  • "How do you right a capsized liferaft?" Turn the raft so the gas cylinder side is placed so the wind helps you. Climb onto the cylinder and take the righting straps. Lean back and pull the raft over, then clear yourself from underneath.
  • "How often must lifeboats be launched in drills?" SOLAS Chapter III requires each lifeboat to be launched with its assigned operating crew and manoeuvred in the water at least once every three months during an abandon ship drill, subject to the provisions for certain boat types.

Answer as the person in charge. Give clear orders, put safety checks first, and refer to the SMS and maker's instructions.

Keeping your PSCRB in date

  • Diary the five-year date as soon as you receive the certificate, and plan the refresher at least one leave period ahead.
  • Align it with your CoC revalidation and other refreshers such as Basic Safety and AFF. Then one trip to the training centre covers several certificates.
  • Use only approved centres. Before paying, check the centre is approved by your administration for that specific course.
  • Check the online record. After the course, confirm the certificate appears on the national verification portal (in India, against your INDoS number).
  • Keep onboard evidence. Log your participation in boat drills and launches. Some administrations take this into account, and it helps at interview anyway.
  • Carry clear scans of the certificate with your other documents, so the agent can verify it quickly.
  • If you will be designated as fast rescue boat crew (common on offshore and some passenger ships), get Proficiency in Fast Rescue Boats before you join, not after.
Key points
  • PSCRB is an STCW certificate of proficiency under Regulation VI/2, with the standard set in Section A-VI/2 and Table A-VI/2-1.
  • Anyone designated to launch or take charge of a survival craft or rescue boat must hold it, and the main STCW officer CoCs require the A-VI/2 standard.
  • Eligibility: at least 18 years old, plus either 12 months' approved sea service or an approved course with 6 months' sea service.
  • Since the Manila amendments you must show maintained competence every five years; the method is set by your administration.
  • Fast rescue boat crew need a separate certificate, and you must hold PSCRB first.
  • Most joining rejections come from expired certificates, unverifiable centres or name mismatches.
  • Book refreshers early and align them with your CoC revalidation.

Frequently asked questions

Is PSCRB mandatory for ABs and ordinary seamen?

STCW requires it for anyone designated to launch or take charge of a survival craft or rescue boat, so it depends on your muster list duties. Many companies require it from the bosun and ABs as standard. Check your company's joining requirements.

How long is a PSCRB certificate valid?

STCW requires evidence of maintained competence every five years. How this appears on the certificate, and whether you need a refresher course or can use other evidence, depends on the issuing administration.

Can I do a refresher if my PSCRB has already expired?

It depends on the administration and the training centre. Some accept a refresher after expiry. Others require the full course if the gap is long. Ask your administration or an approved centre before booking.

What is the difference between PSCRB and Fast Rescue Boat training?

PSCRB covers survival craft and rescue boats other than fast rescue boats. Proficiency in Fast Rescue Boats is a separate STCW certificate under the same regulation, and you must already hold PSCRB to get it.

Do I need a flag state endorsement for my PSCRB?

Flag endorsements under STCW Regulation I/10 mainly apply to certificates of competency, not to proficiency certificates like PSCRB. Some flags or companies may still ask to see proficiency certificates when issuing endorsements, so follow the specific flag's instructions.

Test yourself

Which STCW regulation sets the requirements for the PSCRB certificate?

Regulation VI/2 covers survival craft, rescue boats and fast rescue boats. VI/1 covers basic training and VI/3 covers advanced fire fighting (STCW Convention, Regulation VI/2).

What is the minimum age to be issued a PSCRB certificate under STCW?

Regulation VI/2 requires the candidate to be not less than 18 years of age.

A seafarer named as crew of a fast rescue boat holds only PSCRB. What is the position?

Fast rescue boats need a separate certificate of proficiency, and PSCRB is a prerequisite for it (STCW Regulation VI/2; Section A-VI/2).

How often must PSCRB holders show evidence of maintained competence under the Manila amendments?

STCW as amended in 2010 requires evidence of maintained competence every five years, by means the administration accepts (STCW Regulation VI/2 and Section A-VI/2).

Under SOLAS, how often must each lifeboat be launched with its assigned crew and manoeuvred in the water during drills?

SOLAS Chapter III, Regulation 19 requires each lifeboat to be launched with its assigned operating crew and manoeuvred in the water at least once every three months, subject to the provisions for certain boat types.
