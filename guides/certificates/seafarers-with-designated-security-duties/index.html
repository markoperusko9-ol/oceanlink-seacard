Seafarers with Designated Security Duties (DSD): A Practical Guide

Quick answer

The Seafarers with Designated Security Duties (DSD) certificate is the STCW proof of proficiency for anyone assigned security duties under the ship security plan. It is required by STCW Regulation VI/6 and Section A-VI/6 of the STCW Code. STCW sets no refresher requirement for it, but your flag state or company may set its own rules.

What the DSD certificate is and where it comes from

DSD is one of the security training requirements added to STCW as Regulation VI/6 by the 2010 Manila amendments (in force 1 January 2012), alongside security-related familiarisation and security awareness. The Ship Security Officer requirement (Regulation VI/5) is older: it was added by the 2006 amendments, in force 1 January 2008. All of them support the ISPS Code, which SOLAS chapter XI-2 makes mandatory.

Level Who it is for STCW reference
Security-related familiarisation Everyone employed on board, before taking up duties Reg VI/6, Section A-VI/6
Security awareness All seafarers without designated security duties Reg VI/6, Table A-VI/6-1
Designated security duties Seafarers assigned duties under the ship security plan (SSP) Reg VI/6, Table A-VI/6-2
Ship Security Officer The person designated as SSO Reg VI/5, Table A-VI/5

The certificate is a proof of proficiency, not a certificate of competency. It is issued by, or under the authority of, a maritime administration. Usually this is through a training centre approved by that administration.

IMO publishes a model course for this level. Administrations use it as a guide when they approve courses.

MLC 2006 does not set this requirement. It comes from STCW, backed up by the training provisions in Section 13 of the ISPS Code.

Who actually needs it

The test is simple: does the ship security plan give you a security duty? If it does, you need DSD. If it does not, security awareness meets the STCW minimum.

In practice, the SSP usually assigns duties to:

  • Officers of the watch, deck and often engine
  • Ratings who keep gangway or access-control watch
  • Crew who search baggage, stores or visitors, or patrol restricted areas
  • Anyone on the ship's security team for a raised security level

Gangway watch rotates through most deck ratings, and many crew are named in the SSP. For that reason, many companies simply require DSD for all crew, so there are no gaps when duties are reassigned. That is a company or SMS decision, not an STCW rule. Check your company's crewing matrix rather than relying on what your last ship accepted.

How DSD relates to the other certificates:

  • Security awareness: the DSD syllabus includes the awareness content, so most administrations and companies accept DSD in place of a separate awareness certificate.
  • SSO certificate: an SSO certificate is commonly accepted as covering DSD. How this is treated still depends on the administration, so confirm before you assume.

What the course covers

Table A-VI/6-2 sets out four competences:

  1. Maintain the conditions set out in the ship security plan. This covers:
    • the ISPS framework
    • the three security levels and what changes at each
    • reporting procedures
    • the roles of the SSO, CSO and PFSO
    • your duties under the SSP
  2. Recognise security risks and threats. This covers:
    • recognising weapons and dangerous substances and devices
    • spotting suspicious behaviour
    • techniques people use to get around security measures
    • crowd-management basics
  3. Undertake regular security inspections of the ship. This means:
    • inspecting restricted areas, access points, stores and cargo spaces
    • searching for stowaways or devices
    • knowing what to do if you find something
  4. Use security equipment and systems properly, if any are fitted. This includes:
    • lighting and alarms
    • CCTV
    • detection equipment
    • knowing the limits of each

Duration, mode of delivery (classroom, blended or online), assessment method and fees vary between administrations and training centres. Check with an approved centre. Online courses are only valid if your administration has approved that centre for that mode of delivery.

The course is only the first step. Before you take up duties on a specific ship, you still need ship-specific familiarisation and must understand that ship's SSP.

Validity and revalidation

STCW does not require periodic refresher training for the security certificates under Regulation VI/6. This is different from Basic Training, PSCRB and Advanced Fire Fighting, which do have refresher requirements. As a result, many DSD certificates are issued with no expiry date.

There are cases where this does not hold:

  • Administration rules. Some administrations put their own validity period on the documents they issue, or have changed formats over time. Your certificate shows what applies to you. If it has an expiry date, that date counts.
  • Company policy. Some companies, charterers or vetting regimes expect recent security training, or re-issue in a newer format.
  • Reissue of old certificates. Certificates issued under transitional arrangements around the Manila amendments, or by centres that later lost approval, may be questioned. Some seafarers have had to repeat the course or get a fresh document.

Keeping in practice on board. ISPS Code Part B gives guidance on security drills:

  • generally at least once every three months
  • an extra drill within one week if more than 25% of the ship's personnel have changed at one time and the new personnel have not taken part in a drill on that ship in the last three months

Crew with designated duties take part in these drills. They are part of keeping your competence current, even though they are not a certificate requirement.

Why crew get stopped at joining

Most problems happen at the manning agent or at the gangway, not at the training centre. Common ones:

  • Holding only security awareness when the company requires DSD. This is the most frequent. It often affects ratings moving from a company that accepted awareness to one that requires DSD for all.
  • Certificate from a non-approved centre. Training done abroad, or at a centre without approval for that course, may not be recognised. The same applies to online courses the administration has not approved.
  • Certificate cannot be verified. Many administrations now hold an online record. In India, for example, certificates are checked against the INDoS database. If your record is missing or does not match, you will be held until it is corrected.
  • Name or date of birth mismatch with your passport or seaman's book.
  • Flag state acceptance. Flag endorsement under STCW Regulation I/10 applies to certificates of competency, not normally to VI/6 proofs of proficiency. The flag state must still accept your document, and a few flags have their own conditions. Your company should confirm this before you join.
  • Photocopies only. Originals are usually required at joining, and PSC may ask to see them.

At Port State Control, inspectors may check that crew assigned security duties hold the right training. They may also ask a gangway watchkeeper simple questions, such as:

  • What is the current security level?
  • Who is the SSO?
  • What would you do with an unidentified visitor?

If the watchkeeper cannot answer, that can lead to a deficiency.

Worked scenario: the gangway at security level 2

You are AB on gangway watch at a container terminal. The port has just raised the security level to 2. The OOW tells you, and the SSP now calls for tighter access control.

  1. Check what your SSP says for level 2. Typically this means:
    • fewer access points
    • more ID checks
    • searching a higher proportion of persons and bags
    • extra deck patrols

Do exactly what the plan says. Do not improvise. 2. Verify every visitor against the expected-visitor list and their ID. Log them in and issue a visitor pass. 3. A person claiming to be a repair technician has no prior notice. - Do not let them board. - Hold them at the gangway and inform the OOW. The OOW contacts the SSO. - Boarding happens only once the visit is confirmed with the agent or company. 4. Record everything in the gangway log and the security records. 5. If they refuse to cooperate or try to force access, follow the SSP reporting procedure. That usually means the OOW, then the SSO, then the port facility.

This is the core of DSD: knowing your plan, recognising what is not right, and reporting through the right chain.

Oral exam corner: questions examiners and inspectors ask

  • Q: Under which STCW regulation is DSD training required? A: Regulation VI/6. The minimum standard is in Section A-VI/6, Table A-VI/6-2.
  • Q: What decides whether a seafarer needs DSD rather than awareness? A: Whether the ship security plan assigns them security duties.
  • Q: Name the three security levels. A: Level 1 normal, level 2 heightened, level 3 exceptional. The SSP sets the measures at each level.
  • Q: Who sets the security level for a ship? A: The flag administration (Contracting Government) sets the security level for its ships, and port States set it for their port facilities. A ship in port must comply with the port State's level if it is higher than the level set by its flag.
  • Q: Can you see the full SSP? A: Parts of it are confidential. Access is controlled by the master and SSO. You need to know the parts relevant to your duties.
  • Q: What would you do if you found an unattended bag in a restricted area? A:
    • Do not touch it.
    • Keep people clear.
    • Report it immediately through the SSP chain.
    • Follow the SSO's and master's instructions.
Key points
  • DSD is required by STCW Regulation VI/6 and Section A-VI/6 (Table A-VI/6-2) for anyone given duties under the ship security plan.
  • Without designated duties, security awareness meets the STCW minimum, but many companies require DSD for all crew.
  • DSD generally covers the awareness content; whether an SSO certificate covers DSD depends on the administration.
  • STCW sets no refresher requirement for VI/6 certificates, but administrations and companies may set their own validity.
  • Only training from a centre approved by the issuing administration, in an approved mode of delivery, is safe to rely on.
  • Most joining refusals come from the wrong level of certificate, unverifiable records or name mismatches.
  • Know your ship's SSP duties at each security level; PSC can test this at the gangway.

Frequently asked questions

Is DSD the same as Ship Security Awareness?

No. Awareness (Table A-VI/6-1) is for seafarers without security duties. DSD (Table A-VI/6-2) is for those assigned duties in the ship security plan. DSD is the higher level and generally includes the awareness content.

Does the DSD certificate expire?

STCW does not require refresher training for it, and many are issued without an expiry date. Some administrations or companies apply their own validity or reissue rules. If your certificate shows an expiry date, that date applies.

Can I do the DSD course online?

Only if your administration has approved that training centre to deliver it online or blended. Certificates from unapproved online providers are a common reason for rejection at joining. Check approval with the administration before you pay.

I am a rating with only security awareness. Can I keep gangway watch?

If the ship security plan assigns gangway watch as a security duty, you need DSD for that duty. Many companies require DSD for all crew for exactly this reason. Check your company's training matrix.

Does my DSD certificate need a flag state endorsement?

Endorsement under STCW Regulation I/10 is for certificates of competency, not normally for VI/6 proofs of proficiency. The flag state must still accept the document, and some flags have their own conditions. Your company or manning agent should confirm this before you join.

Test yourself

Which STCW regulation sets the requirement for DSD training?

Security-related training for all seafarers, including designated security duties, is in Regulation VI/6, while VI/5 covers the SSO. Reference: STCW Regulation VI/6, Section A-VI/6.

What determines whether a seafarer needs DSD rather than security awareness?

The requirement follows the duties assigned under the SSP, not rank, size or trading area. Reference: STCW Section A-VI/6.

Under STCW, how often must the DSD certificate be refreshed?

Unlike Basic Training, VI/6 security certificates have no STCW refresher requirement, though the administration may still set a validity. Reference: STCW Regulation VI/6.

Which of these is one of the competences in Table A-VI/6-2?

Security inspections are a DSD competence, while security assessments and plan approval sit with the CSO, the administration or a recognised security organisation. Reference: STCW Code Table A-VI/6-2.

A visitor arrives at the gangway at security level 2 claiming to be a technician, with no prior notice. What should the DSD-trained watchkeeper do?

Unverified persons are denied access and the matter is reported through the SSP chain. Reference: ISPS Code Part A, and the ship security plan.
