Advanced Training for Liquefied Gas Tanker Cargo Operations

Quick answer

Advanced Training for Liquefied Gas Tanker Cargo Operations is the STCW certificate of proficiency required by Regulation V/1-2. Masters, chief engineers, chief mates, second engineers and anyone with immediate responsibility for cargo operations on liquefied gas tankers (e.g. LNG, LPG, ethylene and ammonia carriers) must hold it. To qualify you need the basic gas tanker certificate, qualifying sea service or supervised onboard training, and an approved advanced course. You keep it valid with recent gas tanker service or approved training, under rules that vary by administration.

What the certificate is and where the requirement comes from

This is the senior tier of the two-level gas tanker training structure in the STCW Convention. Regulation V/1-2 sets the minimum requirements for masters, officers and ratings on liquefied gas tankers. Section A-V/1-2 of the STCW Code gives the detail, and Table A-V/1-2-2 lists the competences for the advanced level.

  • Basic training is for people with specific duties and responsibilities related to cargo or cargo equipment.
  • Advanced training is for people who run, plan and supervise cargo operations.

The basic and advanced levels are separate certificates of proficiency (CoP). Holding one does not give you the other.

Gas tanker training is also separate from oil and chemical tanker training, which is covered by Regulation V/1-1. An advanced oil or chemical tanker CoP does not qualify you for a senior cargo role on a liquefied gas tanker. The reverse is also true.

Older documents may use pre-2010 terms such as "tanker familiarisation", "specialised training" or "dangerous cargo endorsement". Your administration decides how older certificates were carried over to the current format. Do not assume an old document counts until it has been converted or confirmed.

Who needs it on board

Under Regulation V/1-2, the following must hold the advanced liquefied gas tanker CoP:

  • Master
  • Chief engineer officer
  • Chief mate
  • Second engineer officer
  • Any other person with immediate responsibility for loading, discharging, care in transit, handling of cargo, tank cleaning or other cargo-related operations

In practice, the last group often includes cargo engineers and gas engineers. On some ships it also includes a junior officer who runs part of the cargo watch. Who counts as having "immediate responsibility" is set by the company's SMS and the manning arrangements, so check your own ship's documents.

Junior officers and ratings with assigned cargo duties normally need at least the basic gas tanker CoP.

The STCW requirement is only the minimum. Charterers and terminals often apply their own officer experience matrices, which ask for more time in rank or on the ship type. These matrices are commercial requirements, not convention requirements, but they can still decide whether you are accepted for a post.

Entry requirements: service plus course

To qualify for the advanced CoP under Regulation V/1-2, you must meet all three conditions:

  1. Hold the basic CoP for liquefied gas tanker cargo operations.
  2. Have qualifying experience, in one of two forms:
    • at least three months of approved seagoing service on liquefied gas tankers, or
    • at least one month of approved onboard training on liquefied gas tankers in a supernumerary capacity. This must include at least three loading and three unloading operations and be documented in an approved training record book.
  3. Complete approved advanced training that meets the standard in Section A-V/1-2.

The basic CoP has its own prerequisites, including STCW basic safety training under Regulation VI/1.

Worked scenario. A third officer has two months on an LPG carrier and has just finished an advanced gas course ashore. They apply for the CoP and are refused. The reason is that the service is short of the three months required, and it was not done as a documented supernumerary programme.

Whether the course can be taken before the service is complete depends on the administration. Some allow the course first and issue the CoP only once the service is proven. Others want the service before you enrol. Check your administration's current rules before booking.

Course length, assessment method and approved training centres are set by each administration. India (DG Shipping) and the Philippines (MARINA) each publish their own approved courses and lists of institutes.

What the course covers

Table A-V/1-2-2 groups the competences broadly as follows.

Design and cargo knowledge - Ship design and cargo containment systems: independent tanks Type A, B and C, and membrane systems - The physics and chemistry of liquefied gases: - pressure-temperature relationships - boiling point - vapour density - reactivity and compatibility - hazards such as toxicity, flammability and cryogenic exposure

Cargo operations planning and control - Planning, performing and monitoring the full sequence of cargo operations: drying, inerting, purging, gassing-up, cool-down, loading, care in transit, discharging, warm-up and gas-freeing - Cargo calculations and the effect of operations on stability and hull stress - Boil-off and vapour management: - pressure control - reliquefaction - vapour return - Emergency shutdown (ESD) systems - The ship-shore interface

Safety and emergencies - Hazards to health and the use of safety data sheets - Personal protective equipment, gas detection and enclosed space precautions - Fire prevention and fire-fighting on gas tankers, including dry chemical powder systems - Emergency response to: - leaks - cargo spills on deck - injuries such as cold burns - loss of containment

Rules and pollution prevention - The IGC Code and the relevant parts of SOLAS and MARPOL - Port and terminal requirements - Pollution prevention and emission control

Validity and revalidation

STCW does not set a fixed validity period for tanker CoPs; the issuing administration decides this. However, Regulation I/11 and Section A-I/11 require masters and officers on ships with internationally agreed special training requirements, which includes gas tankers, to keep up their competence for continued service. They show this in one of two ways:

  • approved seagoing service on liquefied gas tankers, performing duties appropriate to the certificate held, totalling at least three months in the preceding five years, or
  • successfully completing approved relevant training.

How this is applied varies by administration:

  • Many issue the officer's tanker CoP with a five-year validity matched to the CoC.
  • Some link revalidation to CoC revalidation.
  • Rules for ratings who hold advanced CoPs, and the form of any refresher course, also vary.

Always read the expiry date and the conditions printed on your own CoP. Confirm current rules with the issuing authority.

For officers, the STCW recognition rules in Regulation I/10 apply to CoPs issued under V/1-2. If the ship's flag differs from your issuing administration, you will normally need a flag state endorsement that covers the gas tanker CoP, not only your CoC.

Why crew get stopped at joining

Problems with this certificate are found at the manning office, during the crew change, by Port State Control, or by a terminal or vetting inspector. The common causes are:

  • Only a course certificate, no CoP. A training centre's certificate is not the administration's certificate of proficiency.
  • Expired CoP or no revalidation. This often happens after a long spell on bulkers or containerships with no recent gas tanker time.
  • Wrong tanker type. The officer holds an advanced oil or chemical CoP but no gas CoP.
  • Wrong level. A newly promoted chief mate or second engineer still holds only the basic gas CoP.
  • No flag state endorsement, or an endorsement that does not list the gas tanker capacity.
  • Records that don't match. Spelling or date of birth differs between the CoP, the passport and the seaman's book. Sea service is not recorded in the discharge book, or not backed by testimonials.
  • A charterer matrix shortfall. The STCW paperwork is valid, but the officer lacks the time in rank or ship type the charterer requires.

Worked scenario. A third engineer on an LPG carrier is promoted to second engineer. They join a new LNG carrier holding only the basic gas CoP. The vetting check finds this, the company has to replace them, and the ship sails late. This is avoided by getting the advanced CoP issued before accepting the promotion.

Oral exam angle and keeping it in date

Examiners and superintendents often ask questions like these:

  • "Who on board must hold the advanced gas CoP?" Master, chief engineer, chief mate, second engineer, and anyone with immediate responsibility for cargo operations.
  • "What is the alternative to three months' gas tanker service?" One month of supernumerary onboard training with at least three loadings and three discharges, recorded in an approved training record book.
  • "Why does an LNG cargo tank need cool-down before loading?" To avoid thermal stress and excessive vapour generation from loading cryogenic cargo into warm tanks.
  • "What happens to tank pressure if boil-off is not managed?" It rises towards the relief valve setting. This leads into a discussion of reliquefaction, gas combustion and vapour return.

To keep the certificate in date:

  1. Diary the CoP expiry alongside your CoC and medical, and start renewal well before it is due.
  2. Keep copies of discharge book pages and sea service testimonials that show gas tanker service.
  3. If you move to another ship type for a long period, check whether you will still have the service needed to revalidate. If not, plan an approved course in time.
  4. When promoted, check you hold the advanced level before signing on in the new rank.
  5. Before joining under a new flag, ask the company to apply for an endorsement that lists the gas tanker CoP.
Key points
  • The advanced liquefied gas tanker CoP is required by STCW Regulation V/1-2, with competences in Table A-V/1-2-2.
  • Master, chief engineer, chief mate, second engineer and anyone with immediate responsibility for cargo operations must hold it.
  • To qualify you need the basic gas CoP plus three months of gas tanker service, or one month supernumerary with three loadings and three discharges.
  • Oil and chemical tanker CoPs (V/1-1) do not cover liquefied gas tankers.
  • Revalidation under I/11 needs three months of liquefied gas tanker service in the preceding five years, in duties appropriate to the certificate, or approved relevant training. Details vary by administration.
  • Officers serving under a foreign flag usually need a flag state endorsement that covers the gas CoP.
  • Charterer experience matrices can apply on top of STCW minimums.

Frequently asked questions

Can I do the advanced gas tanker course before completing my sea service?

It depends on your administration. Some allow you to take the course first and issue the CoP only once the qualifying service is proven. Others require the service before you enrol. Check your administration's current rules before booking.

Does my advanced oil tanker certificate let me work as chief mate on an LPG carrier?

No. Oil and chemical tanker training falls under Regulation V/1-1, while gas tankers fall under V/1-2. You need the advanced liquefied gas tanker CoP for senior cargo roles on liquefied gas tankers.

Do junior officers need the advanced gas certificate?

Only if they have immediate responsibility for cargo operations. Otherwise, junior officers with assigned cargo duties need at least the basic gas tanker CoP. Many companies and charterers expect junior officers to work towards the advanced level.

How do I revalidate my gas tanker CoP if I have been sailing on bulk carriers?

Without enough recent gas tanker service, you normally revalidate by completing approved relevant training. The service route requires three months on liquefied gas tankers, in duties appropriate to the certificate held, in the preceding five years. Each administration sets which course is accepted and how the renewed CoP is issued.

Is a training institute certificate enough to join a gas carrier?

Usually not. Joining requires the certificate of proficiency issued by the administration, not just the course completion certificate. Officers serving under another flag also typically need a flag state endorsement that covers it.

Test yourself

Which STCW regulation sets the requirements for advanced training for liquefied gas tanker cargo operations?

Gas tanker training is in Regulation V/1-2. V/1-1 covers oil and chemical tankers, V/2 covers passenger ships, and VI/3 covers advanced fire-fighting. Reference: STCW Regulation V/1-2.

Instead of three months of gas tanker service, what onboard training qualifies a candidate for the advanced CoP?

The alternative is at least one month of approved supernumerary onboard training on liquefied gas tankers, including at least three loadings and three discharges, recorded in an approved training record book. Reference: STCW Regulation V/1-2.

Which of these must hold the advanced liquefied gas tanker CoP?

Master, chief engineer, chief mate, second engineer and anyone with immediate responsibility for cargo operations need it. Others need it only if they hold such responsibility. Reference: STCW Regulation V/1-2.

For continued service on gas tankers, what demonstrates continued competence under STCW?

Section A-I/11 provides for three months of appropriate gas tanker service in the preceding five years, performing duties appropriate to the certificate held, or approved relevant training. Reference: STCW Regulation I/11 and Section A-I/11.

An officer holds the advanced chemical tanker CoP. Which statement is correct?

Chemical tanker training falls under V/1-1, and gas tanker training is a separate CoP under V/1-2. A flag endorsement cannot add a qualification the officer does not hold. Reference: STCW Regulations V/1-1 and V/1-2.
