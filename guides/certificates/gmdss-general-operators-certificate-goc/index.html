GMDSS General Operator's Certificate (GOC): what it is, who needs it and how to keep it valid

Quick answer

The GMDSS General Operator's Certificate (GOC) is the radio operator qualification for distress and safety communications on ships trading beyond Sea Area A1. Its requirement comes from SOLAS Chapter IV and STCW Chapter IV, and the certificate itself is issued under the ITU Radio Regulations. Deck officers on ocean-going ships normally need it alongside their CoC, together with a flag state endorsement where the ship's flag requires one. Most joining problems come from an expired GOC or a missing flag endorsement.

What the GOC is and where the requirement comes from

The GOC certifies that you can operate all the GMDSS equipment fitted on a ship trading in Sea Areas A1, A2, A3 and A4. That includes sending, receiving and handling distress, urgency and safety traffic. Three instruments work together:

  • SOLAS Chapter IV, Regulation 16. Every ship must carry personnel qualified for distress and safety radiocommunications to the satisfaction of the Administration. Those personnel must hold the certificates specified in the Radio Regulations. One qualified person must be designated to have primary responsibility for radiocommunications during distress incidents.
  • STCW Chapter IV. Regulation IV/2 sets the minimum requirements for certifying GMDSS radio operators. Section A-IV/2 of the STCW Code sets the knowledge and competence standards. Anyone in charge of or performing radio duties on a ship required to participate in the GMDSS must hold an appropriate certificate issued or recognised by the Administration.
  • ITU Radio Regulations. These define the certificate classes. The GOC and the Restricted Operator's Certificate (ROC) are the two that matter to most deck officers.

The modernised SOLAS Chapter IV came into force on 1 January 2024. Its main change was to define Sea Area A3 by the coverage of a recognised mobile satellite service rather than a single provider. Your GOC competence still covers the same core job: alerting, communicating and receiving maritime safety information.

Who issues it. This varies by country. In some administrations the telecommunications authority issues the radio certificate and the maritime administration issues the STCW endorsement. In others a single maritime authority handles both. Check with your own administration rather than assuming.

Who needs a GOC (and when an ROC is enough)

Situation Typical requirement
Deck officers on ships trading beyond Sea Area A1 (most deep-sea trades) GOC
Ships operating only within Sea Area A1 (VHF DSC coverage) ROC may be accepted by the Administration
Master and navigating officers who keep watch on GMDSS equipment GOC, as required by flag and company SMS
Engineers, ratings, cadets Not normally required, though some cadets complete it before their OOW exam

In practice, most administrations expect an OOW candidate on ocean-going ships to hold a GOC at or before issue of the CoC. STCW requires deck officer candidates to meet the Chapter IV requirements for performing designated radio duties. Whether the GOC is a prerequisite for your CoC exam or can follow it is decided by your administration.

The GOC is an operator certificate, not a maintenance certificate. SOLAS Chapter IV requires ships to ensure equipment availability by duplication, shore-based maintenance or at-sea maintenance capability, or a combination of these. Where at-sea electronic maintenance is the chosen method, a separate radio-electronic qualification is involved. The GOC does not cover it.

What the GOC course and exam cover

Course content follows STCW Section A-IV/2 and the Radio Regulations. Many training providers build their courses on the IMO model course for the GOC. Course length, fees and exam format are set by the approving authority and the institute, so take them from your administration's approved course list, not from crew-mess talk.

Expect to cover:

  • GMDSS principles. The nine functional requirements, the sea areas and carriage requirements by sea area.
  • DSC on VHF, MF and HF. Distress, urgency, safety and routine calls, acknowledgement and relay rules, and the frequencies used.
  • Radiotelephony procedures. Distress (MAYDAY), urgency (PAN PAN) and safety (SECURITE) messages, phonetic alphabet and standard phrases.
  • Satellite communications. Recognised mobile satellite services, distress alerting, and EGC/SafetyNET-type MSI reception.
  • MSI. NAVTEX, NAVAREA warnings and weather.
  • Survival craft equipment. EPIRBs, SARTs (radar and AIS types) and two-way VHF.
  • Search and rescue. The IAMSAR framework, the role of RCCs and coast stations, and communications in SAR.
  • False alerts. How to prevent and cancel them.
  • Routine operations. Equipment tests, battery checks and the radio log.

There is normally a substantial practical element on a GMDSS simulator, followed by written and practical or oral assessment.

Oral exam corner: questions examiners like

1. "You've sent a VHF DSC distress alert by mistake. What do you do?" Stop the transmission immediately and reset the equipment so the alert does not repeat. Then cancel it by voice on VHF Channel 16. Address all stations and give the ship's name, call sign, MMSI and position. Say you are cancelling the distress alert sent at a stated date and time (UTC). Newer equipment may also offer a DSC self-cancellation function. Use it if fitted, but the voice cancellation still follows. Record the incident in the GMDSS log. Examiners want a calm, correct sequence, not panic and silence.

2. "You receive a VHF DSC distress alert from a ship nearby. Do you acknowledge it by DSC?" Normally no. Listen on Channel 16 and give the coast station or RCC time to acknowledge. If you are in a position to assist, acknowledge by voice on Channel 16 and inform the master. Acknowledging by DSC is generally the shore's job. Ships acknowledging by DSC can cause confusion and stop the casualty's alert from repeating.

3. "Name the main frequencies you'd use." The examiner expects these off by heart:

  • VHF Channel 70 for DSC
  • Channel 16 for voice distress
  • 2187.5 kHz for MF DSC distress
  • 2182 kHz for MF voice distress
  • 518 kHz for international NAVTEX

4. "How does a SART show on radar?" A radar SART responds on the X-band (9 GHz) radar. It shows as a line of dots extending outward from the SART's position along its bearing. The dots change to arcs and then circles as you close in.

5. "Who is responsible for radio communications in distress?" The person designated under SOLAS Chapter IV, Regulation 16, as recorded in the muster list and the ship's SMS. Know who it is on your ship.

Validity, revalidation and flag endorsements

Validity. Under STCW Regulation I/11, radio operators serving at sea, or intending to return to sea, must revalidate their certificates at intervals not exceeding five years. Some administrations align the GOC expiry with the CoC; others do not. Your certificate's printed expiry date is what counts, not a rule of thumb.

How you revalidate depends on your administration. Common routes include:

  • approved sea service involving radio duties within a set period
  • a revalidation or refresher course
  • a test or assessment
  • a combination of the above

The exact sea-time thresholds, refresher requirements and documents differ between countries. Check your administration's current circulars before you plan leave around it.

Flag state endorsement. If the ship is not under your national flag, the flag state must recognise your GOC under STCW Regulation I/10, usually by issuing an endorsement. Many open registries issue a GMDSS endorsement separate from the CoC endorsement. Some allow sailing on proof of application for a limited period. STCW Regulation I/10 caps this at three months; the flag may allow less or none. Whether that is accepted, and for how long, is set by the flag state, not by the company.

Why officers get stopped at joining over the GOC

These are the usual problems found by crewing departments, masters at handover and PSC inspectors:

  • Expired GOC. The CoC was revalidated but the GOC was forgotten, or the two expire on different dates.
  • Expired or missing flag GMDSS endorsement. Officers renew the national GOC and then forget that the flag endorsement still shows the old expiry.
  • ROC instead of GOC for a ship trading beyond Sea Area A1.
  • Name or document-number mismatch between the GOC, passport, CDC and flag endorsement.
  • Application receipt not acceptable to that flag, or past the permitted period.
  • Originals not carried. Scans may get you through pre-joining checks, but the flag or PSC may require originals or verifiable certificates on board.

PSC officers can check that the officers keeping radio watch hold valid certificates. A missing GOC on the designated distress radio operator is a straightforward deficiency.

Worked scenario and tips for keeping it in date

Scenario. A 2nd Officer is signed off with a CoC and GOC both valid for another seven months. The next ship is under a different flag. The officer gets a contract offer for a five-month trip starting in six weeks.

Work it through step by step:

  1. Count the end date, not the start. The trip plus a possible extension takes the GOC past expiry while on board.
  2. Revalidate now. Check the administration's current requirements and book any refresher or assessment during this leave.
  3. Apply for the new flag's GMDSS endorsement once the revalidated GOC is in hand. Ask the company which documents the flag requires.
  4. Carry originals and copies, and confirm the names match across all documents.

If the officer joins on the old GOC and plans to sort it out later, revalidation from on board may be slow or impossible, depending on the administration.

Habits that keep you out of trouble:

  • Keep one expiry list covering CoC, GOC, every flag endorsement, medical and STCW courses. Set reminders well ahead.
  • Treat each flag GMDSS endorsement as a separate document with its own expiry.
  • Keep copies of the GMDSS log pages and sign-off documents that show radio duties, in case your administration asks for evidence of sea service.
  • Practise on the real equipment during watches: DSC menus, the cancellation procedure and printer and EGC settings. Simulator skills fade quickly.
Key points
  • The GOC is the GMDSS radio operator certificate needed on ships trading beyond Sea Area A1; an ROC is limited to A1.
  • The requirement comes from SOLAS Chapter IV Reg 16 and STCW Reg IV/2; the certificate classes come from the ITU Radio Regulations.
  • STCW Reg I/11 requires revalidation at intervals not exceeding five years; the method depends on your administration.
  • Foreign-flag service normally needs a separate flag GMDSS endorsement, which has its own expiry date.
  • The most common joining stoppers are an expired GOC or an expired or missing flag endorsement.
  • Know the false-alert cancellation sequence and the DSC acknowledgement rules cold for orals and real watches.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between GMDSS GOC and ROC?

The GOC covers operation of all GMDSS equipment for every sea area, A1 to A4. The ROC is limited to equipment and procedures for Sea Area A1, essentially VHF and VHF DSC. Deep-sea officers need the GOC.

Is a GOC compulsory for an OOW (deck) certificate of competency?

For officers on ships trading beyond Sea Area A1, administrations generally require the GOC alongside the OOW CoC. STCW requires deck officer candidates to meet the Chapter IV requirements for designated radio duties. Whether you must hold it before the CoC exam or can obtain it afterwards is decided by your administration.

How long is a GMDSS GOC valid?

STCW Regulation I/11 requires radio operators to revalidate at intervals not exceeding five years. The actual expiry is whatever is printed on your certificate. Revalidation requirements, such as sea service, a refresher course or a test, are set by your administration.

Do I need a separate GMDSS flag endorsement?

If the ship's flag differs from the country that issued your GOC, the flag state must recognise it under STCW Regulation I/10. Many flags issue a dedicated GMDSS endorsement separate from the CoC endorsement. Whether an application receipt is acceptable while the endorsement is processed depends on the flag.

Can I revalidate my GOC while on board?

Sometimes, but it depends entirely on your administration. Some require attendance at a refresher course or assessment ashore. Others accept applications with evidence of sea service. Plan revalidation during leave if the expiry falls within your next contract.

Test yourself

Which STCW regulation sets the mandatory minimum requirements for certification of GMDSS radio operators?

STCW Regulation IV/2 covers GMDSS radio operator certification, with the standards in STCW Code Section A-IV/2.

A ship trades between Europe and the Far East via the Indian Ocean. Which certificate do its watchkeeping deck officers normally need?

Ships trading beyond Sea Area A1 need personnel holding the GOC; the ROC covers only Sea Area A1 operation (ITU Radio Regulations; STCW Section A-IV/2).

You transmit a VHF DSC distress alert in error. What is the correct action?

False alerts must be cancelled promptly by voice on the associated distress frequency (Channel 16 for VHF) and logged, in line with IMO guidance on avoidance and cancellation of false distress alerts.

Under STCW, at what maximum interval must a radio operator certificate be revalidated?

STCW Regulation I/11 requires revalidation of certificates, including radio operators', at intervals not exceeding five years.

You receive a VHF DSC distress alert from a nearby vessel in Sea Area A1. What should you normally do?

Ships normally do not acknowledge a VHF DSC distress alert by DSC; they monitor Channel 16, allow the coast station/RCC to acknowledge, and respond by voice if able to assist, in line with IMO guidance on DSC distress alert procedures.
