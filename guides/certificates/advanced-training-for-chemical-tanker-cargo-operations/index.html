Advanced Training for Chemical Tanker Cargo Operations

Quick answer

Advanced Training for Chemical Tanker Cargo Operations is the STCW Regulation V/1-1, paragraph 3 certificate of proficiency. It is required for the master, chief engineer, chief mate, second engineer and anyone with immediate responsibility for cargo work on a chemical tanker. To qualify you need basic tanker training, qualifying chemical tanker sea service or supernumerary training, and an approved course. For officers, tanker competence must be revalidated at intervals not exceeding five years.

What the certificate is and where it comes from

This is the senior tanker qualification for chemical ships. It sits above Basic Training for Oil and Chemical Tanker Cargo Operations.

The legal basis is the STCW Convention:

  • Regulation V/1-1, paragraph 3 sets out who must hold it and the entry conditions.
  • STCW Code, Section A-V/1-1, paragraph 3 and Table A-V/1-1-3 set the standard of competence the course and assessment must meet.
  • Section B-V/1 gives non-mandatory guidance, including on the onboard training route.

Once you qualify, your administration issues a Certificate of Proficiency (CoP). Some administrations instead endorse an existing certificate. The form, numbering and any printed expiry date are national matters.

Under STCW Regulation I/1, a chemical tanker is a ship built or adapted, and used, to carry in bulk any liquid product listed in chapter 17 of the IBC Code. If your ship holds a Certificate of Fitness listing chapter 17 products and is actually used to carry them, it is likely a chemical tanker for STCW purposes. How service is credited is for the administration to decide. Many product tankers carry IMO-type chemical certification, so check what the ship is actually trading.

Who needs it on board

Regulation V/1-1 names these positions directly:

  • Master
  • Chief engineer officer
  • Chief mate
  • Second engineer officer
  • Any person with immediate responsibility for loading, discharging, care in transit, handling of cargo, tank cleaning or other cargo-related operations

The last category is where practice varies. Whether a junior officer keeping cargo watches, a cargo engineer or a pumpman counts as having "immediate responsibility" is set by:

  • the company's Safety Management System and manning matrix,
  • the flag administration's interpretation, and
  • charterer and oil-major vetting expectations.

Many operators require advanced chemical training for every officer who will be in charge of a cargo watch. Check the SMS, not just the convention minimum.

Ratings and junior officers with specific cargo duties, but without immediate responsibility, need the basic tanker training under paragraph 1 of the same regulation.

Advanced oil and advanced chemical are separate certificates. Advanced oil comes under paragraph 2 and advanced chemical under paragraph 3. Holding one does not cover the other. Many people get caught by this when moving from crude or clean product ships to IMO chemical tonnage.

Entry requirements: the two sea-service routes

To be certified under paragraph 3 you must meet three conditions.

  1. Meet the requirements for basic oil and chemical tanker training.
  2. While qualified for basic training, complete one of these:
    • at least three months of approved seagoing service on chemical tankers, or
    • at least one month of approved onboard training on chemical tankers as a supernumerary. This must include at least three loading and three unloading operations, documented in an approved training record book.
  3. Complete an approved advanced chemical tanker course and meet the standard of competence in Section A-V/1-1, paragraph 3.

Points that trip people up:

  • Order matters. Under the convention, the chemical tanker service must be completed while you are qualified for basic tanker training. Service before that does not count towards paragraph 3.
  • The service must be on a chemical tanker. Oil tanker time does not satisfy paragraph 3.
  • Supernumerary means supernumerary. The one-month route is for someone carried additional to the safe manning. You cannot use it while counted as a watchkeeper or rating in the crew list.
  • Evidence. Administrations usually want sea service testimonials or discharge book entries showing ship type. Some also want the IMO number, so they can verify the ship is a chemical tanker. Find out what your administration accepts before you apply.

What the course covers

Approved courses are commonly built on IMO Model Course 1.03. Duration, fees and the assessment method are set by the administration and the training provider, so check locally.

Table A-V/1-1-3 groups the competences roughly as follows.

Competence area What you actually learn
Safe performance and monitoring of cargo operations Chemical tanker design and ship types under the IBC Code; cargo and stripping systems, including deepwell pumps; loading, discharge and ballast planning; tank cleaning, prewash and ventilation; inerting, padding and nitrogen use; cargo heating and temperature control
Properties of chemical cargoes Physical and chemical properties; reactivity, polymerisation and inhibitors; cargo compatibility and segregation; use of SDS and cargo data
Hazard prevention Toxicity, flammability, corrosivity; vapour control; tank and coating compatibility
Occupational health and safety Gas measurement, PPE, enclosed space entry, decontamination
Emergency response Cargo spills, leaks, fires, exposure casualties; medical first aid guidance for dangerous goods (MFAG)
Pollution prevention MARPOL Annex II categories, the Procedures and Arrangements (P&A) Manual, Cargo Record Book, prewash and discharge conditions
Legislative compliance IBC Code, MARPOL Annex II, SOLAS and the ship's Certificate of Fitness

Expect scenarios built around the P&A Manual and a cargo plan. Treat the course as preparation for the cargo officer's job, not just a ticket.

Validity and revalidation

There are two separate layers here, and they are often confused.

1. STCW revalidation for masters and officers (Regulation I/11 and Section A-I/11)

Masters and officers continuing in tanker service must establish continued professional competence for tankers at intervals not exceeding five years. You can do this by either:

  • approved tanker service, performing duties appropriate to the tanker certificate held, of at least three months in total during the preceding five years, or
  • successfully completing a relevant approved training course or courses.

2. The administration's own dating of the CoP

Many administrations print an expiry date on the tanker CoP. Many also link renewal to evidence of recent tanker service or a refresher course. The rules for ratings holding advanced chemical training are a national matter. Do not assume your CoP is unlimited because Regulation I/11 talks about masters and officers.

Two more things to keep in mind:

  • Underlying certificates. Your advanced CoP depends on your basic tanker training and STCW basic safety training. Basic safety training has its own refresher requirements. If those lapse, some administrations and companies will treat you as not qualified.
  • What "relevant" service means. Whether oil tanker service counts towards revalidating a chemical certificate is for the administration to decide. If your recent time is all on oil tankers and you hold both certificates, check what your administration accepts before the expiry date is close.

Why crew get stopped at joining

These are the problems superintendents, manning agents and Port State Control inspectors find most often:

  • Wrong tanker CoP. The officer holds advanced oil but is joining an IMO chemical ship, or the reverse.
  • CoP expired or about to expire mid-contract. Many companies will not send you if a tanker CoP expires during the planned contract.
  • No flag state endorsement covering the tanker qualification. Many flags require their recognition endorsement for officers serving on their ships to reflect tanker qualifications. Check the endorsement wording, not just that one exists.
  • Basic tanker or basic safety certificates out of date or missing. The advanced CoP alone is not enough.
  • Certificate not verifiable. Online verification fails because of a typo in a document number, a name mismatch with the passport, or a certificate that was never entered on the administration's database.
  • Course from a provider not approved by the issuing administration. The course certificate exists, but no CoP was ever issued on the strength of it.
  • Sea service that does not qualify. This shows up when a vetting inspector or superintendent checks the ship type behind your testimonial.

Under Regulation I/4, Port State Control can check that every seafarer required to be certificated holds an appropriate certificate or valid dispensation. A missing or wrong tanker CoP for a senior officer is a serious finding.

Worked scenario and oral exam practice

Scenario. A second officer has five years on product tankers and holds basic tanker training and advanced oil. The company offers chief mate on an IMO type 2 chemical tanker.

What needs checking:

  1. Certificate of competency. He must already hold, or obtain, a chief mate CoC under Regulation II/2. The tanker CoP does not replace it.
  2. Advanced chemical. He has none. He needs three months approved chemical tanker service, or the one-month supernumerary route, plus the approved course.
  3. Previous ships. Some of his "product tankers" may have held a Certificate of Fitness for chapter 17 products and actually traded chemicals. He should get testimonials showing ship type and ask the administration whether that service counts.
  4. Flag endorsement. Once the CoP is issued, he needs the new flag's endorsement to reflect it.
  5. Expiry dates. He should check the expiry of all tanker CoPs against the contract length.

Typical oral questions:

  • What is the P&A Manual and who approves it? It sets out the ship's approved procedures and arrangements for handling Annex II cargoes, cleaning and residue discharge. It is approved by the administration or by a recognised organisation on its behalf.
  • What does MARPOL Annex II Category X mean for tank cleaning? It is the most harmful category. A prewash is required, with residues delivered to a reception facility, before the ship leaves the port of unloading, unless the Annex allows otherwise. Details are in Annex II and the P&A Manual.
  • Before loading a cargo that needs an inhibitor, what document do you check? The inhibitor certificate from the manufacturer. Check the inhibitor's name and amount, its effective lifetime against the voyage length, temperature limits and any oxygen dependency.
  • Where do you confirm the ship may carry a product? The Certificate of Fitness and its product list, read with chapter 17 of the IBC Code.
  • Where are Annex II operations recorded? In the Cargo Record Book.

Keeping it in date

  • Keep a certificate tracker. List the expiry of your CoC, basic safety training, basic tanker CoP, advanced chemical CoP, flag endorsements and medical. Review it before every contract.
  • Log tanker service properly. Get sea service testimonials stating ship type (for example, oil/chemical tanker IMO type 2), your duties and the IMO number. Then the three-months-in-five-years route is easy to prove.
  • Apply for renewal early. Administrations can take time, and agents need verified documents before booking travel.
  • Hold the right certificate for the ship type. If you move between oil and chemical tonnage, hold and keep both advanced certificates current.
  • Check verification yourself. Look your certificate up on the administration's online verification system and correct errors before the manning agent finds them.
  • Keep originals with you. Carry the originals on board. Keep scanned copies separately in case documents are lost.
Key points
  • Advanced chemical tanker training is required by STCW Regulation V/1-1, paragraph 3, with the standard in Section A-V/1-1 and Table A-V/1-1-3.
  • It is mandatory for the master, chief engineer, chief mate, second engineer and anyone with immediate responsibility for cargo operations on a chemical tanker.
  • Entry needs basic tanker training first, then three months chemical tanker service or one supernumerary month with three loadings and three discharges, plus an approved course.
  • Advanced oil and advanced chemical are separate certificates, and one does not cover the other.
  • Masters and officers revalidate tanker competence at intervals not exceeding five years, by three months' tanker service in duties appropriate to the certificate held, or an approved course.
  • CoP expiry dates, rules for ratings and flag endorsement requirements vary by administration.
  • Most joining problems come from the wrong tanker CoP, expiry mid-contract, missing flag endorsement or failed online verification.

Frequently asked questions

Can I do the advanced chemical tanker course without chemical tanker sea service?

Many providers will let you attend the course. However, the administration will only issue the Certificate of Proficiency once you also meet the sea-service condition in Regulation V/1-1, paragraph 3. That means three months on chemical tankers, or one month supernumerary with at least three loadings and three discharges. Check your administration's order of steps before booking.

Does my advanced oil tanker certificate allow me to serve as chief mate on a chemical tanker?

No. Advanced oil comes under paragraph 2 of Regulation V/1-1 and advanced chemical under paragraph 3. They are separate qualifications. To hold a senior cargo position on a chemical tanker, you need the chemical one.

How long is the advanced chemical tanker certificate valid?

For masters and officers, STCW requires tanker competence to be re-established at intervals not exceeding five years. You can do this through at least three months' approved tanker service in the preceding five years, performing duties appropriate to the tanker certificate held, or through an approved course. Whether the CoP itself carries an expiry date, and how ratings revalidate, depends on the issuing administration.

Does product tanker service count towards the chemical tanker requirement?

Only if the ship was a chemical tanker in STCW terms. That means it was built or adapted, and used, to carry products listed in chapter 17 of the IBC Code. Many dual-purpose ships qualify, but the administration decides what it accepts. Get testimonials that state the ship type and IMO number.

Do junior officers need advanced chemical training?

The convention requires it for anyone with immediate responsibility for cargo operations. Many companies and charterers interpret that to include officers in charge of a cargo watch. Your company's SMS and manning requirements are the deciding document, so check before joining.

Test yourself

Which STCW provision sets the requirement for Advanced Training for Chemical Tanker Cargo Operations?

Paragraph 3 of Regulation V/1-1 covers advanced chemical training. Paragraph 2 covers advanced oil, V/1-2 covers liquefied gas tankers, and VI/3 covers advanced fire fighting. Reference: STCW Regulation V/1-1.

Which sea-service route qualifies a candidate under Regulation V/1-1, paragraph 3?

The service must be on chemical tankers. The shorter route must be supernumerary and documented in an approved training record book. Reference: STCW Regulation V/1-1, paragraph 3.

How does an officer re-establish continued professional competence for tanker service under STCW?

Regulation I/11 requires this at intervals not exceeding five years, by either of the two routes. Reference: STCW Regulation I/11 and Section A-I/11.

A chief mate holds advanced oil tanker training only. What is his position on an IMO chemical tanker?

Basic training is combined, but the advanced certificates are separate under paragraphs 2 and 3. Reference: STCW Regulation V/1-1.

For STCW purposes, what makes a ship a chemical tanker?

The STCW definition is tied to chapter 17 of the IBC Code, not to tank material or flashpoint. Reference: STCW Regulation I/1.
