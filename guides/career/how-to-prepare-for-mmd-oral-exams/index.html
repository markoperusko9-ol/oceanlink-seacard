How to prepare for MMD oral exams

Quick answer

MMD orals are spoken exams held at the Mercantile Marine Departments, taken one function at a time. An examiner checks whether you can actually do the job your CoC will allow. Under DGS Circular 21 of 2026, you book through DG Shipping's Examination Module (exams.dgma.gov.in), which is linked to seafarer profiles migrated to e-Samudra, and you carry your original documents. To prepare, link every regulation back to a real situation on board. Most candidates fail because of rote answers, weak basics or wrong priorities in emergencies, not because they missed obscure facts.

What the MMD oral actually tests

The oral is where the Administration decides whether you are safe to sign as the officer your certificate describes. The written papers show you can study. The oral checks whether you can think on your feet, set priorities and apply the rules to a real ship.

Indian CoC examinations are held at the Mercantile Marine Departments (MMDs). They are run under the Merchant Shipping (STCW) Rules, 2014 and DG Shipping's Training, Examination and Assessment Programme (TEAP), as amended. Orals are taken one function at a time, following the competence tables in the STCW Code:

Department STCW functions covered in the orals
Deck (2nd Mate, Chief Mate, Master) Navigation; Cargo handling and stowage; Controlling the operation of the ship and care for persons on board
Engine (MEO classes) Marine engineering; Electrical, electronic and control engineering; Maintenance and repair; Controlling the operation of the ship and care for persons on board

For engineers, the oral covers the STCW engine functions. The current DG Shipping notice sets out how the oral parts are grouped for each MEO class.

The level depends on the grade. A 2nd Mate candidate is examined at the operational level (STCW Code, Section A-II/1). Chief Mate and Master candidates are examined at the management level (Section A-II/2).

Take a question about a fire in the hold as an example. A watch officer is expected to raise the alarm and follow the muster list. A Master is expected to cover decision-making, communications with the company and shore, stability and a port of refuge.

Deck candidates should check the current DG Shipping Nautical Wing notice for where and how the signals exam is held.

Step by step: from eligibility to an oral seat

The examination system has changed recently. Follow the latest DG Shipping notices rather than an old WhatsApp forward.

  1. Check eligibility first. The STCW Rules 2014 and DG Shipping's TEAP (as amended) set the requirements for sea time, approved courses, the TAR book (for cadets) and other conditions. If you are not eligible, you will not get a seat however well you prepare.
  2. Make sure your e-Samudra profile is correct. DG Shipping migrated existing seafarer profiles to e-Samudra automatically. Seafarers were asked to make sure their e-mail ID and mobile number were correct beforehand, and to update other details on e-Samudra once told to do so. Under DGS Circular 21 of 2026, eligibility assessment, seat booking and result declaration are handled only on DG Shipping's new Examination Module (exams.dgma.gov.in), which is linked to the migrated profiles. Bookings were paused during the migration, so check the DGMA/DG Shipping site for the current status.
  3. Apply online for the eligibility assessment for your grade on the Examination Module, and pay through its payment gateway. Fees were not refundable under the previous e-Governance system. Check whether the same rule applies on the new module, and confirm the grade, function and centre before you pay.
  4. Document verification. First-time candidates are normally called to have their original documents checked, on dates the MMD publishes.
  5. Book the oral seat for your function(s) once the assessment is approved. The MMDs publish booking windows and exam schedules. Under the previous system, repeaters could see booking, written and oral schedules online. Check how this works on the new module.
  6. Changes after booking. Under the previous system, a booked month could not be changed online, and candidates had to e-mail the examination help-desk of the MMD concerned. Recheck this rule on the new Examination Module.

Fees, booking windows and portal procedures all change. Always check the current notices on the DGMA/DG Shipping website and the Examination Module before you act.

Documents to have ready

The exact list depends on your grade and on the MMD notice. Expect to show the originals of everything you uploaded. Typically you will need:

  • Passport, CDC and INDoS details that match your e-Samudra profile exactly (name, date of birth)
  • Proof of sea service: discharge entries, sea service testimonials and company letters as required
  • Previous CoC (for higher grades) and any endorsements
  • Valid STCW course certificates required for the grade, including the GMDSS certificate where it applies to deck orals
  • Valid medical fitness certificate from a DG-approved doctor
  • Written exam results or admit card, and the booking confirmation for the oral
  • TAR book, if your grade requires it (2nd Mate)

Keep everything in one file, in the same order as your application. Examiners and clerks are busy. A candidate who cannot find his own testimonial makes a poor impression before the first technical question.

How to study: from the ship outwards

Good preparation starts on board, months before the course.

  • Use your own ship. Know her particulars, stability booklet, fire control plan, muster list, steering gear, OWS and emergency generator. Examiners often open with "What was your last ship?" and build on your answers.
  • Know exactly what the Rules of the Road mean. For deck candidates, COLREG is where examiners accept the least vagueness. Practise lights, shapes and sound signals with cards. Explain Rules 7, 8 and 13 to 19 in your own words, with examples.
  • Know how the main instruments are laid out: SOLAS chapters, MARPOL annexes, STCW, ISM, ISPS and MLC. You need to know what is where and why. You do not need to recite the text.
  • Draw while you explain. Practise quick, clean sketches of a fuel oil system, a stability curve, a CO2 release arrangement or a radar plot.
  • Do mock orals. Pair up with a classmate and ask each other open questions with follow-ups. Answering aloud is a different skill from reading.
  • Engineers: expect to draw systems by hand and to explain safe isolation. Know the reasoning behind every alarm and trip, not just the setpoint.

If a requirement varies by flag, company SMS or equipment maker, say so. "It depends on the maker's manual and our SMS, but the principle is..." is a strong answer. Inventing a number is a weak one.

On the day: conduct in the examiner's room

  • Arrive early, in formal dress, with your file in order.
  • Greet the examiner, sit when asked, and keep your phone switched off and out of sight.
  • Listen to the whole question. If it is unclear, ask, for example: "Sir, do you mean at sea or alongside?" Asking for clarification is professional, not a weakness.
  • Answer in order of priority: safety of life, then the ship, then the environment, then cargo and commercial matters.
  • If you do not know, say so plainly. Bluffing on a safety question is the fastest way out of the room.
  • Do not argue. If you are sure you are right, give your reasoning and the reference calmly, once.

Oral exam practice: typical questions and how to answer

Scenario 1 (2nd Mate, Function 1). "You are OOW in restricted visibility. Radar shows a target fine on the starboard bow, CPA 0.5 mile, closing. What do you do?"

A good answer:

  • Call the Master as per standing orders.
  • Confirm you are at safe speed and sounding the correct signals.
  • Plot the target systematically on radar.
  • State that Rule 19 applies, not the steering and sailing rules for vessels in sight of one another.
  • For a vessel forward of the beam that you are not overtaking, avoid altering to port. Alter to starboard in good time and/or reduce speed.
  • Keep monitoring until she is clear.

Scenario 2 (Chief Mate, Function 2). "During loading you find the ship listing and the GM lower than planned."

A good answer:

  • Stop cargo.
  • Work out whether it is a list or a loll by checking free surface, slack tanks and weight distribution.
  • If it is loll, first press up slack tanks or ballast a low-side divided double-bottom tank, then the opposite side. Never shift weight to the high side.
  • If it is a list caused by an off-centre weight and GM is adequate, correct it by transferring weight to the high side.
  • Inform the Master and the terminal.

Scenario 3 (MEO, controlling operation). "Oil mist detector alarm on the main engine."

A good answer:

  • Inform the bridge.
  • Reduce speed or stop as per procedure.
  • Do not open the crankcase until it has cooled for the time given in the maker's instructions.
  • Keep people clear of the relief valves.
  • Find the cause before restarting.

The examiner always looks for the same pattern: immediate safety action, then reasoning, then reporting, then the reference.

Common reasons candidates are turned back or fail

Before the oral:

  • Profile details on e-Samudra do not match the CDC or passport
  • Course or medical certificates have expired by the date of verification
  • Sea service is not properly documented, or testimonials are incomplete
  • The wrong function or month was booked, and the candidate assumed it could be changed online

In the oral:

  • Rote-learned definitions that fall apart at the first follow-up question
  • Wrong action in a COLREG or emergency scenario, such as altering to port in restricted visibility, opening a hot crankcase, or entering an enclosed space without a permit
  • Not knowing the equipment on your own last ship
  • An over-confident, argumentative attitude

After a fail: note the examiner's remarks, work on the weak area and rebook. Repeaters rebook after the gap set in the current DG Shipping examination notice.

Philippine officers: MARINA runs its own assessment system. Check with MARINA directly rather than applying Indian rules.

Key points
  • MMD orals are taken one function at a time and test whether you can act safely as the officer your CoC describes.
  • Eligibility assessment, seat booking and results now run on DG Shipping's Examination Module (exams.dgma.gov.in), linked to e-Samudra profiles.
  • Keep your e-Samudra profile matching your CDC and passport exactly.
  • The old system had no refunds and no online month changes; recheck these rules on the new module.
  • Carry the originals of every uploaded document, with courses and medical valid on the day.
  • Study from your own ship outwards, practise answering aloud, and answer in priority order: life, ship, environment, cargo.
  • Fees, schedules and portal procedures change often, so check the current DGMA/DG Shipping notices before booking.

Frequently asked questions

Which portal do I use to book MMD oral exams?

Under DGS Circular 21 of 2026, eligibility assessment, seat booking and result declaration are handled only on DG Shipping's new Examination Module (exams.dgma.gov.in). The module is linked to seafarer profiles that DG Shipping migrated to e-Samudra automatically. Bookings were paused during the migration, so check the DGMA/DG Shipping site for the current status.

Can I change my oral exam month after booking?

Under the previous e-Governance system, a booked month could not be changed online, and candidates had to e-mail the examination help-desk of the MMD concerned. Fees were also not refundable. Bookings now run on the new Examination Module, so recheck these rules in the current DG Shipping notices before you book.

How long should I prepare for MMD orals?

There is no official figure. Most successful candidates start revising on board, then use their time ashore for structured revision and mock orals. Book only when you can handle follow-up questions confidently, not just recite definitions.

What documents should I carry to the MMD for my oral?

Carry the originals of everything you uploaded. This typically means your passport, CDC, sea service proof and testimonials, previous CoC if any, valid STCW course certificates and a valid DG-approved medical, plus your booking confirmation. The exact list depends on your grade and the MMD notice, so check it before you travel.

What happens if I fail an oral function?

You rebook the failed function as a repeater, after the gap set in the current DG Shipping examination notice. Use the examiner's remarks to focus your revision on the weak area before you go back.

Test yourself

A 2nd Mate candidate is examined orally at which STCW level?

Competence for an OOW on ships of 500 GT or more is set at the operational level. STCW Code, Section A-II/1.

COLREG Rule 19 applies to vessels in which situation?

Rule 19(a) applies to vessels not in sight of one another when navigating in or near an area of restricted visibility. COLREG 1972, Rule 19.

In restricted visibility, you detect a radar target forward of the beam, not one you are overtaking, and risk of collision exists. Which action should you avoid as far as possible?

Rule 19(d) says to avoid altering to port for a vessel forward of the beam, other than one being overtaken. COLREG 1972, Rule 19.

Your vessel hears the fog signal of another vessel apparently forward of the beam, and it has not been determined that no risk of collision exists. What does Rule 19(e) require as a minimum?

Rule 19(e) requires reducing speed to the minimum at which she can be kept on her course, taking all way off if necessary. COLREG 1972, Rule 19.

What is the best response when an examiner asks something you genuinely do not know?

Examiners assess safe judgement, and bluffing on a safety question counts against you more than an honest gap. This is standard examiner practice and advice.
