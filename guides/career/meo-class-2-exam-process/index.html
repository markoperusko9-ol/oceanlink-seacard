MEO Class 2 exam: the process from Class IV to Second Engineer CoC

Quick answer

The MEO Class II certificate is India's management-level Second Engineer CoC (STCW Regulation III/2) for ships of 3000 kW propulsion power or more. You need a Class IV CoC, at least 12 months of approved sea service as an engineer officer on ships of 750 kW or more (with the required propelling time), a DG-approved 4-month preparatory course and the courses on the current DGS checklist. You then apply for assessment online through the DG Shipping system, pass the written examinations as currently notified by DGS and the MMD orals, and apply online for the CoC.

What MEO Class 2 is and where it sits

MEO Class II is the Indian Certificate of Competency for Second Engineer Officer on ships powered by main propulsion machinery of 3000 kW or more. The Directorate General of Shipping (DGS) issues it under STCW Regulation III/2. The competence standard is in STCW Code Section A-III/2.

The Indian ladder looks like this:

Grade STCW level Role
MEO Class IV III/1 (operational) Officer in charge of an engineering watch
MEO Class II III/2 (management) Second Engineer, 3000 kW+
MEO Class I III/2 (management) Chief Engineer, 3000 kW+

STCW Regulation III/2 covers both Second Engineer and Chief Engineer on ships of 3000 kW or more. India runs them as two separate certificates and exams. You take Class II first. Class I needs further sea service after Class II and has its own examination.

MEO Class II is an Indian (DGS) certificate. Filipino engineers take the STCW III/2 Second Engineer CoC through MARINA, which runs its own process and portal. Use this guide to understand the structure, but follow MARINA's current rules for your own application.

Eligibility: sea service and propelling time

DGS rules require not less than 12 months of approved sea-going service as a qualified engineer officer in the engine department (motor or steam, as appropriate) on ships of 750 kW or more. This service counts from after you got your Class IV (III/1) CoC.

The DGS qualifying sea service table has two columns:

  • Column A is the total qualifying sea service.
  • Column B is the minimum watchkeeping on the main propulsion machinery or UMS duties. This is your propelling time.

Maritime institutes generally quote 240 days (8 months) of propelling time within the 12 months after Class IV as the entry requirement for the preparatory course. This figure is not taken from the DGS table, so confirm it in the current DGS EAC notice before you rely on it. Don't count on someone else's spreadsheet.

Things that commonly catch people out:

  • Service on ships below 750 kW does not count towards Class II.
  • Steam and motor endorsements follow the type of plant you actually served on.
  • Periods in drydock, at anchor or laid up count towards total service but may not count as propelling time. This is the usual reason a candidate falls short in Column B.
  • Coastal (NCV) service has its own grades and tables. Don't assume it counts towards the foreign-going Class II.

Courses you must complete before applying

MEO Class II preparatory course. You must complete this DG-approved 4-month course at a DGS-approved institute before you can sit the exams. It is mapped to STCW Section A-III/2 and IMO Model Course 7.02, but always check that the course has DGS approval. To enrol, you will need:

  • Your Class IV CoC
  • Your INDoS number
  • Proof of the qualifying sea service

Engine Room Simulator course, management level. This is a separate DG-approved course. It appears on Class II checklists in use, but confirm it against the current checklist.

STCW modular courses and refreshers. These must be valid on the date of assessment. Checklists in use list:

  • PSSR
  • PSCRB (and PST refresher)
  • Advanced Fire Fighting (and FPFF refresher)
  • Medical First Aid
  • Security training as listed on the current DGS Class II checklist (usually Designated Security Duties)

The DGS checklist changes from time to time, for example to add management-level high-voltage training or IMO-mandated updates. Download the current Class II checklist from dgshipping.gov.in or your MMD before you book anything.

Every course certificate must be from a DGS-approved institute. Each one must also show up in the DGS online database against your INDoS number.

Step-by-step: from sign-off to CoC

  1. Check your sea service on paper. Add up your CDC entries and testimonials. Work out total service and propelling time separately against the DGS table.
  2. Complete the courses. Finish the 4-month preparatory course and the other courses on the current DGS checklist. Renew any modular courses that will expire before your assessment.
  3. Update your profile on the DGS e-governance seafarer system: photo, signature, INDoS, CoC and course records. DGS has moved services between portals over the years, so always start from dgshipping.gov.in and use the link it currently gives.
  4. Submit the online assessment application for MEO Class II and pay the fee online. Check the fee on the portal on the day you pay.
  5. Arrange document verification with your MMD. MMDs may require you to attend in person for original-document verification. Some MMD procedures have also required a hard copy of the online application with self-attested supporting documents. Check your MMD's current notice for booking and submission instructions.
  6. Eligibility assessment. Your sea service, courses and documents are checked, and you are declared eligible (or not). Check the current DGS/MMD instructions for how this is done.
  7. Part A. Part A is a set of written papers on engineering theory. Holders of certain degrees may be exempted, and the MMD records this during assessment. Check the current paper list in the DGS examination rules.
  8. Part B written papers, then orals. The written examinations are conducted as currently notified by DGS. Check dgshipping.gov.in for e-Pariksha / online exam arrangements. The orals are held at the MMD. You can get credit for individual papers you pass, so you don't have to resit everything at once.
  9. Apply for the CoC online. Once you have passed everything, use the CoC application in the DGS online system. Class I and II CoC applications have had to be made online since 2017 (DGS EAC Branch Circular 135 of 2017). Check the current DGS/MMD instructions for how your application is processed after you submit it.

For questions about results or retests, contact the MMD that handled your assessment.

Documents checklist and common rejections

Carry originals plus self-attested copies of:

  • The online application printout and fee payment receipt
  • MEO Class IV CoC
  • Passport and CDC (all entry pages)
  • INDoS certificate
  • A valid medical fitness certificate from a DG-approved doctor
  • Degree certificate (if claiming Part A exemption under current DGS rules)
  • Preparatory course and ERS management-level certificates
  • STCW modular courses and refreshers
  • Sea service testimonials and the company letter in the format the MMD wants

Why files get sent back:

  • Testimonial dates don't match the CDC sign-on and sign-off stamps
  • Testimonials don't show the main engine kW or vessel details
  • Propelling time is short because yard or idle days were counted
  • A refresher or medical expired between applying and assessment
  • A course certificate isn't showing in the DGS database
  • Your name is spelled differently across passport, CDC and INDoS
  • Your photo or signature can't be seen on the online application

Get copies of your testimonials signed and stamped by the Chief Engineer and Master before you sign off. Chasing an office six months later can cost you a whole exam cycle.

Worked scenario: will Ravi's sea time pass?

Ravi got his Class IV CoC and then did two contracts as 3/E:

  • Contract 1: a bulk carrier with a 9,000 kW main engine, 7 months. About three weeks of that was in drydock.
  • Contract 2: a product tanker with a 10,000 kW main engine, 5.5 months. It spent long periods at anchor awaiting cargo.

Total service is about 12.5 months on ships above 750 kW, so Column A is satisfied.

Propelling time is the problem. Ravi can count only the days the main propulsion was running while he kept a watch or did UMS duty. He gets these from the engine logbook or noon reports, and the company letter or testimonial should state them. After taking out the drydock and the long anchor stays, he may be under the propelling time the DGS table requires.

What he should do:

  • Ask the company for a letter showing propelling days for each vessel.
  • Compare it with the current DGS table before paying for the prep course.
  • If he's short, a short top-up contract is cheaper than a rejected file.

Oral exam: what the surveyor is really testing

The Class II oral is a management-level exam. Explaining how a purifier works is no longer enough. The surveyor wants to know how you would run the engine room, decide and report.

STCW A-III/2 has four functions at management level:

  • Marine engineering
  • Electrical, electronic and control engineering
  • Maintenance and repair
  • Controlling the operation of the ship and care for persons on board

Typical lines of questioning:

  • Crankcase oil mist alarm at sea. What do you do first, who do you tell, when is it safe to open the doors, and what do you check before restarting?
  • Blackout during river transit. How do you restore power, how do the preferential trips work, and what goes in the log?
  • Bunkering. How does sampling work under MARPOL, what do you do about a disputed density, and what are your Oil Record Book entries?
  • Planned maintenance. How do you postpone a job, and how do you justify and record it under the company's ISM system?
  • Survey and PSC. How do you prepare for a detention-prone item such as the OWS, the emergency generator or the quick-closing valves?

Tips from the examiner's side of the table:

  • Answer in sequence: safety → isolate → inform → act → record.
  • Say "as per company SMS" only after you have explained the principle behind it.
  • If you don't know, say so. Guessing at a figure for a safety device is how candidates fail.
  • Talk about real incidents you dealt with. Surveyors can tell when an answer comes from memorised notes.
Key points
  • MEO Class II is India's STCW III/2 Second Engineer CoC for ships of 3000 kW or more.
  • You need at least 12 months of approved service after Class IV as an engineer officer on ships of 750 kW or more, plus the required propelling time.
  • The DG-approved 4-month preparatory course and the courses on the current DGS checklist must be done before assessment.
  • Assessment, fee payment and CoC applications go through the DGS online system; check your MMD's notice for original-document verification.
  • Exams are written papers (Part A, which some degree holders may be exempted from, and Part B), held as currently notified by DGS, followed by MMD orals.
  • Most rejections come from mismatched testimonials, short propelling time or expired courses and medicals.
  • Fees, checklists, exam arrangements and portals change, so check dgshipping.gov.in and your MMD before every step.

Frequently asked questions

How much sea time do I need for MEO Class 2 after Class 4?

DGS rules require at least 12 months of approved sea-going service as a qualified engineer officer on ships of 750 kW or more, counted after you got your Class IV CoC. There is also a separate propelling-time requirement. Institutes quote 240 days (8 months), but this is not taken from the DGS table, so confirm both figures in the current DGS qualifying sea service notice.

Is the MEO Class 2 preparatory course compulsory?

Yes. Indian candidates must complete the DG-approved 4-month MEO Class II preparatory course at an approved institute before they can sit the exams. The certificate must appear in the DGS database against your INDoS number.

Can I get exemption from MEO Class 2 Part A?

Holders of certain engineering degrees may be exempted from the Part A written papers. The MMD records this during your eligibility assessment based on your qualifications. Check the current DGS rules for which degrees qualify, and take the original degree certificate with you.

How do I apply for the MEO Class 2 CoC after passing?

Apply online through the DGS e-governance system using the CoC application. Class I and II CoC applications have had to be made online since 2017 (DGS EAC Branch Circular 135 of 2017). Check the current DGS and MMD instructions for how your application is processed after you submit it.

Does MEO Class 2 let me sail as Chief Engineer?

No. MEO Class II is the Second Engineer certificate for ships of 3000 kW or more. To sail as Chief Engineer on those ships you need MEO Class I, which requires more sea service after Class II and a separate examination. Check the DGS sea service table for Class I.

Test yourself

Under which STCW regulation is the MEO Class II (Second Engineer, 3000 kW+) certificate issued?

Second and Chief Engineer on ships of 3000 kW or more are management-level grades under STCW Regulation III/2. III/1 is the watchkeeping (Class IV) level. Ref: STCW Regulation III/2.

What is the minimum main propulsion power of the ships on which your qualifying sea service for Class II must be done?

DGS requires the qualifying service as an engineer officer to be on ships of 750 kW or more. 3000 kW is the power limit the CoC itself covers, not the limit for qualifying service. Ref: DGS EAC qualifying sea service notice.

A candidate has 12.5 months on board but was rejected at assessment. Which is the most likely reason?

Propelling time (Column B of the DGS table) is checked separately from total service, and drydock or idle periods often leave candidates short. Ref: DGS EAC qualifying sea service table.

How must a candidate apply for the MEO Class II CoC after passing all exams?

Class I and II CoC applications have had to be made online since 2017. Ref: DGS EAC Branch Circular 135 of 2017.

Which of these is NOT one of the management-level functions for engineers in STCW Code Section A-III/2?

A-III/2 lists marine engineering; electrical, electronic and control engineering; maintenance and repair; and controlling the operation of the ship and care for persons on board. Navigation is a deck function. Ref: STCW Code, Section A-III/2.
