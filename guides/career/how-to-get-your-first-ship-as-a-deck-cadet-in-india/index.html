How to get your first ship as a deck cadet in India

Quick answer

Your best chance of a first ship is company sponsorship before or during pre-sea training. Without it, you have to find shipboard training yourself, and you should only do that through a valid RPSL listed on the DG Shipping website. Before any company will sign you on, you need an INDoS number, a CDC, a passport, a valid medical from a DG-approved doctor, your pre-sea completion certificate and STCW courses, and a genuine Form-I issued by a valid RPSL.

How the deck cadet route works and why the first ship matters

On the DNS route, you do one year of pre-sea training ashore. You then do a Structured Shipboard Training Programme (SSTP) on board, which covers semesters III, IV and V. The IMU and DG Shipping documents describe this onboard phase as 18 months. After that comes a post-sea course and the Second Mate (FG) examination conducted by the MMD. Other routes, such as B.Sc Nautical Science and older pre-sea schemes, have their own sea-time rules. Check the current MS Notice or training circular that applies to your course.

STCW Regulation II/1 requires approved seagoing service before you can hold an OOW certificate. There are two ways to meet it:

  • at least 12 months within an approved training programme, documented in an approved training record book, or
  • a longer period (36 months) of approved seagoing service.

Either way, none of it counts until you get a ship. That is why the first ship is the hardest step.

It matters for another reason too. Under STCW, service outside an approved training programme needs a much longer period of sea time. Cadets who do not follow a structured training programme after pre-sea therefore face a longer sea-time requirement before they can sit for Second Mate. For the exact requirement for your course, check the current DG Shipping notice or training circular on dgshipping.gov.in.

A first ship with a company that runs a proper SSTP saves you months. Look for a Shipboard Training Officer on board and a Company Training Officer ashore.

Route 1: Company sponsorship (the main route)

Many deck cadets who sail without a long wait were selected by a shipping company or ship manager before pre-sea. The company sends you to a DG-approved institute and commits in writing to give you shipboard training afterwards.

The usual sequence:

  • Meet the education requirement set by the Directorate and the institute (10+2 with PCM, plus English criteria). Sit IMU CET if your chosen course admits through it.
  • Apply to company cadet programmes directly through their official websites. Selection usually includes a written or online test, a psychometric test and an interview.
  • Pass the pre-employment medical with a DG-approved doctor. Eyesight and colour vision are where deck candidates are most often rejected. Get yourself checked before you spend money on coaching.
  • Receive a sponsorship letter, join the nominated institute and complete pre-sea.
  • The company processes your joining.

Read the sponsorship terms carefully. Check:

  • whether a bond applies
  • who pays the course fee
  • what happens if you fail a semester or the medical

Each company sets its own terms, so ask for them in writing.

Route 2: Non-sponsored pass-outs (step by step)

If you finished pre-sea without sponsorship, you can still get a ship, but you need method and patience.

  1. Get your documents in order first (see the next section). Agencies will not shortlist an incomplete file.
  2. Build a list of valid RPSLs only. On the DG Shipping website, open the list of agents holding a Recruitment and Placement Services Licence. DG Shipping Circular 55 of 2025 says this list shows each RPSL's live status as valid, invalid or temporarily blocked. Check the exact company name and licence number.
  3. Apply directly. Use the official careers page or email address of each company, or walk in to their office. Do not go through a 'consultant' who claims to have contacts.
  4. Target the companies that take cadets. Bulk carriers, general cargo and some tanker managers often run cadet intakes. Ask your institute's placement cell which companies took its recent batches.
  5. Keep your record clean. Keep your medical valid and your mobile number and email current on your DG Shipping profile. Reply to company emails the same day.
  6. Never pay for placement. Under MLC 2006 (Regulation 1.4, Standard A1.4 para 5(b)), recruitment and placement services must not charge seafarers, directly or indirectly, for recruitment or placement. The only exceptions are the cost of your national statutory medical certificate, your seafarer's book (CDC) and your passport. Visa costs are borne by the shipowner.

Worked scenario: Ravi completes DNS without sponsorship. He gets his CDC through e-Samudra and renews his medical. He then makes a list of 30 valid RPSLs that recruit cadets.

An 'agent' on social media offers him a 'guaranteed tanker cadet seat' for a placement fee. Ravi looks the company up in the RPSL list and finds it marked invalid. He refuses, reports the agent to DG Shipping and keeps applying directly.

After a written test and an interview, a valid RPSL issues his Form-I and he joins a bulk carrier. That is slower than he hoped, but it is legitimate and his sea time counts.

Documents and portals you need before sign-on

Item Where / how
INDoS number Applied for online through DG Shipping's e-governance system (e-Samudra). Your training institute often applies in bulk for its trainees.
CDC Apply through the DG Shipping/DGMA e-Samudra seafarer portal using your INDoS number. Follow the portal's current CDC application option.
Passport Must be valid for the whole contract. Many companies ask for a long remaining validity, so check their requirement.
Medical fitness certificate DG-approved doctor only. Must still be valid on your joining date.
Pre-sea completion certificate Issued by your DG-approved institute in the approved format.
STCW courses Basic safety (PST, FPFF, EFA, PSSR) and security training. Some trades, such as tankers, also need cargo courses. Check what your pre-sea course already included.
Training record book The approved Deck Cadet Record Book and SSTP workbooks, issued through your institute or company.
Vaccinations / visas Yellow fever where the trade requires it, and a US C1/D visa if the company asks. The company usually tells you.
Form-I (or Form-IA/IB) Generated by the valid RPSL. Carry a printed copy when you join.

The portals are being upgraded, and the Directorate's newer documents also use the name DGMA. Always start from the official DG Shipping website rather than a link someone sends you.

Form-I, e-Migrate and immigration: the last checkpoint

When a valid RPSL engages you, it generates a Form-I (or Form-IA/IB) in the DG Shipping system. The form records your engagement details, including the recruiting agency, the vessel and your seafarer identifiers.

DG Shipping forwards this data to the e-Migrate system used by immigration. You do not register on e-Migrate yourself.

DG Shipping states that seafarers engaged through non-registered recruiters will not be allowed to leave the country at immigration checkpoints. Circular 55 of 2025 also requires you to carry a printed Form-I/IA/IB issued exclusively by a Valid RPSL.

After you join, check the Master Checker on the DG Shipping website regularly to confirm your sea service has been recorded. Circular 55 of 2025 also advises this. If your service has not been recorded, raise it with your company immediately. Do not wait until you need the sea time for your exam.

Common reasons cadets get rejected or stuck

  • Medical: colour vision or eyesight below the deck standard, or a medical that expires before joining.
  • Paperwork: the name differs between passport, CDC and INDoS; the CDC has not been issued yet; the pre-sea certificate is not in the approved format.
  • Interview: weak basics such as rules of the road, lights and shapes, basic stability and fire-fighting, or poor spoken English.
  • Unverifiable sea time: joining through an invalid or blocked agency. That sea time may not be accepted, and you may not get through immigration at all.
  • Unrealistic choices: turning down a bulk carrier while waiting for a tanker or container seat. Your first ship is about getting your record book completed. Being choosy at this stage costs months.

Interview prep: questions companies actually ask cadets

Treat the company interview like a short oral exam. Typical questions:

  • What lights does a power-driven vessel of 50 m or more in length show underway? Two masthead lights, sidelights and a sternlight (COLREG Rule 23).
  • Who is the give-way vessel in a crossing situation? The vessel that has the other on her own starboard side (Rule 15).
  • What is GM, and what happens if it is negative? GM is the metacentric height. With negative GM the ship is unstable when upright and may lie at an angle of loll.
  • What do you do when you hear 'man overboard'? Raise the alarm, throw a lifebuoy, keep the person in sight and inform the OOW.
  • What is your duty as a lookout? Keep a proper lookout by sight and hearing and report everything, as required by Rule 5 and STCW watchkeeping principles.
  • Why do you want to go to sea? Keep it honest and short, and talk about the career.

Answer precisely. If you do not know, say so. Examiners and superintendents trust a cadet who admits a gap more than one who bluffs.

Key points
  • Company sponsorship before pre-sea is the most reliable route to a first ship.
  • Deal only with RPSLs shown as Valid on DG Shipping's live RPSL list.
  • Never pay for a placement. MLC 2006 Standard A1.4 bans recruitment fees apart from your own medical, CDC and passport costs.
  • Have INDoS, CDC, passport, a valid DG-approved medical and STCW courses ready before applying.
  • A printed Form-I/IA/IB issued by a valid RPSL is needed to clear immigration.
  • Following a structured shipboard training programme with a record book means less sea time before Second Mate.
  • After joining, check Master Checker regularly to confirm your sea service has been recorded.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a ship as a deck cadet without sponsorship?

Yes, but it usually takes longer. Apply directly to companies that are listed as valid RPSLs on the DG Shipping website, and keep your documents and medical current. Avoid anyone who asks for money to guarantee a seat.

How do I check if a manning agency is genuine?

Open the DG Shipping website and go to the list of agents holding a Recruitment and Placement Services Licence. The list shows each licence as valid, invalid or temporarily blocked. Match the exact company name, licence number and address before you share documents.

How long is shipboard training for a DNS deck cadet?

IMU and DG Shipping documents describe an 18-month Structured Shipboard Training Programme covering semesters III to V. It is followed by a post-sea course before the Second Mate (FG) exam. Other routes have different sea-time rules, so check the current notice for your course.

Do I need to register on e-Migrate myself?

No. When a valid RPSL generates your Form-I, DG Shipping forwards your engagement data to e-Migrate for immigration. Carry a printed Form-I when you leave.

How do I apply for my CDC as a fresh cadet?

Apply through the DG Shipping/DGMA e-Samudra seafarer portal using your INDoS number. Follow the portal's current CDC application option and upload the documents it asks for. Fees and document lists change, so check the portal itself rather than relying on old guides.

Test yourself

A recruiter offers you a cadet berth. What is the first check you should make?

DG Shipping tells seafarers to deal only with RPSLs shown as Valid on its live list (DGS Circular 55 of 2025).

A seafarer engaged through a non-registered recruiter reaches the airport for a foreign-flag ship. What happens at immigration?

Under DG Shipping's e-Migrate arrangement, seafarers engaged through non-registered recruiters will not be allowed through immigration.

An agent asks for a 'placement fee' to guarantee your first ship. What is the correct position?

MLC 2006 (Regulation 1.4, Standard A1.4 para 5(b)) bars charging seafarers for recruitment or placement. The only exceptions are the statutory medical certificate, the seafarer's book (CDC) and the passport, and visa costs fall on the shipowner.

Why does a structured shipboard training programme with an approved record book matter for a deck cadet?

Under STCW Reg II/1, the shorter 12-month route applies only to service within an approved training programme documented in an approved record book; otherwise 36 months is required. Check the current DG Shipping notice for Indian sea-time rules.

Which document is generated by the RPSL and must be carried in printed form when joining?

DGS Circular 55 of 2025 requires seafarers to carry a printed Form-I/IA/IB issued exclusively by a Valid RPSL.
