Endorsements: DCE (Dangerous Cargo Endorsement) in India

Quick answer

In India, the old Dangerous Cargo Endorsement is now issued as a Certificate of Proficiency (COP) in Basic or Advanced Training for oil, chemical or liquefied gas tanker cargo operations, under STCW Regulations V/1-1 and V/1-2. You complete the approved course, build the required sea time on the right tanker type and apply online through the e-Samudra portal of the Directorate General of Maritime Administration (DGMA, formerly DG Shipping). To revalidate, you need at least three months on that type of tanker in the last five years, or you must repeat the approved advanced course for that tanker type.

DCE, COP, Level 1, Level 2: what the terms mean now

Old hands still say "DCE", crewing managers say "DCE", and some CVs still show "Level 1" and "Level 2". The Directorate General of Maritime Administration (DGMA, formerly DG Shipping) has been clear for years that these are the same certificates under new names. The new names match the STCW 2010 (Manila) amendments:

Old Indian term Current name
DCE Level 1 (operational) COP in Basic Training for Oil and Chemical / Liquefied Gas Tanker Cargo Operations
DCE Level 2 (management) COP in Advanced Training for Oil Tanker / Chemical Tanker / Liquefied Gas Tanker Cargo Operations

Keep these points in mind:

  • Basic training for oil and chemical tankers is one combined course. Liquefied gas has its own separate basic course.
  • Advanced training is split by tanker type. Oil, chemical and gas are three separate certificates. An advanced oil COP does not let you take responsibility for cargo work on a chemical tanker.
  • You may still see old course names in records, such as the Tanker Familiarisation course, TASCO (the Advanced Tanker Safety course), CHEMCO, GASCO and LCHS. DGMA (formerly DG Shipping) FAQs still name the advanced tanker courses when they explain revalidation. The current approved courses are the basic and advanced tanker cargo operations courses listed by DGMA.

Who needs basic and who needs advanced

STCW Regulation V/1-1 (oil and chemical) and V/1-2 (liquefied gas) divide it this way:

  • Basic COP: officers and ratings who have specific duties and responsibilities related to the cargo or cargo equipment. Company policy often extends this to more crew, so check with your company.
  • Advanced COP: the master, chief engineer, chief mate, second engineer and anyone else with immediate responsibility for loading, discharging, care in transit, handling of cargo, tank cleaning or other cargo-related operations.

Many operators set a stricter policy than STCW. For example, a company may want:

  • every deck officer to hold the advanced COP,
  • the pumpman to hold it, or
  • all senior officers to hold both oil and chemical.

These rules come from the company SMS and charterer vetting requirements, not from STCW. Get the requirement in writing from your company before you sign.

If you are joining a foreign-flag tanker, the flag state will normally issue its own endorsement or COP based on your Indian COP. Each flag has different rules, so apply through the company in good time. The flag endorsement page covers this.

Eligibility: courses and sea time

For the basic COP

  • You need STCW basic safety training (STCW Code A-VI/1).
  • You need to complete the DGMA-approved basic course for oil and chemical, liquefied gas, or both.
  • This is how most Indian cadets and ratings get the COP before their first tanker.
  • STCW also allows a sea-service route for basic. Check with DGMA whether it is accepted. In practice Indian candidates take the approved basic course.

For the advanced COP, STCW requires all of the following for the type of tanker concerned:

  1. You meet the basic requirements for that type.
  2. While holding basic, you have one of these:
    • at least three months of approved seagoing service on that type of tanker, or
    • at least one month of approved onboard training in a supernumerary capacity. This must include at least three loading and three unloading operations and be documented in an approved training record book.
  3. You have completed the approved advanced training course for that type.

DGMA (formerly DG Shipping) FAQs ask for at least three months of tanker experience. For the chemical COP, the service must be on a chemical tanker as defined under STCW, meaning one carrying cargoes listed in IBC Code Chapter 17. Confirm with DGMA what evidence they accept.

The supernumerary route is allowed under STCW. Before you rely on it, check with DGMA or your MMD whether it is currently accepted for Indian COPs and what paperwork they want.

DGMA and the approved institute set the course entry criteria. One example is whether you can do the advanced course before finishing your sea time. Confirm these criteria with the institute before you pay, not with a batchmate.

Step by step: applying online with DGMA

Applications are made online through DGMA's e-Samudra portal (esamudra.dgshipping.gov.in, live from 7 April 2026). During the transition, some CoP services may still run on the old e-governance portal. Check the current circular on dgma.gov.in before you start. The interface changes from time to time, so follow the portal as it is when you apply. The general sequence is:

  1. Register on e-Samudra and check your profile. Register with your INDoS number and complete or migrate your seafarer profile first. Your name, date of birth, CDC, passport and existing certificates must all be correct and must match each other.
  2. Make sure the course is uploaded against your INDoS. The approved institute uploads it. If the course does not show in your profile, the application will stall.
  3. For advanced, make sure your sea service is recorded. You need CDC entries, discharge certificates and any sea service testimonial. These should show the vessel name, IMO number, tanker type and sign-on/sign-off dates.
  4. Select the tanker COP / DC endorsement application for the correct level and tanker type. Fill in the details and generate the application number.
  5. Upload scans. These typically include the signed application, photograph, signature, passport, CDC and the relevant course and sea service documents. Upload exactly what the portal asks for.
  6. Pay the fee online. Use the amount the portal shows, not a figure from an old forum post.
  7. Track the status and answer any query raised. Check the portal for how the CoP will be issued. DGMA FAQs say CoPs are sent by post.

Keep a PDF of the application, the payment receipt and the issued COP in your document folder. You will need them when you sign on and during vetting.

Common rejections and how to avoid them

Most delays come from paperwork, not eligibility.

  • Wrong tanker type. Time on an oil-only product tanker does not count towards the advanced chemical COP. Make sure the vessel type in your CDC and service documents matches the COP you are applying for.
  • Chemical time without IBC Chapter 17 cargoes. A chemical/oil tanker that carried only petroleum products during your contract may not count as chemical tanker service. Keep cargo records or a company letter listing the IBC Chapter 17 products you carried.
  • Short sea time. Count the actual days between sign-on and sign-off on that type of tanker. Rounding up to "three months" is a common trap. Days at a shipyard or in lay-up may be questioned.
  • Unverifiable service. DGMA expects Indian seafarers to join ships through recognised RPSL agencies. Service you cannot prove with proper discharge entries causes problems.
  • Profile mismatches. A spelling difference between your passport, CDC and INDoS is enough to send the application back.
  • Course not uploaded, or the wrong course. For example, a basic gas course does not give you a basic oil and chemical COP.
  • Poor scans. Blurred or cropped uploads and unsigned application forms get sent back.

Worked scenarios

Scenario 1: Third Officer, first product tanker contract

Ravi holds a basic oil and chemical COP. He serves 4 months 10 days on a product tanker carrying only clean petroleum products. He then completes the advanced oil tanker course.

  • Advanced oil: he can apply. He has completed the course and has more than three months on oil tankers.
  • Advanced chemical: he does not qualify yet, because he has no service on a chemical tanker carrying IBC Code Chapter 17 cargoes. He needs one of the following, plus the advanced chemical course:
    • three months of that service, or
    • a supernumerary route that DGMA accepts.

Scenario 2: Chief Mate returning to gas after bulk carriers

Maria holds an advanced gas COP that expires next year. She spent her last five years on bulk carriers. She does not have three months on gas carriers in the past 60 months. To revalidate, she must repeat the approved advanced gas tanker course, or follow the route currently shown on e-Samudra. If she rejoins an LPG carrier and completes three months before her COP expires, she can revalidate on sea service instead.

Scenario 3: Second Engineer planning ahead

Arjun holds advanced oil. His company plans to move him to a chemical tanker as 2/E in three months, but he has no chemical tanker time. STCW names the 2/E as a person who must hold advanced training, so he should raise this with the office now. The company may need to arrange a different route or a different post until he qualifies. Joining without the right COP is a certification deficiency. It can lead to detention at Port State Control and a vetting finding.

Revalidation: the five-year rule

Under STCW Regulation I/11 and DGMA practice, tanker COPs are revalidated at intervals of up to five years. To revalidate an advanced COP you need one of the following:

  • At least three months of service on the appropriate type of tanker within the preceding 60 months, or
  • Repeating the approved advanced training course for that tanker type. DGMA (formerly DG Shipping) FAQs set this route; the earlier course names were TASCO, CHEMCO and GASCO. Alternatively, follow the route currently shown on e-Samudra.

The sea service must be on the same type of tanker as the COP. Advanced oil, chemical and gas each need their own service on that tanker type, or their own repeated course. Apply online through e-Samudra, upload proof of service and pay online. During the transition, check the current circular on dgma.gov.in in case a service still runs on the old portal.

If you also hold IGF Code COPs, separate renewal rules apply to them. These were issued in DGS NT/ENG Circular No. 11 of 2025. Read that circular rather than assuming the tanker rules apply.

Practical tip: set a reminder six to nine months before expiry. That leaves time to book the course between contracts if your tanker time has lapsed.

Oral exam corner: what examiners ask

Tanker COPs come up in Second Mate, Chief Mate and MEO orals. Expect questions like these:

  • "What is the difference between DCE and COP?" It is the same certificate under its STCW 2010 name. Level 1 became basic and Level 2 became advanced.
  • "Who must hold advanced training?" The master, chief engineer, chief mate, second engineer and anyone with immediate responsibility for cargo operations (STCW V/1-1 and V/1-2).
  • "Can an advanced oil holder be chief mate on a chemical tanker?" No. Advanced COPs are specific to the tanker type.
  • "How do you revalidate?" Three months on that type of tanker in the last five years, or repeating the approved advanced course for that type.
  • "Where is the chemical tanker code?" The IBC Code. STCW defines a chemical tanker as a ship carrying cargoes listed in IBC Code Chapter 17.

Give the regulation number first, then the practical consequence. Examiners like candidates who link the certificate to what it allows you to do on deck.

Key points
  • In India, DCE Level 1 and Level 2 are now the COP in Basic and Advanced Training for tanker cargo operations.
  • Basic oil and chemical is one combined course; advanced is separate for oil, chemical and gas.
  • Advanced needs the basic COP, the advanced course and three months on that tanker type, or an STCW supernumerary route that DGMA accepts.
  • Advanced chemical needs chemical tanker service as defined under STCW (IBC Code Chapter 17 cargoes); confirm with DGMA what evidence it accepts.
  • Apply online on DGMA's (formerly DG Shipping) e-Samudra portal after registering with your INDoS number, and pay the fee the portal shows.
  • Revalidation needs three months on the relevant tanker type in the last 60 months, or repeating the approved advanced course for that type.
  • Most rejections come from profile mismatches, the wrong tanker type or missing sea service proof.

Frequently asked questions

Is DCE the same as the tanker Certificate of Proficiency?

Yes. DGMA (formerly DG Shipping) renamed DCE Level 1 as the COP in Basic Training and Level 2 as the COP in Advanced Training for oil, chemical or gas tanker cargo operations. Crewing agencies still use 'DCE', but the document you hold is the COP.

How much tanker sea time do I need for advanced DCE in India?

DGMA (formerly DG Shipping) requires at least three months on the relevant type of tanker while you hold the basic COP, plus the advanced course. STCW also allows one month of supernumerary onboard training instead. It must include at least three loading and three unloading operations and be documented in an approved training record book. Check with DGMA whether that route is currently accepted for Indian COPs.

Does product tanker experience count for the chemical DCE?

Only if the service was on a chemical tanker as defined under STCW, meaning one carrying IBC Code Chapter 17 cargoes. Time spent carrying only petroleum products counts as oil tanker experience. Keep cargo records or a company letter, and confirm with DGMA what evidence they accept.

My DCE expired and I have not sailed on tankers for five years. What now?

DGMA (formerly DG Shipping) FAQs say you must repeat the approved advanced training course for that tanker type. Then you apply for revalidation online, normally on e-Samudra. If you rejoin a tanker and complete three months on that type, sea service is the alternative route.

Do I need a separate flag endorsement for my DCE?

On foreign-flag tankers, usually yes. Most flag administrations issue their own endorsement or COP based on your Indian certificate. The procedure and documents differ by flag, so let your company apply well before you join.

Test yourself

What is the current name for the old Indian DCE Level 2 for oil tankers?

DGMA (formerly DG Shipping) renamed Level 2 (management) DCE as the COP in Advanced Training for the relevant tanker type, in line with STCW Regulation V/1-1.

Which of these persons must hold advanced tanker training under STCW?

STCW Regulations V/1-1 and V/1-2 name the master, chief engineer, chief mate, second engineer and anyone with immediate responsibility for cargo operations.

An officer has five months on an oil-only product tanker and holds basic oil and chemical. Which advanced COP can they apply for after the matching course?

Advanced COPs depend on the tanker type. Chemical tanker service under STCW means carrying IBC Code Chapter 17 cargoes, so oil-only service does not count (STCW V/1-1).

What does DGMA (formerly DG Shipping) accept for revalidating an advanced tanker COP?

Tanker COP revalidation needs relevant tanker service or repeating the approved advanced course for that tanker type (STCW Regulation I/11; DGMA FAQs).

Which code lists the cargoes that define chemical tanker service for the advanced chemical COP?

STCW defines a chemical tanker by the cargoes listed in IBC Code Chapter 17. The IGC Code covers gas carriers and the IMDG Code covers packaged dangerous goods.
