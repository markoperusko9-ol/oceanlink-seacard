Seafarer mental health and support helplines: who to call and how to get help on board

Quick answer

SeafarerHelp, run by ISWAN, is a free and confidential helpline that works in several languages. It is open 24 hours a day, every day of the year, for seafarers of any nationality and their families. You can reach it by phone, WhatsApp, email or live chat. Indian seafarers can also find a list of helplines in DG Shipping's 'Sea-ries No. 1' leaflet, and Filipino seafarers can call the OWWA 24/7 hotline 1348. If someone on board is at immediate risk, tell the Master at once and get radio medical advice. The helpline is for support, but the emergency is handled on board.

Why this matters and the warning signs to watch for

Long contracts, isolation from family, fatigue, poor connectivity, bullying and delays in crew change all wear people down. Seafarers have always lived with this. What has changed is that the industry now treats mental health as part of safety. A worried, sleepless watchkeeper is a risk in the same way as a fatigued one.

Watch for these signs in yourself and in your shipmates:

  • Withdrawal: skipping meals in the mess, staying in the cabin, no longer calling home.
  • Sleep changes: cannot sleep off watch, or sleeps through alarms.
  • Irritability or tearfulness that is out of character, and arguments over small things.
  • Performance: mistakes on watch, missed rounds, forgotten tasks.
  • More drinking or smoking, where the company's drug and alcohol policy allows it at all.
  • Hopeless talk: "no point", "family is better off without me", giving away belongings, or asking odd questions about the ship's side or lifesaving appliances.

That last group is a red flag. Do not wait to see if it passes. The crisis steps further down tell you what to do.

Notes that matter:

  • SeafarerHelp is free and confidential, and the team speaks a range of languages, including Hindi and Filipino. Callers can get emotional support as well as practical help, for example with unpaid wages, abandonment or a family emergency.
  • India: DG Shipping's leaflet lists SeafarerHelp along with a toll-free number (1800-102-5110) and a WhatsApp/direct-dial number (+91-9707740567), as listed in Sea-ries No. 1 (check for updates). Use the latest version from dgshipping.gov.in rather than a forwarded screenshot.
  • Philippines: Start with OWWA 1348. Check the DMW and OWWA official websites for current welfare and counselling support.
  • Welfare charities such as the Mission to Seafarers run port centres and wellbeing programmes, including WeCare. Your P&I club or company may also run its own Employee Assistance Programme. Ask your manning agent or check the SMS.

Step by step: reaching out from the ship

  1. Pick your channel. A WhatsApp message, an email or live chat uses very little data and works on a weak VSAT link. The person on the other end can call you back.
  2. Pick your time. Choose an off-watch slot when you can talk privately. Call from your cabin, not the mess.
  3. Say it simply. For example: "I'm a 3rd Officer on a bulk carrier, five months on board, not sleeping and feeling low." You do not need to give your ship's name or company to start the conversation.
  4. Ask what happens next. Helplines can listen, refer you to counselling, contact welfare organisations in port, or help with practical problems such as wages, repatriation or a death in the family.
  5. Tell someone on board you trust if you can. This might be a friend, the Chief Officer or Chief Engineer, or the Master. You do not need to give details, only that you are struggling.
  6. If it involves bullying, harassment or a contract breach, use the on-board complaint procedure as well, and the company's Designated Person Ashore (DPA) if needed. Keep dated notes.
  7. In port, visit the seafarers' centre or welfare visitors. Sometimes a SIM card, a walk ashore and a conversation face to face do more than anything else.

Confidentiality: SeafarerHelp's service is confidential. The exception on almost any helpline is an immediate risk to life. In that case the counsellor will want to get help to you, and that is what you should want too.

Worked scenario: a shipmate at risk on board

Scenario: Mid-ocean, 0300. The AB on your watch tells you the OS has been saying he "won't be going home". He is now on deck alone near the rail.

  1. Safety first. Keep the vessel safe. Call the Master and ask for someone to relieve or support you. Send a responsible person to stay with the OS, and keep yourself and others safe.
  2. Do not leave him alone. Talk calmly, listen and do not argue. It is fine to ask directly whether he is thinking of harming himself. Asking does not put the idea in someone's head.
  3. Master takes charge. Move him to a safe space and set up a continuous watch with trusted crew members on rotation. Remove anything he could use to harm himself.
  4. Get medical advice. The Master contacts the Telemedical Assistance Service or radio medical advice service through the usual channel, and follows the ship's medical guide and company procedure.
  5. Inform the company. Call the DPA or the manager's emergency number. Ask for professional counselling support by phone, and plan for disembarkation, a medical review ashore or a diversion if needed.
  6. Record the facts in the log and the company forms, keeping to what was seen, said and done.
  7. Look after the crew afterwards. Others will be shaken. Share the SeafarerHelp contacts with them and arrange a debrief.

A helpline supports the person and the crew. It does not replace the Master's emergency response on board.

Your rights: what the conventions actually require

  • MLC 2006, Regulation 4.1 and Standard A4.1 (medical care): you are entitled to medical care on board, and to care ashore in port. Flag states must make medical advice by radio or satellite available at any hour. Medical care under Regulation 4.1 is meant to protect seafarers' health and give prompt access to care. It is generally understood to cover mental as well as physical health.
  • MLC 2006, Regulation 4.4 (shore welfare): port states must ensure that shore-based welfare facilities, where they exist, are easy to get to, and should promote their development.
  • MLC 2006, Regulation 4.3 (health and safety): the company's occupational safety and health policy must cover risks on board. The 2016 amendments (in force January 2019) specifically added harassment and bullying to the health and safety provisions under Regulation 4.3.
  • MLC 2006, Regulation 3.1: this covers accommodation and recreational facilities, including reasonable access to communication facilities. Exact arrangements depend on the flag and company.
  • MLC 2006, Regulation 5.1.5 (on-board complaints): you can complain without being victimised, and you can also go directly to the Master or to outside authorities.
  • STCW amendments, resolution MSC.560(108) (adopted May 2024): these add training on preventing and responding to harassment, bullying and sexual assault to basic training (PSSR). The amendments enter into force on 1 January 2026 and also update the leadership and teamwork competences for officers. Check with your administration how this applies to your training and revalidation.
  • ISM Code: your company's SMS and its DPA give you a route to shore management that goes around the ship's hierarchy.

How each convention is put into practice depends on the flag state and the company SMS. Your SEA and the CBA, if there is one, may also add welfare or counselling provisions.

Medical fitness and the fear of 'losing your ticket'

Many seafarers stay silent because they worry that seeking help will end their career. Be honest with yourself about the risk on both sides.

  • Medical fitness is assessed under STCW Regulation I/9, using national standards that generally follow the ILO/IMO Guidelines on the medical examinations of seafarers. A recognised doctor makes the decision case by case. Having a mental health condition does not automatically mean you are unfit.
  • Many common conditions, once treated and stable, are compatible with sea service. Some may lead to restrictions or a review period. The outcome depends on your administration's standards and the approved doctor.
  • Hiding a condition or medication at a medical can create far bigger problems later, for your safety and your certificate alike.
  • Calling a confidential helpline is not the same as declaring something at a medical. Get support early. Problems that are dealt with early tend to cost far less than a crisis at sea.

If you are in doubt about fitness standards, ask your administration's approved doctor. Indian seafarers should use a DG-approved doctor, and Filipino seafarers should check MARINA/DOH-accredited medical facilities.

Oral exam style: what examiners may ask

Mental health and wellbeing can come up in orals under leadership, human element and MLC topics. Expect questions like these:

  • "A crew member says he wants to end his life. Your actions, Master?" Ensure his immediate safety, do not leave him alone, take away means of self-harm, get radio medical advice, inform the DPA and company, plan disembarkation, record everything and support the rest of the crew.
  • "Which MLC regulation covers shore welfare?" Regulation 4.4. For medical care, Regulation 4.1. For complaints, Regulation 5.1.5.
  • "How do you as Chief Officer reduce stress on board?" Manage rest hours properly, plan work fairly, have zero tolerance for bullying, hold regular informal check-ins, encourage shore leave and support connectivity.
  • "What does the 2024 STCW amendment add?" Training on preventing and responding to harassment, bullying and sexual assault in basic training (PSSR), plus updated leadership and teamwork competences for officers (MSC.560(108), in force 1 January 2026).

Answer as a manager. Examiners want to see that you understand the human element as a safety issue, not just as a welfare nicety.

For families at home

SeafarerHelp is open to families too. A spouse or parent worried about a seafarer, or dealing with a crisis at home, can contact it directly.

  • Indian families: DG Shipping's leaflet lists grievance and wellbeing contacts. Your manning agent (RPSL) should also be contacted, and complaints about an RPSL can be raised with DG Shipping. See how to use the Indian seafarer grievance portal.
  • Filipino families: OWWA 1348 and the DMW can help with welfare and repatriation issues.

Give your family your company's emergency contact and your manning agent's details before you sign on.

Key points
  • SeafarerHelp (ISWAN) is free, confidential, available in several languages and open 24/7: +44 20 7323 2737, WhatsApp +44 7909 470732, help@seafarerhelp.org.
  • Indian seafarers: download DG Shipping's 'Sea-ries No. 1' leaflet for the current list of helplines and grievance contacts.
  • Filipino seafarers: the OWWA 24/7 hotline is 1348; check the DMW and OWWA official websites for current welfare and counselling support.
  • If someone on board is at risk of harming himself: never leave him alone, involve the Master, get radio medical advice and inform the DPA.
  • Medical care under MLC Regulation 4.1 is generally understood to cover mental as well as physical health; complaints procedures (Reg 5.1.5) protect you from victimisation.
  • STCW amendments under MSC.560(108), in force 1 January 2026, add harassment, bullying and sexual assault training to basic training and update officer leadership competences.
  • Asking for help early rarely ends a career; hiding problems often makes them worse.

Frequently asked questions

Is SeafarerHelp really free and confidential?

Yes. ISWAN's SeafarerHelp is a free, confidential helpline in several languages, open 24 hours a day, 365 days a year, to seafarers of any nationality and their families. You can ask for a callback so the call costs you nothing. As with any helpline, an immediate risk to life may mean the counsellor tries to get emergency help to you.

Which mental health helpline should Indian seafarers use?

DG Shipping's 'Sea-ries No. 1' leaflet on dgshipping.gov.in lists 24/7 seafarer helplines, including SeafarerHelp, a toll-free number and a WhatsApp/direct-dial number, along with grievance contact points. Check the DG Shipping website for the latest version rather than relying on forwarded messages.

Where can Filipino seafarers get mental health support?

OWWA runs a 24/7 hotline on 1348. Check the DMW and OWWA official websites for current welfare and counselling support. SeafarerHelp also has Filipino-speaking staff.

Will calling a helpline affect my medical certificate or CoC?

A confidential helpline call is not a medical declaration. Fitness is decided by an approved doctor under STCW Regulation I/9 and your administration's standards, which assess each case individually. Many treated conditions are compatible with sea service, but never hide a condition or medication at a medical.

What should I do if I'm being bullied or harassed on board?

Keep dated notes. Use the on-board complaint procedure under MLC Regulation 5.1.5, and go to the DPA if the problem involves your chain of command. SeafarerHelp can advise you confidentially and help you take it further. MLC 2006 protects you from victimisation for making a complaint.

Test yourself

Who operates the SeafarerHelp 24/7 helpline?

SeafarerHelp is operated by ISWAN. The IMO lists it on its Supporting Seafarers page but does not run it.

Which MLC 2006 regulation deals with access to shore-based welfare facilities?

Regulation 4.4 covers access to shore-based welfare facilities. Regulation 2.5 is repatriation, Regulation 5.1.5 is on-board complaints and Regulation 3.2 is food and catering.

A crew member tells you he intends to harm himself. What is the correct first priority?

Immediate risk is a shipboard emergency. Ensure continuous supervision, involve the Master and get radio medical advice, which MLC Standard A4.1 requires to be available at any hour.

What did the STCW Code amendments in resolution MSC.560(108) add?

MSC.560(108), adopted in May 2024, amended the STCW Code to include preventing and responding to harassment, bullying and sexual assault in basic training (PSSR) and in officer leadership competences; in force 1 January 2026. It introduced no psychological testing, contract limits or on-board counsellors.

Under STCW, who decides whether a seafarer with a mental health condition is fit for sea service?

STCW Regulation I/9 requires medical fitness to be certified by a recognised practitioner against the administration's standards. Helplines and Masters do not issue fitness decisions.
