Philippine seafarers: MARINA and DMW basics

Quick answer

MARINA is the Philippine maritime administration for STCW. It issues your SIRB, Certificates of Proficiency (COP) and Certificates of Competency (COC), mostly through appointments booked on its online system, MISMO. The Department of Migrant Workers (DMW) took over from POEA. It licenses manning agencies, processes your overseas employment contract and handles your registration and departure documents. You need both agencies before you can join a foreign-flagged ship.

Who does what: MARINA vs DMW

Most first-timers mix up the two agencies. Put simply, MARINA certifies that you are competent. DMW deals with your overseas employment.

Area MARINA DMW
Main role Maritime administration for STCW in the Philippines Overseas employment and protection of migrant workers (it absorbed POEA)
What you get from them SIRB (seaman's book), SID, COP, COC, endorsements Registration, contract processing, departure documentation
Online system MISMO (MARINA Integrated Seafarers Management Online) DMW online services and the digital OFW Pass
Who you deal with You directly, or an accredited company liaison officer Mostly through your licensed manning agency

Under the Magna Carta of Filipino Seafarers (RA 12021), the work is split by flag. DMW keeps the registry of Filipino seafarers on ocean-going ships of foreign registry. MARINA keeps it for seafarers on domestic ships and on ocean-going ships of Philippine registry.

In practice, if you are heading for a foreign-flagged ship you need valid MARINA documents first. Your manning agency then processes your contract through DMW.

MARINA step by step: MISMO account and appointments

MARINA processes almost everything through the MISMO online appointment system. MARINA Memorandum Circular SC-2021-03 states that the MISMO online appointment system is free of charge. If someone charges you just to get a slot, treat it as a warning sign.

Setting up and using MISMO:

  1. Go to the MARINA online appointment site and create a MISMO account with an email address you will keep for years. Your certificates will be tied to it.
  2. Wait for MARINA to verify and activate the account.
  3. Sign in and complete your personal profile. Your name, date of birth and other details must match your PSA birth certificate and passport exactly.
  4. Choose the transaction you need, for example new SIRB, renewal, a COP, or COC issuance or revalidation.
  5. Upload the required documents and pick an available date and MARINA office.
  6. Pay through the channels MARINA accepts. Fees change, so check the amount on the MARINA site or in MISMO itself.
  7. On the day, bring the originals and a printed copy of your confirmed appointment.

Who can handle it for you: Under MARINA's STCW circulars, you can be represented only by your company's accredited liaison officer, and that officer needs a request letter from the shipping or manning company. A freelance 'fixer' is not an accredited liaison officer.

Your core MARINA documents

  • SIRB (Seafarer's Identification and Record Book): your Philippine seaman's book, and the first MARINA document most new entrants get. Expect to show proof of identity and your Basic Training certificate. Always check the current checklist on MISMO for your exact transaction, because new, renewal and replacement applications have different lists.
  • SID (Seafarer's Identity Document): MARINA also issues this. It is a separate document from the SIRB.
  • COP (Certificate of Proficiency): for STCW proficiencies such as Basic Training, PSCRB, Advanced Fire Fighting, Security Awareness and Designated Security Duties, and ratings forming part of a watch. COPs for Security Awareness and Designated Security Duties are filed online through MISMO.
  • COC (Certificate of Competency): for officers, from OIC-NW and OIC-EW up to Master and Chief Engineer. MARINA's documentary requirements for issuance usually include:
    • your theoretical examination and practical assessment results
    • the company sea service certificate or SIRB
    • a valid medical certificate that meets MARINA's requirements
    • the required training certificates. ECDIS, MLC and updating courses appear on the list depending on the level.

COC applicants must be at least 18 at the time of application. Revalidation follows STCW's five-year cycle, but the evidence MARINA asks for (sea service or updating training) is set out in MARINA circulars. Read the circular for your level instead of relying on what a batchmate did last year.

DMW step by step: agency, contract and departure

1. Use only a DMW-licensed manning agency. Look the agency up in DMW's own directory of licensed recruitment agencies. Search the exact legal name and check that the licence status is valid. A scanned licence sent on Messenger proves nothing.

2. Registration. DMW keeps a seafarer registration that agencies use when they process your contract. Your agency will tell you what you need to complete yourself and what they handle.

3. Medical. Your pre-employment medical examination (PEME) must be done at a DOH-accredited medical facility for seafarers that is acceptable to the employer. Your agency normally refers you. Do not pay a 'medical package' to an unlicensed recruiter.

4. Contract. Overseas seafarers on ocean-going ships sign the standard terms commonly called the POEA-SEC, together with any applicable CBA. Before you sign, check that: - the rank, ship, flag and duration match what you were promised - the contract references the standard terms - you get a copy

5. Departure documentation. The old Overseas Employment Certificate (OEC) is being replaced by DMW's digital OFW Pass, and DMW has announced further integration with travel systems. The requirements for sea-based workers can differ from those for land-based workers. Ask your agency what applies to your departure, and check DMW's official announcements for the current requirement before you leave for the airport.

6. Welfare. Find out how your OWWA membership and benefits work before you sail. Save DMW's contact details in case you need assistance while abroad.

The Magna Carta of Filipino Seafarers (RA 12021) in practice

RA 12021 was signed on 23 September 2024. DMW and MARINA signed its Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) on 8 January 2025. MARINA's STCW Office posts both on its website.

What matters day to day:

  • It sets out the rights of seafarers and cadets in one law, including a full chapter on education and training of seafarers and cadets.
  • It assigns the seafarer registries by flag, as described above.
  • It requires manning agencies to keep an up-to-date registry of the seafarers they recruit or place, open to inspection by DMW and MARINA.

Read the IRR on MARINA's or DMW's website. Social-media summaries often get the details wrong, especially on cadetship and dispute provisions.

Common rejections and how to avoid them

Problem Why it happens Fix
Name or birth date mismatch MISMO profile, PSA certificate, passport and training certificates do not agree Correct your civil registry records first, then update all your documents
Training certificate not accepted Course taken at a centre or programme MARINA does not recognise, or certificate not yet recorded Train only with MARINA-accredited institutions and confirm the course is in their accredited list
Sea service not credited Missing company sea service certificate, or SIRB entries not signed and stamped Get signed sea service certificates at every sign-off and check the SIRB entries before leaving the ship
Medical not accepted Certificate expired, or not in the format MARINA requires Check the validity date against your appointment and contract dates
Appointment wasted Missing originals, or wrong transaction booked Print the checklist for that exact transaction and bring originals plus photocopies
Agency cannot process your contract Agency licence suspended or expired Check the DMW directory before you hand over documents

Fixer red flags: - 'Guaranteed' COC results - Payment for MISMO slots - Requests for your MISMO password - Placement fees collected from you by someone who is not a licensed agency

Worked scenario: maritime graduate to first foreign ship

Marco has finished his maritime course and onboard training. He wants to join a foreign-flagged bulk carrier as a rating. If his programme still requires it, he may join as a cadet for shipboard training instead.

  1. Civil documents: He gets a PSA birth certificate and a passport, and makes sure his name is spelt the same on both.
  2. Training: He completes Basic Training and any other COPs the target company asks for, at MARINA-accredited centres.
  3. MISMO: He opens his account, books his SIRB appointment and his COP applications, and keeps every receipt and confirmation.
  4. Agency: He checks the manning agency in the DMW licensed-agency directory before submitting his CV.
  5. Medical: Once he has a line-up, he takes the PEME at the DOH-accredited facility his agency refers him to.
  6. Contract: He reads the POEA-SEC-based contract and any CBA, and keeps a copy.
  7. Departure: He follows the agency's instructions on DMW registration and the OFW Pass, and checks DMW's official announcements himself.
  8. On board: He has his SIRB signed at sign-on and sign-off and collects a company sea service certificate. These will matter for his future COC.

Typical interview questions at an agency or on board:

  • Which body issues your COC?
    • MARINA.
  • Who licenses your agency?
    • DMW.
  • What proves your sea time?
    • Signed SIRB entries plus company sea service certificates.
  • Where do you book a MARINA appointment?
    • MISMO.

Answering these clearly shows you understand your own paperwork, and that counts with crewing officers.

Key points
  • MARINA certifies you (SIRB, SID, COP, COC). DMW handles overseas employment and licenses manning agencies.
  • MARINA transactions run through MISMO, and MARINA MC SC-2021-03 states the appointment system is free.
  • Only an accredited company liaison officer may process MARINA certificates on your behalf. Fixers are not accredited.
  • Always check an agency's licence in DMW's own directory, never from a scanned copy.
  • RA 12021 (Magna Carta of Filipino Seafarers) and its IRR, signed January 2025, now govern seafarers' rights and the registries.
  • The OEC is being replaced by DMW's digital OFW Pass. Check DMW's official announcements for the current requirement for sea-based workers.
  • Signed SIRB entries and company sea service certificates are your evidence for future COCs.

Frequently asked questions

Is the MARINA MISMO appointment free?

Yes. MARINA Memorandum Circular SC-2021-03 states that the MISMO online appointment system is free of charge. You still pay the official fees for the certificate itself through MARINA's accepted channels. Anyone selling appointment slots is not acting for MARINA.

Is POEA still the agency for seafarers?

No. POEA's functions were absorbed into the Department of Migrant Workers (DMW). The standard contract is still commonly called the POEA-SEC, but agency licensing and contract processing now sit with DMW.

Do I still need an OEC as a seafarer?

The OEC is being replaced by DMW's digital OFW Pass, and DMW has announced further integration with travel systems. Arrangements for sea-based workers can differ from those for land-based workers. Ask your licensed manning agency and check DMW's official announcements for the current requirement before you travel.

Can my agency or a friend process my MARINA documents for me?

Under MARINA's STCW circulars, only your company's accredited liaison officer may represent you, and they need a request letter from the shipping or manning company. Friends and freelance fixers are not accredited representatives.

How do I check if a manning agency is legitimate?

Search the agency's exact legal name in DMW's licensed recruitment agencies directory on the DMW website and confirm the licence is valid. Do not rely on licence images, SEC registration or social-media pages. Report suspicious recruiters to DMW.

Test yourself

Which agency issues a Filipino officer's STCW Certificate of Competency?

MARINA is the Philippine maritime administration that issues and revalidates COCs under its STCW circulars, for example MC SC-2021-03.

Who may represent a seafarer in processing MARINA STCW certificates?

MARINA's STCW circulars allow representation only by the company's accredited liaison officer, backed by a request letter from the shipping or manning company.

Under RA 12021, who keeps the registry of Filipino seafarers on ocean-going ships of foreign registry?

RA 12021 gives DMW the registry for foreign-registered ocean-going ships. MARINA keeps it for domestic ships and Philippine-registered ocean-going ships.

What is the reliable way to confirm a manning agency is licensed?

Only DMW's own directory shows whether a licence is current. SEC registration and images do not prove DMW licensing.

What does a MISMO appointment booking cost?

MARINA's STCW circulars (e.g., MC SC-2021-03) state that the MISMO online appointment system is free of charge. Certificate fees are paid separately through official channels.
