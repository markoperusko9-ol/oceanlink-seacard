Indian seafarer grievance portal: how to complain through e-Navik and other routes

Quick answer

DG Shipping (DGS) handles seafarer complaints through its 24x7 Grievance Redressal Module on the e-Navik platform. The module also takes complaints through toll-free helplines, WhatsApp and email. Choose the right category, describe the problem clearly, state the outcome you want and upload evidence. Then answer any queries quickly. You can also raise the complaint on board under MLC procedures and contact the port state at the same time.

Which portal to use now: the e-Navik grievance module

For years, Indian seafarers raised complaints through the Grievance Redressal Mechanism inside the DG Shipping e-Governance system. You logged in, picked a grievance type, uploaded documents and tracked the status.

In 2026, DG Shipping launched a 24x7 Grievance Redressal Module on the e-Navik platform. The module also takes complaints through toll-free helplines, WhatsApp and email. The older e-Governance grievance route may or may not still accept complaints, so check the Crew Division page.

You can raise issues such as these (examples, not an official list):

  • Recruitment and placement problems, and complaints against RPSL agencies
  • Fraudulent certificates and documents
  • Wage disputes
  • Abandonment, arrest or detention
  • Death, missing seafarer and serious injury cases
  • Certification matters
  • Welfare concerns and other service issues

For other features of e-Navik, check the Crew Division page. Always open the e-Navik grievance page from the Crew Division section of dgma.gov.in, never from a forwarded link. Fake agents do copy government pages.

Step-by-step: registering a grievance on e-Navik

  1. Open the portal from dgma.gov.in. Go to the Crew Division section and find the e-Navik 24x7 Grievance Redressal page. Download the user manual from the same page.
  2. Log in or file as the user manual explains. The manual on the official e-Navik page covers how to log in and how family members can file. If you are abroad, check before you sail that you can reach the portal from overseas.
  3. Register your grievance and choose the category and sub-category carefully. A wrong category sends your file to the wrong desk.
  4. Describe the problem. Include:
    • ship name, IMO number and flag
    • your RPSL or manning agent
    • dates
    • port or position where it happened
    • who you have already contacted
  5. State the outcome you want. For example: "payment of balance wages", "repatriation from port X" or "correction of CDC entry".
  6. Upload evidence (see the next section) and submit. Write down the grievance/ticket ID.
  7. Track it and reply to queries. Use the tracking option on the portal. If the Directorate asks for more information, reply promptly so your case is not held up.

You can also complain through toll-free helplines, WhatsApp and email. Contact details change, so take the current numbers and email address from the official Crew Division / e-Navik page, not from forwarded messages.

Documents and evidence that make a grievance actionable

A complaint without evidence usually comes back with a query, so send everything at once:

Grievance type Attach
Unpaid or short wages SEA (contract) with its wage terms, wage slips or portage bill copy, bank statements showing what arrived, emails with the company
RPSL / agent fraud Receipts or transfer records, chats and emails, the agent's licence number as quoted, any offer letter
Abandonment / repatriation Crew list, last port, date wages stopped, provisions or fuel situation, the master's or company's messages, P&I or insurer details if known
CDC / certificate issue Copy of the document, application number, screenshots of the error
Harassment / safety A written, dated account, names of witnesses, any onboard complaint you made and the reply

Tips:

  • Name files clearly, for example SEA_signed_2026.pdf.
  • Upload readable scans, not blurred photos.
  • Keep originals safe. Never post your passport, CDC or INDoS number on social media.

Why grievances get delayed or closed

These are the problems seen most often:

  • Wrong category. For example, a wage claim filed under certification.
  • Vague text. "Company not paying, please help" with no ship name, dates or amounts.
  • No expected outcome. The officer cannot tell what would count as resolved.
  • Unanswered queries. Reply to queries promptly so your case is not held up.
  • Duplicates across email, X, WhatsApp and the portal. Several threads slow things down. File once on e-Navik and quote the ticket ID everywhere else.
  • Wrong forum. Some matters belong elsewhere first:
    • a dispute under a collective bargaining agreement (CBA) goes to your union
    • a criminal fraud case goes to the police

The Directorate deals directly with RPSLs and Indian documentation. For foreign-flag ships it works through flag states, port authorities and Indian missions abroad, which can take time. - Unregistered agent. If you paid someone who was never an RPSL, the Directorate can flag them. Recovering your money is a police matter. Check the List of Valid / Invalid / Temporarily Suspended RPSL on the Crew Division page before paying anyone.

Complaining on board first: your MLC 2006 rights

Under MLC 2006, Regulation 5.1.5, every ship must have an on-board complaint procedure. Standard A5.1.5 sets out what it must include:

  • You can try to resolve the complaint at the lowest level, usually your head of department, then the master.
  • You always have the right to complain directly to the master and to appropriate external authorities.
  • You may be accompanied or represented during the procedure.
  • You must not be victimised for complaining.
  • You should receive a copy of the on-board procedures. These include contact details for the flag state's competent authority and your country of residence.

In port, Regulation 5.2.2 requires port states to have onshore complaint-handling procedures, so you can go to the PSC inspector.

For abandonment, Standard A2.5.2 requires shipowners to hold financial security. It covers repatriation, essential needs and up to four months' outstanding wages. The insurer's contact details should be posted on board.

Using the on-board route does not stop you filing on e-Navik at the same time. When things are urgent, do both.

Worked scenario: abandoned in a foreign port

Situation: you are 3/O on a foreign-flag bulk carrier, recruited through an Indian RPSL. Wages have not been paid for two months, provisions are low and the owner has stopped answering.

  1. Record the facts. Note the date wages stopped, provisions and fresh water remaining, fuel and generator status, and the crew list. Collect emails and photographs.
  2. Raise it with the master in writing under the on-board complaint procedure. Keep a copy.
  3. Register on e-Navik under the abandonment/detention category, as a group or individually, with all evidence attached. Family ashore can use the helplines, or follow the user manual on the official e-Navik page for how they can file.
  4. Write to the RPSL. Indian RPSLs have obligations to seafarers they place, and the Directorate can act against them.
  5. Find the financial security certificate posted on board and contact the insurer named on it.
  6. Contact the port state authority and the Indian mission for that country. Also contact your union or the ITF if you are a member or the ship is covered.
  7. Do not sign any release, settlement or "voluntary resignation" for less than you are owed without advice.

Quote the same e-Navik ticket ID with every party you contact.

Women seafarers and harassment complaints

DGS Order 01 of 2023 requires gender sensitisation training in pre-sea and post-sea competency courses.

In practice:

  • Follow your company's harassment procedure under its SMS. Put the complaint in writing and date it.
  • File on e-Navik as well, choosing the harassment/welfare category that fits. Do this especially if the company route stalls or the complaint is against senior officers.
  • Write down the names of witnesses and keep all messages.

Company procedures differ, so ask for your company's anti-harassment policy at sign-on.

Oral exam corner: grievance and complaint questions

MLC complaint handling can come up at MMD (Indian Mercantile Marine Department) orals. Expect questions like these:

  • "A rating complains to you about unpaid overtime. What do you do?"
    • Listen and record the complaint.
    • Try to resolve it at the lowest level, then escalate to the master.
    • Remind him he may complain directly to the master or ashore.
    • Make sure there is no victimisation.
    • Log it according to the SMS.
  • "Where on board is the complaint procedure?" In the documents given to each seafarer and in the SMS. It includes contact details for the flag state's competent authority.
  • "What does Regulation 5.2.2 give a seafarer?" The right to complain to the port state while in a foreign port.
  • "What must be posted on board regarding abandonment?" Evidence of financial security under Standard A2.5.2.
  • "How does an Indian seafarer complain against an RPSL?" Through DG Shipping's grievance module on the e-Navik platform, choosing the category that covers RPSL/recruitment complaints.
Key points
  • DG Shipping runs a 24x7 Grievance Redressal Module on the e-Navik platform, which also takes complaints through toll-free helplines, WhatsApp and email.
  • Follow the user manual on the official e-Navik page for how to log in and how family members can file.

Frequently asked questions

What is the official DG Shipping grievance portal for seafarers?

It is DG Shipping's 24x7 Grievance Redressal Module on the e-Navik platform. The module also takes complaints through toll-free helplines, WhatsApp and email. Open it from the Crew Division section of dgma.gov.in. Issues raised there include RPSL complaints, wages, abandonment, detention, documentation, certification and welfare.

Can my family file a complaint while I am on board?

The user manual on the official e-Navik page explains how family members can file. The helplines, WhatsApp and email channels listed on the Crew Division page are other options. Before you join, give your family copies of your SEA, the ship's details and your RPSL's name, so they have what they need.

How do I complain against a fake or unlicensed manning agent?

File on e-Navik under the RPSL/recruitment category with your payment proof and chats. Report the cheating to the police too, because recovering money is a criminal matter. Check the Crew Division's list of valid, invalid and suspended RPSLs before dealing with any agency.

How long does DG Shipping take to resolve a grievance?

Resolution time depends on the category and on whether foreign parties are involved. Check the Crew Division page for any published timelines. Track your case on the portal and answer queries promptly so it is not held up.

Do I have to use the ship's complaint procedure before going to DG Shipping?

No. MLC 2006 encourages resolving complaints at the lowest level, but it also protects your right to go directly to the master and to outside authorities. In urgent cases such as abandonment, use the on-board, e-Navik and port state routes at the same time.

Test yourself

Which platform hosts DG Shipping's current 24x7 grievance redressal module for seafarers?

DG Shipping runs a 24x7 Grievance Redressal Module on the e-Navik platform (DG Shipping Crew Division, e-Navik page). e-Migrate deals with emigration clearance, not complaints.

Under MLC 2006, what right does a seafarer have regarding on-board complaints?

Standard A5.1.5 keeps the seafarer's right to go directly to the master and to appropriate external authorities, with protection from victimisation.

Where should you open the e-Navik grievance page from?

Fake agents copy government pages, so open e-Navik only from the official Crew Division page on dgma.gov.in (DG Shipping Crew Division, e-Navik page).

Before paying a manning agency, how do you best check its status?

The Directorate publishes the current RPSL status list on its Crew Division page. Fake agents often print copied or cancelled licence numbers.

Wages have been unpaid for two months and the owner is not answering. What is the best course of action?

MLC Standard A2.5.2 financial security and Regulation 5.2.2 port state complaints work alongside an e-Navik abandonment grievance. Signing a release for less than you are owed can weaken your claim.
