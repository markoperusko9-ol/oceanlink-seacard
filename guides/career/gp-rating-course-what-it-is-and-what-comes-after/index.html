GP Rating course: what it is and what comes after

Quick answer

The General Purpose (GP) Rating course is a six-month pre-sea course in India. It is approved by the Directorate General of Maritime Administration (DGMA, formerly DG Shipping/DGS) and trains school leavers to join ships as trainee ratings in the deck or engine department. After the course and the basic STCW courses, you get your CDC, sign on as a trainee and build sea time. That leads to watchkeeping rating status (STCW II/4 or III/4), then able seafarer (II/5 or III/5), and later the officer route if you want it.

What the GP Rating course actually is

GP stands for General Purpose. Most pre-sea courses prepare you for a single department. The GP Rating course gives you a grounding in both deck and engine-room work. You can then be placed as a trainee on deck (trainee OS) or in the engine room (trainee wiper). Each company uses its own job titles.

It is a six-month pre-sea course. This is set out in DGS Training Circular 3 of 2004, which is now included in TEAP.

The course is the 'special training' route that STCW allows for ratings. In simple terms:

  • STCW Regulation II/4 covers ratings forming part of a navigational watch.
  • STCW Regulation III/4 covers ratings forming part of a watch in a manned engine room, or designated for duties in a periodically unmanned engine room.
  • Under both regulations, a candidate who completes approved special training needs less seagoing service than one who trains only on board. The Convention sets a minimum of two months for the special training route and six months for the onboard-only route.

The Directorate General of Maritime Administration (DGMA, formerly DGS) sets the course content, duration and approval conditions through its training circulars. Older circulars were issued under the DGS name. Only institutes on the approved list can run the course. A GP Rating certificate from an unapproved institute is worthless at the Mercantile Marine Department (MMD) counter.

Who can join: eligibility in outline

DGMA (DGS) sets three kinds of entry requirement:

  • Academic: a pass in Class 10 from a recognised board. You need minimum percentages in aggregate and in English, with Science and Mathematics as subjects. There is an alternative route for ITI pass-outs, with its own minimum percentage.
  • Age: a minimum and a maximum age, measured at the date the course starts.
  • Medical: fitness to DGMA standards, including eyesight and colour vision, certified by a DG-approved doctor.

The exact percentages and age limits have been revised over the years. Before you pay anything, read one of these:

  • the current DGMA training circular, or
  • the admission notice of an approved institute that quotes it.

Don't rely on a figure from a forum post, a coaching centre or an old prospectus.

Colour vision is the requirement that ends careers before they start. Get a proper eyesight and colour-vision test early, before you commit money. A candidate who fails the colour-vision standard cannot hold a deck watchkeeping certificate. Engine-side standards are set separately, so ask the DG-approved doctor exactly which standard you meet.

Step by step: from application to first ship

  1. Verify the institute. Check that it appears on the DGMA (DGS) list of approved Maritime Training Institutes for the GP Rating course. Also check its approval status, because approvals can be suspended.
  2. Get a medical from a DG-approved doctor. DGMA publishes the list of approved doctors. A certificate from any other doctor will not be accepted.
  3. Get your INDoS number. Every approved course is recorded against your INDoS (Indian National Database of Seafarers) number. The institute usually helps you apply for it at admission.
  4. Complete the course and the assessments. Attendance and internal assessments matter. A shortfall can hold back your course completion certificate.

  5. Complete the basic STCW courses. You need five:

    • the four basic safety training modules under STCW A-VI/1: Personal Survival Techniques (PST), Fire Prevention and Fire Fighting (FPFF), Elementary First Aid (EFA), and Personal Safety and Social Responsibilities (PSSR)
    • Security Training for Seafarers with Designated Security Duties (STSDSD, A-VI/6)

    You need all five for your CDC application. Some institutes build them into the GP Rating package and others make you book them separately, so confirm this before you join. 6. Apply for your CDC. You need the Continuous Discharge Certificate before you can sign on. Apply through the DGMA (DGS) e-governance system once your course data is uploaded against your INDoS. 'Open' CDCs, issued on basic courses alone, have been discontinued. As a pre-sea trainee, you become eligible for a CDC by completing the approved GP Rating course and the basic STCW courses. Check the current CDC rules in the Crew FAQ on the DGMA portal. 7. Get your passport and find a placement. Approach a shipping company or a licensed RPS agency (RPSL). Verify the agency's licence on the DGMA website before you hand over documents. 8. Sign on as a trainee rating. Keep a record of your onboard training as your company and DGMA require. Make sure every period of sea service is entered in your CDC and signed off.

Typical document set at sign-on:

  • passport
  • CDC
  • INDoS-linked course certificates
  • GP Rating completion certificate
  • STCW basic course certificates
  • valid medical fitness certificate
  • any flag endorsement or visa the company arranges

What comes after: the rating career ladder

Stage STCW reference What you need, in principle
Trainee OS / trainee wiper — GP Rating course, CDC, basic STCW courses, medical
Watchkeeping rating (deck) II/4 Approved special training plus the required onboard service, with the onboard training documented
Watchkeeping rating (engine) III/4 As above, engine side
Able Seafarer Deck (AB) II/5 Already qualified under II/4, plus further deck seagoing service (STCW sets 18 months, or 12 months with approved training)
Able Seafarer Engine (Motorman/Oiler) III/5 Already qualified under III/4, plus further engine seagoing service (STCW sets 12 months, or 6 months with approved training)
Bosun / Fitter / Pumpman — Company promotion based on experience and appraisals. There is no separate STCW rank certificate, but tanker roles such as pumpman also need STCW V/1-1 tanker training and endorsement

The STCW figures are the international minimum. The administration that issues your certificate (DGMA for Indian seafarers) decides how sea time is counted, which onboard records it accepts, and whether it adds anything to the minimum.

Choose deck or engine early. Sea time in one department generally doesn't count towards the other department's able seafarer certificate.

Moving from rating to officer

Plenty of masters and chief engineers started as ratings. India provides routes for experienced ratings to qualify as officers in charge of a watch. The conditions change through DGMA (DGS) rules and circulars. They cover:

  • the qualifying sea service
  • any bridging or preparatory courses
  • the academic qualifications needed

Alongside the existing NWKO (NCV) route, DGMA has recently announced a newer competency-course route for deck ratings leading to the Second Mate (FG) CoC. Read the current circulars on the DGMA portal for the start dates, eligibility and sea-service conditions before you plan around any route.

Practical advice:

  • Keep every document clean. That means CDC entries, sea service testimonials, appraisal reports and course certificates. The examiner and the MMD will check them against INDoS.
  • Study while you sail. Rule of the Road, chartwork, stability and basic engineering don't change much. A rating who knows the COLREGs cold gets noticed.
  • Ask the officers. Most will happily walk an interested AB or motorman through a passage plan or an engine-room round.

Common rejections and how to avoid them

  • Unapproved institute or lapsed approval. The certificate will not be accepted for the CDC. Check the DGMA (DGS) approved list first.
  • Medical from a non-approved doctor, or an expired medical. Your sign-on or CDC application will be refused.
  • INDoS data mismatch. Your name, date of birth or photo differs between your passport, Class 10 certificate and INDoS. Correct this before you apply for anything else.
  • Missing basic STCW courses. One of the five (PST, FPFF, EFA, PSSR, STSDSD) was not in the package. This blocks your CDC application, or the company finds out at document check.
  • Agents offering a CDC without the course. Open CDCs on basic courses alone have been discontinued. A new entrant needs an approved route such as the GP Rating course.
  • Unsigned or incomplete onboard training records. Without them, your sea time can't be credited towards a watchkeeping certificate. Get entries signed before the officer who supervised you signs off.
  • Fake agents. Anyone who promises a guaranteed ship for a 'placement fee' is a red flag. Under MLC 2006, recruitment and placement fees are prohibited. The only charges national law may allow are for your own statutory documents, such as a medical certificate, CDC or passport. Check the agency's RPSL licence on the DGMA website.

Oral exam style: what an examiner or master will ask a new rating

Q: What does the GP Rating course allow you to do? It is approved special training. Together with the required onboard service, it lets me qualify as a rating forming part of a navigational or engineering watch under STCW II/4 or III/4.

Q: On lookout at night, what do you report? Every light, shape, sound or object, its bearing, and any change in it. I report it to the officer of the watch straight away, even if I think he has already seen it.

Q: You are on the wheel and the officer orders 'Port twenty'. What do you do? I repeat the order, apply 20° of port helm, and report 'Twenty port' when the helm indicator shows it.

Q: What do you do if you find an oil leak on your engine-room round? I report it to the engineer of the watch immediately. I don't attempt a repair beyond my authority or without the permit-to-work process.

Q: What is your duty at the general alarm? I go to my muster station as shown on the muster list, wearing the correct clothing and lifejacket, and report to the person in charge.

A note for Filipino seafarers

The Philippines has no course called 'GP Rating', but the STCW standards are the same.

  • MARINA, the Philippine Maritime Industry Authority, certificates ratings under Regulations II/4, III/4, II/5 and III/5, through MARINA-accredited training and assessment.
  • You also need a Seafarer's Identification and Record Book (SIRB).
  • Overseas deployment goes through DMW-licensed manning agencies.

Check MARINA's current circulars for the exact training and sea-service rules for each certificate.

Key points
  • The GP Rating course is a six-month DGMA (formerly DGS)-approved pre-sea course that prepares you to join as a trainee rating in the deck or engine department.
  • It counts as 'special training' under STCW II/4 and III/4, which shortens the minimum onboard service needed for watchkeeping rating status.
  • Check the institute, the medical doctor and any recruitment agency against current DGMA (DGS) lists before paying anything.
  • Before signing on you need an INDoS number, a CDC, the five basic STCW courses (PST, FPFF, EFA, PSSR, STSDSD) and a valid DG-approved medical.
  • The usual ladder is trainee, then watchkeeping rating (II/4 or III/4), then able seafarer (II/5 or III/5), then senior rating or the officer route.
  • Eligibility percentages, age limits and officer routes change through DGMA circulars, so read the current notice.
  • Get your onboard training records signed as you go. Unsigned records are a common reason sea time is not credited.

Frequently asked questions

Is the GP Rating course for deck or engine?

Both. It covers basic deck and engine-room work, and you can be placed as a trainee in either department. Over time you specialise, because able seafarer certificates are department-specific.

Can a GP Rating become an officer?

Yes. India provides routes for experienced ratings to qualify as officers, including the NWKO (NCV) route and a newer competency-course route for deck ratings towards Second Mate (FG). DGMA (DGS) sets the qualifying sea service, preparatory courses and academic conditions, and these change from time to time. Check the current DGMA circulars before planning.

Do I need an INDoS number before joining the GP Rating course?

Every approved course is recorded against your INDoS number, so you need one at or before admission. Most approved institutes help you apply. Make sure your name and date of birth match your passport and school certificate exactly.

What is the eligibility for the GP Rating course?

DGMA (DGS) sets minimum Class 10 marks (aggregate and English, with Science and Maths as subjects), an alternative ITI route, a minimum and maximum age, and medical standards including eyesight. The exact figures are revised from time to time. Check the current DGMA circular or an approved institute's admission notice.

Can I get a job without paying an agent after the GP Rating course?

Yes. MLC 2006 prohibits charging seafarers recruitment or placement fees, and legitimate companies and licensed RPS agencies don't demand them. The only costs national law may let you bear are for your own statutory documents, such as a medical certificate, CDC or passport. Verify any agency's RPSL licence on the DGMA website before you share documents.

Test yourself

Under STCW, which regulation covers a rating forming part of a navigational watch?

STCW Regulation II/4 covers navigational watch ratings. II/1 is for officers in charge of a navigational watch, II/5 is Able Seafarer Deck, and III/4 is the engine-side rating.

What must you hold before you can be certificated as Able Seafarer Engine (III/5)?

STCW Regulation III/5 requires the candidate to be qualified to serve as an engine watch rating under III/4, in addition to the required seagoing service.

Which medical certificate is accepted for an Indian GP Rating candidate?

DGMA (DGS) accepts only medical fitness certificates issued by its approved doctors, in line with STCW Regulation I/9 and Section A-I/9.

An agent offers a guaranteed ship after your GP Rating course in exchange for a placement fee. What is the correct view?

MLC 2006, Regulation 1.4 and Standard A1.4, prohibits recruitment and placement fees. National law may only allow charges for personal statutory documents such as a medical certificate or passport.

Why does the GP Rating course shorten the onboard service needed for a II/4 or III/4 certificate?

STCW II/4 and III/4 allow shorter seagoing service (not less than two months) for candidates who complete approved special training, compared with six months for onboard-only training.
