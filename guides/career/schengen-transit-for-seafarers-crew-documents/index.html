Schengen transit for seafarers: crew documents, visas and border checks

Quick answer

Indian and Filipino seafarers usually need a Schengen short-stay (type C) visa to fly into Europe, join a ship, sign off or transit between airports. You apply at the consulate of the country where you join the vessel. Your file is built around your seaman's book and a signed invitation or guarantee from the shipowner or local agent. A visa issued at the border and visa-free shore leave are narrow exceptions controlled by each member state, so don't plan around them.

Which crew document you actually need

The Schengen area currently has 29 countries. They include Bulgaria and Romania, plus non-EU Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland. Cyprus and Ireland are EU members but are not Schengen, and they run their own visa rules. Indian and Philippine passport holders need a visa for the Schengen area. Match your movement to the document:

Your movement Document normally required
Fly in to join a ship in a Schengen port Schengen short-stay (type C) visa
Sign off in a Schengen port and fly home Type C visa (the agent arranges this before you sign off)
Change planes at a Schengen airport without leaving the international transit area Airport transit visa (type A), only if that country requires it for your nationality
Change airports, overnight at a hotel, or clear immigration Type C visa
Go ashore while the ship is in port Shore pass under national rules, using your seafarer identity document and the crew list

Two instruments drive all of this. The EU Visa Code (Regulation (EC) No 810/2009, amended by Regulation (EU) 2019/1155) covers visas. The Schengen Borders Code (Regulation (EU) 2016/399) covers border checks, including crew lists and shore leave.

Where and when to apply

Which country. You apply to the member state that is the main destination of the trip. For a seafarer, that normally means the country of the port where you join. The German mission in India, for example, asks for an invitation from the company or agency in the member state where you will board. For pure transit, the rules are:

  • Through one member state: apply to that state.
  • Through several: apply to the state whose external border you cross first to start the transit.

When. You can lodge a Schengen application no more than six months before travel. Seafarers have a special nine-month window for work-related travel, introduced by the 2019 amendment. The general advice is to apply at least 15 calendar days before travel. Several missions in India recommend four weeks or more, and longer in peak season.

How. Most consulates in India and the Philippines take applications through an appointed visa centre. Always start from the embassy's own website and its seafarer checklist, not from a third-party site. Fees, appointment systems and processing times change, so check the official mission page on the day you apply.

Document checklist for a seafarer Schengen visa

Each mission publishes its own seafarer checklist. Germany and Spain both publish one for India, and Greece has a specific procedure. The core is broadly the same:

  • Completed, signed Schengen application form
  • Passport with enough validity and blank pages. Check the mission's exact rule.
  • Recent biometric photographs to the mission's specification
  • Seaman's book: the CDC for Indian seafarers, the SIRB for Filipinos. Missions usually want the issue date and validity.
  • Employment contract or appointment letter showing the duration
  • Covering letter from the employer or manning agent giving your name, rank and the vessel
  • Invitation or guarantee letter from the shipowner or local maritime agent in the joining country. Missions typically want it signed and stamped, with:
    • your name, date and place of birth
    • passport and seaman's book numbers
    • vessel name and flag
    • port and date of joining
    • confirmation of who pays for your stay and repatriation
  • Travel itinerary or flight reservation. Some missions advise booking flights only after the visa is granted.
  • Travel medical insurance where the mission asks for it. Some missions accept cover that seafarers already have through their employment, but many checklists still list it, so follow the mission's checklist.

Some missions, for example Greece, require the agent's declaration to be sent directly to the consulate in a prescribed form. Check the mission's own seafarer page, and if this applies, make sure your agent sends it before your appointment.

Worked scenario: joining a ship in a Schengen port

Situation: a 3rd Officer from Mumbai is joining a product tanker in Rotterdam, flying via a German hub.

  1. Confirm the joining port and dates with the manning agent. The Netherlands is the main destination, so the application goes to the Dutch mission, not the German one.
  2. Get the Dutch port agent's invitation or guarantee letter early. Check the spelling of your name and your passport and CDC numbers against the originals. A single wrong digit can get the file returned.
  3. Collect the employment documents: the contract, the manning agent's covering letter, and copies of your CDC and passport.
  4. Book the appointment through the channel shown on the Dutch mission's website. If the joining date is months away, use the nine-month window.
  5. Check the routing. A type C visa covers the German connection, because a uniform visa is valid across the Schengen area unless the sticker says otherwise. Check the territorial validity box on the sticker. Germany's 2026 airport-transit waiver for Indians does not help here. It only covers onward flights to a non-Schengen country, so your type C visa covers the Frankfurt connection.
  6. At the first Schengen airport, show:
    • your passport and visa
    • your CDC
    • the agent's letter
    • the ship's details

Keep paper copies in your hand luggage, not in the checked bag. 7. Use the visa only for the stated purpose and period. Arriving well ahead of the joining date without a reason invites questions.

Signing off and transiting: watch the airport transit visa

The EU has a common list of nationalities that always need an airport transit visa (ATV) to change planes inside a Schengen airport. Neither India nor the Philippines is on that list. However, individual member states can add nationalities nationally. Some member states have required an ATV from Indian nationals under national rules, but this is changing fast. France lifted it for Indian ordinary-passport holders from 10 April 2026, and Germany from 3 June 2026. Both waivers apply only while you stay in the international transit area and fly on to a non-Schengen country. Always check the transit country's official mission page for your nationality before accepting a routing.

Practical rules:

  • An ATV only lets you stay airside in the international transit area. If you have to:
    • change airports
    • collect bags and re-check them
    • stay overnight in a hotel

then you need a type C visa, not an ATV. - Before you accept a sign-off ticket routed through Europe, check the transit country's official visa page for your nationality. Some missions exempt travellers who already hold certain valid visas or residence permits, and the details vary by state. - If you sign off in a Schengen port, the agent must arrange your visa or border clearance before the relief date. Don't assume the border police will sort it out on the day. - Philippine seafarers must also have their deployment and contract processed through the DMW system, as usual. A Schengen visa does not replace any national exit requirement.

Visas at the border and shore leave: the narrow exceptions

Visa issued at the border (Visa Code, Article 36 and Annex IX). A seafarer who needs a visa can, exceptionally, be given a transit visa at the external border. This applies when they are crossing to embark on, re-embark on or disembark from a ship they work on, and they meet the general conditions for border visas. Those conditions include not having been able to apply in advance for unforeseeable and imperative reasons. The port and airport authorities must exchange the seafarer-in-transit form in Annex IX before the visa is issued. Treat this as an emergency tool the agent requests from the authorities, not a normal routing.

Shore leave (Schengen Borders Code, Annex VII, point 3). Member states may let crew go ashore in the port area, or neighbouring municipalities, without a visa. The conditions are:

  • you hold a seafarer identity document under ILO Convention No 108 or No 185
  • you are on the crew list checked by the border guards

The master or agent hands the crew list to the border authority on arrival at the latest. Whether passes are granted, and how, depends on the member state and the port. Ask the agent before you plan to go ashore.

Common refusals, EES and how to avoid problems

Typical reasons files are refused or returned:

  • Invitation letter details don't match the passport or seaman's book
  • Applying to the wrong member state, such as the transit country instead of the joining country
  • Seaman's book expired or close to expiry, or the passport doesn't meet the validity rule
  • No clear link between the manning agent, the shipowner and the vessel
  • Unexplained earlier overstays or refusals
  • A purpose stated on the form that differs from the letters, such as tourism instead of joining a vessel

Tips from people who have seen it go wrong:

  • Ask for a multiple-entry visa if you regularly join and leave ships in Europe. The 2019 rules created a cascade towards longer multiple-entry visas for applicants with a clean record, though each consulate decides. Indian applicants also benefit from an India-specific multiple-entry cascade adopted by the EU in 2024. See the current Common Information Sheet for India for the conditions.
  • Keep copies of every earlier Schengen visa. A lawful travel history helps.
  • Entry/Exit System (EES): launched on 12 October 2025 and fully operational at all Schengen external borders since 10 April 2026. Passport stamping has ended for non-EU short-stay travellers. Expect fingerprints and a facial image to be taken at your first entry, and allow extra time at the border.
  • ETIAS is for visa-exempt nationalities. If you need a visa, ETIAS does not replace it.
  • Never pay an unofficial 'visa fixer'. Use only the mission's website and its appointed centre.

Questions you may be asked at the consulate or border

Have short, consistent answers ready:

  • Which vessel, which port, which date? These must match the agent's letter exactly.
  • Who is your employer and who is the local agent? Know both names.
  • Where will you stay before joining? Often the agent arranges a hotel, so carry the booking.
  • How will you leave the Schengen area? Sailing out on the vessel is a valid answer. Say so.
  • Why are you arriving on this date? Explain any gap between arrival and joining.

An officer judges you on consistent documents and calm, specific answers. If something changes, such as a delayed vessel or a new port, get an updated agent letter before you fly.

Key points
  • Indian and Filipino seafarers usually need a Schengen type C visa to join, sign off or change airports in Europe.
  • Apply to the member state where you join the ship; for transit only, follow the Visa Code transit rule.
  • Seafarers may lodge applications up to nine months ahead, versus six months for other applicants.
  • The core file is a seaman's book, the employment contract and a signed invitation or guarantee from the shipowner or local agent.
  • Airport transit visa rules for Indians and Filipinos are set nationally and change often (Germany and France lifted theirs for Indians in 2026), so check before accepting a routing.
  • Visas issued at the border and visa-free shore leave are discretionary exceptions, not a plan.
  • Since 10 April 2026, EES biometric registration has replaced passport stamping at Schengen external borders.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a Schengen visa just to join a ship in Europe?

Yes, if your nationality needs a Schengen visa, as Indian and Philippine nationals do. You need a short-stay visa to fly in and travel to the port. Apply to the member state where you will join the vessel, with an invitation or guarantee letter from the shipowner or local agent.

How early can a seafarer apply for a Schengen visa?

Since the 2019 Visa Code amendment, seafarers travelling for work can lodge applications up to nine months before travel. Other applicants are limited to six months. Missions advise applying at least 15 days before travel and often recommend several weeks.

Do I need an airport transit visa when changing flights in Europe?

India and the Philippines are not on the EU-wide ATV list. A few member states have added nationalities nationally, though Germany and France lifted their ATV requirement for Indian ordinary-passport holders in 2026. Check the transit country's official mission website. If you must leave the international transit area, change airports or stay overnight, you need a type C visa.

Can I get a visa on arrival at a Schengen port or airport?

Only exceptionally. The Visa Code allows a transit visa to be issued at the border to a seafarer joining or leaving a ship. You must meet strict conditions, including not having been able to apply in advance, and the authorities must exchange the Annex IX seafarer form. Your agent has to arrange it in advance with the authorities.

Can I go ashore in a European port without a visa?

Member states may allow shore leave in the port area or neighbouring municipalities to crew who hold an ILO 108 or 185 seafarer identity document and appear on the crew list. It is discretionary and varies by state and port, so check with the agent before going ashore.

Test yourself

An Indian AB will fly via Frankfurt to join a ship in Antwerp. Where should he apply for his Schengen visa?

Under the Visa Code, the competent member state is the main destination. For a seafarer that is normally the joining port (Regulation (EC) No 810/2009, Article 5).

What is the maximum period before travel that a seafarer can lodge a Schengen visa application for work?

The 2019 amendment allows seafarers performing their duties to apply up to nine months ahead. The general limit is six months (Regulation (EU) 2019/1155 amending Article 9).

A Filipino officer's sign-off routing needs an overnight hotel stay and a change of airport in Paris. What does he need?

An airport transit visa only covers staying airside in the international transit area. Leaving it requires a uniform short-stay visa (Visa Code, Articles 2 and 3).

Which statement about shore leave in a Schengen port is correct?

The Borders Code lets member states authorise seafarers to go ashore in the port area or adjacent municipalities, subject to conditions (Regulation (EU) 2016/399, Annex VII, point 3).

Under Article 36 of the Visa Code, a seafarer may be issued a transit visa at the border when:

Article 36 limits border visas to seafarers embarking or disembarking who meet the Article 35 conditions, after the Annex IX form has been exchanged (Regulation (EC) No 810/2009, Article 36 and Annex IX).
