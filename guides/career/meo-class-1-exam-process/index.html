MEO Class 1 Exam: The Full Process from Class II to Chief Engineer CoC

Quick answer

To sit MEO Class 1 you need a valid MEO Class II CoC, enough qualifying sea service under the DG Shipping notice, the DGS-approved MEO Class I preparatory (Engineering Management) course and the Diesel Engine Combustion Gas Simulator (DECGS) course for MEO Class I. You apply online through the DG Shipping e-Governance system, the Mercantile Marine Department (MMD) checks your documents, and you pass the written Engineering Management exam and the oral before applying online for the CoC. Fees, exam dates, checklists and portal details change often, so confirm them on the Directorate's official website before you book anything.

What MEO Class 1 is and who runs the exam

The MEO Class I Certificate of Competency is India's management-level certificate for Chief Engineer Officer on ships of 3,000 kW propulsion power or more. It is issued under STCW Regulation III/2. The exam is held at the Mercantile Marine Departments (MMDs) under the Directorate General of Shipping (DGS), Mumbai.

Check the authority's current name and portal. Some approved institutes now refer to the Directorate as 'DGMA'. Older circulars and notices say DGS. Confirm the current name, portal address and procedure on the Directorate's official website before you apply.

At Class I the examiners are not checking whether you can overhaul a purifier. They want to know whether you can run an engine department. That means statutory compliance, surveys and dry-docking, port State control, budgets and spares, managing people, and making decisions when things go wrong. DGS has published model question papers on its website; look in the Examination & Certification section. Read them before you join the course, not after.

Motor, steam or combined: DGS sets separate sea-service rules for motor, steam and combined (motor + steam) certificates. Make sure you are reading the right column of the table for the certificate you want.

Eligibility: sea service is where most candidates go wrong

STCW international minimum (Regulation III/2): for chief engineer on ships of 3,000 kW or more, STCW requires at least 36 months of approved seagoing service. STCW allows this to be reduced to at least 24 months if at least 12 months were served as second engineer. This is the international floor. It is not the Indian figure.

DGS requirement: Under the DGS EAC notice on qualifying sea service, the 36 months are reduced to 30 months (or pro rata) if 12 months were served as Second Engineer on ships of 3,000 kW propulsion power or more. Separately, check two columns in the DGS table:

  • Column A: total approved sea service, counted after you obtain your MEO Class II CoC
  • Column B: minimum propelling (watchkeeping) time on main propulsion machinery

The figures differ for motor, steam and combined certificates. Read the exact numbers in the current DGS notice. Don't rely on what a batchmate told you in the institute canteen.

Points that catch people out:

  • Time served as 2/E on a ship under 3,000 kW does not count towards the reduction
  • Time in port, at anchor or in dry dock may count as sea service but not as propelling time
  • Sea time served before your Class II CoC was issued does not count towards Column A
  • Sea time on foreign-flag ships must be backed by a proper sea-service testimonial and CDC entries; see the article on sea time verification

Mandatory courses before you apply

Before the MMD will assess you, you normally need:

  1. MEO Class I preparatory course (Engineering Management, EM) at a DGS-approved institute. Institutes advertise it as roughly two months long, but confirm the current duration and assessment with the institute and DGS. The preparatory course assessment is governed by DGS EAC Circular 138 of 2017.
  2. Diesel Engine Combustion Gas Simulator (DECGS) course for MEO Class I. This is a separate DGS-approved simulator course. Check the current approved course list.
  3. Valid basic and advanced STCW modular courses listed on the current DGS checklist for MEO Class I. Check which apply to you, including whether you need management-level High Voltage, which DGS lists as an approved course.

After the course, the institute must upload your batch and certificate details to the DGS online system. If the institute's upload is missing or wrong, your application stalls. Before you apply for assessment, check that every course certificate appears in your e-Governance profile.

Step-by-step: from sea service to CoC in hand

  1. Finish your qualifying sea service. Get sea-service testimonials from the company or RPSL and make sure your CDC sign-on and sign-off entries match them.
  2. Complete the EM course and the Diesel Engine Combustion Gas Simulator (DECGS) course for MEO Class I at DGS-approved institutes. Renew any modular courses that are about to expire.
  3. Update your DGS e-Governance profile with your INDoS number, CDC, passport, Class II CoC, medical fitness, sea service and course certificates. Names and dates of birth must match exactly across every document.
  4. Apply online for assessment/examination on the DGS e-Governance portal. Follow the current procedure on the portal for choosing the MMD and paying fees. Fees change, so check the portal on the day.
  5. MMD document verification. Bring originals and self-attested copies. The MMD confirms your eligibility.
  6. Book and sit the written exam (Engineering Management). DGS publishes the schedule on its website.
  7. Book and sit the oral exam at the MMD.
  8. Apply online for issue of the CoC. DGS EAC Circular 135 of 2017 sets out the online CoC application procedure for MEO Class I and Class II. Track the status on the portal.
  9. Update your flag endorsements. Most foreign flags need a new endorsement for the new CoC before you can sail as Chief Engineer under their flag. Check your flag administration's requirements.

Documents checklist and common rejections

Typical documents (always check the current DGS checklist):

  • MEO Class II CoC (original)
  • CDC and passport, both valid, with all sea-service entries
  • Sea-service testimonials on company or RPSL letterhead, showing ship names, IMO numbers, kW, rank and dates
  • Propelling-time statement, if the checklist asks for one
  • EM course and DECGS course certificates, plus modular course certificates
  • Valid medical fitness certificate from a DGS-approved doctor
  • INDoS number and photos as specified
  • Online fee payment receipt

Common reasons for rejection or delay:

Problem Fix
Sea-service dates differ between CDC and testimonial Get a corrected testimonial before you apply
Engine kW not stated, or under 3,000 kW for 2/E time Ask the company for a letter stating main engine power
Course certificate not visible on DGS system Chase the institute to upload it
Name spelt differently on passport, CDC and INDoS Correct it at source first, or it will keep coming back
Expired medical or modular certificate Renew before you book
Not enough propelling time Sail more. There is no workaround

Worked scenario: does this engineer qualify?

Case: Ravi got his MEO Class II (motor) CoC in January. Since then he has done two contracts:

  • Contract 1: Second Engineer on a 9,000 kW bulk carrier, about 6 months, with roughly 4 months of propelling time
  • Contract 2: Second Engineer on a 2,500 kW coaster, about 7 months

How an examiner checking eligibility thinks it through:

  1. Only service after the Class II CoC counts, so both contracts are in.
  2. Only the 9,000 kW contract counts as 2/E service on ships of 3,000 kW or more (about 6 months). The DGS notice applies the reduction pro rata, so he gets only a partial reduction, not the full one. Check the exact figures in the current DGS notice.
  3. Propelling time is checked separately against Column B. Port and dry-dock time do not count towards it.

Advice to Ravi: before booking the EM course, list every contract in a table (ship, IMO number, kW, rank, sign-on, sign-off, propelling days) and compare it with the current DGS table. Many candidates finish the course and only then find they are short.

Oral exam: what the examiner is really testing

Class I orals are management conversations. Typical opening questions:

  • 'You join as Chief Engineer and find the OWS overboard valve unsealed and the Oil Record Book doesn't add up. What do you do?' Secure and seal it, investigate, report to the Master and the company under the SMS, record honestly, and consider MARPOL Annex I and PSC exposure.
  • 'Your ship is detained by PSC for a defective emergency generator. Walk me through it.' Rectification, class surveyor and flag involvement, reporting to the company, root cause, ISM non-conformity and corrective action.
  • 'Plan a dry-dock.' Specification, survey status, class requirements, budget, spares, safety during the dock period, sea trials.
  • 'Crankcase oil mist alarm at full sea speed.' Immediate actions, why you don't open crankcase doors early, and when it is safe to investigate.
  • 'Your Second and Third Engineers won't speak to each other.' Leadership, MLC welfare, fatigue and hours of rest, documentation.

Tips:

  • Answer as the person in charge: decide, report, record.
  • Quote the correct instrument (SOLAS chapter, MARPOL annex, ISM element), then explain it in your own words.
  • Say 'I would check the company SMS and flag requirements' where practice genuinely varies, but don't hide behind it.
  • If you don't know, say so and reason it through. Bluffing fails faster than honest uncertainty.
  • Don't argue with the examiner. If you fail, read the remarks and fix that specific weakness before you rebook.

A note for non-Indian candidates and holders of foreign CoCs

'MEO Class 1' is the Indian name. The international equivalent is the STCW III/2 Chief Engineer CoC, and each administration runs its own exam system. Filipino engineers go through MARINA, and other flags have their own processes. Do not assume an Indian EM course, or exam passes from another country, will be credited elsewhere. Before you spend money on courses, ask the administration that will issue the CoC, in writing.

Key points
  • MEO Class I is the STCW III/2 Chief Engineer CoC for ships of 3,000 kW or more, examined at MMDs under DG Shipping.
  • Sea service counts only after your Class II CoC; check both total service (Column A) and propelling time (Column B) against the current DGS table.
  • STCW's international minimum is 36 months (24 with 12 months as 2/E); the DGS notice reduces 36 months to 30, or pro rata, for 2/E time on 3,000 kW+ ships.
  • You need the DGS-approved Engineering Management course, the DECGS simulator course for MEO Class I and valid modular certificates before applying.
  • Apply for the exam, book exams and later apply for CoC issue online through the DGS e-Governance system.
  • Most rejections come from CDC and testimonial mismatches, missing kW details, or course data the institute never uploaded.
  • Fees, dates, checklists and portal details change; always confirm on the Directorate's official website.

Frequently asked questions

How much sea time do I need for MEO Class 1 after Class 2?

The STCW international minimum is 36 months of approved service, reducible to 24 months with 12 months as second engineer. Under the DGS notice, the 36 months are reduced to 30 months (or pro rata) if 12 months were served as Second Engineer on ships of 3,000 kW propulsion power or more. You must also meet the DGS table's Column A (service after the MEO Class II CoC) and Column B (propelling time). The figures differ for motor, steam and combined certificates, so read the current DGS notice.

Can I do the MEO Class 1 course before completing my sea time?

Institutes set their own entry rules in line with DGS guidelines, and many expect you to have completed, or nearly completed, your qualifying service. Either way, the MMD will not assess you until your sea service meets the requirement. Check your eligibility first so your course certificate doesn't sit unused.

Does Second Engineer time on a small ship count for the reduced sea time?

Not towards the reduction. Under the DGS notice, the reduction applies to time served as Second Engineer on ships of 3,000 kW propulsion power or more, on a pro rata basis. Service on smaller ships may still count towards total sea service, depending on the DGS table.

Where do I apply for the MEO Class 1 exam?

Apply online through the DG Shipping e-Governance system on the Directorate's official website. Follow the current procedure there for choosing an MMD, paying fees and uploading documents. The MMD then verifies your originals and you book the written and oral exams.

What is asked in the MEO Class 1 oral?

Mostly management scenarios: PSC detentions, ISM non-conformities, MARPOL compliance, dry-dock planning, machinery emergencies and crew issues. DGS has published model question papers on its website. Examiners expect you to decide, report and record like a Chief Engineer.
