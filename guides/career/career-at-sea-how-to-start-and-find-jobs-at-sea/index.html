Career at sea: how to start and find jobs at sea

Quick answer

A career at sea starts in one of four departments: deck, engine, electro-technical (ETO) or catering. You can join as a cadet on an officer training programme, or as a rating (OS, wiper, messman) and work up. Every seafarer needs to be at least 16 (MLC 2006), hold a valid seafarer medical certificate, and complete STCW Basic Training and Security Awareness before joining. Find your first ship through a licensed manning agency or directly through a shipping company. Under MLC 2006 they may not charge you for recruitment or placement.

Four departments, four ways in

Every merchant ship is run by the same four departments. The one you choose decides your training, your certificates and your career path.

DepartmentWhat you doEntry ranksTop of the ladder
DeckNavigation, cargo, mooring, safety and maintenance on deckDeck cadet, Ordinary Seaman (OS)Master
EngineMain engine, generators, pumps, boilers, fuel and machinery maintenanceEngine cadet, wiperChief Engineer
Electro-technicalElectrical systems, automation, control and high-voltage equipmentElectro-technical cadet or ratingElectro-Technical Officer (ETO)
CateringFood, provisions, galley and accommodationMessman, stewardChief Cook

Officers hold a Certificate of Competency (CoC) under STCW. Ratings hold certificates of proficiency, for example as a rating forming part of a navigational or engineering watch, and later as Able Seafarer Deck or Engine.

The minimum you need before your first ship

These requirements apply worldwide. Your flag state and your national administration add their own documents on top.

  • Age. MLC 2006 sets 16 as the minimum age for work on board. For an officer CoC as Officer in Charge of a Navigational Watch (STCW Regulation II/1), you must be at least 18.
  • Medical certificate. You need a valid seafarer medical certificate under MLC Regulation 1.2 and STCW Regulation I/9. It is normally valid for a maximum of two years, or one year if you are under 18.
  • STCW Basic Training (Regulation VI/1). This has four parts: Personal Survival Techniques, Fire Prevention and Fire Fighting, Elementary First Aid, and Personal Safety and Social Responsibilities. See STCW Basic Safety Training.
  • Security Awareness (Regulation VI/6). This applies to everyone employed on board. See Ship Security Awareness.
  • Seafarer identity documents. This means a passport and your national seaman's book. In India that is the CDC (plus an INDoS number), and in the Philippines the SIRB.

Cadets on officer programmes also do approved seagoing service. For a deck OOW CoC under STCW Regulation II/1, that is at least 12 months as part of an approved training programme recorded in a training record book. Without such a programme, it is 36 months.

Officer route vs ratings route

Officer route (cadet). You join a maritime academy or a company-sponsored cadet programme. You complete shore training and sea time as a cadet, then sit your CoC exams. This is the faster way to 3rd Officer or 4th Engineer. The hard part is finding a cadet berth, so a sponsored cadetship is worth chasing.

Ratings route. You join as OS, wiper or messman after a short pre-sea course. Then you build sea time and move up to AB, oiler or motorman, or cook. Many ratings later study for a CoC. See how to move from ratings to officer.

Whichever route you take, your sea time is your currency. Keep every sign-on and sign-off record and testimonial from day one. You will need them for every exam and upgrade.

How to find jobs at sea (and your first ship)

  1. Get your documents complete first. Agencies shortlist candidates whose papers are ready to join. Expired Basic Training or a missing medical certificate gets you skipped. See how crewing agencies shortlist candidates.
  2. Apply to licensed manning agencies and shipping companies directly. In India, check the agency's RPSL licence. In the Philippines, check its DMW licence.
  3. Send a seafarer CV, not an office CV. Put your rank, sea time by vessel type, certificates with expiry dates and availability at the top. See writing a seafarer CV.
  4. Target vessel types where you have an edge. Bulk carriers and general cargo ships often take more freshers. Tankers and LNG carriers usually want tanker training and experience. See switching vessel type.
  5. Ask the right questions before you sign. Ask about wages, contract length, the flag and the CBA. See questions to ask a crewing agency and what to check in your SEA.
Rule that protects you

MLC 2006, Standard A1.4, paragraph 5(b) says no fees or other charges for recruitment or placement may be borne by the seafarer, directly or indirectly. The exceptions are the cost of your national medical certificate, seaman's book, and passport or similar travel documents. Visa costs are paid by the shipowner. Anyone asking for a "placement fee", "processing fee" or "deposit" to get you on a ship is a warning sign. See fake shipping agents.

What life at sea actually looks like

  • Contracts. Contract length depends on rank, company and flag. Officers often sail 3 to 6 months and ratings longer. Under MLC 2006, the maximum service period before you are entitled to repatriation must be less than 12 months.
  • Watches. Most watchkeepers work a fixed rota, such as 4 hours on and 8 off. MLC and STCW set minimum rest hours: at least 10 hours in any 24 and 77 hours in any 7 days.
  • Pay. Wages depend on rank, vessel type and whether the ship is under an ITF or other collective agreement. Tankers, gas carriers and offshore usually pay more than dry cargo.
  • Time ashore. Long leave between contracts is one of the main attractions of the job.

FAQ

Can I get a job on a ship with no experience?

Yes. Entry-level ratings (OS, wiper, messman) and cadets are hired without sea experience, as long as their documents are complete: medical certificate, STCW Basic Training, Security Awareness and a seaman's book. Expect more competition for first ships, so apply widely and keep every certificate in date.

What is the minimum age to work at sea?

MLC 2006 sets 16 as the minimum age for work on board ship. To hold a deck officer CoC as Officer in Charge of a Navigational Watch under STCW Regulation II/1, you must be at least 18.

Do I have to pay a manning agency to get a job at sea?

No. Under MLC 2006, Standard A1.4, recruitment and placement fees must not be charged to seafarers. You pay only for your own national medical certificate, seaman's book and passport. If someone asks for a placement fee, treat it as a warning sign and verify the agency's licence.

How long is a contract at sea?

It varies by rank, company and flag. Officers often sail 3 to 6 months and ratings longer. Under MLC 2006, the maximum service period before you are entitled to repatriation must be less than 12 months.
