Seafarer Identity Document (SID / BSID): What It Is and How to Get One

Quick answer

The Seafarer Identity Document (SID) is a verifiable seafarer ID issued under ILO Convention No. 185. India issues it as a chip-based Biometric SID (BSID) through the Directorate General of Maritime Administration (DGMA, formerly DG Shipping). Indian seafarers need a valid Indian CDC and passport, apply on the BSID portal with their INDoS login and then attend a centre for facial biometric capture, while Filipino seafarers get their SID from MARINA and, since 13 May 2026, book new appointments only through the eGovPH App.

What the SID and BSID actually are

The Seafarer Identity Document comes from the ILO Seafarers' Identity Documents Convention (Revised), 2003 (No. 185). For states that ratify it, C185 revises and replaces Convention No. 108, because ratifying C185 automatically denounces C108. The International Labour Conference amended C185 in 2016.

The purpose is simple. A port or immigration officer abroad should be able to confirm two things: that you really are a seafarer, and that the document really belongs to you. When C185 was first adopted, the plan was to store a fingerprint template in a barcode. The 2016 amendments updated the technical annexes to match current biometric practice, which is built around the facial image.

Keep these three documents separate in your head:

  • Passport: proves your nationality and is your main travel document.
  • CDC / Seaman's Book (India) or SRB (Philippines): your record of sea service.
  • SID / BSID: an identity document that proves you are a seafarer and can be checked against a national database.

The SID does not replace your passport or any visa a country requires. It is an extra document, not a substitute.

India: the BSID legal basis and who issues it

India has ratified C185. It brought the convention into Indian law through the Merchant Shipping (Seafarer's Bio-metric Identity Document) Rules, 2016. These Rules were notified on 11 April 2016 under section 99A of the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958.

DG Shipping launched BSID issuance for Indian seafarers in April 2019 under Merchant Shipping Notice No. 02 of 2019. The scheme is now run by the Directorate General of Maritime Administration (DGMA, formerly DG Shipping). DGMA Circular No. 44 of 2026 rolled out BSID Version 2.0, together with a new seafarer user manual.

Key facts from DGMA's BSID page, the 2016 Rules and the circulars:

  • The BSID is a smart card with an embedded biometric chip that follows ILO C185.
  • At data capture, your live face is cross-matched against your passport photo using facial-matching software.
  • The designated issuing authorities are the Shipping Masters at the Government Shipping Offices in Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai.
  • Under Rule 7 of the 2016 Rules, a BSID is valid for a maximum of ten years, subject to renewal after the first five years (Rule 8(1)). DG Shipping's draft crew circular of February 2025 says you can apply for renewal within six months before it is due, at any Shipping Master Office. Check the current DGMA circular before you rely on this.
  • To be eligible, you must be an Indian national holding a valid Indian CDC and a valid Indian passport.

Step by step: applying for a BSID in India

These steps follow DGMA's published instructions for BSID Version 2.0 (DGMA Circular No. 44 of 2026 and its seafarer user manual). Get the current BSID portal link from the BSID page on dgma.gov.in instead of relying on an old bookmark or a link someone sends you.

  1. Check your records first. Your name, date of birth and other details must match exactly across your INDoS profile, CDC and passport. Fix any mismatch before you apply.
  2. Log in to the BSID portal with your INDoS number and INDoS password.
  3. Go to Forms > Apply for SID.
  4. Check the personal details. The system pulls these into the form from INDoS. Then continue.
  5. Fill in your address details and continue.
  6. Pay through the portal's e-payment gateway. Check the fee on the portal on the day you apply. Never pay into anyone's personal account.
  7. Book a data-capture appointment at an available centre. Print your acknowledgement or print report.
  8. Attend the appointment with:
    • the printed acknowledgement or print report
    • your original passport
    • your original CDC
    • your payment receipt
  9. Staff check your originals, hand them back and capture your facial biometric (photograph).
  10. The designated authority prints and issues the BSID. Track the status on the portal, not through middlemen.

Duplicate SID and renewal: you also apply through the portal for a duplicate SID (if your card is lost or damaged) and for renewal. Follow the current BSID 2.0 user manual linked from DGMA for the exact steps.

Practical point: plan the appointment around your leave. Don't leave it until the week you're due to fly out.

Practical checks to avoid delays in India

Most of these checks come down to making sure your data lines up across all your records before you apply.

Check What to do
Name or date of birth differs between INDoS, CDC and passport Correct the record at its source (INDoS profile, CDC or passport) before you apply
CDC or passport expired, or close to expiry Renew it first. You are only eligible with a valid CDC and passport
Forgotten INDoS login or password Recover it through the official DGMA portal, not through an agent
Payment taken but not showing Keep the transaction reference and receipt, then raise it through official DGMA help channels
You look very different from your passport photo Your live face is matched to the passport photo, so a big change in appearance can cause problems. Consider renewing an old passport first
Arriving without originals or the printout Bring everything on the list. Photocopies alone won't do

One more tip: anyone offering to "get your BSID fast" for extra cash is a warning sign. The whole process runs through the official portal and the official centres.

Philippines: SID through MARINA

In the Philippines, the Maritime Industry Authority (MARINA) issues both the Seafarer's Record Book (SRB) and the Seafarer's Identity Document (SID). Older seafarers will know these as the combined SIRB. MARINA's Citizen's Charter lists SRB and SID issuance as a service of its Seafarer's Certification and Documentation Division.

What has changed:

  • Under MARINA Advisory No. 2026-24, from 13 May 2026 new appointments for issuance, renewal and replacement of the SID and SRB are made only through the eGovPH App. The app is on the App Store, Google Play and AppGallery.
  • Appointments booked on the old OAS website before 13 May 2026 remain valid.
  • To book in eGovPH: choose MARINA, then MARINA OAS, then your appointment details, then confirm and pay in the app.
  • The full current list of documents, fees and validity periods is in the MARINA Citizen's Charter and the relevant MARINA circular on marina.gov.ph. Check them there. Blog lists go out of date, and the requirements differ for new, renewal and replacement applications.

Practical points for Filipino officers and ratings:

  • Make sure your name and date of birth match exactly across your PSA documents, passport and MARINA records.
  • For a lost or damaged SID, MARINA usually asks for extra supporting documents. Check the replacement category in the Citizen's Charter before you book.
  • Don't pay "fixers" for appointment slots. Book through eGovPH yourself.

What the SID does and doesn't do for you on the ground

C185 aims to make shore leave easier and to give port and immigration officers a document they can verify. In practice:

  • It only has legal weight in countries that have ratified C185. Even there, the port state's immigration and security rules still apply.
  • It is not a visa. If a country requires a crew or transit visa, such as the US C1/D, you still need one.
  • Joining and signing off almost always need your passport plus CDC/SRB, plus whatever the immigration authority asks for. Your company and agent will tell you what the port and flag require.
  • Shore authorities and companies ask for different documents. Carry your SID with your other documents and let the agent tell you what that port wants to see.

Oral exam style: questions an examiner or crewing officer may ask

You may get these at MMD orals, in company interviews or during document checks.

  • Q: Which instrument does the SID come from? A: ILO Convention No. 185, Seafarers' Identity Documents Convention (Revised), 2003, as amended in 2016.
  • Q: How is a SID different from a CDC? A: The CDC (Seaman's Book) records your sea service and identifies you as a seafarer. The SID/BSID is a standardised ID document whose biometric data can be checked against the issuing state's database.
  • Q: What biometric does the Indian BSID use? A: A facial biometric. At capture, your live face is matched to your passport photo, and the data is stored on the card's chip.
  • Q: Who issues the BSID in India? A: The Shipping Masters at the Government Shipping Offices in Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai, under the Directorate General of Maritime Administration (DGMA, formerly DG Shipping).
  • Q: Can you travel on your SID instead of a passport? A: No. It works alongside your passport and does not replace a passport or a visa.
  • Q: What do you need before applying in India? A: A valid Indian CDC, a valid Indian passport and a working INDoS login.
Key points
  • The SID comes from ILO Convention No. 185 (2003, amended 2016). India issues it as a chip-based Biometric SID (BSID).
  • Indian applicants need a valid Indian CDC and passport. They apply on the DGMA BSID 2.0 portal with their INDoS number and password, under Forms > Apply for SID.
  • Get the current BSID portal link from the BSID page on dgma.gov.in. Duplicate SIDs and renewals are also applied for through the portal.
  • Bring the acknowledgement printout, your original passport, your original CDC and the payment receipt to the data-capture appointment.
  • Under the 2016 Rules, a BSID is valid for up to ten years, subject to renewal after the first five years.
  • In the Philippines, MARINA issues the SID and SRB. From 13 May 2026, new appointments are booked only through the eGovPH App.
  • The SID does not replace your passport or any visa a country requires. Fix any mismatched names or dates of birth before you apply.

Frequently asked questions

Is the BSID mandatory for Indian seafarers?

The BSID is India's SID under ILO C185 and the Merchant Shipping (Seafarer's Bio-metric Identity Document) Rules, 2016. DG Shipping's Crew Manual (draft) describes the SID as mandatory for seafarers working internationally. Confirm the current position in the latest DGMA circular.

What documents do I need to get a BSID in India?

You need a valid Indian CDC, a valid Indian passport and a working INDoS login. At the appointment, bring the printed acknowledgement, your original passport, your original CDC and the payment receipt.

How long is the Indian BSID valid?

Under Rule 7 of the Merchant Shipping (Seafarer's Bio-metric Identity Document) Rules, 2016, a BSID is valid for a maximum of ten years, subject to renewal after the first five years (Rule 8(1)). DG Shipping's draft crew circular of February 2025 says you can apply for renewal within six months before it is due, at any Shipping Master Office. Check the current DGMA circular before you rely on this.

Can I travel or sign on using only my SID?

No. The SID is an extra identity document and does not replace your passport or any visa a country requires. Joining and signing off normally need your passport and CDC/SRB, plus whatever the immigration authority asks for.

How do Filipino seafarers book a SID appointment now?

Under MARINA Advisory No. 2026-24, from 13 May 2026 new SID and SRB appointments for issuance, renewal and replacement are made only through the eGovPH App. In the app, choose MARINA, then MARINA OAS, then your appointment details, then confirm and pay. Appointments booked on the old OAS website before 13 May 2026 remain valid. Check the MARINA Citizen's Charter on marina.gov.ph for the current documents and fees.
