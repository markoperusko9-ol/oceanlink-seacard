DNS (Diploma in Nautical Science) explained

Quick answer

The Diploma in Nautical Science (DNS) is a one-year residential pre-sea course for future deck officers. It runs under the Indian Maritime University (IMU) and is approved by DG Shipping. After DNS you complete 18 months of structured shipboard training as a deck cadet, do the four-month Second Mate (FG) competency course and then pass the DG Shipping written and oral exams for your first CoC. The programme leads to a B.Sc (Applied Nautical Science) from IMU, but you need to pass IMU-CET and meet strict age, marks, marital-status and eyesight criteria to get in.

What DNS actually is

DNS is the shortest standard route from school to the bridge in India. IMU lists it as a 1-year DNS course leading to B.Sc (Applied Nautical Science). It is residential and runs at IMU campuses or at DG Shipping-approved institutes affiliated to IMU.

The whole route has four stages:

Stage What happens Typical length
Pre-sea DNS Ashore at the academy: navigation, seamanship, cargo, stability, safety 12 months
Shipboard training Deck cadet under a structured programme, with your Training Record Book filled in on board 18 months
Competency course Second Mate (FG) course at an approved institute About 4 months
CoC exams DG Shipping written and oral exams Depends on your exam slots

People often get one thing wrong. DNS by itself is not a certificate of competency. It makes you eligible to go to sea as a trainee. Your watchkeeping CoC comes only after the sea time, the competency course and the exams.

Eligibility: the criteria that decide your application

IMU and DG Shipping set the eligibility rules, and IMU reissues them each admission cycle. The headline criteria in IMU's current published charter and admissions pages are below. Always check the current IMU-CET notification on IMU's admissions page before you apply.

  • Academics: 10+2 (or equivalent) with Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics, averaging at least 60% across PCM. You also need at least 50% in English in Class 10 or 12. Confirm in the current IMU-CET notice that your Class 10 mark can meet the English requirement, because some IMU programme pages state it against 10+2 only. Some academies also accept graduates (for example B.Sc PCM or B.E./B.Tech) with set minimum marks. Check the exact graduate route in the current notice.
  • Age: IMU calculates age as on the date the academic session starts. Check the current notice for that date. The current maximums for men are General 25, OBC (NCL) 28 and SC/ST 30. For female and transgender candidates they are 27, 30 and 32.
  • Marital status: you must be unmarried.
  • Eyesight: for the nautical programmes, IMU requires unaided distance vision of 6/6 in the better eye and 6/9 in the other. Colour vision must be normal on the Ishihara test. You also need good binocular vision and a full field of vision.
  • Medical: you need a fitness certificate from a DG Shipping-approved doctor. Some sponsors run their own medical and psychometric tests on top.
  • Passport: IMU strongly recommends having one. Some sponsoring companies insist on a valid Indian passport at interview.

SC/ST candidates get a 5% relaxation in the PCM eligibility marks. This does not apply to the English marks. Details can change between cycles, so read the notice.

Step-by-step: from Class 12 to Second Mate (FG)

  1. Check your eyes first. Get an Ishihara test and a distance-vision check before you spend money on coaching or forms. Failing colour vision ends a deck career and cannot be fixed with glasses.
  2. Register for and pass IMU-CET. Admission to DNS goes through IMU's Common Entrance Test. Use only IMU's official website (imu.edu.in) and the links it gives.
  3. Secure a sponsorship or academy seat. Many DNS seats are tied to shipping-company sponsorship. Companies run their own written tests, interviews and medicals, and only take candidates who have qualified in IMU-CET. Be clear about what the offer covers. Some sponsors say plainly that the offer is for training only and does not guarantee a job afterwards.
  4. Complete admission formalities. Your institute registers you on the DG Shipping system and gets your INDoS number created. You will need INDoS for every course, certificate and exam from now on.
  5. Complete the 12-month pre-sea course. This includes the STCW basic safety modules your institute builds into the programme. Pass the semester exams.
  6. Sail 18 months as a deck cadet. This is structured shipboard training with your Training Record Book. Your academy and IMU will tell you what academic work and assessments you need to complete during and after sea time to get the B.Sc (Applied Nautical Science) degree.
  7. Collect your sea-time proof. Get the sea-time completion letter from your company, your completed Training Record Book and correct CDC entries.
  8. Do the Second Mate (FG) competency course at a DG Shipping-approved institute. Then sit the DG Shipping written exams and the MMD oral exams. Check dgshipping.gov.in for the latest circulars on exam phases and eligibility.
  9. Apply for your CoC once you have passed everything, along with the other mandatory certificates.

Documents you will be asked for

Requirements differ slightly between IMU, individual academies and sponsors. Expect most of these:

  • Class 10 and 12 mark sheets and pass certificates (plus degree mark sheets if you apply on the graduate route)
  • IMU-CET admit card and result
  • Proof of age (usually the Class 10 certificate)
  • Medical fitness certificate from a DG Shipping-approved doctor, including the eyesight and colour-vision results
  • Valid Indian passport, or at least proof that you have applied
  • Caste certificate (SC/ST/OBC-NCL) if you claim a relaxation
  • Recent photographs and an identity document
  • Sponsorship or offer letter from the company, if your seat is sponsored

Names must match exactly. IMU warns candidates to make sure the name on the passport is the same as the name on the application. A spelling difference between your passport, mark sheets and INDoS record will hold you up at admission and again later at CDC, INDoS and exam-application stages.

Common rejections and how to avoid them

  • Colour vision or unaided eyesight failure. This is a frequent rejection, and it is final. Test early and use a DG-approved doctor.
  • PCM average worked out wrongly. The 60% is the average of Physics, Chemistry and Maths, not your overall percentage. Do the arithmetic yourself before you apply.
  • Over age on the reference date. IMU counts age as on the date the academic session starts. Candidates who count it on the application date can miss the limit by a few weeks.
  • English below 50% in both Class 10 and 12.
  • Married candidates. DNS is open only to unmarried candidates.
  • Name or date-of-birth mismatches between documents.
  • Unapproved institutes or fake 'guaranteed sponsorship' agents. Any institute you pay must appear on DG Shipping's list of approved institutes. Sponsorship comes from a shipping company or its approved crewing arm, never from an agent selling a 'seat'.

Tip: if you have no sponsorship, ask the academy directly how cadets get their 18 months of sea time. Without a structured shipboard placement you cannot complete the route, and that is where many DNS students get stuck.

Worked scenario: will Ravi be eligible?

Ravi scored Physics 58, Chemistry 62 and Maths 64 in Class 12. He scored 72 in English in Class 10 and 48 in English in Class 12. He is unmarried, general category and male, and he turns 24 a month before the admission cut-off date.

  • PCM average: (58 + 62 + 64) / 3 = 61.3%. He passes the 60% average, even though one subject is below 60.
  • English: he needs 50% in Class 10 or Class 12. His Class 10 score of 72 covers this. Ravi should still confirm in the current IMU-CET notice that a Class 10 mark is accepted, because some IMU programme pages state the English requirement against 10+2 only. If only 10+2 counts, his 48 would not be enough.
  • Age: he is 24 on the cut-off date, which is under the general maximum of 25.
  • Still outstanding: IMU-CET qualification, the DG-approved medical with unaided 6/6 and 6/9 and normal Ishihara, and a seat or sponsorship.

On paper Ravi is eligible. Whether he gets in depends on his eyesight, his CET result and the sponsor's selection.

DNS for Filipino readers and other nationalities

DNS is an Indian route tied to IMU and DG Shipping. Some sponsors restrict it to Indian nationals. In the Philippines, the usual officer route is the BS Marine Transportation degree with shipboard training, and certification goes through MARINA. Do not assume an Indian DNS fits the Philippine system or the other way round. Ask MARINA about Philippine requirements and DG Shipping about Indian ones.

Oral-exam corner: questions examiners like to ask DNS cadets

When you sit your Second Mate orals, examiners sometimes test whether you understand your own route to certification. Have clear answers ready:

  • 'What does STCW require for an OOW certificate?' STCW Regulation II/1 requires at least 12 months of approved seagoing service as part of an approved training programme, with onboard training recorded in an approved record book. Otherwise the requirement is 36 months of approved seagoing service. The Administration can set more, which is why India asks DNS cadets for 18 months.
  • 'Why does a B.Sc Nautical Science cadet need less sea time than you?' The longer shore-based programme counts towards training. Indian rules accept 12 months of structured sea service after the B.Sc route, against 18 months after DNS.
  • 'Who signs your Training Record Book, and what happens if it's incomplete?' The designated shipboard training officer and the Master sign it. Incomplete tasks or missing signatures can get your exam application rejected, so get them signed before you sign off.
  • 'What is the Indian legal basis for your eligibility?' The Merchant Shipping (Standards of Training, Certification and Watch-keeping for Seafarers) Rules, 2014, together with DG Shipping's orders, circulars and approved-course guidelines, which put STCW into effect in India.

Keep your answers short, give the source, and say so if you are not sure.

Key points
  • DNS is a 1-year residential pre-sea course under IMU, approved by DG Shipping, leading to B.Sc (Applied Nautical Science).
  • After DNS you need 18 months of structured sea time as a deck cadet, compared with 12 months after the 3-year B.Sc Nautical Science.
  • Admission needs IMU-CET, and many seats are also tied to company sponsorship.
  • Key criteria: 60% PCM average, 50% English (check the current notice for whether Class 10 counts), unmarried, and an age cap of 25 for general-category men (higher for reserved categories, women and transgender candidates).
  • Unaided 6/6 and 6/9 vision with normal Ishihara colour vision is non-negotiable, so test before you apply.
  • The CoC comes only after the Second Mate (FG) competency course and the DG Shipping written and oral exams.
  • Pay fees only to DG Shipping-approved institutes and treat 'guaranteed sponsorship' agents as a red flag.

Frequently asked questions

Is DNS better than B.Sc Nautical Science?

Neither is better outright. DNS is shorter ashore (one year) but needs 18 months at sea before the Second Mate exam. The B.Sc is three years ashore and needs 12 months at sea. DNS seats are usually tied to sponsorship, so if you have none, the B.Sc route may be more realistic.

Can I join DNS without IMU-CET?

In general, no. IMU admits DNS candidates who qualify in IMU-CET, and sponsoring companies choose from CET-qualified candidates. Check the current IMU admission notice for any exceptions in that year.

Can I do DNS if I wear glasses?

IMU's nautical criteria are based on unaided distance vision: 6/6 in the better eye and 6/9 in the other, plus normal colour vision on the Ishihara test. If you need glasses to reach those figures, you will probably not meet the standard. Get an eye test from a DG Shipping-approved doctor before you apply.

Can a graduate apply for DNS?

Yes, through a separate route. IMU and some academies accept graduates such as B.Sc (PCM) or B.E./B.Tech holders who meet set final-year marks and the English requirement. The age limit still applies, so check the current IMU notification for the exact marks and degrees accepted.

Does DNS guarantee a job after the cadetship?

No. Some sponsors say in writing that selection covers only the course and the shipboard training, not later employment. Read your sponsorship agreement carefully before you sign.

Test yourself

How much structured sea service does a DNS cadet need before the Second Mate (FG) competency course in India?

The DNS route requires 18 months of structured shipboard training. The 12-month figure applies to the B.Sc Nautical Science route (Merchant Shipping (STCW) Rules, 2014 and DG Shipping Second Mate (FG) guidance).

A candidate scored P 58, C 62, M 64 in Class 12. What is the position on the 60% PCM requirement?

IMU's requirement is a minimum 60% average across PCM, not 60% in each subject (IMU eligibility criteria).

Which eyesight standard does IMU apply to DNS candidates?

IMU's nautical programmes require unaided 6/6 and 6/9 distance vision and normal colour vision on the Ishihara test (IMU eligibility criteria).

Under STCW Regulation II/1, what is the minimum approved seagoing service for an OOW certificate within an approved training programme with a record book?

STCW Regulation II/1 sets at least 12 months within an approved programme. Administrations such as India may require more, which is why DNS cadets need 18 months.

What does passing the DNS course itself give you?

DNS is pre-sea training. The CoC follows only after sea service, the competency course and the DG Shipping exams (STCW Regulation II/1).
