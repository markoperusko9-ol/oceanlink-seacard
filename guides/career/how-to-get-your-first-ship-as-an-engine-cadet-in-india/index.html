How to Get Your First Ship as an Engine Cadet in India

Quick answer

To get your first ship as an engine cadet (trainee marine engineer) in India, you need four things. First, a DG Shipping-approved pre-sea engineering route (B.Tech/B.E. Marine Engineering or GME). Second, an INDoS number and CDC through the DG Shipping e-Governance portal. Third, the mandatory STCW basic safety courses. Fourth, a valid medical from a DG-approved doctor. After that, apply only to shipping companies or RPSL-licensed manning agencies. Nobody is allowed to charge you for a berth, and you need an approved training record book for your sea time to count towards MEO Class IV.

First, understand what the 'first ship' is for

Your first contract as an engine cadet is not just a job. It is the onboard part of an approved training programme. Under STCW Regulation III/1, a candidate for Officer in Charge of an Engineering Watch needs combined workshop skills training plus approved seagoing service as part of an approved programme. That onboard training must meet Section A-III/1 of the STCW Code and be documented in an approved training record book.

What this means for you:

  • Sea time served without a proper sign-on, CDC entry and training record book can be rejected when you apply for MEO Class IV.
  • How much sea time you need, and how much of it must be propelling time, depends on your entry route and the scheme approved for it. Institutes and routes differ. Check your institute's DGS approval and the current DG Shipping training circular. Don't rely on what a senior did ten years ago.
  • Near-coastal (NCV) certification is a separate track with its own sea-service rules. Don't assume NCV time counts for foreign-going certification, or the other way round.

Note: Under Section 7 of the Merchant Shipping Act, 2025, the Director-General of Shipping has been renamed the Director-General of Maritime Administration (DGMA, dgma.gov.in). Rules and circulars issued under the old Merchant Shipping Act, 1958 may be revised or re-notified, so always check the latest version on dgma.gov.in.

Crewing departments check these first. A gap in any one of them usually ends the application.

Company-dependent extras include a yellow fever vaccination, a US C1/D visa and particular vaccinations. Ask the company. Don't spend money on these on spec.

Fix your Seafarer Profile first. Keep your e-Governance profile updated before you apply for anything. If your name, date of birth or address is wrong, use the profile correction request option under the Seafarer module of the e-Governance portal. Check the current DGMA crew FAQ for the exact option name. Mismatches between passport, INDoS and CDC are one of the commonest reasons a joining is delayed.

Step-by-step: from course completion to sign-on

  1. Check your route. Make sure your course and institute are DGS-approved and that you know how much sea time your route requires.
  2. Complete STCW basic safety training at a DGS-approved institute. Then check that the certificates show against your INDoS on the portal.
  3. Update your Seafarer Profile on the DG Shipping e-Governance portal: photo, signature, education and passport details.
  4. Apply for your CDC online. You need your INDoS and a complete profile first.
  5. Get a medical from a DG-approved doctor. Time it so it is still valid when you are likely to join.
  6. Build a shortlist of employers. Use the DG Shipping list of valid RPSL holders and the in-house crewing departments of shipping companies. Check every agency's licence number on the official list before you send documents.
  7. Apply properly. Use a one-page CV with INDoS, CDC and passport numbers, course and marks, STCW certificates with dates, and medical validity. Then sit company tests and interviews.
  8. Before you sign, read the Seafarer Employment Agreement. Check the rank stated (trainee/cadet), the contract length and the vessel type. Confirm that you will be issued and supervised in an approved training record book.
  9. On board, get your training record book signed regularly by the designated shipboard training officer and the Chief Engineer. Make sure sign-on and sign-off are correctly entered in your CDC.
  10. Before you sign off, collect your sea service testimonial and a completed training record book. You will need both for MEO Class IV.

Where engine cadet berths actually come from

  • Sponsored seats. Many shipping companies and ship managers sponsor B.Tech or GME cadets and place them on their own fleet. Some sponsorships come with a bond or service commitment. Read the terms before you accept.
  • Campus placement. Established institutes run placement drives with companies. A company that sponsored or recruited from your batch is the most realistic first target.
  • Direct application to RPSL agencies and company crewing offices. This is slower. Expect written tests and interviews.
  • Near-coastal (NCV) and coastal fleets. DG Shipping has issued schemes for engine cadets on near-coastal, coastal and river-sea vessels. If you are considering this route, read the circular itself on the official site, not a third-party summary. Confirm how the sea time will count towards the certificate you are aiming for.

Be realistic. The fleet type matters less than getting approved, properly documented sea time with a Chief Engineer who will actually train you.

Scenario: the 'guaranteed ship' offer

Situation: You finished GME four months ago. Someone on a messaging group offers a 'confirmed TME berth on a foreign tanker within 30 days' for a 'processing and documentation fee'. They send a scanned 'appointment letter' with a company logo.

What to do:

  1. Stop. MLC 2006 (Regulation 1.4 and Standard A1.4) says recruitment and placement services must not charge seafarers for finding them a job. DG Shipping has repeatedly warned seafarers against sub-agents and middlemen.
  2. Check the RPSL list on the official DG Shipping/DGMA site. Is the agency there, with a valid licence? Does the address match?
  3. Contact the shipping company directly using details from its own official website, not the number on the letter. Ask whether the offer is genuine.
  4. Never hand over original documents or pay anything in cash or by UPI to an individual.
  5. Report it to DG Shipping through its official complaint channel. The DG Shipping website lists the current one.

A genuine company or RPSL agency will not ask you to pay for a job, a visa, joining tickets or a 'processing fee'.

Why engine cadet applications get rejected

  • Course or institute not approved by DG Shipping for that route, or documents that can't be verified.
  • Details don't match across passport, INDoS, CDC and marksheets.
  • Incomplete STCW basic safety, or certificates not showing online.
  • Medical issues, or a medical that expired while you waited.
  • Failed company test or interview, usually on basics: pumps, the two-stroke and four-stroke cycles, purifiers, safety systems.
  • Unrealistic expectations, such as turning down a first offer because of vessel type, trade or contract length.
  • Long unexplained gap since finishing the course, with nothing to show for it. Use the gap: revise, do useful approved courses where relevant, and keep your profile current.
  • Poor CV: missing INDoS/CDC numbers, no certificate dates, or a photo and email that look careless.

Oral-style questions you should expect at a company interview

Company technical interviews for engine cadets feel like a mini oral. Expect questions like these:

  • Walk me through the four-stroke and two-stroke cycles. Where are the differences in scavenging?
  • Why does a centrifugal pump need priming, and what is cavitation? Mention NPSH.
  • What does a purifier do, and what is the role of the gravity disc?
  • What is the 15 ppm oily water separator for, and where is its use recorded? Answer: the Oil Record Book, under MARPOL Annex I.
  • What are your first actions on discovering a fire in the engine room?
  • What is a permit to work, and why do we lock out and tag out before maintenance?
  • What precautions do you take before entering an enclosed space such as a tank?
  • What is a crankcase explosion, and what protects against it?

What the examiner or Chief Engineer is really checking: do you think about safety first, can you admit 'I don't know, but I would find out by checking the manual and asking the duty engineer', and have you actually opened your course notes since the final exam?

Key points
  • Your first ship is the onboard part of an approved training programme; sea time without a proper CDC entry and training record book may not count.
  • The sea time you need depends on your entry route and its approved scheme; check your institute's DGS approval and current circulars.
  • Keep your DG Shipping e-Governance Seafarer Profile accurate; mismatched name or date of birth delays joinings.
  • You need INDoS, a CDC, STCW basic safety training, a passport and a medical from a DG-approved doctor before sign-on.
  • Apply only to shipping companies or agencies on the official RPSL list.
  • No genuine company or RPSL agency charges you for a berth; this is prohibited under MLC 2006.
  • Collect your testimonial and completed training record book before signing off; you need both for MEO Class IV.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a ship as an engine cadet without a CDC?

In practice, no. Indian seafarers sign on and off against their CDC, and that record is how your sea service is later verified. Apply for it on the DG Shipping e-Governance portal once your INDoS and profile are complete.

How many months of sea time do I need as an engine cadet for MEO Class IV?

It depends on your entry route (B.Tech/B.E. Marine Engineering, GME or another approved scheme) and whether you are going foreign-going or NCV. STCW sets the international baseline, and DG Shipping's approved schemes and circulars set the Indian detail. Check your institute's approved course structure and the current circular on the official DGMA site (dgma.gov.in).

Is it legal for an agent to charge money for an engine cadet berth?

No. MLC 2006 prohibits recruitment and placement services from charging seafarers for finding them work, and DG Shipping has repeatedly warned against agents who do. Check the agency on the official RPSL list, and report any demand for money.

Who issues the INDoS number for a fresh engine cadet?

INDoS is DG Shipping's national seafarer database number. For a fresher it is normally generated when you do your first DGS-approved course. Seafarers who don't have one can apply online through the e-Governance portal.

Does it matter which type of ship I join first?

Less than most cadets think. What matters is that the time is approved sea service, properly recorded in your CDC and training record book, with real training from the engineers on board. Vessel type affects later career moves, but the first priority is getting documented sea time.

Test yourself

Under STCW Regulation III/1, how must an engine cadet's onboard training be documented to count towards certification?

STCW Regulation III/1 requires onboard training meeting Section A-III/1 to be documented in an approved training record book. The engine log and Oil Record Book have other legal purposes.

An agent asks for a 'processing fee' to guarantee your first ship. What is the correct position?

MLC 2006, Regulation 1.4 and Standard A1.4, prohibit charging seafarers for recruitment or placement. Holding an RPSL does not make such fees legal.

Your passport is correct, but your INDoS/e-Governance profile shows a different date of birth. What should you do first?

The e-Governance portal has a profile correction request under the Seafarer module; check the current DGMA crew FAQ for the exact option. Mismatches delay CDC issue and sign-on.

How should you check whether a manning agency in India is genuine?

Recruitment and placement of Indian seafarers must be through an entity holding a valid Recruitment and Placement Service Licence, shown on the official DG Shipping/DGMA list. Logos and signboards are easy to fake.

A friend says his near-coastal (NCV) sea time will automatically count in full for foreign-going MEO Class IV. What is correct?

DG Shipping sets separate sea-service requirements for NCV certification. How NCV time counts must be checked against the current circular, not assumed either way.
