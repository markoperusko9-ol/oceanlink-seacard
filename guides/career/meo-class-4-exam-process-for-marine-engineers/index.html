MEO Class 4 exam: the process for marine engineers in India

Quick answer

The MEO Class IV Certificate of Competency is India's STCW Regulation III/1 certificate for an officer in charge of an engineering watch on ships of 750 kW or more. You finish your qualifying sea service and training record book, complete the modular courses and the training DG Shipping requires, pass an online eligibility assessment, then pass the written papers and the function-based oral exams at an MMD. Eligibility assessment, seat booking and results now go through the DG Shipping/DGMA exam module at exams.dgma.gov.in. Check the current circulars there, because the rules have changed several times since 2022.

What the MEO Class IV CoC is and who it is for

MEO Class IV is the first certificate of competency for an Indian marine engineer. It is the national grade for STCW Regulation III/1, officer in charge of an engineering watch in a manned engine room or designated duty engineer in a periodically unmanned engine room, on ships of 750 kW propulsion power or more.

The Directorate General of Shipping runs the exam through the Mercantile Marine Departments (MMDs), which also hold the orals. The Directorate now also appears under the name Directorate General of Maritime Administration (DGMA) on dgma.gov.in. Older circulars, forms and screenshots use the DG Shipping name, so expect to see both.

Typical candidates:

  • GME (Graduate Marine Engineering) passouts after their shipboard training
  • B.Tech / B.E. Marine Engineering graduates
  • Candidates from other approved routes, such as some mechanical engineering diploma or degree holders and commissioned Indian Navy engineer officers. Their Part A and Part B requirements differ.

Filipino engineers: this page covers the Indian process. In the Philippines the equivalent III/1 certificate comes through MARINA, and its requirements are different.

Eligibility: sea service, training record and courses

You need all of the following before the MMD will clear your eligibility assessment:

  • Qualifying sea service in the engine department on ships of 750 kW or more. For a motor ship this is widely applied as about 6 months of service, of which about 4 months must be propelling time (engine running, ship under way). Steam and combined steam/motor service follow different splits. The exact figures are in DG Shipping's engineering circulars on qualifying sea service. Check the current qualifying sea service circular on dgma.gov.in rather than an old blog.
  • Completed and signed onboard training record (TAR book), with the training officer's or chief engineer's details entered. Gaps, missing signatures or tasks signed off in bulk on the last day get noticed.
  • Pre-sea course certificate from a DG-approved institute, or a Part A pass or exemption for routes that need it.
  • STCW basic safety training and the modular courses for the operational level, as listed in the current DGMA engineering examination requirements. These normally include Advanced Fire Fighting, Medical First Aid, Proficiency in Survival Craft and Rescue Boats, and the engine room simulator course at operational level. Check the current list in the portal, because more can be added by circular.
  • Medical fitness certificate from a DG-approved doctor, valid on the date you apply.
  • Mandatory e-learning course. DG Shipping has required first-time MEO Class IV candidates to complete a DG Shipping e-learning course (see DGS Circular 02 of 2022 and any later circulars). Check the current circular for who it applies to.
  • Preparatory course (proposed). DGMA has published a draft circular proposing preparatory courses for MEO and ETO grades. Check dgma.gov.in to see whether a final circular has been issued and who it applies to.

Part A vs Part B: Part B (written papers and orals) applies to everyone. Part A applies to candidates whose pre-sea route does not give an exemption. GME and marine engineering degree holders are normally exempt. Some routes may have to pass Part A before starting their trainee sea time, as listed in the current DGMA engineering examination requirements. Confirm your own route early.

Step-by-step: from sign-off to CoC

  1. Before you sign off, get your sea service testimonials signed by the Chief Engineer and Master. They must show sailing days and propelling days, the ship's name, IMO number and kW. Make sure your CDC is stamped and your TAR book is complete.
  2. Get the original sea service letter from the owner or RPSL (manning agency). Check it before you leave the office.
  3. Complete the modular courses and any e-learning or preparatory course required by the current circular at DG-approved institutes. Make sure the institute has uploaded each certificate against your INDoS number.
  4. Update your seafarer profile on the DG Shipping portal. Your name, date of birth and INDoS details must match your CDC and passport exactly.
  5. Apply for eligibility assessment online. Under DGS Circular 21 of 2026 (dated 30 April 2026), eligibility assessment, seat booking and result declaration move to the new examination module at exams.dgma.gov.in. Engineering-grade data is being migrated there from the legacy e-Governance/e-Pariksha system. Bookings were paused during the migration. Upload clear scans of every document.
  6. Wait for the MMD to verify the assessment. If it is returned, fix exactly what the remark says and resubmit.
  7. Pay fees and book seats for the written papers and orals through the portal. Booking windows and seat availability are announced on the portal, so do not rely on old fee lists.
  8. Sit the written papers. The paper pattern has changed over the years, so check the current pattern in the latest circulars on the portal before you prepare.
  9. Attend the orals at the MMD, or online if the current notice allows it, with original documents.
  10. CoC issue. When every function is passed, follow the issue and dispatch instructions on the current exam module/MMD notice.

Common reasons an assessment gets returned

  • Propelling time missing or not separately shown on the testimonial.
  • Ship below 750 kW or kW not stated, so the service cannot be counted.
  • Name or date-of-birth mismatch between passport, CDC, INDoS and certificates. This is the most avoidable one.
  • TAR book incomplete: blank pages, no Chief Engineer signature, or training officer details missing.
  • Course certificate not visible against your INDoS because the institute did not upload it.
  • Expired medical on the date of application.
  • Blurred or partial scans, or the wrong document uploaded in a slot.
  • Profile not updated or migrated to the new exam module during the 2026 changeover.
  • Sea service letter from the wrong issuer, or a letter that does not match the CDC dates.

Worked scenario: Rahul signs off after 6 months 10 days on a 9,000 kW bulk carrier. His testimonial shows only total days. The MMD returns his assessment asking for propelling time. He has to get the company's technical department to issue a corrected testimonial from the engine log abstracts, and that costs him a booking cycle. If he had checked the testimonial before leaving the ship, it would have taken ten minutes.

Oral exam: what examiners actually probe

The orals follow the STCW III/1 functions: marine engineering; electrical, electronic and control engineering; maintenance and repair; and controlling the operation of the ship and care for persons on board. Examiners want to see that you could keep a safe watch tomorrow, not that you can recite a textbook.

Typical questions and the line of a good answer:

  • "You take over the watch. What do you check?" Talk to the off-going engineer, then check main engine and generator parameters, alarms, tank levels, bilges, steering gear, purifiers and the log book. Sign in only once you are satisfied.
  • "Signs of a scavenge fire and your actions?" High exhaust temperature on the affected unit, scavenge temperature alarm, possibly turbocharger surging. Inform the bridge and the Chief Engineer, reduce load, cut fuel to that unit if the engine design allows, and keep lubrication going. Do not open the scavenge doors until the fire is out and things have cooled.
  • "Why do you megger a motor, and what is a bad reading?" To check insulation resistance before you energise the motor. Compare the reading with the maker's limits and the trend from earlier tests.
  • "How does the OWS stop you discharging over 15 ppm?" The oil content meter alarms and the three-way valve recirculates or stops overboard discharge. Never tamper with the sample line.
  • "Blackout at sea: first actions?" Inform the bridge. Check the emergency generator has taken the emergency load. Quickly check for an obvious fault that would trip the incoming set, start and close a generator on the board, restart essential services in sequence, then investigate the root cause.

Answer in the order you would actually do things on board, say "inform the Chief Engineer and the bridge" where it applies, and admit it when you do not know something.

Practical tips from the examiner's side

  • Start the paperwork on board: testimonials, TAR signatures and log abstracts are hard to get once you have left the ship.
  • Read the latest circulars and booking notices on dgshipping.gov.in and dgma.gov.in before each booking. Fees, windows and course rules change.
  • Use only official portals. Treat anyone selling "guaranteed eligibility" or seats with suspicion.
  • Prepare for orals with your own ship in mind. Draw the fuel, lube oil, cooling and air systems of the ship you sailed on from memory.
  • Pass the written papers while the theory is fresh, but do not ignore the orals. They are where most candidates lose time.
  • After you pass, read your CoC and its limitations carefully, then sort out your flag state endorsement before joining a foreign-flag ship.
Key points
  • MEO Class IV is India's STCW III/1 certificate for officer in charge of an engineering watch, on ships of 750 kW or more.
  • Testimonials must show propelling time as well as sailing time; a missing propelling-time entry is a common reason for return.
  • First-time candidates must also complete the DG Shipping e-learning course required by the current circular; check dgma.gov.in for any preparatory course requirement.
  • Eligibility assessment, seat booking and results move to exams.dgma.gov.in under DGS Circular 21 of 2026.
  • Part B (written papers and orals) applies to everyone; whether you must sit Part A depends on your pre-sea route.
  • The orals follow the four STCW III/1 functions and test safe watchkeeping judgement.
  • Fees and booking windows change, so check the official portal before every booking.

Frequently asked questions

How much sea time do I need for MEO Class 4?

For motor ships, the widely applied requirement is about 6 months of engine-department service on ships of 750 kW or more, with about 4 months of propelling time. Steam and combined service have different splits. Check the current DG Shipping engineering circular on qualifying sea service before you apply.

Is Part A compulsory for GME candidates?

GME and marine engineering degree graduates are normally exempt from Part A and only take Part B written papers and orals. Some other routes must pass Part A, in some cases before starting their trainee sea service. Confirm the rules for your route on the official portal.

Where do I apply for MEO Class 4 eligibility assessment now?

DG Shipping announced under DGS Circular 21 of 2026 that eligibility assessment, seat booking and result declaration move to a new exam module at exams.dgma.gov.in. Data is being migrated there from the legacy e-Governance/e-Pariksha system. Make sure your profile is updated and migrated.

Can I give MEO Class 4 orals online?

Whether orals are held online or at the MMD depends on the booking notice in force for that cycle. Read the current notice on the official website before booking, and carry your original documents whichever format you are given.

Why was my MEO Class 4 eligibility assessment rejected?

The usual causes are missing propelling time, name mismatches across documents, an incomplete TAR book, course certificates not linked to your INDoS number, or an expired medical. Read the MMD's remark, fix exactly that point, and resubmit.

Test yourself

Which STCW regulation does the Indian MEO Class IV CoC correspond to?

MEO Class IV is the operational-level engineering certificate under STCW Regulation III/1 (STCW Code Section A-III/1).

Your testimonial shows 190 days on board but no propelling time. What is the most likely result at eligibility assessment?

Qualifying service for MEO Class IV includes a propelling-time element under DG Shipping's qualifying sea service rules. Check the current qualifying sea service circular on dgma.gov.in for the exact figures.

Above what oil content must the oily water separator system stop overboard discharge of machinery-space bilge water?

MARPOL Annex I, Regulations 14 and 15, limit the oil content of discharged effluent to 15 ppm, with an alarm and automatic stopping of discharge above that level.

Under DGS Circular 21 of 2026, where are MEO Class IV eligibility assessment and seat booking carried out?

DGS Circular 21 of 2026 moves eligibility assessment, seat booking and result declaration to the new examination module at exams.dgma.gov.in.

What are the MEO Class IV orals structured around?

STCW Code Table A-III/1 sets out competence across four functions, and the orals assess those functions.
