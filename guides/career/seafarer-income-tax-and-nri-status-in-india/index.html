Seafarer Income Tax and NRI Status in India

Quick answer

An Indian seafarer who leaves India for employment or as crew is non-resident for income tax if they spend less than 182 days in India in the financial (tax) year. CDC sign-on to sign-off dates are used to count days for eligible voyages. A non-resident's salary for services rendered outside India on board foreign-going ships is not taxed in India just because it is credited to an NRE account. Indian income such as NRO interest, rent or capital gains is still taxable, and you file the return on the incometax.gov.in e-filing portal, normally on ITR-2.

The rule that matters: 182 days in India

Your residential status is decided fresh for every financial year (1 April to 31 March). Your nationality, your passport and the flag of your ship do not decide it. What decides it is the number of days you spend physically in India.

Under the general test, an individual is resident if either of these is true:

  • they are in India for 182 days or more in the year, or
  • they are in India for 60 days or more in the year and 365 days or more in the four preceding years.

For an Indian citizen who leaves India for employment outside India or as a crew member of an Indian ship, the 60 days in the second test is replaced by 182 days. In practice, only 182 days or more in India makes them resident.

The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act from tax year 2026-27. Reports indicate the crew/employment 182-day rule is retained, but section and rule references may differ, so check the current text on incometax.gov.in. If a tax adviser or bank quotes 1961 Act section numbers for tax year 2026-27 onwards, ask them to confirm the current reference.

What each status means:

Status What India taxes
Non-resident (NR) Only income received, or accruing or arising, in India
Resident and ordinarily resident (ROR) Worldwide income, including your sea wages
Resident but not ordinarily resident (RNOR) Income received, accruing or arising in India, plus foreign income from a business controlled or profession set up in India. Foreign salary received abroad is generally not taxed. Get professional advice

Counting your days: the CDC is your evidence

Rule 126 of the Income-tax Rules, 1962 was inserted in 2015 to settle disputes about how crew days are counted. It applies to an Indian citizen serving as crew on a ship making an eligible voyage. Broadly, that is a voyage in international traffic to or from an Indian port. For such a voyage, the period from the sign-on date entered in your CDC to the sign-off date entered in your CDC is not counted as a stay in India. This applies even if the ship is in Indian waters for part of that time.

What this means for you on board:

  • Your Continuous Discharge Certificate (CDC) is the primary proof. Before you leave the ship, check that every sign-on and sign-off is entered, stamped and correct. A missing sign-off entry is a common reason a seafarer struggles to prove their days later.
  • Days on a ship trading entirely abroad are days outside India anyway.
  • Coastal service on Indian ships is different. Days on a purely coastal run within Indian waters are generally not covered by the voyage exclusion. A seafarer on Indian coastal ships can easily reach 182 days in India.
  • Leave, training ashore, CoC exam preparation and waiting for a ship in India all count as days in India. Many officers become resident in an exam year without noticing.

Keep a simple spreadsheet for each financial year. Record the date of every movement and the evidence for it, such as CDC entries, passport immigration stamps and air tickets. If you come within a couple of weeks of 182 days in any year, treat that year as high risk and get your count checked.

Worked scenarios

Scenario 1: Second Officer, one long contract. His CDC shows sign-on abroad on 10 May and sign-off on 20 December. He is at home in India from 1 April to 9 May and from 21 December to 31 March. That is about 39 + 101 = 140 days in India, so he is non-resident for that year.

Scenario 2: Third Engineer in an exam year. She does a four-month contract. She spends the rest of the year in India on a preparatory course and her MEO Class 2 exams. Her days in India come to well over 182, so she is resident for that year. Circular 13/2017 no longer protects her. Wages credited to an Indian account (including NRE) count as received in India and are taxable. If she is ordinarily resident, wages paid abroad are taxable too. If she is RNOR, they may not be, so get her RNOR status checked. She should plan for this in advance rather than find out from a tax notice.

Scenario 3: AB on an Indian coastal tanker. He is on board for nine months, but the ship trades only between Indian ports. Those days are generally days in India. He is therefore likely to be resident, even though he was "at sea" for most of the year.

Scenario 4: Two short contracts, borderline. A Chief Officer counts 178 days at home. He then finds that a delayed sign-off was never entered in his CDC. His only proof is the agent's letter and an air ticket. He should get the CDC entry corrected before filing, through his company/RPSL agency and the Mercantile Marine Department or Shipping Master as the DG Shipping process requires. He should not wait for a query to arrive.

Salary in the NRE account: what is and is not taxed

CBDT Circular No. 13/2017 deals with salary that accrues to a non-resident seafarer for services rendered outside India on board foreign-going ships. It clarifies that this salary is not included in total income merely because it is credited to an NRE account in India. Circular No. 17/2017 later amended its wording, so read the current text on the Income Tax Department website. Both circulars were issued under the 1961 Act. For tax year 2026-27 onwards, check that the department still treats them as applying under the 2025 Act.

The circular, as amended, applies where:

  • you are non-resident for that year (the 182-day test above)
  • the salary is for services rendered outside India on board foreign-going ships
  • the salary is credited to an NRE account. The circular deals with salary credited to an NRE account, and crediting it there does not by itself make it taxable.

Even when you are non-resident, Indian income is still taxable:

  • NRO account interest. Banks normally deduct TDS on this.
  • Rent from property in India.
  • Capital gains on Indian shares, mutual funds or property.
  • Interest on fixed deposits in resident accounts you forgot to convert.

Interest on NRE deposits is exempt while you qualify as a person resident outside India under FEMA. This is the same test that lets you hold an NRE account. The exemption is not tied to your income-tax residential status.

The "deemed resident" trap. An Indian citizen can be treated as resident (RNOR) if two things are true: their Indian-source income (not foreign-source income) goes above a statutory threshold, and they are not liable to tax in any other country. Most seafarers pay no tax in the flag state. This matters if you have large rent or capital gains in India, so take professional advice in that situation.

FEMA is a separate test. Whether you may hold NRE accounts depends on your residential status under FEMA, not under the Income-tax Act. Your bank applies the FEMA rules. When you stop sailing or settle ashore, tell your bank so it can redesignate your NRE accounts.

Filing your return: step by step

You file on the Income Tax Department's e-filing portal (incometax.gov.in). Many seafarers whose only Indian income is NRO interest file anyway. Filing lets them reclaim excess TDS and gives them a filed return to show for loans and visas.

Documents to collect:

  • PAN, passport, and CDC with every sign-on and sign-off entry
  • seafarer employment agreements and wage slips, or the company's salary certificate
  • statements for NRE, NRO and any resident accounts
  • TDS certificates from banks or tenants
  • your day-count working for the year

Steps:

  1. Register or log in on the e-filing portal with your PAN. Update your mobile number and email so verification codes reach you, including abroad.
  2. Download your Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Form 26AS. Check that every interest credit, TDS entry and high-value transaction is yours and is correct. If anything is wrong, submit feedback on the AIS.
  3. Choose the form. Non-residents cannot use ITR-1. The department lists ITR-2 for individuals (resident or non-resident) without business or professional income. It lists ITR-3 for those who have such income.
  4. In the residential status section, select non-resident and fill in the details asked for (days in India and jurisdiction). Only declare non-resident status if your day count supports it.
  5. Report your Indian income. Treat NRE-credited sea wages as the circular allows only if you are non-resident.
  6. Declare your NRE/NRO bank accounts and choose one account for the refund.
  7. Submit the return, then e-verify it within the time limit shown on the portal. An unverified return is treated as not filed.

Due dates and the consequences of late filing are set by law and are sometimes extended. Check the portal each year rather than relying on last year's date.

Common mistakes and why notices arrive

  • Claiming NR status in a year you were resident. Exam years and long leave are the usual cause.
  • Salary paid into a resident savings account, or no NRE account opened after sign-on. This causes avoidable questions about your status and where the salary was received.
  • CDC gaps. Missing sign-off entries or wrong dates leave you unable to prove your days.
  • Ignoring the AIS. NRO interest, mutual fund redemptions and property sales appear there. If your return leaves them out, expect a mismatch notice.
  • Not filing a return even though TDS was deducted, so the refund is never claimed.
  • Out-of-date contact details on the portal. Notices go unanswered while you are at sea. Give access to someone ashore whom you trust, or use a tax practitioner.
  • Taking tax advice from a crewing agent or a WhatsApp forward. Use the department's FAQs or a chartered accountant who regularly works with seafarers.
  • Not telling the bank when your status changes, so your accounts stay wrongly designated under FEMA.

Year-end checklist before 31 March

Ask yourself these questions every year:

  • How many days have I spent in India since 1 April, and what proves each one?
  • Are all my CDC sign-on and sign-off entries for this year complete and correct?
  • If I take leave, a course or an exam before 31 March, will I cross 182 days?
  • Has every salary credit gone into my NRE account?
  • What Indian income do I have (NRO interest, rent, gains), and has TDS been deducted?
  • Are my portal login, mobile number and email up to date, and can I e-verify from abroad?

If any answer is unclear, sort it out before the year closes. After 31 March, your day count for the year can no longer change.

Key points
  • An Indian citizen working as crew or abroad is resident only with 182 days or more in India in the financial (tax) year.
  • For eligible voyages, time between CDC sign-on and sign-off does not count as days in India, so keep your CDC entries complete.
  • Long leave, courses and CoC exam periods ashore count as days in India and can make you resident.
  • A non-resident's salary for services outside India on foreign-going ships is not taxed merely because it is credited to an NRE account (CBDT Circulars 13/2017 and 17/2017).
  • NRO interest, rent and capital gains in India remain taxable even for non-residents.
  • Non-residents file on the incometax.gov.in portal, normally on ITR-2, and must e-verify the return.
  • The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act from tax year 2026-27. Check incometax.gov.in for current section and rule references.

Frequently asked questions

Is merchant navy salary tax-free in India?

It is not taxed in India if you are non-resident for that year and the salary is for services rendered outside India on board foreign-going ships. Crediting it to an NRE account does not by itself make it taxable. If you are in India for 182 days or more in a year, you are resident. Your sea wages are then taxable in India if you are ordinarily resident, or if the wages are received in India (for example, credited to an Indian account).

How do I count 182 days as a seafarer?

Count every day you were physically in India between 1 April and 31 March. For eligible voyages, leave out the period between the sign-on and sign-off dates entered in your CDC. Keep CDC entries, passport stamps and tickets as evidence.

Do I need to file an income tax return if I am an NRI seafarer?

It depends on your Indian income and the other conditions the department sets each year. Many seafarers file anyway, to claim refunds of TDS deducted on NRO interest and to have a filed return for loans and visas. Check the current conditions on the e-filing portal.

Which ITR form should an NRI seafarer use?

Non-resident individuals cannot use ITR-1. The department lists ITR-2 for individuals, resident or non-resident, who have no business or professional income. It lists ITR-3 for those who have such income.

Does working on an Indian ship make me resident?

Not automatically. Indian crew on foreign-going ships are assessed under the same 182-day test as other seafarers. However, days on purely coastal voyages within Indian waters are generally days in India, so coastal crew can easily become resident.
