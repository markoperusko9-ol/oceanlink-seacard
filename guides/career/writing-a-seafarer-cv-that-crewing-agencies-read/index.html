Writing a seafarer CV that crewing agencies read

Quick answer

A seafarer CV that gets read puts your rank, CoC grade, vessel-type experience, availability and certificate expiry dates on the first half-page. Each sea-service line should match your CDC or SIRB exactly. Send it only to agencies you have checked on the official licence list (DG Shipping RPSL list in India, DMW licensed agency list in the Philippines), and never pay to have it 'registered'.

What the crewing desk looks at first

A crewing officer filling a vacancy is not reading your CV for pleasure. They have a vessel, a rank, a joining window and a client's requirements. Their first look decides whether they read any further. In practice they check:

  • Rank applied for, and CoC grade held. These must match. A 3rd Officer with an OOW CoC applying for 2nd Officer is fine if the company promotes on that CoC. A 2nd Officer applying for Chief Officer without the CoC is not.
  • Vessel type and size. Tanker clients want tanker time. Bulk clients want bulk time. Write "Product/Chemical tanker, 50,000 DWT", not "various vessels".
  • Last sign-off date and availability. A clear "Available from: [date]" saves them a phone call.
  • Certificate validity. CoC, medical, STCW courses, tanker/gas endorsements and passport. Anything expiring before the end of a normal contract is a problem.
  • Travel documents. Passport validity, and visas the trade needs (for example a US C1/D for US-calling ships).

If any of these are missing or buried on page three, many screeners will simply move on to the next CV.

Layout that works: section by section

Keep it to two pages if you can, plus a sea-service table. Use a plain layout with no graphics, and send it as a PDF unless the agency asks for their own form. Name the file sensibly, e.g. Surname_Firstname_3rdOfficer_CV.pdf.

1. Header block - Full name exactly as in passport - Rank applied for / rank held - Nationality, date of birth, phone (with WhatsApp if used), email - Available from (date)

2. Certificates of competency and endorsements

Certificate Grade / Regulation Issued by No. Expiry
CoC OOW (II/1) Issuing administration xxxx dd/mm/yyyy
Tanker CoP/endorsement (e.g. DCE) Oil – advanced (V/1-1) ... ... ...
Flag endorsement e.g. Liberia/Panama/MI ... ... ...

3. STCW courses and other certificates. List each one with its expiry or "no expiry": BST modules, PSCRB, AFF, Medical First Aid/Care, SSO/DSD, ECDIS generic plus type-specific makes, BRM/ERM, HV and so on.

4. Identity and travel documents. Passport, CDC/Seaman's Book or SIRB, INDoS number (India), visas, yellow fever certificate, and seafarer medical with expiry dates.

5. Sea-service table. This is the core of the CV, and is covered in the next section.

6. Short skills/remarks block. Put specific items here, such as "STS operations, 14 transfers", "BWTS operation (UV type)", "cargo planning on loading computer". Avoid lines like "hard-working team player".

7. References. Give your last two Masters or Chief Engineers, or the company's crewing contact, but only with their permission.

The sea-service table: get it exact

The crewing desk will check your sea service against your CDC/SIRB and, where they can, against previous employers. Dates that don't match are one of the quickest ways to lose trust.

Recommended columns:

Vessel IMO no. Type DWT/GT Flag ME make/kW Rank Sign on Sign off Company

Rules I give every candidate:

  • Most recent first.
  • Use the dates in your CDC/SIRB, not the dates you left or reached home.
  • IMO number, not just the name. Ships get renamed, but the IMO number stays the same.
  • Show the actual rank you served in. If you were "Junior 3rd Officer" or "4th Engineer", write that. Upgrading the rank on paper gets found out during reference checks.
  • Explain gaps in one line, e.g. "Jan–Aug: ashore, study and exam for CoC Class IV". An unexplained gap gets asked about anyway, and it looks worse when it's hidden.
  • Engineers: add main engine make/model and power (e.g. MAN B&W ME-C or WinGD X-type, with kW), plus aux engine makes. Clients with electronically controlled engines specifically look for that experience.
  • Deck officers: add trade and cargo (crude, CPP, chemicals IMO type, LPG/LNG, grain, coal, containers) and the ECDIS makes you used.
  • Ratings: state your watchkeeping CoP and Able Seafarer Deck/Engine status, plus trade skills (welding, crane operation, mooring on large ships).

Matching the CV to official records (India and the Philippines)

Agencies increasingly check your documents online before they call you. Make sure what you write is what the administration's system shows.

Indian seafarers - Your INDoS number and CoC details should match DG Shipping records. DG Shipping provides an online Certificate of Competency checker and runs CoC/CoP processes, including revalidation, through its e-Governance portal on dgshipping.gov.in. - DG Shipping is updating its online services. Check dgshipping.gov.in for the current portal and CoC verification link. - If a certificate is under revalidation, say so ("CoC revalidation applied, awaiting issue") rather than listing an expired date with no note.

Filipino seafarers - MARINA-issued CoCs and CoPs can be checked on MARINA's online verification facilities. Older certificates can be searched by certificate number on the STCW Administration Office site (stcw.marina.gov.ph). Certificates issued online since 2022 carry a QR code and are verified with the certificate's serial number or QR code. Check marina.gov.ph for the current verification page. - Your SIRB entries should match your sea-service table. Your licensed manning agency will handle the DMW side of deployment, so follow their instructions and check current requirements on dmw.gov.ph.

Everyone: if your flag endorsements were issued by Panama, Liberia, Marshall Islands or another flag, list the flag, number and expiry. Verification methods differ by administration, so don't assume the agency can check them the same way.

Common reasons a CV is rejected

From the crewing side, these are the repeat offenders:

  1. Sea-service dates that don't match the CDC/SIRB.
  2. Expired or soon-to-expire certificates with no plan stated. Write "AFF refresher booked" if it is booked.
  3. No vessel type or size, just ship names.
  4. Rank inflation, or applying for a rank your CoC does not support.
  5. Mass mailing with every agency in CC. It looks careless and exposes your data.
  6. Huge attachments, such as 30 phone-photo scans in one email. Send the CV first and give documents when asked.
  7. Wrong or dead phone number/email. It sounds obvious, but it happens all the time.
  8. Copy-paste from a friend's CV, complete with their vessel names. Yes, really.
  9. Unexplained long gaps or very short contracts. A short contract with a reason ("signed off on medical grounds" or "ship sold") is fine. Silence makes people wonder.

For freshers and cadets, the CV is mostly about academy, course, sponsor (if any), STCW basic courses, INDoS/SIRB and medical. Don't pad it. Agencies hiring cadets know you have no sea time.

Worked example: a 3rd Officer moving to tankers

Situation: a 3rd Officer has bulk carrier sea time as cadet and 3rd Officer, holds an OOW CoC, and has completed Basic Training for Oil and Chemical Tanker Cargo Operations. She wants a product tanker.

Weak line: "3rd Officer – MV Sea Star – 2 contracts – good experience."

Strong lines: - Rank applied: 3rd Officer – product/chemical tanker - Tanker CoP: Basic Training for Oil & Chemical Tanker Cargo Operations (STCW V/1-1), plus DCE where issued (India), expiry dd/mm/yyyy - Sea service: MV Sea Star, IMO 9xxxxxx, Bulk carrier, 82,000 DWT, Panama flag, 3rd Officer, sign-on/off per CDC - Remarks: "Ballast operations under BWM plan, LSA/FFA maintenance as 3rd Officer, ECDIS: [makes]"

That CV is honest about her bulk background, shows she already holds the tanker CoP, and tells the crewing officer what she can do on day one. Whether a tanker operator takes bulk-to-tanker officers is company policy. The CV just makes sure she is considered.

Sending it safely, and what the crewing manager will ask

Before you send: - India: check the agency on the current RPSL list on the DG Shipping website. The list shows each licence's status (e.g. valid, invalid, suspended or blocked). Deal only with agencies shown as valid. - Philippines: check the agency on the DMW licensed recruitment agency list on dmw.gov.ph. Search using the agency's exact legal name. - Under MLC 2006 Standard A1.4, recruitment and placement costs must not be passed to the seafarer. The limited exceptions cover your own national statutory medical certificate, seafarer's book and passport or similar travel document. Visas are for the shipowner to pay. Anyone asking for a "CV registration" or "guaranteed joining" fee is a red flag. - Don't send full passport or CoC scans to an unverified email or social-media recruiter. If you must share copies, consider marking them "For [agency] application only".

Oral-exam style: questions a crewing manager will ask, so prepare the answers - "Your last ship: type, DWT, main engine, trade?" Answer without looking at the CV. - "Why only four months on this contract?" Give a one-line factual reason. - "Which of your certificates expire in the next 12 months?" Know the dates. - "Which ECDIS makes / ME types have you worked with?" - "When can you join, and is your medical valid for the full contract?" - For tankers: "How many STS operations / cargo grades / cleaning operations?"

If your answers match the CV line for line, you have done the job properly.

Key points
  • Put rank applied, CoC grade, vessel type, availability date and certificate expiries on the first half-page.
  • Sea-service dates must match your CDC or SIRB exactly; always include IMO numbers.
  • Engineers list main engine make/model and power; deck officers list trades, cargoes and ECDIS makes.
  • Explain every gap in one honest line rather than hiding it.
  • Indian seafarers check the agency on the DG Shipping RPSL list; Filipinos check the DMW licensed agency list.
  • Under MLC 2006 a seafarer should not pay recruitment or placement fees, apart from limited personal-document costs.
  • Send a clean PDF first and share document scans only with a verified agency.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a seafarer CV be?

Ideally two pages plus the sea-service table. Experienced officers with many contracts can run longer, but the key details (rank, CoC, vessel type, availability, expiries) must still be on page one. Agencies with their own application form will want it filled in as well.

Should I attach all my certificates with the CV?

Not with the first email. Send the CV with certificate numbers and expiry dates listed, then provide clear scans when a verified agency asks. That keeps the email small and limits how far your personal documents travel.

Do I need a photo on my seafarer CV?

Many crewing agencies and their application forms expect a passport-style photo. That is common practice in the industry, not a legal rule. Use a recent, plain-background photo, not a selfie on the bridge wing.

Can a crewing agency charge me to register my CV?

MLC 2006 Standard A1.4 requires that recruitment and placement costs are not borne by the seafarer, with limited exceptions for personal documents such as your national statutory medical certificate, seafarer's book and passport. A 'registration' or 'guaranteed job' fee is a warning sign. Check the agency on the DG Shipping RPSL list or the DMW licensed agency list, and report suspicious recruiters.

Should I make a different CV for each vessel type?

Keep one master CV and adjust the top block and remarks for the vessel type you are applying for. The sea-service table must stay the same everywhere. Only the emphasis changes, never the facts.

Test yourself

Under MLC 2006 Standard A1.4, which cost may a seafarer legitimately be expected to bear?

The exceptions are limited to personal documents such as the national statutory medical certificate, seafarer's book and passport. Visas are for the shipowner. MLC 2006, Standard A1.4, paragraph 5(b).

Sign-on and sign-off dates in your CV sea-service table should match which record?

Agencies verify sea service against the official discharge book entries. Other dates create mismatches. See DG Shipping and MARINA seafarer record systems.

A 2nd Engineer applies to a fleet with electronically controlled main engines. Which CV entry helps most?

Clients screen for specific engine experience, and a make/model entry shows it at a glance. This is industry crewing practice, not a convention requirement.

An Indian seafarer wants to confirm a manning agency is licensed. What is the reliable check?

DG Shipping publishes the RPSL list showing each licence's status (e.g. valid, invalid, suspended or blocked); deal only with agencies shown as valid. Letters and badges are easily faked. Merchant Shipping (Recruitment and Placement of Seafarers) Rules, India.

You were ashore for nine months studying for your CoC exam. How should the CV show it?

An honest, brief explanation answers the question before it is asked. Altered dates fail verification against the CDC/SIRB. Industry crewing practice.
