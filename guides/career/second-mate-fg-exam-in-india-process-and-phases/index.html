Second Mate (FG) exam in India: process and phases, step by step

Quick answer

The Second Mate (FG) CoC is the first foreign-going deck officer CoC in India. It is issued under STCW Regulation II/1 for an officer in charge of a navigational watch. You first complete pre-sea training, approved sea time with a signed Training Record Book, and the post-sea competency course. You then apply for eligibility assessment on e-Samudra, the MMD checks your documents, and you book the written and oral exams through the portal named in the current DGMA examination notice. The Second Mate (FG) exam has no Phase I and Phase II; that split belongs to Chief Mate (FG).

Who runs the exam and what you are actually sitting

The Director-General of Shipping has been renamed the Director-General of Maritime Administration (DGMA) under Section 7 of the Merchant Shipping Act, 2025. Older notices and portals may still carry DG Shipping branding.

  • The Chief Examiner of Masters and Mates sets examination policy.
  • The Mercantile Marine Departments (MMDs) check documents and hold the orals.

The Second Mate (FG) Certificate of Competency is the STCW Regulation II/1 certificate for an officer in charge of a navigational watch on ships of 500 GT or more. You are tested against STCW Code Table A-II/1, which covers three functions at operational level:

  • Navigation
  • Cargo handling and stowage
  • Controlling the operation of the ship and care for persons on board

A common mix-up is talking about "2nd Mate Phase I". Second Mate (FG) has no Phase I/II split. That split belongs to Chief Mate (FG); see our Chief Mate exam in India guide. When people talk about the "phases" of the Second Mate exam, they mean the stages of the route below.

The route in phases: from cadet to CoC

Stage What happens Where
1. Pre-sea DNS, B.Sc (Nautical Science) or another approved route DGMA-approved institute
2. Sea time Approved seagoing service with TRB/TAR entries completed and signed On board, through your sponsoring company or RPSL agency
3. Post-sea Second Mate (FG) competency course plus the required modular and simulator courses DGMA-approved institutes
4. Eligibility assessment Apply online, generate the application form, MMD verifies documents e-Samudra, then MMD
5. Written exams Book a seat, download your admit card, sit the papers Online portal named in the current DGMA examination notice; exam centre
6. Oral exam Booked after you pass the writtens; face-to-face with a surveyor Online portal named in the current DGMA examination notice; MMD
7. CoC issue Certificate issued once all requirements are met DGMA/MMD

DGMA's entry routes, as listed on its Career as a Nautical Officer page (check the current page before applying):

  • DNS: one year at the institute plus 18 months of sea time.
  • B.Sc (Nautical Science): three years plus 12 months of sea time.

STCW itself sets the minimum:

  • At least 12 months of approved seagoing service as part of an approved training programme with a Training Record Book, or 36 months of seagoing service otherwise.
  • In either case, at least six months of bridge watchkeeping under supervision.

The Indian requirement for your route can be longer than the STCW minimum. Count your sea time against the Indian rule, not the convention minimum.

Ratings route: DGMA has been revising the route for deck ratings to Second Mate (FG). Read the latest Examination, Assessment and Certification circulars on dgma.gov.in before enrolling.

Eligibility assessment: e-Samudra, the application form and your document file

All CoC applications go through e-Samudra (e-governance.dgshipping.gov.in). The steps are:

  1. Log in with your INDoS-linked profile and apply for eligibility assessment for Second Mate (FG).
  2. Generate the application form on e-Samudra and print it with your photograph.
  3. Pay online and keep a printout of the receipt.
  4. Submit your documents to the MMD, or upload them if your MMD's current notice allows it.
  5. Watch your registered email and your portal status for approval or queries.

A typical file for a cadet contains:

  • Printed e-Samudra application form and payment receipt
  • CDC and passport: copies of the relevant pages, with originals for verification
  • Original sea service letters from the owner or RPSL agency, in the format required by your MMD's current notice
  • Completed Training Record Book / TAR with Master's and company sign-offs
  • Pre-sea course certificate and Second Mate (FG) competency course certificate
  • Modular and simulator course certificates required for the grade
  • Valid medical fitness certificate from a DGMA-approved doctor

The exact checklist, and whether you need to visit in person, varies between MMDs and changes over time. Each MMD publishes a monthly notice. Follow your MMD's latest notice exactly rather than relying on a senior's old list.

Booking and sitting the written exams

Written and oral seats are booked online through the portal named in the current DGMA nautical examination notice. Check dgma.gov.in (Nautical Wing → Examination Notices) for booking windows, booking limits and helpdesk details.

Points to know:

  • Booking windows can be short. Read each notice as soon as it is published.
  • If your profile locks or a payment fails, contact the helpdesk listed on the current notice. Do not keep paying again.
  • Bring your admit card and your original CDC, passport or CoC as ID.

Published paper formats for Second Mate (FG) have covered these subjects:

  • Terrestrial and coastal navigation (chartwork, tides, sailings, chart corrections)
  • Celestial navigation
  • Bridge equipment and watchkeeping
  • Meteorology
  • Cargo handling and stowage (including basics of tanker, chemical, gas and dangerous goods cargoes)
  • Ship construction, stability, safety and environmental protection

Download the current paper format and syllabus from the DGMA examination section. Results are also published there.

The oral: what the MMD surveyor is checking

You can book the oral once you have passed the writtens. A nautical surveyor at the MMD conducts it, following the DGMA oral syllabus for Second Mate (FG) function by function. The examiner is not checking whether you can recite rules word for word. They want to know whether they would trust you alone on the bridge at 0200.

Typical oral-style questions:

  • COLREG: "You are stand-on, the other vessel isn't altering, range closing. Walk me through Rules 17 and 34." Expect lights and shapes on a board or model, and restricted visibility under Rule 19.
  • Watchkeeping: "What do you check at handover? When do you call the Master?" Answer from your company's standing orders and STCW Code Section A-VIII/2.
  • Navigation: "How did you check gyro error on your last ship? What is the safety contour on ECDIS, and how did you set it?"
  • Emergencies: man overboard, steering failure, blackout on the bridge, fire. Give your actions in order.
  • Cargo: what you did on your own ship type, its main hazards and the relevant code (IMSBC, IMDG, IBC, IGC).
  • Function 3: MARPOL record keeping, LSA/FFA checks, and stability basics such as GM and free surface effect.

If you fail the oral, expect to rebook in a later cycle. Ask the surveyor which areas let you down, and work on those first.

Common rejections and how to avoid them

Most delays happen at the assessment stage, not in the exam hall:

  • TRB/TAR gaps: unsigned tasks, missing Master's or company sign-off, or pages that don't match your sea service dates.
  • Sea service letters: original letters missing, not in the format your MMD requires, no vessel IMO number or GT, or dates that don't match your CDC entries.
  • Sea time shortfall: periods such as dry-dock, lay-up, leave or non-seagoing service may not count in full. Check the current DGMA sea-time rules before you apply.
  • Expired documents: medical, passport or STCW certificates running out between assessment and the oral.
  • Name or date-of-birth mismatches between INDoS, CDC, passport and course certificates.
  • Course certificates from an institute or course not approved for the grade, or not uploaded to your INDoS record.
  • Old checklists: using a senior's list from three years ago instead of this month's MMD notice.

Worked scenario: a DNS cadet signing off

Ravi signs off after his final contract as a deck cadet, believing he has 18 months of sea time.

  1. At home: he adds up the CDC dates for each vessel and finds a short gap, because one sign-off was entered at the agent's office rather than on board. He asks the company for corrected sea service letters straight away.
  2. TRB: he finds two tasks the Master has not signed and gets them signed through the company's training department.
  3. Post-sea: he joins the Second Mate (FG) competency course at a DGMA-approved institute and completes his outstanding modular courses during it.
  4. Assessment: he applies on e-Samudra, generates the application form and pays online. Following the current monthly notice, he submits originals and copies at his MMD.
  5. Writtens: after approval, he books his papers through the portal named in the current DGMA examination notice as soon as the window opens. He sits the papers and checks his results on dgma.gov.in.
  6. Oral: he books the oral and revises from his own ship's procedures and real incidents. He passes on the second attempt after working on emergency procedures.
  7. Before applying for his CoC: he checks that his medical and other certificates are still valid, and that he holds a valid GMDSS GOC, which is needed alongside the Second Mate (FG) CoC. He checks the current DGMA requirement.
Key points
  • Second Mate (FG) is the STCW II/1 officer of the watch CoC for ships of 500 GT or more; it has no Phase I/II split.
  • The Director-General of Shipping is now the Director-General of Maritime Administration (DGMA) under the Merchant Shipping Act, 2025.
  • Eligibility assessment goes through e-Samudra, and the MMD then checks your documents.
  • Written and oral seats are booked online through the portal named in the current DGMA examination notice on dgma.gov.in.
  • DGMA lists DNS with 18 months of sea time and B.Sc (Nautical Science) with 12 months; the route for deck ratings is being revised.
  • Most rejections come from TRB gaps, sea service letters and mismatched documents, not from the exam itself.
  • Always work from the current MMD monthly notice and DGMA circulars, not old checklists.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a Phase I and Phase II for the Second Mate (FG) exam?

No. Second Mate (FG) is a single grade with written papers and an oral. The Phase I/II split applies to Chief Mate (FG).

Which portal do I use to apply for the Second Mate exam in India?

You apply for eligibility assessment on e-Samudra (e-governance.dgshipping.gov.in). Written and oral seats are booked online through the portal named in the current DGMA nautical examination notice. Check dgma.gov.in for booking windows, booking limits and helpdesk details.

How much sea time do I need for Second Mate (FG)?

DGMA's Career as a Nautical Officer page lists 18 months for DNS cadets and 12 months for B.Sc (Nautical Science) cadets, both with a completed Training Record Book. Check the current page before applying. STCW sets the minimum and also requires at least six months of supervised bridge watchkeeping. Check exactly which periods count before you apply.

Can a deck rating become Second Mate (FG)?

Yes, but DGMA has been revising the route for deck ratings to Second Mate (FG). Read the latest Examination, Assessment and Certification circulars on dgma.gov.in before enrolling in any course.

Why was my eligibility assessment sent back?

The usual reasons are unsigned TRB tasks, missing original sea service letters, name or date mismatches between documents, and expired medicals or certificates. Fix every point the MMD raises at once, then resubmit following the current monthly notice.

Test yourself

Under which STCW regulation is the Second Mate (FG) certificate issued?

Officers in charge of a navigational watch on ships of 500 GT or more are certificated under STCW Regulation II/1. II/2 covers Master and Chief Mate, and II/3 covers ships under 500 GT.

Where do Indian candidates apply for Second Mate (FG) eligibility assessment?

You apply for eligibility assessment on e-Samudra, and the MMD then verifies your documents.

Which grade has a two-phase written exam in India?

Chief Mate (FG) is split into Phase I and Phase II. Second Mate (FG) is not.

Under STCW, at least how much supervised bridge watchkeeping must a cadet on an approved training programme complete within their sea service?

STCW Regulation II/1 requires at least six months of bridge watchkeeping under supervision, in addition to the overall seagoing service requirement.

What is the most reliable source for the current document checklist for your assessment?

MMDs publish monthly notices and DGMA issues circulars that change requirements. Only these are authoritative.
