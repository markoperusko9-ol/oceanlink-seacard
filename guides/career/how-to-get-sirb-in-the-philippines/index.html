How to Get Your SIRB (Seaman's Book) in the Philippines: SRB and SID Explained

Quick answer

MARINA no longer issues new Seafarer's Identification and Record Books (SIRB). Two documents have replaced the book: the Seafarer's Record Book (SRB), which records your sea service, and the Seafarer's Identity Document (SID), your identity document under ILO Convention 185. You apply for both through MARINA's SID/SRB online appointment system, then appear in person at your chosen MARINA office with your original documents. The rules in force are in MARINA Memorandum Circular MD-2024-01.

SIRB, SRB and SID: what you are actually applying for

Most Filipino seafarers still say "SIRB" or "seaman's book". Your manning agency will probably use the same words. The document has changed, though, and you need to know the current names before you book.

  • SIRB (Seafarer's Identification and Record Book) was the old combined book. It served as both your identity document and your record of sea service. MARINA stopped issuing it in 2019.
  • SRB (Seafarer's Record Book) is the record-book part. MARINA describes it as the document formerly known as the SIRB. Your ships, rank, and sign-on and sign-off entries go in here, certified by the Master.
  • SID (Seafarer's Identity Document) is a separate identity document. It follows the ILO Seafarers' Identity Documents Convention (Revised), 2003 (No. 185).

The split was first made under MARINA Circular MD-2019-01. The rules now in force are in MARINA Memorandum Circular No. MD-2024-01. It sets out the policies and standards for issuing, revoking and reissuing the SRB and SID.

If you search "how to get SIRB" today, what you actually book in MARINA's system is an SRB application, a SID application, or both. That is the most common point of confusion.

Who needs an SRB/SID, and when

In general, Filipino seafarers working on commercial ships of 35 GT and above need the record book. For fishing vessels the threshold is 50 GT and above. This applies whether you serve on foreign-going or domestic trade. Cadets going for shipboard training also need an SRB before boarding.

Smaller craft come under a different document. MARINA Circular MD-2024-02 covers the Seafarer's Identification Book (SIB) for seafarers on small Philippine-registered ships and fishing vessels operating in Philippine waters. Check the circular for the exact tonnage limits. If you work on small domestic boats, confirm which document applies to you before you book.

What this means in practice:

  • First-time applicant (cadet or new rating): apply for the SRB. Your school or manning agency will tell you whether you also need the SID for your first contract.
  • Holder of an old SIRB that is still valid: you can keep using it until it expires. On renewal you move to the SRB (and the SID if you need it).
  • Officer moving to foreign-going service: most principals now expect both documents. Check with your manning agency what they need for the flag and trading area of the ship.

Documents to prepare

The exact checklist depends on your transaction type. The online system shows it to you after you choose the transaction, and that checklist is the one that counts. Bring your originals and photocopies in case they are asked for.

Transaction Typical documents
New SRB (rating or cadet) PSA birth certificate (on security paper) or Philippine passport; valid NBI clearance; Basic Training (BT/BST) certificate from a MARINA-accredited institution; cadets also need school documents such as the transcript of records or a shipboard training endorsement; printed appointment confirmation; proof of payment (if the system charges a fee)
New SID Valid SIRB or SRB (original); printed appointment confirmation; proof of payment
Renewal Original expired or expiring SIRB/SRB; valid NBI clearance; Basic Training certificate; PSA marriage contract (married women using married name); documentary stamp if required; printed appointment confirmation; proof of payment. Follow the in-system checklist
Lost or damaged Notarised affidavit of loss or damage; the damaged book if you still have it; supporting ID

Some transactions also need a documentary stamp. The checklist in the system will tell you if yours does.

MARINA Advisory 2022-06 made the first-ever SRB free of charge for applicants who have never held a SIRB or seaman's book. Check the portal or the MARINA Citizen's Charter to see whether this still applies and what fee, if any, applies to your transaction.

Two points that catch people out:

  • Your name must match on every document. That means your PSA birth certificate, passport, NBI clearance and training certificates. A missing middle name or a misspelt surname is enough to stop the application.
  • Your BT certificate must be current. Older MARINA guidance asked for refresher evidence where the Basic Safety Training certificate was more than five years old. Make sure your refresher is done before you book.

Step-by-step: booking and appearing at MARINA

Applications go through MARINA's SID/SRB online appointment system (sidsrb.marina.gov.ph). Do not expect to be served as a walk-in.

  1. Register an account with a personal email address you will keep. Do not use your agency's email. Verify the account, then enter your personal details exactly as they appear on your passport or PSA birth certificate.
  2. Choose the transaction: new SRB, new SID, renewal, reprint, or reissuance for lost or damaged documents. Ashore and on-board applications are separate categories.
  3. Upload what the system asks for. For a SID, for example, this is usually a clear scan of the first page of your valid SIRB or SRB.
  4. Pick the MARINA office and a date. You can choose the Central Office, a regional office, or any other site the system offers.
  5. Pay, if a fee applies, using the reference number the system gives you and one of the payment channels it lists. MARINA Advisory 2022-06 made the first-ever SRB free for applicants who have never held a SIRB or seaman's book. Fees are set by MARINA and change from time to time. Check the amount, and whether the free first issuance still applies, on the portal or in the MARINA Citizen's Charter. Do not rely on a blog or a fixer's price list.
  6. Print the appointment confirmation and the payment proof (if you paid).
  7. Go in person on your appointment day with your originals and photocopies. Expect a document check and biometrics (photo, signature and fingerprints for the SID). An evaluator may also interview you to confirm a document is genuine.
  8. Collect the document following the release instructions you are given. Check every entry before you leave the counter.

Renewal, replacement and applying while on board

Validity. In 2016 MARINA extended the old SIRB's validity from five to ten years (MARINA Advisory 2016-18). Under the current system, the SRB is valid for ten (10) years from the date of issuance and the SID for five (5) years (MARINA). Check the expiry date printed in each document, because the two will usually expire on different dates.

Renew before your contract, not during it. Your manning agency will check that your book stays valid for the full contract period. If it does not, expect them to send you back to MARINA.

On board when the book expires. MARINA has rules for seafarers who are on board. Your manning agency or shipping company files the reissuance or replacement for you through the Online Appointment System, acting as your authorised representative. Start early and send the agency what it needs. This is usually scans of your current book, plus an affidavit if the book was lost.

Watch MARINA advisories. MARINA has issued advisories in the past that extended the validity of SIRB, SRB and SID for seafarers who could not get ashore, such as the pandemic-era extensions. Each advisory applies only to the people it names.

In March 2026, in light of tensions in the Middle East, MARINA issued a Notice to the Public. It reminded seafarers that reissuance of an expiring, lost or damaged SRB and/or SID while on board is applied for through the Online Appointment System. A duly authorised representative of the shipping company or manning agency files it (MD-2024-01, General Provisions Item 6). That notice does not extend validity.

Check the advisory list on marina.gov.ph before you assume any extension covers you.

Common reasons applications are held up or refused

  • Name or birth-date mismatch between the PSA birth certificate, passport, NBI clearance and BT certificate.
  • An NBI clearance with a "hit" that is not yet cleared, or a clearance that has expired.
  • A training certificate the evaluator cannot verify. This happens with certificates from an institution that is not MARINA-accredited, or certificates that are not in MARINA's records. Fake certificates can lead to refusal and revocation, and you could be charged.
  • Wrong transaction booked. A common example is booking "new" when you already hold a SIRB and should book a renewal or a SID.
  • Arriving without originals or without the printed confirmation and payment proof.
  • Arriving late or at the wrong branch. Slots are tied to the office you chose.
  • Pending cases. If you have an unresolved administrative or criminal case connected to your seafaring documents, MARINA may hold or revoke your record book and certificates until it is cleared.

Only MARINA issues the SRB and SID. Anyone who offers to "guarantee" a seaman's book, skip the appointment queue or supply training certificates without the training is running an illegal fixing scheme. You will be the one who loses the document.

Worked scenarios

Scenario 1: deck cadet, first ship next month. The sponsoring company has given you a training slot. Book a new SRB in the online system. Bring your PSA birth certificate, NBI clearance and BT certificate, plus the school or company documents that the checklist lists for cadets. Check with the company whether they also want a SID for the vessel's trading pattern. If they do, book the SID after the SRB is issued, because a SID application normally needs a valid record book.

Scenario 2: AB with an old SIRB expiring in eight months, joining for nine months. The book won't cover the contract, so your agency will likely not deploy you on it. Book a renewal now, which moves you to the SRB. Get the SID at the same time if your principal needs it.

Scenario 3: 3rd Officer whose book was lost at home. Execute a notarised affidavit of loss. Book a reissuance (lost), ashore, and bring the documents the in-system checklist asks for. Expect closer checks of your identity.

Scenario 4: Motorman on board when the SRB is about to expire. Tell the Master and the manning agency straight away. The agency files the on-board reissuance as your authorised representative. Check whether any current MARINA advisory extends validity for your ship's situation.

Practical tips before you book

  • Fix name discrepancies first, at the PSA or the DFA. MARINA will not correct your civil-registry data for you.
  • Keep colour scans of every page of your SRB, SID, passport and certificates in personal cloud storage.
  • Have the Master certify each sign-on and sign-off entry before you leave the ship. Gaps in your record book will cause trouble later when you apply for a certificate of competency.
  • Book early in your vacation. Slots at the busy Manila offices can fill up.
  • Use only official channels:
    • marina.gov.ph for circulars, advisories and the Citizen's Charter.
    • The SID/SRB appointment system for booking.
    • The DMW, not MARINA, for deployment matters such as contracts and OFW processing.
Key points
  • MARINA no longer issues new SIRBs; you now apply for the SRB (record book) and the SID (identity document).
  • The rules in force are in MARINA Memorandum Circular MD-2024-01.
  • Applications go through MARINA's SID/SRB online appointment system, followed by a personal appearance.
  • Core documents for a new SRB: PSA birth certificate or passport, valid NBI clearance, Basic Training certificate, appointment printout, and payment proof if a fee applies.
  • The SRB is valid for 10 years and the SID for 5 years; renew before you sign on so your book covers the whole contract.
  • If you are on board, the manning agency files for you as your authorised representative.
  • Check fees and validity extensions only on marina.gov.ph, never through fixers.

Frequently asked questions

Is SIRB the same as SRB?

Yes, in practice. The SRB is MARINA's current record book, formerly known as the SIRB. The identity function moved to a separate document, the SID. You can use a valid old SIRB until it expires, and you move to the SRB when you renew.

Can I walk in at MARINA to apply for a seaman's book?

Plan on not being able to. Applications go through MARINA's SID/SRB online appointment system. You book, pay if a fee applies, and then appear in person on the date you chose. Check the MARINA website for the current branches and booking rules.

Do I need both the SRB and the SID?

The SRB is the basic document for serving on ships covered by the MARINA rules. Whether you also need the SID depends on your deployment and your principal's requirements, so ask your manning agency before you book. A SID application normally needs a valid SIRB or SRB first.

How long is the seaman's book valid?

The SRB is valid for ten (10) years from the date of issuance and the SID for five (5) years (MARINA). Check the expiry date printed in each document. You can use an old SIRB that is still valid until it expires.

My SRB will expire while I'm on board. What do I do?

Tell the Master and your manning agency early. The agency can file an on-board reissuance through the Online Appointment System as your authorised representative. Also check MARINA's advisories, because MARINA has extended validity in the past for crews in particular situations.

Test yourself

A first-time rating opens the MARINA appointment system today to get a seaman's book. Which transaction should they book?

MARINA stopped issuing SIRBs in 2019. The record book is now the SRB, and the SID is a separate identity document (MARINA MC MD-2024-01).

Which body issues the SRB and SID to Filipino seafarers?

MARINA is the only issuing authority. Manning agencies can only file certain on-board applications as your authorised representative (MARINA MC MD-2024-01).

A seafarer's PSA birth certificate shows a middle name that is missing from his NBI clearance and BT certificate. What is the most likely result at MARINA?

Identity details must match across the supporting documents, and MARINA cannot correct civil-registry records. Fix them at the PSA or DFA first (MARINA SRB/SID requirements).

A seafarer works on a 20 GT Philippine-registered boat in domestic waters. Which document is most likely to apply?

MARINA MC MD-2024-02 covers the SIB for seafarers on small Philippine-registered ships and fishing vessels operating in Philippine waters. Check the circular for the exact tonnage limits.

An AB is on board and his SRB will expire before the contract ends. What is the correct course of action?

MARINA rules let seafarers on board apply through an authorised representative of the shipping company or manning agency, using the Online Appointment System. Only MARINA can extend or reissue the document (MARINA MC MD-2024-01, General Provisions Item 6).
