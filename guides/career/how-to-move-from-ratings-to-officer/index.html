How to Move from Ratings to Officer

Quick answer

Under STCW, a rating can qualify as an officer of the watch without a cadetship. You need at least 36 months of approved sea service (for engine ratings, 36 months of combined workshop training and seagoing service, at least 30 of them at sea in the engine department), including at least 6 months of supervised watchkeeping, plus the approved education, training and CoC examinations. In India, deck ratings can use the existing NWKO (NCV) route or the six-month competency course for the Second Mate (FG) CoC introduced by DGS Circular 26 of 2026, while Filipino and other seafarers must follow the scheme set by their own administration.

The STCW baseline: why 36 months matters

Every administration builds its own scheme, but all of them start from the same STCW minimum. If you understand this, you can understand any flag's rules.

Deck (Officer in Charge of a Navigational Watch, ships of 500 GT or more – Regulation II/1):

  • Minimum age 18.
  • Either 12 months of approved seagoing service as part of an approved training programme with a Training Record Book (the cadet route), or otherwise at least 36 months of approved seagoing service (the ratings route).
  • At least 6 months of bridge watchkeeping under the supervision of the master or a qualified officer, completed within that service.
  • Approved education and training that meets the competence standards in Section A-II/1, plus the GMDSS requirements of Chapter IV.

Engine (Officer in Charge of an Engineering Watch – Regulation III/1):

  • Minimum age 18.
  • Either 12 months of combined workshop skill training and approved seagoing service within an approved programme with a TRB, or otherwise 36 months of combined workshop training and seagoing service, of which at least 30 months must be seagoing service in the engine department.
  • At least 6 months of engine-room watchkeeping under the supervision of a qualified engineer officer.
  • Approved education and training to the Section A-III/1 standard.

The convention doesn't excuse ratings from any of the knowledge. You sit the same written and oral examinations as an ex-cadet. All the 36 months does is replace the structured cadet programme with longer practical experience.

India, deck side: NWKO (NCV) and the six-month competency course

In India, certification is run by the Directorate General of Maritime Administration (DGMA), formerly the Directorate General of Shipping. It was renamed in 2026 under Section 7 of the Merchant Shipping Act, 2025. Circulars are published on the DGMA website (dgma.gov.in) under DGS Circulars. Deck ratings now have two routes to the Second Mate (FG) CoC.

1. The established NWKO (NCV) route. This has long been the standard ratings route. The 2015 DGS TEAP consolidated document lists the Foundation Course and the NWKO (NCV) course for ratings making lateral entry as officers before the Second Mate (FG) exam. Course structure and durations have been revised more than once, so check the current DGMA circular for today's requirements rather than relying on what a senior told you years ago.

2. The six-month competency course (DGS Circular 26 of 2026). The Nautical Wing published this circular, and the course is due to start from July 2026. It is an additional pathway and does not replace NWKO (NCV). The headline eligibility is:

  • Deck ratings (and deck cadets) who have completed approved pre-sea training
  • At least 36 months of approved sea service on foreign-going ships
  • At least 6 months of supervised watchkeeping, recorded in an approved Training Record Book. DGMA has said candidates may use any approved deck cadet TAR book and complete its bridge watchkeeping section.

Once you complete the course and meet all other requirements, you become eligible for the Second Mate (FG) examination. Read the full circular and syllabus on dgma.gov.in before choosing an institute. Only approved institutes count.

Some company-run schemes combine GP Rating pre-sea training with structured shipboard training leading to a Second Mate (NCV) CoC, a near-coastal certificate. Know which certificate a scheme actually leads to before you sign up.

India, engine side: from motorman or fitter to MEO Class IV

The STCW alternative for engine ratings is 36 months of combined workshop training and sea service, with at least 30 months at sea in the engine department and 6 months of supervised engine-room watchkeeping. How India applies this to ratings (qualifying vessel power, how propelling time is counted, which bridging or competency courses are needed) is set out in DGMA's MEO Class IV examination rules and circulars. These have been amended several times.

Practical points that apply whatever the current detail:

  • Vessel power matters. STCW Regulation III/1 covers ships of 750 kW propulsion power or more. Check DGMA's current MEO Class IV rules for how time on lower-powered vessels is counted.
  • Propelling time is not calendar time. Time alongside or at anchor may not count fully. Keep your own record of propelling hours from the engine log.
  • Watchkeeping must be documented. Hours spent on maintenance work in the workshop are not supervised watchkeeping. Ask the chief engineer early to put you in the watch and sign it off.

Check the current MEO Class IV eligibility table on dgma.gov.in for your category. If it isn't clear, put a written query to the Mercantile Marine Department (MMD) before paying for a course.

Filipino seafarers and other administrations

In the Philippines, MARINA issues and revalidates STCW certificates. It sets the requirements for upgrading from rating to OIC-NW or OIC-EW, including any course, assessment and sea service conditions, through its STCW circulars. DMW deals with deployment and employment matters, not CoCs. Check the current MARINA STCW circular for your route and make sure your training centre is MARINA-accredited for that course.

Other administrations (for example the UK MCA) run their own ratings-to-officer schemes, each with its own sea time split, training record book and exams. Whatever the flag:

  • Your national CoC comes from your own administration. A Panama, Liberia or Marshall Islands endorsement is issued on the strength of that CoC and does not replace it.
  • Sea time is assessed by the administration issuing the CoC, using its rules on vessel type, tonnage or power, and documents.
  • Never assume a rule from one country applies in another.

Step-by-step plan: a worked scenario

Scenario: Ravi finished approved GP Rating pre-sea training and has sailed 22 months as OS and AB on foreign-going bulk carriers. He wants the Second Mate (FG) CoC.

  1. Count only approved, verifiable time. Ravi adds up sign-on and sign-off dates from his CDC. Twelve days in port waiting for a vessel don't count. Neither does a short stint on a harbour craft unless the rules allow it.
  2. Start the watchkeeping record now. He needs at least 6 months of supervised bridge watchkeeping, and the six-month competency course requires it to be recorded in an approved TRB. He asks the master for a TRB and to be put on the 8–12 with the third officer for real lookout and helm duties.
  3. Get the TRB signed every contract. Entries are signed and stamped on board by the supervising officer and the master. They are not filled in at home before the exam.
  4. Collect testimonials as he goes. Each sign-off gets a master's testimonial, and the sea service is recorded in the CDC.
  5. Keep STCW courses and medical valid. Basic safety courses, the medical fitness certificate and passport/CDC validity must line up with the application.
  6. At 36 months, pick the route. He compares the NWKO (NCV) route with the six-month competency course on eligibility, approved institutes and timelines, using the current circulars.
  7. Complete the required courses. For the OIC-NW CoC this includes the modular and simulator courses and the GMDSS GOC.
  8. Apply for the exams through the DGMA e-governance system and sit the written exams and MMD orals.

The common mistake is to start step 2 at month 34. Do it from the first contract as AB.

Documents and the reasons applications get rejected

Typical documents (the exact list is in the current DGMA or MARINA notice):

  • CDC or seaman's book (SIRB in the Philippines) with all sign-on and sign-off entries
  • Sea service testimonials signed by the master or chief engineer
  • Completed and signed Training Record Book, where required (for example, India's six-month competency course)
  • Pre-sea training certificate (e.g. GP Rating)
  • Required STCW and modular course certificates, valid
  • Medical fitness certificate from an approved doctor
  • Passport, INDoS number (India) and academic certificates

Common rejections:

Problem How to avoid it
Name or date of birth differs between CDC, passport and certificates Correct the record before applying
Sea service not in CDC or not verifiable Insist on proper CDC entries at every sign-off
Watchkeeping not recorded, or TRB unsigned Get the TRB signed on board every contract
Time on non-qualifying or non-trading vessels counted as full time Check the reduced rates and caps in the circulars
Expired STCW courses or medical Track every expiry date
Course done at a non-approved institute Check the approved-institute list on the official portal
Fake or bought testimonials This can lead to debarment. Don't do it

Oral exam: what examiners look for in a former rating

Examiners generally welcome former ratings because they know their way round a ship. The weak spots are usually theory and officer-level judgement, so expect questions that check you have made the jump from doing to deciding:

  • Rules of the Road: "A vessel is detected by radar alone, fine on your starboard bow, bearing steady, in restricted visibility (not in sight). What do you do?" A former helmsman must answer as the OOW. Answer under Rule 19, avoiding an alteration to port for a vessel forward of the beam (Rule 19(d)(i)), not with Rule 15 crossing logic.
  • Watchkeeping: "When would you call the master?" Know your standing orders logic and STCW watchkeeping principles.
  • Stability and cargo: GM, free surface effect, and why you check the loading computer instead of trusting the ballast pump running hours.
  • Engine candidates: fault-finding in a logical order, with the safety reason behind every step. For example, why you don't open a crankcase door immediately after an oil mist alarm.

Examiners notice when a candidate uses real shipboard examples and stays honest about what he didn't do. It goes down well. Don't bluff.

Key points
  • STCW allows ratings to qualify as watchkeeping officers with at least 36 months of approved sea service instead of a 12-month structured cadet programme.
  • You need at least 6 months of supervised watchkeeping. India's six-month course requires it to be recorded in an approved TRB.
  • Engine ratings need at least 30 of the 36 months as seagoing service in the engine department.
  • Indian deck ratings can choose NWKO (NCV) or the six-month competency course under DGS Circular 26 of 2026.
  • Filipino seafarers follow MARINA's STCW circulars; other flags set their own schemes.
  • Most rejections come from unverifiable sea time, unsigned TRBs and mismatched personal details.
  • Always check the current circular on the official portal; course structures change.

Frequently asked questions

Can a GP rating become a Second Mate in India?

Yes. Deck ratings with approved pre-sea training and at least 36 months of approved foreign-going sea service, including at least 6 months of supervised watchkeeping, can work towards the Second Mate (FG) CoC through NWKO (NCV). They can also take the six-month course in DGS Circular 26 of 2026, which requires the watchkeeping to be recorded in an approved TRB. Check the full eligibility in the current circulars on dgma.gov.in.

Does my time as OS count towards the 36 months?

Approved seagoing service in a deck capacity generally counts, provided it is on qualifying vessels and properly recorded in your CDC and testimonials. Service on non-trading or smaller vessels may be counted at reduced rates or capped. The administration issuing your CoC decides, so check its current sea time rules.

Do I need a Training Record Book as a rating?

For the Indian six-month competency course, yes: the 6 months of supervised watchkeeping must be recorded in an approved TRB, and DGMA has said any approved deck cadet TAR book may be used. Even where a TRB isn't strictly required, a properly signed watchkeeping record makes verification far easier.

Can engine ratings like motorman or fitter become engineers?

Yes. STCW Regulation III/1 allows 36 months of combined workshop training and seagoing service, with at least 30 months at sea in the engine department and 6 months of supervised engine-room watchkeeping. The exact Indian or Philippine requirements, such as courses, vessel power and propelling time, are in the current DGMA or MARINA rules.

Will a Panama or Liberia endorsement let me sail as officer without a national CoC?

No. A flag state endorsement recognises a CoC already issued by your own administration. You must first hold the national OIC-NW or OIC-EW certificate.

Test yourself

Under STCW Regulation II/1, what minimum seagoing service applies to a candidate NOT following an approved cadet training programme?

Regulation II/1 requires 12 months within an approved programme with a TRB, or otherwise at least 36 months of approved seagoing service. (STCW Regulation II/1)

How much supervised bridge watchkeeping must an OIC-NW candidate have completed?

STCW requires at least 6 months of bridge watchkeeping under the supervision of the master or a qualified officer during the required service. (STCW Regulation II/1)

For the engine ratings route under Regulation III/1, how much of the 36 months must be seagoing service in the engine department?

The alternative route is 36 months of combined workshop training and seagoing service, of which at least 30 months must be at sea in the engine department. (STCW Regulation III/1)

What is the status of the six-month competency course under DGS Circular 26 of 2026?

DGMA introduced it as an additional pathway to the Second Mate (FG) CoC for deck ratings and deck cadets, alongside the existing NWKO (NCV) route. (DGS Circular 26 of 2026)

Which seafarer is most likely to have the sea time application rejected?

Supervised watchkeeping must be verifiable. Under DGS Circular 26 of 2026 it must be recorded in an approved TRB, and unsigned entries cannot be verified. (DGS Circular 26 of 2026)
