Fake Shipping Agents: Warning Signs and How to Report Them

Quick answer

A real manning agency holds a current licence you can check yourself: an RPSL listed on the DG Shipping website for Indian seafarers, or a DMW licence for Filipino seafarers, and under MLC 2006 a licensed agency should not charge you for finding you a job. Check the licence on the official portal before you hand over money, a passport or original documents. If you have been cheated, report it to DG Shipping or DMW and file a police complaint, and keep every chat, receipt and bank transfer record as evidence.

Why a fake agent can cost you more than money

Most cadets and junior ratings think the worst case is losing the money they paid. It is often worse than that. In its December 2025 advisory to seafarers (DGS Circular 55 of 2025), DG Shipping lists the problems reported by people recruited through unauthorised agents:

  • No support after accidents, injuries or medical emergencies, or if you are left stranded abroad.
  • Sea service that does not count. DG Shipping says sea service gained through unregistered agents is invalid and cannot be uploaded to the DGS e-governance system. That can wipe out months of time you were counting towards a CoC.
  • Problems at immigration. You risk being stopped at immigration if your Form-1 or e-Migrate reference (ARN) is fake or does not match.
  • No compensation or insurance cover, and wage disputes with nobody accountable.
  • Limited help from DG Shipping. It has limited legal reach over cases that went through unauthorised channels.

On the Philippine side, the Department of Migrant Workers (DMW) has warned that illegal recruiters are pretending to work for well-known licensed manning agencies on social media. They then ask for bank transfers, often described as visa fees.

Red flags: how fake agents usually operate

No single sign proves fraud, but two or three together should stop you.

  • They ask for money to get you a ship. Under MLC 2006, Regulation 1.4 and Standard A1.4, licensed recruitment and placement services must not charge seafarers, directly or indirectly, for recruitment, placement or finding them a job. The narrow exceptions are personal documents such as the national medical certificate, seafarer's book and passport. Visa costs are not one of the exceptions. Treat any 'job fee', 'joining fee', 'security deposit' or 'visa processing' payment as a warning sign.
  • Payment goes to a personal account. That includes UPI, GCash or a bank transfer to an individual rather than a company.
  • Recruitment happens only on WhatsApp, Telegram or Facebook. There is no office you can visit, or the 'office' is a hotel, café or training centre.
  • They use a real agency's name but their own phone number. The licence number and logo may be genuine and still be stolen.
  • They promise things a real company can't. Examples are a guaranteed ship for a fresher with no sponsorship, 'CDC entries' or 'sea time' for sale, or promotion without the required CoC.
  • They want your original passport, CDC or certificates, supposedly 'for processing'.
  • The paperwork is vague. There is no vessel name or IMO number, no draft Seafarer Employment Agreement (SEA) before you travel, or a contract that names a different company from the one that recruited you.
  • They tell you to travel on a tourist or visit visa and say you will 'join a ship there'.
  • The licence is 'under renewal'. That is a well-known excuse. Check the status yourself.

DG Shipping has also issued circulars against RPSL holders selling or sharing their licence with private manning agents, and against forged CDC entries. A licence number on a letterhead proves nothing by itself.

Indian seafarers: step-by-step RPSL check

Do this before you send any documents or money.

  1. Get the agency's exact legal name and RPSL number in writing. Numbers look like RPSL-MUM-162xxx or RPSL-KOL-0xx, but the format alone proves nothing. Check the list.
  2. Check the DG Shipping website. DGS publishes a live list that sorts RPSL companies into Valid, Invalid and Temporarily Blocked. Circular 55 of 2025 tells seafarers and parents to check the current status before any contact, and not to deal with any agency that is Invalid or Temporarily Blocked.
  3. Call the agency using the number on the official record, not the number in the WhatsApp message. Ask whether the person recruiting you works for them.
  4. Ask for vessel details: name, IMO number, flag and the shipowner or manager. Check that the ship exists and is trading. A free Equasis account will show this. Also check that the vessel is not on DG Shipping's list of vessels restricted or banned from employing Indian seafarers.
  5. Ask for a copy of the SEA before you sign. Read it against MLC requirements (see the related SEA guide).
  6. Before you fly, check your Form-1 and e-Migrate ARN. For a foreign-flag vessel you need a printed Form-1 issued by a valid RPSL company. Form-1A covers Indian seafarers on Indian-flag vessels (RPSL or Indian shipowner), and Form-1B covers non-seafarer categories. Immigration will reject a form that is altered, handwritten or unofficial. With a fake or mismatched ARN, you risk being stopped at immigration.
  7. After you join, check whether your sign-on appears on your DGS e-governance profile. If nothing appears, ask questions straight away.

The DGS website addresses have changed recently. Circulars currently appear on both dgshipping.gov.in and dgma.gov.in. Always reach them through the official government domain, never through a link someone sends you.

Filipino seafarers: checking with DMW

Since DMW took over from POEA, manning agency licensing and anti-illegal recruitment enforcement sit with DMW. MARINA handles certificates and training, not agency licences.

  1. Search the agency's exact name in the licensed agency listing on the DMW website, dmw.gov.ph, and check that its status is current.
  2. Check that the job matches an approved job order. That means the same agency, principal, position and vessel or jobsite.
  3. Check the person, not just the company. Get the agency's contact details from DMW records and ask whether the recruiter is an authorised representative. Scammers often borrow the name of a large, well-known manning agency.
  4. Be wary of any request for money by bank transfer, including so-called visa fees. DMW has flagged recruiters who use licensed agencies' names on social media to collect such payments.
  5. If the agency is not listed, you can ask DMW to confirm in writing whether the entity is licensed.

Under Philippine law, recruiting without a DMW licence is illegal recruitment and a criminal offence. A licensed agency can also break the rules, for example by overcharging or misrepresenting a job.

How to report a fake agent

First, gather your evidence. Don't delete anything.

  • Screenshots of chats and adverts, with phone numbers, profile links and dates visible
  • Bank, UPI or remittance receipts, plus the account name and number you paid
  • Offer letters, contracts, Form-1 copies, tickets and visa documents they gave you
  • Names of anyone else who was cheated, if they are willing to give statements

Philippines

  • Report to the DMW Anti-Illegal Recruitment unit. DMW advisories have given airtipinfo@dmw.gov.ph and the unit's official Facebook page as reporting channels. Check dmw.gov.ph for the current contacts.
  • You can also go to the DMW main office or a regional office for help preparing a criminal complaint.
  • If a licensed agency broke the rules, file an administrative complaint with DMW as well. That can lead to suspension or cancellation of its licence.

Worked scenarios

Scenario 1: a WhatsApp offer for a GP rating. A 'crewing manager' offers an OS berth on a foreign-flag bulk carrier and asks for a 'documentation charge' by UPI to a personal account. The letterhead shows a real RPSL number.

Action: Check the RPSL on the DGS list. Call the agency on the number from the official record. If they say they don't know this person, don't pay. Report the WhatsApp number and UPI ID to DG Shipping and through cybercrime.gov.in.

Scenario 2: 'Fly to Dubai on a visit visa and join there.' A cadet is told to travel on a visit visa and sign on to a small vessel at anchor. There is no Form-1 or ARN, and no SEA.

Action: Stop. Without a valid RPSL Form-1 and ARN, you risk being stopped at immigration at an Indian airport. Even if you get out, the sea service may never be accepted. DG Shipping has issued advisories on Iran, the Strait of Hormuz and the Persian Gulf (for example DGS Circulars 01, 09 and 10 of 2026), so check the latest Crew Branch circulars first.

Scenario 3: already on board, and things are wrong. Wages are unpaid, there's no contract copy, and your sign-on does not show on the DGS e-Gov profile.

Action: Quietly take photos of any contract, crew list and ship's documents you can see. Contact DG Shipping, your union and the nearest Indian Mission. In port, you can also ask the port state control authority or the port's seafarer welfare centre for help. Your MLC rights to wages and repatriation still apply against the shipowner.

Oral exam style: questions you should be able to answer

  • Q: Who may recruit Indian seafarers for foreign-flag ships? A: Only an entity holding a valid RPSL under the Merchant Shipping (Recruitment and Placement of Seafarers) Rules 2016, shown as Valid on the DG Shipping RPSL list.
  • Q: Which MLC regulation covers manning agencies, and what is its key protection? A: Regulation 1.4 and Standard A1.4. Services must be regulated or licensed. Standard A1.4.5(b) bars them from charging seafarers for recruitment or placement, except for the national medical certificate, seafarer's book and passport. Visas are the shipowner's cost.
  • Q: A junior tells you he paid an agent for his berth. What do you do? A: Record the facts. Tell the Master and the company crewing or HR department, and use the on-board complaint procedure under MLC Regulation 5.1.5. Help him report to DG Shipping or DMW. The company has its own MLC obligations here.
Key points
  • Check the agency's RPSL status (Valid, Invalid or Temporarily Blocked) on the DG Shipping website, or its licence on dmw.gov.ph, before any contact.
  • Under MLC Standard A1.4, licensed agencies must not charge you for recruitment or placement, and visa costs are not your expense.
  • A real licence number on a letterhead proves nothing. Call the agency on the number from the official record.
  • Sea service through an unregistered agent may be invalid and impossible to upload to the DGS e-governance system.
  • For a foreign-flag ship, carry a printed Form-1 issued by a valid RPSL company. With a fake or mismatched ARN, you risk being stopped at immigration.
  • Keep all chats, receipts and documents, and report to DG Shipping or DMW and the police or cybercrime portal.
  • Never hand over original passports, CDCs or certificates to a recruiter.

Frequently asked questions

How do I check if a shipping agency in India is genuine?

Ask for its exact name and RPSL number. Then check the live RPSL lists on the DG Shipping website, which show each company as Valid, Invalid or Temporarily Blocked. Contact the company using the details from the official record, not the recruiter's own number, to confirm that the recruiter really works for them.

Is it legal for a manning agency to charge me for a job on a ship?

Under MLC 2006, Standard A1.4, a licensed agency must not charge seafarers for recruitment or placement. Apart from personal documents such as your passport, seafarer's book and national medical certificate, the cost falls on the employer, and that includes visas. Details of national law can differ, so if you are asked to pay, check with DG Shipping or DMW before paying anything.

Will sea time on a ship joined through an unlicensed agent count for my CoC?

It may not. DG Shipping's Circular 55 of 2025 says sea service gained through unregistered agents is invalid and cannot be uploaded to the DGS e-governance system. Without that record, you will struggle to prove the sea time when you apply for your exam.

Where do Filipino seafarers report illegal recruitment?

Report to the Department of Migrant Workers' Anti-Illegal Recruitment unit, either through the contacts on dmw.gov.ph or at a DMW office. You can ask DMW to confirm in writing whether the entity is licensed. If a licensed agency broke the rules, you can also file an administrative complaint against its licence.

I already paid a fake agent online. What should I do first?

Report the payment right away. In India, use the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal or helpline 1930, or contact your bank, because fast reports give the best chance of freezing funds. Then file a police complaint and send the details to DG Shipping. Keep every screenshot and receipt.

Test yourself

Which document must an Indian seafarer carry, printed, when travelling to join a foreign-flag vessel through an RPSL company?

For a foreign-flag vessel, the seafarer must carry a genuine printed Form-1 issued by a valid RPSL company, and immigration rejects altered, handwritten or unofficial forms (DGS Circular 55 of 2025; DGS Form-1/1A/1B circular, 11/12/2025).

Under MLC 2006, who should normally bear the cost of a work visa for joining a ship?

Standard A1.4 lets seafarers be charged only for personal documents such as a passport. Visas are excluded and are the shipowner's cost (MLC 2006, Standard A1.4).

An agent shows you a letterhead with a real RPSL number. What is the best next step?

Licence numbers are often stolen or misused, so check the live status and confirm the recruiter with the company directly (DGS Circular 55 of 2025).

Which body licenses Philippine manning agencies and handles illegal recruitment complaints?

DMW took over POEA's functions, so it licenses manning agencies and runs anti-illegal recruitment enforcement, while MARINA deals with certification and training (DMW, dmw.gov.ph).

What does DG Shipping say about sea service obtained through an unregistered agent?

Such sea service is invalid and cannot be uploaded to the DGS e-governance system (DGS Circular 55 of 2025).
