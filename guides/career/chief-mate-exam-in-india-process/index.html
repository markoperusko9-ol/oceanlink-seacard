Chief Mate Exam in India: The Process from Sea Time to CoC

Quick answer

To get a Chief Mate (Foreign-Going) CoC in India you need a valid Second Mate (FG) CoC and at least 18 months of approved sea service after it was issued. That is more than the STCW minimum of 12 months. The exam is split into two phases: Phase I (course and exams) is open after at least 9 months of that service, and Phase II (course and exams) after at least 18 months. You apply online and sit the written and oral exams under the Directorate General of Maritime Administration (DGMA, formerly DG Shipping/DGS) through the Mercantile Marine Department (MMD). Always check the current notices on dgma.gov.in before you plan your leave.

What the Chief Mate CoC is and where the requirement comes from

The Chief Mate CoC is a management-level deck certificate. It is issued under STCW Regulation II/2. Section A-II/2 of the STCW Code sets the standard of competence, and the same standard covers both chief mates and masters of ships of 3,000 GT or more.

India applies this through its national STCW rules for seafarers and through orders and circulars from the Directorate General of Maritime Administration (DGMA, formerly the Directorate General of Shipping or DGS). The Directorate was renamed under the Merchant Shipping Act, 2025. Current orders are published on dgma.gov.in, and older notices may still be on dgshipping.gov.in.

In practice:

  • STCW sets the minimum. For a chief mate on ships of 3,000 GT or more, you must meet the OICNW requirements and have at least 12 months of approved seagoing service as OICNW.
  • India adds its own detail. DGMA decides:
    • which ships and voyages count
    • how sea time is verified
    • which courses you must hold
    • how the exam is split into phases
    • how the orals are conducted

For example, India requires 18 months of approved sea service for Chief Mate (FG), which is more than the STCW minimum of 12. - There are separate grades for Near-Coastal Voyages (NCV). This guide covers the Foreign-Going (FG) route.

When a senior or a coaching institute quotes a fixed rule ("you need X months" or "Phase I is allowed after Y"), check it against the current DGMA notice. These details have changed several times over the years.

Eligibility: sea time and what actually counts

Your sea service decides whether your application succeeds. STCW sets a 12-month minimum, but a DGS notice requires more for Chief Mate (FG) in India.

Indian sea-time thresholds (all counted after your Second Mate (FG) CoC was issued):

Stage Minimum approved sea service
Phase I course and exams 9 months
Phase II course and exams 18 months
Chief Mate (FG) CoC 18 months

Check the current DGMA notice in case these figures have been revised.

Before you apply, check yourself on these points:

  • Capacity: the time must be served as an officer in charge of a navigational watch, after your OICNW CoC was issued. Sea time as a cadet, trainee or rating does not count towards Chief Mate.
  • Type of service: only actual seagoing service counts. Leave, travel, shore standby and yard time are generally excluded or limited. DGMA sets exactly how port stays and dry-dock periods are treated, so check the current rules.
  • Ship size and trade: the tonnage and voyage limits of the ships you served on must match the certificate you want (FG or NCV, and the 3,000 GT threshold).
  • Proof: every voyage must appear in your CDC. It must be backed by a sea service testimonial from the company and, where the system requires it, verified online.
  • Foreign-flag service: this is normally accepted when the discharge and testimonial are in order. Check how DGMA currently verifies service on foreign-flag ships.

Worked scenario: Ravi got his Second Mate CoC in March. His service since then:

  • 7 months as 3rd Officer on a bulk carrier
  • 3 months' leave
  • 1 month in a dry dock
  • 6 months as 2nd Officer

His leave does not count. The dry-dock month may count only in part, depending on current DGMA rules. He has 13 months of clear service. That meets the 9-month requirement for Phase I, but he must reach 18 months before Phase II and the CoC. He should get the dry-dock month confirmed before relying on it.

Courses and documents to have ready

DGMA publishes the list of mandatory courses for each grade, and the list gets revised. Expect to need:

  • Phase I and Phase II competency courses. The DGS scheme is split into Phase I (course and exams, after at least 9 months of approved sea service after Second Mate) and Phase II (course and exams, after at least 18 months). Each phase has a DGS-approved competency course at an approved maritime training institute (MTI). Check the current notice for the exact course length and content.
  • Management-level STCW courses. These include Medical Care (STCW Code A-VI/4), plus the other management-level courses in the current DGMA notice.
  • Valid basic and advanced safety certificates. Basic Safety Training and Advanced Fire Fighting must be in date, so do any refreshers before you apply.
  • A valid GMDSS GOC, as required by DGMA.

Document checklist (keep scanned copies and the originals):

Document Common problem
Passport, CDC, INDoS Name or date of birth spelled differently across them
OICNW CoC Wrong issue date entered on the portal
Sea service testimonials Not signed, unstamped, or dates differ from CDC
STCW course certificates Expired, or not verified online
Medical fitness certificate (DG-approved doctor) Expires before the oral date
Course completion certificates from the MTI Not yet uploaded by the institute

Use the course names exactly as they appear on the DGMA list. A course with a similar name from an unapproved provider will not be accepted.

Step-by-step: from application to CoC

  1. Count your sea time from your CDC and testimonials, against the 9-month (Phase I) and 18-month (Phase II) thresholds. Leave a margin rather than applying on the exact day you reach a threshold.
  2. Check the current DGMA notices on dgma.gov.in for the exam scheme, phases, course list and fees. Older notices may still be on dgshipping.gov.in. The online seafarer platform has changed in recent years. Always use the link on the official website, never one forwarded on WhatsApp.
  3. Finish the Phase I competency course and the other mandatory courses at an approved MTI once you have 9 months of approved service. Make sure the MTI has uploaded the certificates to your profile. Do the Phase II course once you have 18 months.
  4. Apply online through your seafarer login on the current DGMA e-governance portal. Choose the grade or phase, upload your documents and pay the fee shown on the portal.
  5. Get your documents assessed. The MMD or DGMA checks your sea time and certificates. If they raise a query, answer it quickly and in full.
  6. Sit the written exams for each phase as set out in the current DGMA notice. The papers cover the management-level functions:
    • navigation
    • cargo handling and stowage
    • controlling the operation of the ship and care for persons on board
  7. Take the oral exam(s) with an MMD examiner once you are eligible. Follow the current DGMA portal instructions for booking.
  8. CoC is issued once every part is passed and your documents are valid. Check the printed details and the endorsement as soon as you receive it.

DGMA rules decide whether written and oral results can be carried forward, and for how long. Confirm this when you book, so that a failed attempt does not cancel parts you have already passed.

Common rejections and how to avoid them

  • Sea time short by days. Testimonials often count sign-on to sign-off differently from your own count. Apply only when you are clearly over the threshold for your phase.
  • Mismatched particulars. Your name, date of birth or INDoS number differs between passport, CDC and certificates. Get it corrected at the source before you apply.
  • Expired STCW certificates. BST or AFF refreshers lapse while you are on leave studying. Check every validity date against the date you expect to sit the oral, not the application date.
  • Unverified courses. The MTI has not uploaded your certificate, or the institute or course was not approved when you took it.
  • Missing testimonials. The company has since closed or changed its name. Get your testimonial when you sign off, not two years later.
  • Medical expiry. Your medical certificate runs out partway through the process.
  • Agents promising "settings". Results cannot be arranged. Anyone offering this is committing fraud, and taking part can lose you your certificate.

Oral exam: what examiners look for

At management level the examiner wants decisions and responsibility, not textbook definitions. Expect follow-up questions that start with "and then what?"

Typical areas:

  • Stability: GM and free surface during ballast exchange, angle of loll versus list (and how to correct each), and damage stability basics.
  • Cargo: liquefaction of solid bulk cargoes and when you would refuse to load, grain heeling moments, tanker cargo safety and IMDG segregation.
  • Ship operation: your role under ISM, PSC detainable deficiencies, MARPOL record books, Ballast Water Management and enclosed space entry.
  • Emergencies: collision, grounding, fire in the hold, steering failure and abandoning ship. In each case say what you do as Chief Officer and what you report to the Master.
  • People: MLC hours of rest, handling a fatigued or injured crew member, and leadership.

Sample exchange:

Examiner: You are loading a cargo that should have a certificate showing its moisture content is below the TML (the transportable moisture limit), but you have doubts about it. What do you do?

Good answer:

  1. Stop or suspend loading, and inform the Master.
  2. Ask for the shipper's cargo declaration and moisture certificate under the IMSBC Code.
  3. If permitted and safe, do a can test as a check.
  4. Record everything.
  5. Do not resume loading until the Master is satisfied. Bring in the P&I club or the company if needed.

Tips:

  • Answer in order: safety, then reporting, then records.
  • Admit it when you don't know something rather than bluffing.
  • Back your answer with the right instrument (SOLAS, MARPOL, the IMSBC Code, ISM).

Practical tips from the examining side

  • Study while you are at sea. Go through the loading computer, the stability booklet, the SMS emergency checklists and the PSC history of your own ship. Real examples come across far better in an oral than memorised notes.
  • Keep a sea-time file. Scan your CDC pages, testimonials, course certificates and medical, and name each file by date.
  • Plan your leave around validity dates and phase thresholds. It is a waste to pass the orals and then have the CoC held up by a lapsed refresher.
  • Read every new DGMA circular on dgma.gov.in before you book. The scheme for phases, papers and courses gets revised.
  • Make sure you are actually ready. The MMD examiner is judging whether you can take over as Chief Officer tomorrow.
Key points
  • Chief Mate CoC is issued under STCW Regulation II/2, and the standard of competence is in STCW Code Section A-II/2.
  • STCW minimum is 12 months as OICNW, but India requires 18 months of approved sea service after the Second Mate (FG) CoC.
  • The Indian scheme has two phases: Phase I (course and exams) after 9 months, and Phase II (course and exams) after 18 months.
  • DGMA (formerly DG Shipping) sets the Indian detail, so check the current notices on dgma.gov.in.
  • Apply only through the official DGMA online portal, never through links or agents on WhatsApp.
  • Most rejections come from short sea time, mismatched particulars, expired certificates or courses that have not been uploaded.
  • Management-level orals test decisions, reporting and responsibility, not just definitions.

Frequently asked questions

How much sea time do I need for the Chief Mate exam in India?

STCW sets a 12-month minimum, but India requires at least 18 months of approved sea service after your Second Mate (FG) CoC: 9 months for the Phase I course and exams, and 18 months for Phase II and the CoC. DGMA decides which service counts (for example dry-dock and port periods). Check the current DGMA notice and leave a margin above each threshold.

Can I take the Chief Mate written exam before completing my sea time?

Yes, under the phased scheme. The DGS scheme has Phase I (course and exams) after at least 9 months of approved sea service after Second Mate, and Phase II (course and exams) after at least 18 months. Check the current notice on dgma.gov.in before you book a course or exam, as the scheme has been revised before.

Which courses are mandatory for the Chief Mate (FG) CoC?

Expect the DGS-approved Phase I and Phase II competency courses, Medical Care (STCW A-VI/4) and the other management-level courses in the current DGMA notice. You also need valid BST and AFF and a valid GMDSS GOC as required by DGMA. Download the current list and make sure your MTI is approved for each course.

Does sea time on a foreign-flag ship count for Chief Mate in India?

Generally yes, if it is properly recorded in your CDC and backed by a valid testimonial and any verification DGMA asks for. Problems usually come from missing or mismatched testimonials, so get yours from the company when you sign off.

What is asked in the Chief Mate oral exam?

Expect questions on stability, cargo work, ship operations under ISM, MARPOL and SOLAS, PSC, emergencies and managing people. The examiner wants to hear what you would do as Chief Officer, whom you would report to, and what records you would keep.

Test yourself

Under STCW, what is the minimum approved seagoing service as OICNW for a Chief Mate CoC on ships of 3,000 GT or more?

Regulation II/2 requires at least 12 months' approved seagoing service as OICNW for chief mate on ships of 3,000 GT or more. Masters need 36 months, or 24 months if at least 12 were served as chief mate. Note that India requires 18 months for Chief Mate (FG). Ref: STCW Regulation II/2; DGS notice.

Which sea time counts towards the Chief Mate requirement?

The service must be served as an OICNW, so cadet, trainee and leave periods do not count. Ref: STCW Regulation II/2, applied through DGMA rules.

In an oral exam, the examiner asks what slack tanks do to a ship's stability. Which answer is correct?

Free surface causes a virtual rise of G, so the effective GM is reduced. Centreline tanks still have free surface. Ref: STCW Code Table A-II/2 (stability competence).

Which part of the STCW Code sets the competence standard for Chief Mates and Masters of ships of 3,000 GT or more?

A-II/2 covers management-level deck officers. A-II/1 is for OICNW, A-II/4 is for watch ratings and A-VI/1 is basic safety training. Ref: STCW Code, Section A-II/2.

Where should you confirm the current Chief Mate exam scheme, course list and fees?

DGMA (formerly DGS) issues and revises these through official notices and its portal, and older or informal sources may be out of date. Ref: Directorate General of Maritime Administration, https://dgma.gov.in.
