Manning agencies in the Philippines: how to find and verify one

Quick answer

Only manning agencies licensed by the Department of Migrant Workers (DMW) may recruit Filipino seafarers for foreign ships. Before you send documents or money, check the agency's exact name on the DMW website's list of licensed agencies and confirm its licence is active. Confirm the principal and job with DMW or the agency's official office. Under MLC 2006, you must not be charged a recruitment or placement fee. DMW has repeatedly warned seafarers not to deal with unlicensed "consultancy" firms.

What a licensed manning agency is

In the Philippines, overseas jobs on ships are handled by manning agencies. The Magna Carta of Filipino Seafarers (Republic Act No. 12021, 2024) defines a manning agency as a person or company duly licensed by the Secretary of the DMW to recruit and place seafarers.

DMW was created by Republic Act No. 11641 and took over the licensing work of the former POEA. If an old ad or certificate says "POEA-licensed", check the agency again today under DMW.

Two other bodies are often confused with the agency:

  • MARINA issues your SIRB, COC and COP. It certifies you, but it does not find you a job. See MARINA and DMW basics.
  • The foreign principal is the shipowner or ship manager. The licensed agency recruits for its accredited principals.

"Offshore agency" means two different things

Filipino seafarers often search for an "offshore agency" when they mean any agency that sends crew to overseas, ocean-going ships. In the industry, "offshore" usually means the oil and gas sector: supply vessels, anchor handlers, drilling units and FPSOs. Those jobs often need extra training such as BOSIET/HUET or DP.

Either way, the rule is the same. The agency recruiting you must be DMW-licensed, whether the ship is a bulk carrier or an offshore supply vessel. See BOSIET and HUET for offshore-specific training.

How to verify a manning agency, step by step

  1. Get the exact registered name and office address in writing. Scammers use names that are one word off from a real agency.
  2. Search the DMW website yourself. Type dmw.gov.ph into your browser and open the list of licensed agencies. Do not use a link someone sends you on Messenger or WhatsApp.
  3. Check the status. The licence must be active, not suspended, cancelled, revoked or expired. Check that the address matches the office you were told to visit.
  4. Confirm the job. Ask which principal and vessel you are being recruited for, then confirm with DMW or the agency's official landline that the principal is accredited to that agency.
  5. Confirm the recruiter. A licensed agency does not authorise everyone who uses its name. Call the agency's official number and confirm the person contacting you works there.
  6. Keep evidence. Screenshot the DMW result with the date. Keep every receipt and message.

What you should never pay

The Philippines has ratified MLC 2006. Under Standard A1.4, paragraph 5(b), no fees or other charges for recruitment or placement may be borne by the seafarer, directly or indirectly. You pay only for your own national medical certificate, seaman's book (SIRB) and passport. Visa costs are paid by the shipowner.

Warning signs:

  • A "placement fee", "processing fee", "reservation fee" or "deposit" to guarantee a ship
  • Payment into a personal bank or e-wallet account instead of the company's official account
  • A "consultancy" or "training centre" that promises an overseas ship job. DMW has shut down unlicensed consultancy firms offering seafarer jobs abroad and warned seafarers to check its licensed-agency list.
  • Deployment on a tourist visa, or being told to say you are a tourist at the airport
  • No physical office, or an office address that does not match the DMW record

If you have been asked to pay or have already paid, report it to DMW. See also fake shipping agents: warning signs.

How to find agencies that hire for your rank

  • Start from the DMW list, then look at each agency's own website and official social pages for current openings. Job ads must still match a licensed agency.
  • Match the agency to your vessel type. Many agencies recruit for one or two principals. An agency staffing tankers will not help a bulk carrier AB with no tanker training.
  • Use a job board that shows the agency behind every posting. Ocean Link lists jobs with the agency that posted them, so you can check that agency on DMW before you apply.
  • Go in person with complete documents. Bring your SIRB, COC/COP, medical certificate and sea service records. See how crewing agencies shortlist candidates.

FAQ

How do I check if a manning agency in the Philippines is licensed?

Search the agency's exact registered name on the official DMW website's list of licensed agencies (type dmw.gov.ph yourself), and confirm that its licence is active and the address matches. Then confirm the principal and job with DMW or the agency's official office.

Is it legal for a manning agency to charge a placement fee to a seafarer?

No. The Philippines has ratified MLC 2006, and Standard A1.4 says recruitment and placement fees must not be charged to seafarers. You pay only for your own national medical certificate, seaman's book and passport.

What is the difference between MARINA and DMW?

MARINA certifies you: SIRB, COC and COP. DMW licenses the manning agencies and handles overseas employment, including contract processing for seafarers on foreign-flagged ships.

What does 'offshore agency' mean in the Philippines?

Many seafarers use it for any agency deploying crew to overseas, ocean-going ships. In the industry, 'offshore' usually means oil and gas vessels and units. In both cases the agency must be DMW-licensed.

Test yourself

Which government agency licenses manning agencies that recruit Filipino seafarers for foreign ships?

Under RA 12021, a manning agency is licensed by the Secretary of the DMW. MARINA issues certificates such as SIRB, COC and COP.

A recruiter asks you to pay a 'reservation fee' into a personal e-wallet to secure a ship. What should you do?

MLC 2006 prohibits charging seafarers for recruitment or placement, and payment to a personal account is a classic scam sign.

Which of these costs may a seafarer legitimately pay under MLC 2006, Standard A1.4?

The exceptions are the national medical certificate, the national seaman's book, and the passport or similar travel documents. Visa costs are borne by the shipowner.
