Questions to ask a crewing agency before joining

Quick answer

Before you talk about the job, check that the agency's licence shows as valid. In India that means the DG Shipping RPSL list, and in the Philippines the DMW list of licensed agencies. Then get written answers on who the shipowner is, the ship's details and flag, your contract terms, who pays for what, insurance and abandonment cover, and how you get home. Under MLC 2006, a licensed agency may not charge you any fee for recruitment or placement.

Step 1: Check the licence before you ask anything else

Interview the agency only after you have checked it. A smooth recruiter with a nice office tells you nothing. The licence status on the government's list is what counts.

India (RPSL): DG Shipping runs a live, continuously updated list of agents holding a Recruitment and Placement Services Licence. The list shows whether each licence is valid, invalid or temporarily blocked. DG Shipping has told seafarers and their families to check the live status before any contact. They should deal only with valid RPSL companies.

DG Shipping's website and RPSL module have recently changed. Use the official DGS site (dgshipping.gov.in / dgma.gov.in, Crew Division > RPSL) and check the latest DGS circulars.

Philippines: Manning agencies are licensed by the Department of Migrant Workers (DMW). Search the agency's exact registered name on the DMW list of licensed agencies. Check that the licence covers sea-based (manning) recruitment and that it hasn't expired, been suspended or been cancelled.

Other countries: Every state that has ratified MLC 2006 must regulate private recruitment and placement services under Regulation 1.4. Check with your national maritime administration or labour ministry.

What to check:

  • The registered company name matches the name on the letterhead, the offer letter and the bank account. Small differences matter.
  • Licence number and status (India: valid / invalid / temporarily blocked).
  • Office address. Visit if you can.
  • Whether the person contacting you actually works for that licensed company. Many scams use a real agency's name with a fake email or phone number.

Questions about the agency and the principal

Once the licence checks out, ask:

  1. What is your licence number? Can I see the certificate? A genuine agency won't mind.
  2. Who is the shipowner or ship manager? MLC requires the shipowner to be named, with an address, in your Seafarer Employment Agreement (SEA). A vague "European principal" is not an answer.
  3. Are you recruiting directly for this principal, or through another agency? A chain of agents usually means less accountability.
  4. Will you charge me anything? The correct answer is no recruitment or placement fee. MLC Standard A1.4 allows only narrow exceptions (see the fees section below).
  5. Who do my family contact while I'm on board, and how? MLC Standard A1.4 requires licensed agencies to ensure, as far as practicable, that families' enquiries are handled promptly and sympathetically and at no cost.
  6. How do I raise a complaint, and who handles it? Licensed agencies must have a complaint procedure and must look into complaints properly.

Questions about the ship, the contract and repatriation

Get these answers in writing, by email from the agency's official domain or on letterhead:

Ask Why it matters
Ship name and IMO number Lets you check the ship really exists, and her type and flag, on public databases
Flag state Decides which flag endorsement you need and which administration's rules apply
Ship type and trading area Tanker, gas or other ships need specific STCW Chapter V training. War-risk or high-risk areas may carry extra conditions
Contract length and any +/- tolerance Know your maximum time on board before you sign
Is there a CBA? Which one? A collective bargaining agreement can form part of your SEA
Joining port and travel arrangements Who books and pays for travel, and the visa (see the fees section below)
Repatriation terms MLC Regulation 2.5 gives you the right to repatriation at no cost to you, in the circumstances set out in the SEA and national law
Leave, working hours and hours of rest Paid annual leave must be written in the SEA (Standard A2.1.4). Hours of work and rest are covered by Regulation 2.3 and a posted table of shipboard working arrangements, so ask how watches and rest are organised

Ask for a draft SEA before you travel. MLC Regulation 2.1 says you must be able to examine the agreement and seek advice before you sign it. You must also get a signed original. Don't sign anything at the airport you haven't read. If something is unclear, ask a union or welfare body, or a senior officer you trust.

Fees, documents and who pays for what

This is where most disputes start, so be clear about the rule.

The MLC position (Standard A1.4, paragraph 5): a licensed agency must not charge the seafarer, directly or indirectly, any fee for recruitment, placement or providing employment. The only costs the seafarer can be asked to bear are:

  • the national statutory medical certificate
  • the national seafarer's book (CDC / SIRB)
  • a passport or similar personal travel document

Visa costs are expressly excluded. The shipowner pays for visas.

So be very wary of anyone asking for a "processing fee", "documentation charge", "security deposit" or "interview fee". The same goes for anyone insisting you do a course at one particular institute before they will "confirm" your ship.

Questions to ask about documents:

  • Which flag endorsements do I need, and who applies for them? It depends on the flag administration and the company. Some companies apply, others ask you to.
  • Which STCW courses must be valid for the whole contract, not just on the day I sign on?
  • Do I need a company-specific medical as well as my national medical certificate?
  • Do I need ship-type training (oil/chemical/gas tanker, IGF, ECDIS type-specific)? Who arranges it, and when?
  • India: Will the RPSL company issue the Form-I needed for immigration/e-Migrate clearance when you leave India to join a foreign-flag ship? DGS Circular 55 of 2025 says this must come only from a valid RPSL company. Revised Form-I/IA/IB were introduced by DGS Circular 56 of 2025.

Charges for courses, medicals and endorsements are set by the administrations and training providers and change regularly. Check the current figures on the official portal or with the institute, not with the recruiter.

Insurance, abandonment cover and welfare

Ask: Is the ship covered for MLC financial security, and which provider gives it?

Under MLC Standard A2.5.2 (abandonment) and Standard A4.2.1 (death and long-term disability), ships that need MLC certification must carry certificates from the financial security provider. These are usually issued by the P&I club. The certificates are posted on board where the crew can see them. Look for them on the crew notice board when you join.

Also ask:

  • Does the agency have insurance or an equivalent arrangement to compensate seafarers for money they lose if the agency or shipowner fails to meet its obligations under the SEA? MLC Standard A1.4 requires the national system to provide this kind of protection.
  • What medical care and sick pay apply if you are signed off sick abroad?
  • How do you get internet or other communication on board? Not a legal entitlement in every case, but worth knowing before a long contract.

If an agency can't name the P&I club, or seems not to have heard of the MLC certificates, that tells you how seriously they take crew welfare.

Worked scenario: the WhatsApp offer

A 3rd Officer gets a WhatsApp message offering an immediate join on a bulk carrier in West Africa. The offer letter is on a Gmail address, and there is a "visa and documentation fee" to pay to a personal bank account.

Working it through:

  1. Licence check: the company name in the letter is close to, but not the same as, an RPSL company listed as valid. Red flag.
  2. Contact check: the officer calls the real company's landline from the DG Shipping list. HR confirms they have no such vacancy and the person who messaged doesn't work there.
  3. Fee check: visa costs belong to the shipowner under MLC, and recruitment fees are banned. Second red flag.
  4. Ship check: the IMO number in the letter belongs to a ship of a different type and flag.
  5. Action: pay nothing and send no original documents. Report it to DG Shipping (India) or DMW (Philippines) and warn your batchmates.

Now compare a genuine offer. It comes from the official company domain, from a licensed agency, and names the shipowner, ship, IMO number, flag, contract length and joining port. It includes a draft SEA and does not ask for money.

Why agencies turn candidates down, and how to avoid it

Agencies screen you too. Common reasons an application stalls:

  • Documents expiring during the contract. Passport, CDC/SIRB, medical, STCW refreshers or the CoC itself running out mid-voyage.
  • Mismatched personal details. Name, date of birth or INDoS number not matching across your CDC, passport and certificates.
  • Missing ship-type training for tanker, gas or LNG-fuelled ships.
  • Sea service that can't be verified, or testimonials that don't match the CDC entries.
  • No US C1/D visa when the company trades to the US regularly. Many companies treat this as a requirement, but practice varies.
  • A CV that doesn't match. Ship types, tonnage or engine makes that don't line up with your discharge book.

Keep a spreadsheet of every document number and expiry date. Send clear scans. Answer questions about your experience honestly. Experienced crewing staff can spot padding, and so can examiners.

Oral-exam style: what an examiner might ask

Recruitment and the SEA can come up in oral exams on MLC and Merchant Shipping legislation. Be ready for:

  • Q: Which MLC regulation covers recruitment and placement, and what is its key protection? Regulation 1.4. You must have access to an efficient, regulated system for finding work on board without charge to you.
  • Q: Which costs may a seafarer lawfully bear? Only the national medical certificate, the national seafarer's book and a passport or similar travel document. Visas are excluded.
  • Q: As Master, how would you show PSC that abandonment cover is in place? By the financial security certificate under Standard A2.5.2, posted on board.
  • Q: What must the seafarer be given before signing the SEA? The chance to examine it and seek advice, then a signed original.
  • Q: In India, where is the licensing of manning agencies set out? The Merchant Shipping (Recruitment and Placement of Seafarers) Rules, 2016 (made under the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958; check DG Shipping for any rules updated under newer legislation), under which DG Shipping issues RPSLs.
Key points
  • Check the licence on the official list first: DG Shipping RPSL list (India) or the DMW licensed agency list (Philippines).
  • Under MLC Standard A1.4, a licensed agency may not charge you any recruitment or placement fee.
  • You can only be charged for the national medical certificate, seafarer's book and passport. Visas are the shipowner's cost.
  • Get the shipowner's name, ship name, IMO number, flag, contract length and joining port in writing.
  • Ask for the draft SEA in advance. MLC gives you the right to examine it and seek advice before signing.
  • Ask which provider gives MLC financial security cover for abandonment and death/disability, and check the certificates when you join.
  • In India, the Form-I you need for emigration clearance should come only from a valid RPSL company.

Frequently asked questions

How do I check if a crewing agency in India is genuine?

Go to the official DG Shipping site (dgshipping.gov.in / dgma.gov.in, Crew Division > RPSL) and open the live list of agents holding a Recruitment and Placement Services Licence. It shows valid, invalid and temporarily blocked licences. Only deal with a company listed as valid, and confirm contact details through the company's official landline or address, not the number that messaged you. The site and RPSL module have recently changed, so check the latest DGS circulars too.

Can a manning agency charge me a fee to get a job on a ship?

No. MLC 2006 Standard A1.4 bans licensed recruitment and placement services from charging seafarers for recruitment, placement or providing employment. The narrow exceptions are your national medical certificate, seafarer's book and passport. Visa costs are the shipowner's.

How do Filipino seafarers check a manning agency?

Search the agency's exact registered name on the DMW list of licensed recruitment agencies. Check that the licence covers sea-based manning and is not expired, suspended or cancelled. If the agency isn't on the list, don't apply or pay anything.

What should I check in the contract before signing?

Check the shipowner's name and address, ship details, contract length, wages and how they are paid, paid annual leave, repatriation terms and any CBA that applies. Ask separately how hours of work and rest are organised, as these are set out in the posted table of shipboard working arrangements. MLC entitles you to examine the SEA and seek advice before signing, and to keep a signed original. Ask for the draft well before your travel date.

What is the MLC abandonment certificate and why ask about it?

It is documentary evidence, usually from the P&I club, that financial security is in place to cover wages, repatriation and essential needs if the shipowner abandons the crew (Standard A2.5.2). Ships that need MLC certification must post it on board. If an agency can't tell you who provides it, treat that as a warning sign.

Test yourself

Under MLC 2006, which of these costs may lawfully be charged to the seafarer?

Standard A1.4.5(b) allows the seafarer to bear only the cost of the national medical certificate, seafarer's book and passport. Visas are expressly the shipowner's cost.

Where should an Indian seafarer confirm that a manning agency's RPSL is valid?

DG Shipping publishes the live list of valid, invalid and temporarily blocked RPSL companies and has advised seafarers to check it before any contact (DGS Circular 55 of 2025).

Which document on board shows that cover is in place if the crew is abandoned?

MLC Standard A2.5.2 requires ships that need MLC certification to carry and post a certificate from the financial security provider covering abandonment.

What does MLC require before a seafarer signs the Seafarer Employment Agreement?

Regulation 2.1 and Standard A2.1.1(b) say the seafarer must be able to examine and seek advice on the agreement before signing.

The DG Shipping list shows an agency's RPSL as 'temporarily blocked'. What should you do?

DG Shipping advises seafarers to deal only with companies whose RPSL shows as valid on its live list (DGS Circular 55 of 2025).
