ETO Course and Career Path in India: From Pre-Sea Training to the ETO CoC

Quick answer

To become an Electro-Technical Officer in India, first complete a 4-month pre-sea ETO course at a maritime training institute approved by the Directorate General of Maritime Administration (DGMA, formerly DG Shipping). Next, complete approved shipboard training recorded in a Training Record Book, then pass the Mercantile Marine Department (MMD) written and oral exams for a Certificate of Competency under STCW Regulation III/6. From 1 January 2027, first-time candidates must also complete a one-month ETO preparatory course before eligibility assessment or the exams. Check current entry criteria and any progression routes on dgma.gov.in.

What the ETO route actually is

The ETO is the ship's electrical, electronic and control engineering specialist. On most ships the ETO reports to the Chief Engineer. Certification falls under STCW Regulation III/6 and the competence tables in Section A-III/6 of the STCW Code. Under STCW the ETO is an operational-level certificate. Unlike deck and engine officers, ETOs have no separate STCW management-level grade.

In India the route is run by the Directorate General of Maritime Administration (DGMA), which was formerly called the Directorate General of Shipping. Its circulars are published on dgma.gov.in. Older circulars on dgshipping.gov.in still carry the DGS name, so you will see both names on paperwork for some time.

The standard path in brief:

  1. Meet the academic, age and medical entry criteria.
  2. Complete the 4-month pre-sea ETO course at a DGMA-approved institute.
  3. Complete approved shipboard training as a trainee ETO, documented in an approved Training Record Book (TAR).
  4. Complete the mandatory one-month ETO preparatory course. This applies to candidates applying or appearing for the first time from 1 January 2027.
  5. Pass eligibility assessment, then the MMD written and oral examinations.
  6. Receive the ETO CoC under STCW III/6.

Entry criteria for the pre-sea ETO course

Check the current DGMA pre-sea eligibility criteria on dgma.gov.in before you apply anywhere. The criteria cover three areas:

  • Schooling and technical qualification: a recognised diploma or degree in electrical, electronics or a related discipline, with minimum marks. The accepted branches, the cut-offs and any schooling or English requirements are set by DGMA. Check the current table rather than relying on a friend's batch.
  • Age: an upper age limit applies at the start of training. The DGS eligibility table (2013) historically listed a maximum of 35 years. Confirm the current figure on dgma.gov.in.
  • Route-specific rules: diploma holders and degree holders may enter on different terms, so read the criteria for your own background.

You also need:

  • Medical fitness from a DGMA-approved medical examiner under the Merchant Shipping medical rules. Eyesight and colour vision matter for a job spent reading cable cores and indicator lamps.
  • An INDoS number, which institutes treat as mandatory for admission.

You do not need IMU-CET for ETO pre-sea admission. Sponsoring companies run their own written tests and interviews, and they often set a lower age limit or higher marks than the DGMA minimum. Meeting the regulatory minimum does not guarantee a sponsorship.

Pre-sea course, shipboard training and sea time

Pre-sea course: the course is 4 months ashore. It covers marine electrical systems, power electronics, automation and control, and safety, with the basic STCW safety courses built in or added alongside. Only join an institute on the DGMA approved-institutes list, and check that the course itself is approved, not just the institute.

Shipboard training: STCW III/6 sets the international minimum. You need at least 12 months of combined workshop skills training and approved seagoing service, of which at least 6 months must be seagoing service in an approved training programme documented in an approved TAR. The alternative is a longer route with mostly engine-department sea service.

The Indian scheme for your batch is set by current DGMA circulars. Confirm it with your MTI and on dgma.gov.in, and get it in writing.

Two points matter for sea time:

  • Under STCW, ETO service has to be on ships powered by main propulsion machinery of 750 kW or more. Confirm your ship qualifies before you sign on.
  • Get each task signed by the designated shipboard training officer as you complete it. Add the countersignatures and stamps the approved TRB requires. A TAR "completed" in the last week before sign-off is the classic way to fail assessment.

The ETO CoC exam, the new preparatory course and the documents

Preparatory course (new): DGMA EAC Branch Circular No. 49 of 2026, issued 15 August 2026, makes a one-month offline ETO preparatory course mandatory from 1 January 2027. It applies to candidates applying for eligibility assessment, or sitting the ETO written or oral exam, for the first time on or after that date. It also covers candidates who did not appear for any function in 2026. Check the exact wording in the circular.

Only take the course at an institute that holds DGMA approval for this course. Some institutes sell non-approved "value-added" ETO coaching under similar names.

Exams: the ETO CoC is examined through the MMD, with a written stage and an oral. Applications, eligibility assessment and exam bookings go through DGMA's online e-governance system, linked from dgma.gov.in. Check the DGMA portal for the current list of MMDs that hold ETO exams.

Documents typically checked at eligibility assessment:

Item What examiners look for
INDoS, CDC, passport Details match exactly across all documents
Pre-sea ETO course certificate Approved course at an approved institute
TAR Completed task by task, signed and stamped
Sea service testimonials Dates, ship particulars and kW match the CDC entries
STCW safety and other modular courses Valid on the date of application (exact list per DGMA requirements)
Medical certificate Valid, from a DGMA-approved doctor
Preparatory course certificate Required from 1 January 2027 for first-time candidates

The list of modular courses (for example advanced survival-craft, fire-fighting or medical courses) is set by DGMA and changes over time. Check the current eligibility requirements on the portal before you book anything.

Common rejections and how to avoid them

  • Age or qualification outside the current criteria: you are over the age limit at course start, your branch is not on the accepted list, or your marks are below the cut-off. Check the current DGMA criteria before you pay any institute.
  • Non-approved course or institute: the certificate is worthless to the MMD if the course was not approved on that date.
  • Sea time on an ineligible ship: the propulsion power is below the STCW threshold, or the service was not part of the approved training programme.
  • CDC and testimonial mismatch: sign-on and sign-off dates, vessel names or IMO numbers that differ by even a day trigger queries.
  • Weak TAR: blank tasks, back-filled entries, missing stamps, or missing reviews and countersignatures.
  • Expired STCW courses on the date of application.
  • Medical from a non-approved doctor.
  • Paying agents for "guaranteed" ships: use only RPSL-licensed agencies, and walk away from anyone asking for money for a placement.

A scanned, organised file helps. Keep every document in one PDF folder, named by date, with a running sea-service table that you check against your CDC after every contract.

Worked scenarios

Scenario 1: B.Tech EEE graduate, age 24.

  1. Check the current DGMA eligibility criteria and confirm your marks meet the cut-off.
  2. Get your INDoS and a medical from a DGMA-approved doctor.
  3. Apply for company sponsorship, which usually means a written test and interview.
  4. Join the approved 4-month course.
  5. Sail as trainee ETO and keep the TAR signed as you go.
  6. Complete the one-month preparatory course if your first application falls on or after 1 January 2027.
  7. Apply for eligibility assessment, then sit the MMD written and oral exams.

Scenario 2: serving Electro-Technical Rating with an Indian ETR CoP. Do not assume the standard route's age and entry rules apply to you, or that they don't. Check dgma.gov.in for any current circular on ETR-to-ETO progression. Note the age, sea-time and course conditions it sets before you approach an institute.

Filipino readers: the Philippine ETO certificate is also issued under STCW III/6, but by MARINA, with its own training and assessment rules. Check MARINA's STCW requirements rather than the Indian ones.

Oral exam: what examiners like to probe

ETO orals are practical. Expect "you are on board and this happens" questions:

  • Earth fault on a 440 V insulated system: how you locate it, which equipment you isolate first, and why ships use insulated neutral systems.
  • Megger testing: the test voltage you would choose, and what you do with low readings on a motor in damp conditions.
  • Blackout: the recovery sequence, preferential tripping, and the emergency generator starting and connecting automatically.
  • Synchronising and load sharing: what happens if you close the breaker out of phase.
  • High voltage safety: isolation, earthing down, proving dead, permit-to-work and lock-out/tag-out.
  • Bridge equipment: gyro, radar, ECDIS and GMDSS power supplies, and what fails over to the emergency supply.
  • Statutory knowledge: SOLAS requirements on emergency power, and your duties under the ship's SMS.

Tip: always state the safety step (isolation) first in your answer.

Career path after the ETO CoC

Under STCW the ETO is a single grade, so progression is about the ships you sail on and the responsibility you take, not a further CoC exam. Typical directions:

  • Larger and more complex ships: LNG carriers, cruise ships, offshore DP vessels and car carriers carry heavy automation and often high-voltage plant. Companies frequently ask for type-specific experience and additional courses such as high-voltage or DP-related training.
  • Senior ETO roles on fleets with more than one ETO on board, such as passenger ships.
  • Shore roles: technical superintendent, service engineer for equipment makers, or instructor at an MTI. The DGMA sets faculty qualification rules for instructors.

If you are interested in moving to the engine-officer ladder, check dgma.gov.in for any current DGMA circular on such routes.

To stay sailing, keep your CoC revalidated within its validity and keep your STCW refresher courses current. The revalidation rules are on the DGMA portal.

Key points
  • The ETO CoC is issued under STCW Regulation III/6, in India by DGMA (formerly DG Shipping) after MMD written and oral exams.
  • The standard entry is a 4-month DGMA-approved pre-sea ETO course; confirm current age and academic criteria on dgma.gov.in.
  • STCW III/6 requires at least 12 months of combined workshop training and seagoing service, with at least 6 months at sea.
  • Shipboard training must be in an approved programme and documented task by task in an approved TAR.
  • From 1 January 2027, a one-month DGMA-approved ETO preparatory course is mandatory for first-time candidates (Circular 49 of 2026).
  • Common causes of rejection include CDC and testimonial mismatches, weak TARs, ineligible ships and non-approved courses.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need IMU-CET to join the ETO course?

No. The pre-sea ETO course is not admitted through IMU-CET. You need to meet DGMA's academic, age and medical criteria, and you usually also have to pass a sponsoring company's own selection tests and interview.

What is the age limit for the ETO course in India?

An upper age limit applies at the start of training. The DGS eligibility table (2013) historically listed 35 years, but you should confirm the current figure in DGMA's pre-sea eligibility criteria on dgma.gov.in. Companies may also set a lower limit of their own.

Is the ETO preparatory course compulsory?

Yes, from 1 January 2027. Under DGMA EAC Branch Circular 49 of 2026, it applies to candidates applying for eligibility assessment, or sitting the ETO written or oral exam, for the first time on or after that date. Take it only at an institute approved by DGMA for this specific course.

How much sea time do I need before the ETO CoC exam?

STCW III/6 requires at least 12 months of combined workshop skills training and approved seagoing service, including at least 6 months at sea in an approved training programme recorded in a TAR. DGMA's current scheme structures the shipboard training phase in more detail, so confirm the exact requirement for your batch with your MTI and on the DGMA portal.

Does the ETO CoC need to be revalidated?

Yes. Under STCW Regulation I/11, officers must revalidate their certificates at intervals not exceeding five years to continue serving at sea. The exact Indian revalidation requirements, including sea service and refresher courses, are published on the DGMA portal.

Test yourself

Under which STCW regulation is the Electro-Technical Officer certificate issued?

STCW Regulation III/6 (with Section A-III/6) covers ETOs. III/1 covers engineering watch officers, III/7 covers Electro-Technical Ratings, and II/1 covers deck watch officers.

From 1 January 2027, which first-time ETO CoC candidates must complete the one-month preparatory course?

DGMA EAC Branch Circular 49 of 2026 makes the course mandatory for first-time applicants and examinees from 1 January 2027, whatever their route.

An Indian ETR CoP holder wants to move up to the pre-sea ETO course. What should he do first?

Entry conditions for ETR CoP holders are set by DGMA circulars, so check the current circular on dgma.gov.in for the age and sea-time rules (DGMA EAC circulars).

Which of these is most likely to trigger a query at ETO eligibility assessment?

Foreign-flag service is normally acceptable if the other conditions are met. Mismatched dates and vessel details between testimonials and the CDC are a cause of queries at MMD assessment.

Before an ETO opens a high-voltage switchboard cubicle for maintenance, which step must come first?

Standard HV safety practice and the ship's SMS require isolation, lock-out, proving dead and earthing down under a permit to work before access; megger testing comes only after the equipment is safe.
