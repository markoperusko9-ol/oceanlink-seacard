Seafarer unions and welfare bodies in India: who does what and how to use them

Quick answer

Indian seafarers are supported by: - registered trade unions such as NUSI, MUI and FSUI - the Seafarers' Welfare Fund Society (a registered society chaired by the Director General of Shipping) - the Seamen's Provident Fund Organisation - ITF inspectors in ports Unions negotiate agreements and help with disputes. SWFS pays welfare benefits and holds gratuity paid under collective agreements. SPFO runs the statutory provident fund. Use the right body for the problem, keep copies of your papers and claim in writing.

The main bodies and what each one actually does

Many seafarers waste weeks writing to the wrong office. Start by working out what kind of body you need.

Body Type Go to them for
National Union of Seafarers of India (NUSI) Trade union, Mumbai Membership, help with disputes, union welfare schemes, CBA matters
Maritime Union of India (MUI) Trade union, Mumbai Representation of officers and ratings, member welfare and medical support
Forward Seamen's Union of India (FSUI) Trade union Representation and dispute help for its members
Seafarers' Welfare Fund Society (SWFS) Registered society and public trust, chaired by the Director General of Shipping Death, invalidity, old-age, family and maternity benefits; gratuity held under CBAs
Seamen's Provident Fund Organisation (SPFO) Statutory fund, Mumbai Provident fund registration, nomination, balance and withdrawal
ITF inspectors and ITF Seafarer Support International union federation Urgent problems on board or in port: unpaid wages, abandonment, contract breaches

The Directorate General of Shipping (DGS) also runs a crew branch, shipping masters and a grievance route. These are covered in the separate guide on the seafarer grievance portal.

Seafarers' unions: membership, CBAs and realistic expectations

Seafarers' unions in India are registered under the Trade Unions Act, 1926. NUSI, MUI and FSUI are the main seafarers' unions and the ITF's Indian maritime affiliates.

What a union does for you:

  • Negotiates collective bargaining agreements (CBAs) with shipowners and manning associations. Your pay scale, leave, gratuity, death or disability compensation and repatriation terms often come from a CBA. They are not always set by your individual contract alone.
  • Takes up disputes with the company or RPS agency. Examples are unpaid wages, wrongful termination, disability compensation and delayed death claims.
  • Runs its own welfare schemes. These vary by union and change over time. Examples include medical help, scholarships for children and counselling. Check the union's own website or app for current schemes.
  • Represents seafarers on government welfare bodies, including the National Welfare Board for Seafarers.

How to join (practical steps):

  1. Find out whether your vessel or company is under a CBA and which union signed it. The crewing office or your SEA should tell you.
  2. Contact that union's office or official app. Ask for its current membership form and the list of documents it needs.
  3. Keep copies of your CDC, INDoS number, passport, SEA and any union receipts. You will need proof of membership if you ever make a claim.
  4. Save the union's helpline and email in your phone before you join a ship, not after something goes wrong.

Know the limits:

  • A union cannot overrule a flag state.
  • A union cannot get you money that your contract or CBA never provided for.
  • On ships that are not under a CBA, your SEA and MLC rights are your main protection.

Seafarers' Welfare Fund Society (SWFS): schemes and claims

SWFS is the central welfare body for Indian seafarers and their families. It was formed on the recommendation of the National Welfare Board for Seafarers. It is registered under the Societies Registration Act, 1860 and as a public trust under the Bombay Public Trust Act, 1950, and is based in Mumbai.

The Director General of Shipping is its ex-officio chairman. Its Committee of Management is nominated by the Central Government (Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways). RPS agencies and shipping companies pay a welfare contribution into the fund under the RPS Rules.

Schemes listed on the DGS welfare page include:

  • Survival benefit for nominees of a deceased Indian seafarer
  • Invalidity benefit, for example after being declared permanently unfit by a DGS-approved doctor
  • Maternity benefit for Indian women seafarers
  • Old-age benefit, including assistance to very senior seafarers
  • Family benefit welfare scheme
  • Ex-gratia assistance on death

Eligibility: this depends on how and where the seafarer was engaged and on timing. SWFS rules refer to seamen engaged under section 101 of the MS Act, 1958. Read the current scheme rules before you apply.

Time limit: ex-gratia death applications must reach SWFS within 2 years of the date of death. This is stated in the SWFS FAQ; confirm the current rules before you apply.

Gratuity: you may have sailed under a CBA between the unions and industry associations such as INSA, ICCSA, MASSA or FOSMA. If so, your gratuity may have been deposited with SWFS. You claim it with the SWFS gratuity application form. SWFS is not a party to these agreements. It only holds and pays out what was deposited, so the amount is decided by your CBA or SEA.

How to claim:

  1. Download the current form and scheme rules from the DGS welfare measures page.
  2. Attach CDC copies, discharge entries, INDoS, ID and bank details. Add the documents specific to the scheme, such as a death certificate, a permanent-unfit certificate or proof of the relationship to the seafarer.
  3. Submit to SWFS in Mumbai. Your union can forward and follow up the claim.
  4. Keep proof of submission and follow up in writing.

Amounts and eligibility windows are revised by circular. Read the current DGS circular rather than relying on what a batchmate was paid.

Seamen's Provident Fund Organisation (SPFO)

SPFO runs the Seamen's Provident Fund Scheme, 1966. A Board of Trustees under the Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways manages the fund.

Whether you are covered depends on your employment and the ship. Seafarers on Indian-flag ships are the usual members. If in doubt, ask SPFO directly.

Common tasks, from SPFO's official FAQ:

Task How
Register Fill DN Form I (from spfo.gov.in). Post it to the SPFO office with copies of your CDC first and last pages and your Aadhaar card. Take the current postal address from spfo.gov.in
Update details or nominee Use DN Form IV, posted to SPFO
Check balance Email spfo-commr@spfo.gov.in (confirm on spfo.gov.in)
Claim PF Download the claim form and checklist from spfo.gov.in. Send the application with the required documents to SPFO by email (spfo-commr@spfo.gov.in) or by post
Claim on a member's death Use the deceased-claim route on spfo.gov.in

Tip: keep your nomination up to date. A missing or outdated nominee is one of the biggest causes of delay when a family has to claim.

ITF inspectors and help in foreign ports

The International Transport Workers' Federation (ITF) has a worldwide network of inspectors. ITF inspectors cover several major Indian ports; check the ITF Seafarers app for current locations. NUSI, MUI and FSUI are ITF affiliates.

Contact an ITF inspector or ITF Seafarer Support when:

  • wages are unpaid or underpaid on a foreign-flag ship
  • you are kept on board past your contract with no relief plan
  • the ship has been abandoned: no wages, food, fuel or repatriation
  • you are being pressured to sign a second contract with worse terms

How to contact them: use the free ITF Seafarers app or itfseafarers.org to find the nearest inspector. You can also email the ITF Seafarer Support team in London. Inspector names and numbers change, so always take them from the app or website rather than an old forwarded message.

Use your ship's on-board complaint procedure as well. MLC, 2006 Regulation 5.1.5 requires one. A complaint on record helps any later claim.

Worked scenarios: which body, in what order

1. AB on a foreign-flag bulker, three months' wages unpaid, ship idle at anchor abroad.

  1. Note the dates and amounts owed. Keep copies of your SEA and wage slips.
  2. Raise a written complaint through the on-board procedure.
  3. Contact the ITF inspector for that port. Contact your union as well if you are a member.
  4. Report the RPS agency that placed you through the DGS grievance route.
  5. If the ship is abandoned, the shipowner's abandonment financial security should cover up to four months' outstanding wages, repatriation and essential needs. MLC Standard A2.5.2 requires this security. The certificate is posted on board and usually comes from the P&I club. The ITF and your union can help you claim it.

2. Widow of a 2nd Engineer who died at home four months after sign-off from an RPSL-placed ship.

  • SWFS: check the death and survival schemes. Eligibility depends on how and where the seafarer was engaged and on timing. SWFS rules refer to seamen engaged under section 101 of the MS Act, 1958, so read the current scheme rules.
  • Ex-gratia deadline: ex-gratia death applications must reach SWFS within 2 years of the date of death. This is stated in the SWFS FAQ; confirm the current rules.
  • SPFO: check for any PF balance, using the deceased-claim route.
  • Contract and union: check the CBA or SEA for contractual death compensation. Check any union scheme if he was a member.
  • Documents: collect his CDC, death certificate, legal heir or nominee proof and bank details before you start.

3. Officer leaving an Indian company after years under a CBA.

  • Gratuity: ask the company whether gratuity was deposited with SWFS, then apply with the SWFS gratuity form.
  • Provident fund: settle this separately through SPFO.

Common rejections, delays and how to avoid them

  • Wrong body: gratuity sent to SPFO, or PF claimed from SWFS. Match the claim to the body using the table above.
  • Name mismatch: your name, date of birth or nominee is spelled differently on the CDC, passport, bank account and nomination. Correct this through DGS or SPFO before you need to claim.
  • No proof of sea service: discharge entries or sign-off papers are missing. Get every sign-on and sign-off entered, and see the guide on sea-time verification.
  • Out-of-date form: schemes and forms are revised by circular. Always download from dgshipping.gov.in or spfo.gov.in on the day you apply.
  • Not eligible under the scheme rules: SWFS eligibility depends on how and where the seafarer was engaged and on timing. SWFS rules refer to seamen engaged under section 101 of the MS Act, 1958. Read the current scheme rules before applying, and always join through a licensed RPS agency so your engagement is properly recorded.
  • Late application: ex-gratia death applications must reach SWFS within 2 years of the date of death. This is stated in the SWFS FAQ; confirm the current rules.
  • No follow-up: keep a file of reference numbers, dates and emails. Ask your union to follow up if the claim stalls.

Oral exam angle: welfare questions examiners ask

MMD examiners for Second Mate, MEO Class 4 and above often test MLC welfare and complaint procedures. Have short answers ready:

  • Q: Where does MLC deal with shore welfare facilities? Regulation 4.4. Each Member must ensure that shore-based welfare facilities in its ports, where they exist, are easily accessible to all seafarers. It must also promote their development.
  • Q: What protects a crew if the owner abandons the ship? The financial security system under Standard A2.5.2. It covers outstanding wages, repatriation and essential needs. A certificate must be carried on board.
  • Q: Complaint routes? On-board procedures (Regulation 5.1.5) and onshore complaint handling in port states (Regulation 5.2.2).
  • Q: Name Indian bodies for seafarer welfare. The Seafarers' Welfare Fund Society (chaired by the Director General of Shipping), the Seamen's Provident Fund Organisation, the National Welfare Board for Seafarers and the registered unions.
Key points
  • NUSI, MUI and FSUI are registered unions. They negotiate CBAs, take up disputes and run their own welfare schemes.
  • SWFS, a registered society chaired by the DG Shipping, runs death, invalidity, maternity, old-age and family benefits and holds CBA gratuity deposits.
  • SPFO runs the Seamen's Provident Fund. Register with DN Form I, update with DN Form IV, and send claims by email or post using the forms on spfo.gov.in.
  • For urgent problems on foreign-flag ships, contact an ITF inspector through the ITF Seafarers app or website.
  • Forms, amounts and eligibility change by circular, so always download them from dgshipping.gov.in or spfo.gov.in.
  • Matching names and nominees across your CDC, passport, bank and nomination records prevents most claim delays.
  • SWFS eligibility depends on how and where the seafarer was engaged and on timing. Ex-gratia death applications have a 2-year deadline.

Frequently asked questions

Is it compulsory to join NUSI or MUI?

No Indian law requires every seafarer to join a union. Some companies and CBA vessels expect membership, so ask the crewing office before you sign. Without membership, you are still protected by your SEA, MLC, 2006 and the DGS grievance route.

How do I claim gratuity deposited with the Seafarers' Welfare Fund Society?

Download the SWFS gratuity application form from the DGS welfare measures page. Attach your CDC, ID, bank details and service proof, and submit it to SWFS in Mumbai. The amount depends on your CBA or SEA, not on SWFS. Your union can help follow up the claim.

How can I check my Seamen's Provident Fund balance?

SPFO's FAQ says you check your balance by emailing spfo-commr@spfo.gov.in. Confirm the current address on spfo.gov.in before you write. Include your PF details and identity information so they can trace your account.

My family needs help after a seafarer died ashore. Can SWFS still pay?

Possibly. Eligibility depends on how and where the seafarer was engaged and on timing. SWFS rules refer to seamen engaged under section 101 of the MS Act, 1958, so read the current scheme rules on dgshipping.gov.in. Ex-gratia death applications must reach SWFS within 2 years of the date of death (per the SWFS FAQ; confirm current rules), with the death certificate, CDC and nominee or legal heir proof.

Who do I call if I'm unpaid on a foreign ship in a foreign port?

Contact the nearest ITF inspector through the ITF Seafarers app or itfseafarers.org. Contact your union too if you are a member, and file a written complaint on board. If an Indian RPS agency placed you, also raise a grievance with DG Shipping against that agency.

Test yourself

Which body administers the Seamen's Provident Fund Scheme, 1966?

The Seamen's Provident Fund is administered by SPFO, Mumbai, under a Board of Trustees, and is separate from EPFO. Ref: SPFO, spfo.gov.in.

Under CBAs with associations such as INSA or FOSMA, where is seafarers' gratuity typically deposited?

The SWFS FAQ says gratuity provided for under these agreements is deposited with SWFS, which is not itself a party to the CBA. Ref: DGS SWFS FAQ.

Which form does SPFO use for registration and nomination?

SPFO's FAQ lists DN Form I for registration and nomination, and DN Form IV for updating details. Ref: DGS FAQ on SPFO.

Which MLC, 2006 provision requires financial security to cover crew in case of abandonment?

Standard A2.5.2 sets out the abandonment financial security system. Regulation 4.4 covers shore welfare and Regulation 5.1.5 covers on-board complaints. Ref: MLC, 2006, Standard A2.5.2.

Who is the ex-officio chairman of the Seafarers' Welfare Fund Society?

The DGS welfare page states that the Director General of Shipping is the ex-officio chairman of SWFS. Ref: DGS Welfare Measures page.
