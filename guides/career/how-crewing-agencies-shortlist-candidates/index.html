How crewing agencies shortlist candidates

Quick answer

A crewing agency shortlists you by comparing your rank, vessel-type experience and documents with a specific requirement from a shipowner or manager, then checks your references and interviews you before the principal gives final approval. Most rejections come from documents expiring in the middle of a contract, the wrong vessel-type experience, unexplained gaps or sea time that can't be verified. Before you apply, confirm the agency is licensed on the official regulator's website: DG Shipping (dgshipping.gov.in) in India or DMW (dmw.gov.ph) in the Philippines.

Who is actually choosing you

When an agency rejects you, it's easy to think the agency made the decision. Usually it didn't. A crewing or manning agency recruits for a principal, which is the shipowner or ship manager. The principal sends a requirement, and the agency's job is to find candidates who match it and forward their CVs.

The agency has legal duties as well as commercial ones:

  • India: recruitment and placement agencies must be licensed by the Directorate General of Shipping (DGS) under the Merchant Shipping (Recruitment and Placement of Seafarers) Rules, 2016. These are known as RPSL agencies. Rules and the RPSL online process are updated from time to time, so check the current RPSL circulars on dgshipping.gov.in.
  • Philippines: manning agencies must be licensed by the Department of Migrant Workers (DMW), which took over this role from POEA. MARINA issues and verifies seafarer certificates.
  • MLC 2006, Standard A1.4: agencies must make sure the seafarers they place are qualified and hold the documents needed for the job. Agencies may not charge seafarers for recruitment or placement, apart from a few personal documents that are listed in the Standard. Agencies are also not allowed to use blacklists or similar methods to stop qualified seafarers from finding work.
  • ISM Code and STCW: ISM Code section 6 requires the company to ensure each ship is manned with qualified, certificated and medically fit seafarers. STCW Regulation I/14 makes the company responsible for ensuring each seafarer holds the appropriate certificates and that the ship is manned in line with its safe manning document.

Because of these duties, the screening process is built around documents and verifiable experience. A good interview manner helps, but it doesn't replace them.

The shortlisting process step by step

The details differ between companies, but most agencies follow this sequence:

  1. The requirement arrives. The principal specifies the rank, vessel type and size, engine type (for engineers), trading area, joining port and window, and any extras. Examples of extras are a DCE or tanker endorsement, a flag endorsement, ECDIS type-specific training, DP or high-voltage training.
  2. Database search and CV screening. The crewing executive searches the agency database and recent applications. They filter by rank, then vessel type, then document validity. A CV that doesn't clearly show vessel type, size and engine make is often skipped at this stage.
  3. Document check. The agency checks your CoC, CDC or SIRB, passport, STCW courses, medical certificate and any endorsements. They want each document to stay valid for the whole contract, not just on the day you join.
  4. Verification. In India, CoC, CDC and INDoS details can be checked through DG Shipping's online services. For Filipino seafarers, certificates are checked against MARINA records. Portal names and access change, so use the verification links on dgshipping.gov.in, marina.gov.ph and dmw.gov.ph. Agencies also contact previous companies for references and sea time.
  5. Interview and assessment. This is usually a phone or video call with the crewing team. A technical interview by a marine or technical superintendent often follows. Many companies also use computer-based competence or English tests.
  6. Principal approval. Your CV, documents and interview notes go to the principal. The principal makes the final decision.
  7. Pre-joining. You complete a pre-employment medical with an approved doctor and any drug and alcohol test the company requires. The agency arranges visas and company training. Before you travel, you sign the Seafarer Employment Agreement (SEA).

Documents they check and what "valid" really means

Document What the agency looks at
CoC / CoP Correct level and capacity, any limitations, and that it can be verified online
CDC (India) / SIRB (Philippines) Sea service entries that match your CV
Passport Validity left for the contract plus travel; enough blank pages
STCW courses Refresher/revalidation status: STCW requires continued proficiency every five years for Basic Training, PSCRB and Advanced Fire Fighting
Medical fitness certificate Issued by an approved doctor, with no restrictions that clash with the job
Tanker/gas endorsements Correct level for the rank (basic or advanced)
Flag endorsement Required by the flag; often arranged after selection
Visas Often a US C1/D for certain trades, depending on the principal
Sea service testimonials / appraisals Proof of rank and vessel-type experience

The rule is simple: if a document expires before the contract ends, you're a risk. Rules on how much validity must remain vary by company and administration, so ask the agency what their principal requires. Renew documents early. Don't wait until you're offered a ship.

Common reasons candidates are rejected

These come up again and again:

  • Wrong vessel-type experience. A bulk carrier second officer applying for a chemical tanker without the right endorsement, or an engineer without experience of the engine make the principal uses. Tanker principals often follow crew experience matrices that set minimum time in rank and on the vessel type.
  • Document validity gaps. CoC revalidation is due during the contract, the medical is about to expire, or STCW refreshers are lapsed.
  • Sea time that can't be verified. CDC entries don't match the CV, or a previous company is unreachable or no longer exists.
  • Unexplained gaps and early sign-offs. One early sign-off on medical grounds is understandable. A pattern of them is not. Explain gaps honestly.
  • Poor or vague CV. Missing GRT/DWT, engine type, trading area or dates.
  • Communication. Weak English in the interview, or poor answers to basic technical and safety questions for your rank.
  • Previous appraisals and references. A poor report from a principal you worked for before travels quickly.
  • Medical findings. These are handled by the approved doctor under the applicable standards. Agencies cannot overrule them.

Worked scenario: third officer moving to tankers

Situation: You're a third officer with 18 months on bulk carriers. You apply for a third officer post on a product tanker.

What the agency sees:

  • Your CoC is valid and verifies online. That's a pass.
  • You have basic oil and chemical tanker training but no tanker sea time. That's a weakness, because many tanker principals want previous tanker experience for officers.
  • Your medical expires three months into a six-month contract. Most principals will put you on hold until it is renewed.

What to do:

  1. Renew the medical first, with an approved doctor.
  2. Ask the agency directly whether the principal accepts officers moving across from dry cargo. Some do, especially junior officers. Others will offer you the same rank on a bulk carrier, or a lower rank on a tanker so you can build tanker time.
  3. Check what your administration requires for the advanced tanker endorsement. Talk to your current company about a route into tankers that doesn't stall your CoC progression.
  4. Update your CV to show cargo work you can transfer: ballast operations, hold cleaning, cargo hold ventilation and atmosphere monitoring (e.g. coal cargoes), enclosed space entry, and permit-to-work experience.

Oral-exam style: questions crewing and technical interviews ask

Superintendents tend to ask practical questions for your rank. Prepare short, honest answers to these:

  • Deck officers: "Walk me through your passage plan check before departure." "What do you do if the ECDIS safety contour alarm activates in a TSS?" "How do you prepare for pilot boarding?" "Give the enclosed space entry checks."
  • Engineers: "Describe your last overhaul of a fuel injector or purifier." "What are the first actions on a blackout?" "How do you handle an oily water separator alarm?" "Explain how you take over a UMS watch."
  • Ratings: "Your duties at mooring stations." "Lifeboat and fire drill duties." "How do you rig a pilot ladder?"
  • Everyone: "Why did you sign off early from that ship?" "Have you ever had a PSC or vetting observation in your area?" "Why this vessel type?"

If you don't know something, say so and explain how you would find out. Superintendents mark bluffing down harder than honest gaps.

Protect yourself: verify the agency before you send documents

Shortlisting works both ways. You're also checking them.

  • India: check the agency's licence on the RPSL list on dgshipping.gov.in. DGS has issued circulars (e.g. 07/03/2016) warning against RPSL licences being sold or shared with private or unauthorised manning agents. The office you deal with should be the licensed entity.
  • Philippines: search the agency's exact legal name in DMW's list of licensed agencies on dmw.gov.ph, and check that the principal and vessel are properly accredited and enrolled. Portal names and access change, so use the verification links on dmw.gov.ph and marina.gov.ph.
  • Fees: MLC Standard A1.4 prohibits recruitment or placement charges to seafarers, apart from a few listed personal document costs such as your medical certificate, seafarer's book and passport. Visa costs are not among them. Any demand for a "processing", "training guarantee" or "selection" fee is a red flag. Report it to DGS or DMW. In India, DGS Circular No. 5 of 2020 specifically warns RPSL holders against demanding fees from seafarers.
  • Documents: send colour scans only when there is a specific vacancy. Never hand over your original CoC or passport to be "held".

Once you're selected, read the SEA carefully before signing. Check the wages section, contract duration, repatriation terms and the applicable collective agreement. Ask for anything you don't understand to be explained.

Key points
  • The principal (shipowner or manager) sets the requirement and gives final approval. The agency screens against it.
  • Most principals expect your documents to stay valid for the whole contract, not just on the joining date.
  • Vessel-type and engine-type experience you can verify counts most at CV screening.
  • Sea time is checked against your CDC/SIRB and references, so your CV must match them exactly.
  • Technical interviews test practical knowledge for your rank. Honesty beats bluffing.
  • Under MLC Standard A1.4, agencies may not charge recruitment or placement fees or use blacklists against qualified seafarers.
  • Check that an agency is licensed on the DG Shipping (RPSL) or DMW website before sending documents.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the agency keep saying my CV is "on hold"?

Usually there is no current requirement from a principal that matches your rank, vessel type and availability. Sometimes a document will expire during the likely contract period. Ask the agency which of these it is, and keep your availability date and documents up to date with them.

Can a crewing agency charge me a fee for getting a ship?

Under MLC 2006 Standard A1.4, seafarers must not bear recruitment or placement fees, either directly or indirectly. The only exceptions are a few listed personal items, such as the statutory medical certificate, seafarer's book and passport. In India, report fee demands to DG Shipping. In the Philippines, report them to DMW.

How do agencies verify my CoC and sea time?

For Indian certificates, they use DG Shipping's online services for CoC, CDC and INDoS details. For Filipino seafarers, they check MARINA records. Portal names and access change, so use the verification links on dgshipping.gov.in and marina.gov.ph. Sea time is cross-checked against your CDC or SIRB entries, testimonials and references from previous employers.

Will one early sign-off ruin my chances?

Not usually, if there was a genuine reason such as a medical issue or a family emergency and you explain it honestly. A pattern of early sign-offs, or a gap you haven't explained, is what makes principals cautious.

Do I need a flag endorsement before I apply?

Often not. Many companies apply for the flag endorsement after you're selected, because it depends on the specific ship's flag. Requirements and processing differ by flag administration, so confirm with the agency.

Test yourself

Who normally gives final approval for a candidate to join a ship?

The agency forwards candidates and the principal approves them, because the company carries the manning responsibility under the ISM Code, section 6, and STCW Regulation I/14.

Under MLC 2006, which cost may a seafarer be expected to bear?

Standard A1.4 prohibits recruitment or placement charges to seafarers. Exceptions are personal items such as the statutory medical certificate, seafarer's book and passport, but not visas.

Your medical certificate expires two months into a six-month contract. What is the agency most likely to do?

Most principals require key documents to stay valid for the whole contract, so the agency will usually hold you until the medical is renewed. MLC Standard A1.2 and STCW Regulation I/9 only allow a certificate that expires during a voyage to continue until the next port with a recognised doctor (maximum 3 months), which is a safeguard, not a planning tool.

What does MLC 2006 say about agencies keeping lists to stop qualified seafarers getting work?

MLC Standard A1.4 requires member states to prohibit agencies from using means, mechanisms or lists intended to prevent or deter qualified seafarers from gaining employment.

In India, where do you confirm that a manning agency is legally allowed to recruit seafarers?

Agencies recruiting Indian seafarers must hold an RPSL licence issued by DGS under the Merchant Shipping (Recruitment and Placement of Seafarers) Rules, 2016. Check the current RPSL circulars on dgshipping.gov.in, because the rules and online process are updated from time to time.
