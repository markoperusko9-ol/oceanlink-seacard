Master (FG) Exam in India: The Process, Step by Step

Quick answer

To get a Master (FG) CoC in India you need a valid First Mate/Chief Mate (FG) CoC and enough verified sea service after it. You then complete the DGMA-approved Master's Advanced Shipboard Management (ASM) course and any other courses on the current checklist, apply online and sit the Master (FG) examinations. The required sea service, the current written/oral exam structure and the booking route are all set out in DGMA notices on dgshipping.gov.in. Check them before you pay for a course or book a slot.

Who sets the rules and where to check them

The maritime administration that used to be called the Directorate General of Shipping (DGS) has been renamed the Directorate General of Maritime Administration (DGMA) under Section 7 of the Merchant Shipping Act, 2025. The DGS announced the change on its official channels. The website is still dgshipping.gov.in. The Mercantile Marine Departments (MMDs) handle examinations and CoC processing.

There are three layers of rules:

  • STCW Regulation II/2 and Section A-II/2 set the international minimum standard of competence for a master of ships of 3,000 GT or more.
  • Indian national rules and DGMA circulars/notices set Indian eligibility, sea time and course prerequisites. A national administration can set requirements stricter than the STCW minimum.
  • The MMD checks your documents, conducts examinations and processes the CoC, as set out in current DGMA notices.

This page covers the Indian CoC only. Filipino officers going for Master follow the MARINA process, which has its own requirements. Don't assume the Indian steps below apply to you.

Eligibility: what you need before you start

The checklist below is a general guide. Confirm every item against the latest DGMA eligibility notice on dgshipping.gov.in before you plan your leave.

Requirement What it means in practice
CoC A valid First Mate / Chief Mate (FG) CoC
Sea service after Chief Mate (FG) CoC Set by DGMA. Check the current eligibility notice on dgshipping.gov.in
Records CDC entries, company/RPSL testimonials and sea service updated in your online seafarer profile
Courses ASM course plus any modular or simulator courses on the current checklist
Medical Valid medical fitness from a DGMA-approved doctor

For background, STCW Regulation II/2 requires at least 36 months of approved seagoing service as an officer in charge of a navigational watch. This can come down to at least 24 months if at least 12 months of it were served as chief mate. That is the international minimum. The Indian requirement is the one in the current DGMA notice, so plan your sea time from that notice.

Some guidance has listed a ship manoeuvring simulator course for Master (FG). Course names and combinations have changed over the years. Ask your institute for the current list of required courses, check it against the DGMA notice and keep a copy.

The process, step by step

  1. Audit your sea time first. Go through your CDC, company testimonials and online profile and check that rank, ship, GT, flag and sign-on/sign-off dates are the same everywhere. Count only service that counts under Indian rules (see the related sea time guide).
  2. Update your seafarer profile on the DGMA online system (reached from dgshipping.gov.in). Upload or verify your Chief Mate CoC and sea service. Institutes say that once these details are updated, you can submit the profile for course eligibility.
  3. Book the Master's Advanced Shipboard Management (ASM) course at a DGMA-approved institute. DGS Training Circular 19 of 2004 set this course up to prepare candidates for the Master (FG) examinations. Check the course length with your institute.
  4. Finish any e-learning modules and course exit assessments. Institutes report that for many approved courses you must complete the DGMA e-learning module before the exit exam. Use only the e-learning link published on dgshipping.gov.in.
  5. Complete any other required courses (for example, a simulator course if it is on your checklist). Keep your STCW modular certificates valid.
  6. Apply for the examination. The current booking route is set out in DGMA notices on dgshipping.gov.in. Start from the official website rather than a saved link. Check fees and slot availability on the official portal, and don't rely on figures from agents or WhatsApp groups.
  7. Sit the written examination. The current written structure (which papers, and who sets them) is set out in DGMA notices. If you fail a paper, check the current rules on re-sits and on how long passed papers stay valid.
  8. Sit the oral examination as set out in the current DGMA notice.
  9. Apply for CoC issue once you have passed everything. Track the application online and check the details on your CoC (name, INDoS, limitations) as soon as you receive it.

Documents to keep ready

Keep a scanned set and an original set together. MMDs can ask to see originals at any stage.

  • Passport and INDoS number
  • CDC with all sign-on/sign-off entries stamped and legible
  • First Mate / Chief Mate (FG) CoC and any endorsements (GMDSS, DCE, flag endorsements if needed for verification)
  • Sea service testimonials from each company or RPSL, on letterhead, with ship names, IMO numbers, GT, rank and dates
  • ASM course certificate and any other required course certificates, plus e-learning completion where applicable
  • Valid STCW modular course certificates (basic training and the advanced courses on your checklist)
  • Valid medical fitness certificate from a DGMA-approved doctor
  • Passport-size photographs and signature scans in the format the portal asks for

If you sailed on foreign-flag ships, keep copies of the flag endorsements as well. They help prove your rank on board when CDC entries look thin.

Common reasons applications get held up

These are problems candidates often run into. They are not official statistics.

  • Rank mismatch. The CDC says "2nd Officer" for a contract where the testimonial says "Chief Officer". The examiner will go by the documented rank, and your Chief Officer time may not be counted.
  • Dates that don't match. Sign-off dates in the CDC differ from the testimonial or profile by a few days, and the total then falls just short.
  • Ship size. Service on ships below the GT limit in the rules is counted differently or not at all.
  • Expired certificates. An STCW modular certificate or the medical expires between the course and the exam.
  • Profile not updated. Sea service or the Chief Mate CoC is not verified in the online profile, so the course or exam application can't go through.
  • Personal details that don't match. Name spelling or date of birth differs between passport, CDC and INDoS.

Tip: Before you sign off from your qualifying contract as Chief Officer, ask the master and company to make sure the CDC entry and testimonial show the correct rank and dates. Fixing them years later through the office is slow.

What the Master orals test, with worked scenarios

In the Master orals the examiner is checking whether you can take command. The question behind every answer is "what does the master do, and why?" Expect questions on ship-handling, emergencies, stability, ISM/ISPS/MLC, PSC, commercial matters and law. Examiners differ, so these are typical patterns, not a fixed syllabus.

Scenario 1 – PSC detention. "Your ship is detained in port. What do you do?" A good answer covers these points:

  • Read the deficiency list and detention grounds carefully.
  • Tell the company/DPA, flag and class straight away.
  • Agree a rectification plan and timeline.
  • Use the ISM non-conformity process.
  • Arrange class attendance where needed.
  • Request re-inspection.

Also mention that the flag and the company's safety management system (SMS) need to be informed, and that the detention may be published.

Scenario 2 – Overriding authority. "The charterer pushes you to sail with a defect you consider unsafe." The ISM Code requires the SMS to state clearly that the master has overriding authority and responsibility for decisions on safety and pollution prevention. Explain how you would use it: record the facts, inform the company/DPA, put the decision in writing and log it. Commercial pressure does not override that responsibility.

Scenario 3 – Collision in restricted visibility. Expect to run through the immediate actions:

  • Sound the alarms and assess the damage.
  • Sound the tanks and check stability.
  • Stand by the other vessel.
  • Preserve the VDR data.
  • Report to the coastal state, flag and company.
  • Make the log entries.

Then expect a COLREG Rule 19 cross-examination on what the OOW should have done.

How to answer well: start with immediate safety (people, ship, environment), then reporting, then documentation. Name the instrument behind each point (SOLAS, MARPOL, ISM, MLC, COLREG) without trying to recite it.

Preparation tips from the examining side

  • Prepare while you are still Chief Officer. Watch how your master deals with PSC, agents, charterers, P&I correspondents and the DPA. Your best oral answers will come from real cases you were part of.
  • Know your company's SMS well enough to explain how you would report a non-conformity, a near miss or a detention.
  • Revise ship-handling as a commander would. Think about wind, current, squat, tug use and anchoring in an emergency, not just theory.
  • Read the current DGMA notices before each step. Eligibility, course combinations and portal procedures have changed several times, and advice from a batch two years ago may be out of date.
  • Answer in order. Examiners notice when a candidate goes to paperwork before people and the ship are safe.
  • Say when you don't know. "I would refer to the company and flag guidance on that" is better than making up a rule.
Key points
  • You need a valid First Mate/Chief Mate (FG) CoC and verified sea service after it before you can start the Master process.
  • DGMA sets the sea service required after your Chief Mate (FG) CoC. Check the current eligibility notice on dgshipping.gov.in.
  • The Master's Advanced Shipboard Management (ASM) course is a mandatory step before the Master (FG) exams.
  • Keep your seafarer profile on the DGMA online system updated. If sea service is unverified, the application can't go through.
  • Rank and date mismatches between the CDC and testimonials are a common cause of delays.
  • Master orals test command judgement: safety first, then reporting, then documentation.
  • Check fees, slots, the exam structure and current rules on dgshipping.gov.in, not with agents.

Frequently asked questions

Can I join the Master (FG) ASM course straight after getting my Chief Mate CoC?

Generally no. You need qualifying sea service after the First Mate/Chief Mate (FG) CoC, and that service has to be verified in your online seafarer profile. DGMA sets the amount required, and the rules have been revised before. Check the current eligibility notice on dgshipping.gov.in.

Does sailing as Chief Officer reduce my total sea time requirement?

Under STCW Regulation II/2, the international minimum of 36 months as OOW can come down to at least 24 months if at least 12 months were served as chief mate. The exact Indian requirement is set by DGMA, so check the current eligibility notice on dgshipping.gov.in. Whatever the rule, Chief Officer time only counts if your CDC and testimonials show that rank consistently.

Where do I apply for the Master (FG) exam?

Start from the official DGMA website, dgshipping.gov.in. The current booking route and exam structure are set out in DGMA notices there. Portal names and links have changed over time, so don't rely on a saved link or an agent.

What are Master (FG) orals like compared with Chief Mate orals?

They focus more on command decisions, such as PSC detentions, the master's overriding authority under ISM, dealing with charterers and P&I, emergencies and reporting. Examiners expect you to prioritise as a master would and to name the relevant instrument, not just repeat technical theory.

I am a Filipino Chief Mate. Does this process apply to me?

No. This page covers the Indian CoC issued through DGMA and the MMDs. Filipino officers get a Master certificate through MARINA, which has its own eligibility, course and assessment requirements. Check those directly with MARINA.

Test yourself

Which CoC must you already hold to start the Indian Master (FG) process?

Holding a Chief Mate (FG) CoC is an Indian (DGMA) eligibility requirement for the ASM course and the Master (FG) exams. STCW Regulation II/2 sets only the minimum seagoing service and competence.

What is the Master's Advanced Shipboard Management (ASM) course?

DGS Training Circular 19 of 2004 set up the course to prepare candidates for the Master (FG) examinations. It does not replace the written or oral exams.

Where is the international standard of competence for a master of ships of 3,000 GT or more set out?

Section A-II/2 sets the competence standard for masters and chief mates on ships of 500 GT or more, including ships of 3,000 GT or more. A-II/1 is the OOW standard, A-VI/1 is basic safety training and A-VIII/2 covers watchkeeping principles.

Your CDC shows '2nd Officer' for a contract where your testimonial says 'Chief Officer'. What is the likely result?

Rank-specific service such as Chief Officer time must be backed up consistently across your records. Mismatches are a common reason applications are held up.

Under the ISM Code, where must the master's overriding authority be clearly stated?

ISM Code Section 5 requires the SMS to contain a clear statement of the master's authority, including overriding authority on safety and pollution prevention.
