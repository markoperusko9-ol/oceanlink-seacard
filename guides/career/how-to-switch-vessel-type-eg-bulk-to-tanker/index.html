How to switch vessel type (e.g. bulk to tanker)

Quick answer

Moving between most dry-cargo ship types needs only company familiarisation. Moving to tankers needs certificates under STCW Chapter V. First you need a Certificate of Proficiency (COP) in basic tanker training, known in India as Level 1 DCE. Then you need either three months of service on that type of tanker or one month as a supernumerary with at least three loadings and three discharges. You also need the approved advanced course before you can hold the Level 2 COP that senior officers need. Apply to your own administration: DG Shipping in India or MARINA in the Philippines.

Know which switch you are making

Not every change of ship type works the same way. Before you spend money on courses, work out which kind of move you are making.

Move Extra STCW certificate? What usually stands in your way
Bulk ↔ general cargo ↔ container ↔ car carrier Usually none under STCW Chapter V Company familiarisation, ship-specific training, ECDIS type-specific training, experience the charterer or company asks for
Any dry ship → oil or chemical tanker Yes, under STCW Reg. V/1-1 Basic COP, then tanker sea time, then the advanced course
Any ship → LPG/LNG carrier Yes, under STCW Reg. V/1-2 (separate from oil/chemical) Gas basic COP, gas sea time, gas advanced course
Any ship → gas-fuelled ship Yes, IGF Code training under STCW Reg. V/3 IGF basic or advanced, depending on your duties
Any ship → passenger ship or ro-pax Yes, under STCW Reg. V/2 Crowd management, crisis management and similar courses

This guide concentrates on the move most people ask about: dry cargo to tanker. The same logic applies to gas carriers, but the certificates are separate. An oil tanker COP does not make you eligible for a gas carrier.

The STCW ladder: basic, sea time, advanced

STCW Chapter V sets a two-level structure. Your flag and your company can add to it, but cannot go below it.

Level 1: Basic training for oil and chemical tanker cargo operations (Section A-V/1-1, para 1) - You need this if you are an officer or rating with specific duties connected to cargo or cargo equipment on a tanker. - You can qualify in two ways: complete the approved basic course, or serve at least three months of approved service on oil or chemical tankers and meet the competence standard. In both cases you must already hold STCW basic training (A-VI/1). - In practice most companies will not sign you onto a tanker without it, so do the course ashore first.

Level 2: Advanced training (A-V/1-1 para 2 for oil, para 3 for chemical) - The convention requires it for the master, chief engineer, chief mate, second engineer and anyone with immediate responsibility for loading, discharging, care in transit, cargo handling or tank cleaning. - To qualify, you must hold the basic certificate. Then, while qualified for basic certification, you need either three months of approved service on that tanker type, or one month of approved onboard training as a supernumerary. That month must include at least three loading and three unloading operations and be recorded in an approved training record book. - You must also complete the approved advanced course for that tanker type.

Oil and chemical are separate at advanced level. Service on oil tankers does not qualify you for the chemical advanced COP, and chemical service does not qualify you for the oil one. Gas carriers have their own basic and advanced ladder under Reg. V/1-2.

Step-by-step: bulk carrier officer to oil tanker

  1. Check your existing documents. Your CoC, STCW basic safety COPs, medical and CDC/SIRB must all be valid. Check that the names match exactly in every document.
  2. Do the approved basic oil and chemical tanker course at an institute approved by your administration. Make sure the institute is on the official approved list.
  3. Apply for the basic COP. In India this is called Level 1 DCE and you apply to DG Shipping. In the Philippines you apply to MARINA's STCW Office for the COP.
  4. Get your first tanker berth. Junior officers and ratings can sail on a tanker with the basic COP. Senior ranks cannot, because the convention requires them to hold the advanced COP. Common routes:
    • joining as a junior officer;
    • for an experienced chief mate or second engineer, joining one rank lower for a contract;
    • a supernumerary month arranged by the company, with three loadings and three discharges recorded in an approved record book.
  5. Build your evidence on board. Make sure the ship type is correctly recorded in your CDC/SIRB and on your sea service testimonial. If you sail supernumerary, get every loading and discharge signed off as it happens.
  6. Do the approved advanced oil tanker course.
  7. Apply for the advanced COP. In India this is called Level 2 DCE.
  8. Keep it valid. STCW Reg. I/11 requires masters and officers who continue serving on tankers to prove continued tanker competence at intervals of no more than five years. The exact revalidation conditions (qualifying tanker service or a course) are set by your administration, so check DG Shipping or MARINA before your COP expires.

Some companies also have their own rules on top of STCW, such as an in-house tanker course or a minimum amount of tanker experience before promotion. These are company rules, not convention rules.

India: DG Shipping specifics

  • DCE (Dangerous Cargo Endorsement) is the old, familiar name. DG Shipping now calls these the COP in Basic Training (formerly Level 1 DCE) and the COP in Advanced Training (formerly Level 2 DCE) for oil, chemical or gas tanker cargo operations. Its 'Sea-ries No. 2' FAQ still uses both names.
  • DGS Training Circular No. 06 of 2020 (18.02.2020) sets the course guidelines for the advanced oil, chemical and gas courses. Section 4 of the guidelines sets the entry standards. Read the circular for the current list.
  • DGS Circular No. 22 of 2021 (29.07.2021) is an addendum to Training Circular 06 of 2020. If you only want the advanced course completion certificate, a DGS-approved basic tanker course is enough to enrol. You will not be eligible for the advanced COP/DCE until you complete the tanker sea time STCW requires.
  • Do not assume that doing the advanced course early gets you the DCE early. Read the current circulars and the Sea-ries FAQ on dgshipping.gov.in before you book.
  • Apply online through the seafarer section of dgshipping.gov.in. Check the portal for the current application route. Course certificates must come from DG-approved institutes.

Philippines: MARINA specifics

  • MARINA's STCW Office issues COPs for:
    • basic training for oil and chemical tanker cargo operations;
    • advanced oil tanker training;
    • advanced chemical tanker training;
    • basic and advanced liquefied gas tanker training.
  • MARINA's published documentary requirements list these items:
    • a valid MARINA-recognised medical certificate;
    • the certificate of training completion from a MARINA-accredited course;
    • the Training Completion and Record of Assessment (TCROA), which your MARINA-accredited training institution submits to the STCW Office;
    • the prerequisite COP and proof of qualifying sea service, where these apply.
  • Requirements for issuing and for revalidating a COP are listed separately. Check which one applies to you.
  • These documents date from 2018. Check stcw.marina.gov.ph for the current version.
  • MARINA updates its forms and online appointment steps from time to time. Use the current checklist on stcw.marina.gov.ph rather than an old one from a manning agency.
  • DMW deals with your deployment and contract. It does not issue tanker COPs.

Common rejections and how to avoid them

  • Your sea service does not show the ship type. Your CDC/SIRB or testimonial must clearly say oil tanker or chemical tanker. Ask the master or company for a corrected testimonial before you sign off.
  • The wrong type of tanker. Chemical service submitted for the oil advanced COP, or the other way round. A product/chemical ship may be certified for both. Get the vessel's certification details from the company.
  • A supernumerary month without the evidence. The application fails if it has fewer than three loadings and three discharges, or if the record book is not approved.
  • The advanced course was done before the sea time. Under DGS Circular 22 of 2021 this does not give you DCE eligibility.
  • Tanker sea time served before you qualified for basic certification. Advanced-level sea time must be served while you already qualify for basic certification.
  • An unapproved institute. The course is worthless for your COP if the institute is not on DG Shipping's or MARINA's approved list.
  • Names, dates of birth or INDoS/SRN details that do not match across your certificates.

Oral exam and interview: what examiners and superintendents ask cross-over officers

Crossing over to tankers is often tested in two places: your next CoC oral and the company's technical interview. Expect questions like these.

  • What does the inert gas system do, and what oxygen limits apply to cargo tanks? Answer from SOLAS Chapter II-2 and the FSS Code, not from memory of one ship.
  • What are the flammable range and the 'too lean' and 'too rich' zones? Why do we purge before gas freeing?
  • Talk me through the ship/shore safety checklist (ISGOTT) at the start of a discharge.
  • How do you enter a cargo tank safely? Cover the permit, ventilation, gas readings for oxygen, hydrocarbon and toxic gas, the standby person and rescue equipment.
  • Where does tank washing go, and what goes in the Oil Record Book Part II? (MARPOL Annex I).
  • For chemicals, where do you find the carriage requirements for a product? The IBC Code Chapter 17 and the ship's Certificate of Fitness. Also explain what an SDS tells you.
  • Why does free surface matter more on a tanker during cargo and ballast operations?

If you can explain why each answer is true, not just recite it, you are ready.

Key points
  • Dry-cargo to dry-cargo moves usually need company familiarisation, not a new STCW certificate.
  • To join a tanker you first need the basic COP for oil and chemical tankers (Level 1 DCE in India) under STCW Reg. V/1-1.
  • For the advanced COP you need three months on that tanker type, or one supervised supernumerary month with 3 loadings and 3 discharges, plus the approved advanced course.
  • Oil, chemical and gas experience are not interchangeable at advanced level.
  • Master, chief mate, chief engineer and second engineer must hold the advanced COP, so experienced dry-cargo seniors often cross over at a lower rank or as supernumerary.
  • In India, DGS Circular 22 of 2021: doing the advanced course without the sea time does not give you a DCE.
  • Make sure the ship type is correctly recorded in your CDC/SIRB and testimonials; a missing or wrong ship type is a common reason applications are rejected.

Frequently asked questions

Can I join an oil tanker with only the basic tanker course?

Yes, if your role does not include immediate responsibility for cargo. The basic COP covers officers and ratings with specific cargo-related duties. Master, chief mate, chief engineer, second engineer and anyone directly responsible for cargo operations must hold the advanced COP. Companies may set stricter rules.

Does my bulk carrier sea time count towards the advanced tanker COP?

No. The advanced COP needs service on the relevant tanker type, either three months or a qualifying supernumerary month. Bulk carrier time still counts towards your CoC sea time in the normal way.

I have the oil tanker DCE. Can I sail as chief mate on a chemical tanker?

Not on the strength of your oil DCE alone. Advanced chemical tanker certification needs chemical tanker service or a chemical supernumerary month, plus the advanced chemical course. Check the ship's certification with your company, because some product/chemical ships are certified for both.

Can I do the advanced tanker course before I have any tanker sea time in India?

Under DGS Circular 22 of 2021 you can attend it to improve your knowledge. You will not be eligible for the DCE until you complete the sea service required by STCW. Check the latest DG Shipping circulars before booking in case anything has changed.

Is a gas tanker certificate the same as an oil tanker certificate?

No. Gas carriers come under STCW Reg. V/1-2, with their own basic course, their own sea time requirement and their own advanced course. An oil or chemical COP does not make you eligible for a gas carrier.

Test yourself

Which supernumerary route can replace three months of oil tanker service for the advanced oil tanker COP?

STCW Reg. V/1-1 allows one month of approved supernumerary training with at least three loading and three unloading operations, documented in an approved training record book.

A chief officer with ten years on bulk carriers wants to sail as chief officer on an oil tanker. What must he hold?

STCW Reg. V/1-1 requires masters, chief engineers, chief mates and second engineers on oil tankers to hold advanced oil tanker certification.

Under DGS Circular 22 of 2021, what happens if you complete the advanced tanker course without the required tanker sea time?

DGS Circular 22 of 2021 lets candidates attend to improve their knowledge, but they get no DCE until they meet the STCW sea service requirement.

Which experience counts towards the advanced chemical tanker COP?

STCW Reg. V/1-1 requires the qualifying service or supernumerary training for advanced chemical certification to be on chemical tankers.

In the Philippines, who issues the tanker Certificate of Proficiency?

MARINA, through its STCW Office, issues COPs. The training centre only issues the course completion certificate, which is one of the documents MARINA requires.
