Seafarer Employment Agreement (SEA): what to check before signing

Quick answer

Under MLC 2006, your Seafarer Employment Agreement must state certain particulars, including wages, paid leave, termination terms, repatriation, and health and social security cover. You must also get time to read it and take advice before you sign. Check every line against what the agency promised, make sure you get your own signed original, and do not sign anything with blanks, in a language you cannot read, or tied to a fee for getting the job.

What the SEA is and why it matters

The SEA is the contract between you and the shipowner, or the shipowner's authorised representative. It governs your service on board. On ships covered by MLC 2006, Regulation 2.1 requires every seafarer to have one, and the flag state must make sure the ship complies.

The Convention sets the minimum. The flag state's national law decides the exact form of the document and how its particulars are recorded. So an SEA on a Panama ship, a Liberia ship and an Indian ship can look quite different, but each must still cover the same core terms.

Many SEAs are short documents that bring in a collective bargaining agreement (CBA) by reference. If yours does, the CBA is part of your terms. A copy of it must be available on board.

When something goes wrong, such as a wage dispute, an injury, early sign-off or repatriation, the first question asked is "What does your SEA say?" Whatever you sign at the agency office or in the master's cabin is what you'll be held to.

The particulars MLC requires in every SEA

Standard A2.1 lists the particulars that every SEA must contain, in all cases. Check that each one is present and filled in:

Particular What to check
Your full name, date of birth or age, and birthplace Spelling matches your passport and CDC/SIRB exactly
Shipowner's name and address The shipowner as MLC defines it: the owner, or the manager or bareboat charterer that has taken on the owner's MLC responsibilities. It should not be just a local manning agent with no stated principal
Place and date the SEA is signed Filled in, not left blank
Capacity (rank) Matches the rank you were offered and hold a valid CoC or endorsement for
Wages, or the formula used to work them out Basic pay, overtime and allowances are clearly stated, not just "as per company scale"
Paid annual leave, or the formula No less than the MLC minimum (see below)
Termination and its conditions Fixed-term: the expiry date. Voyage: the destination port and how long after arrival you are discharged. Indefinite: the conditions for either side to end it, and notice periods, which must not be shorter for the shipowner than for you
Health and social security benefits from the shipowner Medical care, sick pay, and compensation for injury, disability and death
Repatriation entitlement Who pays, to where, and when you become entitled
Reference to a CBA, if one applies The CBA's name and parties are identified
Any other particulars national law requires These vary by flag

If any of these are missing, ask before you sign, not after you join.

Step-by-step: checking your SEA before signing

  1. Ask for the draft early. MLC says you must have the chance to examine the agreement and seek advice before signing. A reputable agency will give you the draft ahead of your joining date, not at the airport.
  2. Check the ship and the owner. The vessel name, IMO number, flag and owner should match the offer letter. If the agent signs on the owner's behalf, it should be clear who the employer is.
  3. Check duration and tolerance. Note the contract period and any "plus or minus" option. Work out the latest date you could be kept on board. MLC requires the maximum service period before you're entitled to repatriation to be less than 12 months. The flag or CBA may set a shorter limit.
  4. Check wages line by line. Look at the currency, the monthly breakdown, overtime (fixed or hourly), leave pay and allotment arrangements. MLC requires wages to be paid at least monthly, with a monthly account to you.
  5. Check termination. Look at the notice periods. Check what happens if you have to leave for compassionate reasons, or if the ship is sold, laid up or sent into a war-risk area.
  6. Check medical and compensation cover. Find the clauses on sick pay, medical treatment ashore, and compensation for disability and death. Note whether they're in the SEA itself or in a CBA.
  7. Check repatriation. The SEA should say where you'll be repatriated to and that the shipowner pays.
  8. Check the CBA reference. If a CBA applies, ask for a copy or at least its name. Ask whether it's an ITF-approved agreement or a national union agreement.
  9. Read the version you sign on board. Compare it word for word with what you were shown ashore.
  10. Keep your original. You and the shipowner must each have a signed original. A photo on your phone is not a substitute.

Minimum terms you can check against

These are MLC 2006 minimums. Your flag state, a CBA or national law can give you better terms, but not worse ones.

  • Notice of early termination: national law must set minimum notice periods for both you and the shipowner. These cannot be shorter than seven days. When setting these rules, national law must take into account your need to end the agreement without penalty, on shorter notice or with no notice, for compassionate or other urgent reasons.
  • Paid annual leave: at least 2.5 calendar days per month of employment, worked out as national law sets. Agreements to give up the minimum leave are generally prohibited, except where the authority allows it.
  • Repatriation: you are entitled to repatriation at no cost to you. The shipowner cannot ask you for an advance towards it, and cannot recover it from your wages except where you are found in serious default of your obligations under national law, regulations or the applicable agreement.
  • Hours of work or rest: the flag state sets either maximum hours of work (no more than 14 in any 24 hours and 72 in any 7 days) or minimum hours of rest (at least 10 in any 24 hours and 77 in any 7 days).
  • Medical care: medical care on board and when landed in a foreign port is generally at no cost to you.
  • Financial security: the shipowner must have financial security covering abandonment and its liability for death or long-term disability. On ships that must hold MLC certification, the financial security certificates must be posted in a conspicuous place on board where crew can see them.
  • Record of employment: you must get a document recording your service. It must not comment on how well you worked or what you were paid.

India and the Philippines: national layers to know

India. The Directorate General of Shipping regulates recruitment and placement of seafarers under the Merchant Shipping (Recruitment and Placement of Seafarers) Rules, 2016. MLC 2006 requirements are given effect through the Merchant Shipping (Maritime Labour) Rules, 2016. No person may carry on recruitment and placement of seafarers in India, for an Indian or a foreign ship, without a valid RPSL (Recruitment and Placement Service Licence). Check the agency's licence on DG Shipping's RPSL page (https://betadgs.dgshipping.gov.in/crew-division/crew-rpsl, or its current equivalent on dgshipping.gov.in) before you deal with them. For Indian-flag crew agreement requirements, check the current Merchant Shipping Act and DG Shipping circulars. Do not rely on what a senior or an agent tells you.

Philippines. Filipino seafarers' contracts follow the DMW-administered Standard Terms and Conditions (formerly the POEA Standard Employment Contract), as updated. A CBA may give you better terms on top. Before signing, check the current version, any changes under the Magna Carta of Filipino Seafarers (RA 12021), and the agency's licence status on https://www.dmw.gov.ph.

Other flags. Where the flag differs from your nationality, both the flag's MLC laws and your home country's recruitment rules apply. If they conflict, get advice from the relevant administration or your union before you sign.

Worked scenario: the contract changes at sign-on

Situation. A 3rd Officer accepts an offer for a contract of a stated number of months on a bulk carrier. The agency shows a wage breakdown and names a CBA. On joining, the master hands over an SEA. The period now has a longer "plus or minus" tolerance, the wage is a single consolidated figure, and there's no CBA reference.

What to do:

  1. Do not sign straight away. You are entitled to read it and take advice first.
  2. Politely point out the differences to the master, and ask that the office or the agency confirm in writing which version applies.
  3. Contact the agency through official channels and keep the emails.
  4. If it isn't resolved, use the on-board complaint procedure. Under MLC, you must be given a copy of it, and it names contact points, including the flag state.
  5. Further options are the flag state, your union or welfare body, and port state control in the next port. Indian seafarers can also use the complaint facility currently listed on dgshipping.gov.in. Filipino seafarers can approach the DMW.

Lesson: "contract substitution" is one of the most common problems seafarers report. Keep a copy or photo of everything the agency showed you before departure, so you can prove what was agreed.

Red flags that should stop you signing

  • A fee for the job. Under MLC, a recruitment and placement service must not charge you, directly or indirectly, for finding you employment. The only exceptions are the cost of your national statutory medical certificate, national seafarer's book, and passport or similar personal travel document. Visa costs must be borne by the shipowner.
  • Blank spaces for wages, dates, rank or ship name, or a blank page you're asked to sign.
  • A contract you cannot read. Ask for a version in a language you understand. Where the SEA or CBA is not in English, ships must also keep an English copy of the standard-form SEA and the CBA sections that port state control inspects.
  • No copy for you. Refusing to give you a signed original is a breach in itself.
  • The agency cannot be verified on DG Shipping's RPSL list or as a DMW-licensed agency on dmw.gov.ph.
  • Waivers in which you give up your leave, repatriation or compensation rights.
  • Wage terms that only say "as per company policy" with no figure or formula.
  • A rank different from your certificate, for example signing as a rating while doing an officer's duties.

Oral exam corner: SEA questions examiners ask

MLC questions come up in deck and engine oral exams, and the SEA is a frequent topic. Be ready for these:

  • "What particulars must an SEA contain?" Go through the Standard A2.1 list. Examiners want to hear name, owner, capacity, wages, leave, termination, health and social security benefits, repatriation and the CBA reference, not just "wages and duration".
  • "As master, what do you check when a seafarer signs on?" The SEA is signed by both parties, the seafarer has had a chance to examine it, each side has an original, the CoC/endorsements and medical certificate are valid, and the copies held on board are in order.
  • "A crew member's contract has expired and no relief is arranged. What now?" Look at the repatriation entitlement, the maximum service period under the flag's law, the duty to repatriate at no cost to the seafarer, and the need to inform the company and record what happened.
  • "What documents relating to the SEA might a PSC officer ask for?" The SEAs, the English copy of the standard-form SEA and the relevant CBA parts (where these are not in English), wage accounts, hours of rest records, and the financial security certificates.

Answer from the Convention, then add "subject to flag state requirements and the company SMS". Examiners like candidates who know where the minimum ends and where flag rules take over.

Key points
  • MLC 2006 requires every seafarer to have an SEA signed by both the seafarer and the shipowner or their representative.
  • You are entitled to examine the SEA and seek advice before signing, and to keep your own signed original.
  • Every SEA must state wages, paid leave, termination conditions, health and social security benefits, and repatriation entitlement.
  • Minimum notice for early termination cannot be shorter than seven days, and minimum paid leave is 2.5 calendar days per month.
  • The maximum service period before you're entitled to repatriation must be less than 12 months. Your flag or CBA may set a shorter limit.
  • Recruitment agencies must not charge you for finding you a job. Verify RPSL (India) or DMW (Philippines) licences on the official portals.
  • Compare the SEA you sign on board with what the agency showed you ashore, and keep evidence of both.

Frequently asked questions

Can I take my SEA home to read before signing?

MLC 2006 requires that you have the chance to examine the agreement and seek advice before signing. In practice, ask the agency for the draft before your joining date. If you're pressured to sign at once without reading it, that's a warning sign.

Is it legal for an agency to charge me a placement or processing fee?

Under MLC 2006, recruitment and placement services must not charge seafarers for finding them employment. The only exceptions are the cost of your national statutory medical certificate, national seafarer's book, and passport or similar personal travel document. Visa costs must be borne by the shipowner. National rules also apply, so check the current requirements with DG Shipping (India) or the DMW (Philippines) and report any fee demands to them.

My contract says 'plus or minus' some months. Can the company keep me on board as long as it likes?

No. The tolerance must stay within the maximum service period set by the flag state's law, which under MLC must be less than 12 months. A CBA may set a shorter limit. Work out your latest possible sign-off date before you sign.

What if the SEA refers to a CBA I have never seen?

The CBA becomes part of your terms. MLC requires a copy to be available on board. Ask the agency or the master for it, or at least for its name and the parties to it, so you know which wage scale, compensation and leave terms apply.

Who do I complain to if my SEA is not honoured?

Start with the on-board complaint procedure, a copy of which must be given to you. Then go to the company, the flag state, your union or welfare body, or port state control. Indian seafarers can also use the complaint facility currently listed on dgshipping.gov.in, and Filipino seafarers can approach the DMW.

Standard A2.1 says minimum notice periods for early termination must not be shorter than seven days. National law must also take into account the seafarer's need to end the agreement on shorter notice or with no notice, without penalty, for compassionate or urgent reasons.

Who must hold a signed original of the SEA?

Standard A2.1 requires that both the seafarer and the shipowner each have a signed original of the agreement.

What is the MLC minimum paid annual leave?

Standard A2.4 sets a minimum of 2.5 calendar days of paid annual leave per month of employment, worked out as national law sets.

Under MLC 2006, which cost may a seafarer be expected to bear when recruited?

Standard A1.4 bans recruitment charges to seafarers, except only the costs of the national statutory medical certificate, national seafarer's book and passport or similar personal travel document. Visa costs are borne by the shipowner.

What does MLC 2006 say about the maximum period of service on board before a seafarer is entitled to repatriation?

Standard A2.5.1 requires national law to set maximum service periods of less than 12 months, after which the seafarer is entitled to repatriation.
