IMU CET: the entrance exam for merchant navy courses

Quick answer

The Indian Maritime University Common Entrance Test (IMU CET) is the main entrance test for pre-sea merchant navy officer courses in India. Under DG Shipping Training Circular 12 of 2020, it covers IMU campuses and DGS-approved private institutes, so you usually need a valid CET rank for DNS, B.Sc Nautical Science or B.Tech Marine Engineering, even with a company sponsorship. DGS Training Circular 21 of 2025 also provides for other admission pathways and for filling seats left unfilled after IMU CET, so check the current DGS circulars and the institute's admission notice, and use the official IMU website (imu.edu.in) for registration, admit cards, rank cards and allotment letters.

What IMU CET is and why it matters for pre-sea officer courses

The Indian Maritime University runs IMU CET once a year. It is a computer-based test for admission to IMU's undergraduate and postgraduate programmes. If you are heading to sea, the undergraduate courses that matter are the pre-sea ones:

  • DNS (Diploma in Nautical Science)
  • B.Sc Nautical Science
  • B.Tech Marine Engineering

The test also matters outside IMU's own campuses. DG Shipping Training Circular 12 of 2020 set minimum eligibility criteria for admission to pre-sea maritime courses. It also introduced the CET and online counselling. This is why DGS-approved private institutes also look for a CET rank. Training Circular 16 of 2021 added to these rules.

Training Circular 21 of 2025 provides for other admission pathways and for filling seats left unfilled after IMU CET. Check the current DGS circulars and the institute's admission notice for your route. Not every route to sea goes through the CET. This page covers the pre-sea officer courses.

In practice:

  • IMU campuses fill their seats through IMU's online counselling, based on your CET rank.
  • Private and company-run academies usually ask for a CET rank as a minimum. Many then run their own written test and interview. Some academies say openly that their merit list combines your Class XII PCM percentage, their own test, an interview and your IMU CET rank.
  • A sponsorship letter does not normally replace the CET. Expect to need both.

This is an Indian route only. Philippine maritime programmes follow CHED and MARINA rules, so Filipino readers should not use this page for admissions.

Eligibility: check these before you pay a single rupee

IMU publishes the exact criteria for each year in its information brochure. Read the current one, because coaching websites often copy old figures.

IMU's past eligibility documents (2016 onward) have set these benchmarks for the nautical and marine engineering programmes:

Criterion From past IMU documents, 2016 onward (verify in the current brochure)
Academics 10+2 or equivalent with Physics, Chemistry and Maths, minimum 60% average in PCM
English Minimum 50% in English in Class 10, Class 12 or degree
Age Minimum 17 years. The upper limit for general candidates has been 25, with relaxations for some categories
Eyesight (nautical courses) Unaided distance vision of 6/6 in the better eye. Standards for the other eye and for colour vision are in the current brochure
Medical You must be medically fit to sea-going standards before admission is confirmed

Points that catch people out:

  • Age is calculated on a cut-off date set in the brochure. It is not your age on the exam day.
  • Reserved category relaxations. Category-wise age relaxations are listed in the IMU brochure. DGS has amended Training Circular 12 of 2020 over time, so check the current brochure. Have your category certificate in the format the brochure asks for.
  • English requirement. Mark relaxations for reserved categories do not always apply to it. Read the brochure footnotes.
  • Eyesight. Deck candidates should get their eyes tested, including colour vision, before sitting the CET. If you fail the eye test at verification, you lose the seat however good your rank is.
  • Extra conditions. Some programmes and sponsors add their own conditions, such as holding a valid passport by course start or marital status. Check the brochure and the sponsor's advert.

Step-by-step: from registration to joining

IMU announces each stage as a numbered notification on its Notifications page. Recent cycles have followed this order:

  1. Read the notification and information brochure on imu.edu.in. These are the only binding documents for dates, fees, exam pattern and eligibility.
  2. Register online on the IMU admission portal. Use your own email address and mobile number, not an agent's. Upload your photo, signature and documents in the sizes stated. IMU has extended deadlines by notification in past cycles, but don't plan around it.
  3. Select your test city when the city-selection window opens. In recent cycles, IMU has warned that a centre can sometimes be allotted outside your preferred choice. Check the current notification on imu.edu.in.
  4. Download the admit card from the admission portal. IMU tells candidates to check the details on it. Bring a printed copy and a valid photo ID to the centre.
  5. Sit the test. In recent cycles, IMU has published a normalisation procedure for comparing scores when the test runs in more than one session. Check the current notification on imu.edu.in.
  6. Results and rank card. IMU declares results by notification and opens a portal where you download the rank card. In recent cycles it has also published cut-off percentiles for the UG programmes (DNS, B.Sc, B.Tech). Check the current notification on imu.edu.in.
  7. Register for online counselling. Enter your programme and campus preferences in order.
  8. Seat allotment. Log in and download the provisional seat allotment letter. Confirm the seat by paying the programme fee online before the deadline.
  9. Report for verification at the allotted campus within the window given. Expect checks of original documents, confirmation of medical fitness and payment of first-semester fees.

If you are aiming for a private or company academy, apply to them as well. Their application windows often run alongside the CET cycle, and some close before CET results are out.

The exam itself and how to prepare

For the undergraduate technical programmes, the CET is a single computer-based multiple-choice paper at Class 11–12 level. It covers:

  • Mathematics
  • Physics
  • Chemistry
  • English
  • General aptitude and awareness

The brochure for your year gives the number of questions, the duration and the marking scheme. Coaching sites disagree on details such as negative marking, so take these from the brochure only. If wrong answers are penalised, don't guess blindly.

Preparation that works:

  • Maths and Physics carry the technical weight. Revise trigonometry, vectors, basic calculus, mechanics, and heat and waves. Your later navigation and engineering studies build directly on these.
  • English is both an eligibility requirement and part of the paper. Expect comprehension, grammar and vocabulary.
  • Aptitude gives quick marks if you practise timed sets.
  • Use IMU's own sample or previous papers where they are published. Sit at least two full papers in exam time.
  • Practise on a screen. It is a CBT, so get used to flagging and reviewing questions on a computer.

On exam day, bring the printed admit card and the photo ID named on it. Bring nothing else unless the instructions allow it. IMU publishes penalty and debarment notices for malpractice, and a debarment can affect you in later cycles. A borrowed calculator or a phone in your pocket is not worth the risk.

Documents for counselling and verification

IMU's instructions to candidates with a provisionally allotted seat have listed the originals and copies to bring. Build this file before results day:

  • Printed IMU CET rank certificate or rank card
  • Provisional seat allotment letter (the latest one, if you were upgraded)
  • Proof of age, such as your Class 10 certificate or marksheet
  • Class 10 and Class 12 marksheets and certificates, plus degree marksheets if your English percentage comes from your degree
  • Category, domicile or NCC certificates if you are claiming them, in the current format
  • Medical fitness certificate in the format the brochure prescribes. IMU requires this for B.Sc Nautical Science, DNS and B.Tech Marine Engineering. For sea-going courses, expect to see a doctor approved by DG Shipping. The brochure says who may issue it.
  • Passport, where applicable
  • Passport-size photographs (IMU has asked for two copies in past cycles)
  • Receipt for the programme fee you paid online

The allotment letter is strictly provisional. Your seat depends on paying the fee on time, passing document verification and confirming medical fitness. IMU has stated that the programme fee paid to accept a seat is non-refundable. Check the current terms before you pay.

Common reasons candidates lose a seat

Most candidates who lose a seat do so because of paperwork or medical problems, not a low rank:

  • Missed fee deadline. Under past IMU instructions, a candidate who does not pay the programme fee by the date given is treated as a drop-out from the counselling process.
  • Eyesight or colour vision failure at the medical. This is the most painful one, because you can usually avoid it by getting tested early.
  • English below 50% in every qualifying exam.
  • Over age on the brochure's cut-off date, or a category certificate in the wrong format, so the relaxation cannot be applied.
  • Name or date-of-birth mismatches between your marksheets, Aadhaar, passport and application. Get these corrected by the issuing authority before you apply.
  • Not reporting to the allotted campus within the verification window.
  • Malpractice at the test centre, which can lead to debarment.

About upgrades: if your allotted seat was not your first preference, IMU can upgrade you in a later allotment list if a seat becomes available. Read each round's instructions carefully. Choosing to freeze your seat or to allow upgrades has consequences, and each round's instructions explain them.

Worked scenario and interview-style questions

Scenario. Priya is 18. She has 72% in PCM and 64% in Class 12 English. She wants a deck cadetship with a shipping company that runs its own DNS academy.

  1. She gets a full eye and medical check-up before CET registration. Her vision is within the brochure limits.
  2. She registers for IMU CET herself on imu.edu.in. She picks DNS and B.Sc Nautical Science as preferences.
  3. At the same time, she applies to the company academy, which runs its own online test and interview.
  4. She gets a CET rank. The academy builds its merit list from her PCM percentage, its own test, the interview and her CET rank, and makes her an offer.
  5. She compares that offer with her IMU counselling allotment before paying any non-refundable fee.

Questions sponsors and academies commonly ask. Have short, honest answers ready:

  • Why sea, and why deck rather than engine (or the other way round)?
  • What is the difference between DNS and B.Sc Nautical Science, and where does each lead?
  • What does a deck cadet do on board in the first month?
  • How will you cope with months away from home and with a multinational crew?
  • Can you explain a simple physics idea, such as buoyancy or why a ship floats?

After admission, the usual next steps are an INDoS number, STCW basic safety courses and a CDC. Your institute or sponsor will guide you through them.

Key points
  • Under DGS TC 12 of 2020, IMU CET is the main entrance route for DGS-approved pre-sea courses. TC 21 of 2025 adds other pathways, so check current DGS circulars.
  • A company sponsorship normally does not replace a CET rank. Most academies use both.
  • Treat only the current IMU information brochure and the notifications on imu.edu.in as binding for eligibility, exam pattern, fees and dates.
  • IMU has published a benchmark of 60% in PCM and 50% in English for the nautical and marine engineering programmes. Check the age cut-off date and relaxations every year.
  • Get your eyes and medical fitness checked before you register. A failed medical cancels a seat whatever your rank.
  • A seat allotment is provisional until you pay the fee on time, pass document verification and confirm medical fitness.
  • Register with your own email address and mobile number, and ignore unofficial 'guaranteed seat' offers.

Frequently asked questions

Is IMU CET compulsory for DNS at a private maritime institute?

Usually, yes, but see DGS Training Circular 21 of 2025. Training Circular 12 of 2020 introduced the CET and online counselling as part of the minimum admission criteria for pre-sea courses. Training Circular 21 of 2025 also provides for other admission pathways and for filling seats left unfilled after IMU CET. Private academies usually ask for a valid CET rank and may add their own test and interview, so check the current DGS circulars and the institute's admission notice.

What marks do I need in Class 12 for IMU CET nautical courses?

IMU's published criteria have required an average of at least 60% in Physics, Chemistry and Maths. They have also required at least 50% in English in Class 10, Class 12 or a degree. Relaxations for reserved categories are listed in the brochure and may not apply to the English requirement. Check the current year's brochure before you register.

Can I apply for IMU CET if I wear spectacles?

For nautical programmes, IMU has required unaided distance vision of 6/6 in the better eye, along with other vision standards. Engineering programmes have different limits. The brochure sets out the rules on corrective surgery and colour vision. Get a proper eye test before you register, not after you get a rank.

Where do I download the IMU CET admit card and rank card?

Both come from the IMU admission portal, which is linked from imu.edu.in. IMU announces on its Notifications page when each one is ready. Bring a printed admit card and a valid photo ID to the exam centre.

What happens if I don't pay the programme fee after seat allotment?

Under IMU's past counselling instructions, a candidate who misses the payment deadline is treated as a drop-out from that counselling process. IMU has also stated that the fee to accept a seat is non-refundable. Read the instructions for each round before you decide.
