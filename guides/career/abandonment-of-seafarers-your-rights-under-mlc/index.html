Abandonment of seafarers: your rights under MLC 2006 and how to use them

Quick answer

Under MLC 2006 as amended, you are abandoned if the shipowner does any of three things. They fail to pay for your repatriation, they leave you without the support you need to live, or they otherwise cut ties with the crew, which includes leaving contractual wages unpaid for at least two months. Every certified ship must carry financial security that pays up to four months of outstanding wages, repatriation costs and essential needs. You can claim directly from the security provider named on the certificate posted on board.

What counts as abandonment under MLC

The 2014 amendments to MLC 2006 added a formal definition of abandonment to Standard A2.5.2. They also made financial security compulsory. These amendments came into force in January 2017.

You are treated as abandoned if the shipowner does any one of the following:

  • Fails to pay for your repatriation.
  • Leaves you without the support you need to live. Food, water, fuel for the generator and medical care stop arriving.
  • Otherwise cuts ties with the crew. This specifically includes not paying contractual wages for at least two months.

You don't need all three. A ship with full provisions is still an abandonment case if wages have been unpaid for two months and the owner has gone quiet.

Two points the exam and the insurers both care about:

  • "Shipowner" in MLC means whoever has taken on the owner's responsibilities. That can be a manager or bareboat charterer, not only the registered owner.
  • Abandonment is different from an ordinary wage dispute. A one-month delay with the owner still talking to you is a complaint under Regulation 5.1.5 (on-board complaint procedures), not yet abandonment.

What the financial security must pay for

Flag States must make sure their ships have a financial security system in place. In practice this is usually a P&I club or an insurer. The system must give you direct access to money, and must do so quickly.

Cover What it includes
Outstanding wages Wages and other entitlements under your SEA, any collective agreement or national law. Limited to four months.
Repatriation Travel home by appropriate and quick means (normally air), food and accommodation from leaving the ship until you reach home, necessary medical care, transport of personal effects, and any other reasonable costs or charges arising from the abandonment
Essential needs Adequate food, clothing where needed, accommodation, drinking water, fuel needed to survive on board, and necessary medical care

Other things to know:

  • Request and evidence: Assistance must be given promptly once you, or a representative you nominate, make a request backed by evidence that you are entitled to it.
  • Notice before the cover ends: The provider cannot simply end the cover partway through. It must give the flag State at least 30 days' notice first.
  • Your other claims stay alive: Being paid by the insurer does not stop you pursuing the balance by other legal routes. This applies especially to wages beyond the four-month cap.
  • The insurer takes over the claim: Once the provider pays, it takes over your rights against the owner (subrogation). That is its problem, not yours.

The certificate on board: check it before you need it

Certain ships must carry a certificate or other document proving this financial security. These are ships covered by the MLC certification regime: ships of 500 GT and above engaged in international voyages or operating from a port, or between ports, in another country, plus any other ship whose owner asks to be certified (Regulation 5.1.3). The certificate must be:

  • Posted in a conspicuous place where the crew can see it.
  • In English, or accompanied by an English translation.
  • Complete. It must show the information listed in Appendix A2-I. This includes the provider's name and contact details, the validity period and a statement that it meets Standard A2.5.2.

There is also a separate requirement for a certificate covering shipowners' liability for death and long-term disability (Standard A4.2.1). Depending on the provider and flag, the two may be issued as separate certificates or as one combined document.

Do this within your first week on board:

  1. Find the notice. It is usually near the crew mess, the gangway or the master's office.
  2. Photograph it. Make sure the provider's name, policy number, expiry date and contact numbers are readable.
  3. Save the photo off the ship, for example to your email or cloud storage.

If the ship is later arrested or loses power, you may not be able to get back into the office. A missing or expired certificate is a point that port State control inspectors check under the MLC.

Step-by-step: what to do if you think you are being abandoned

  1. Keep the ship safe and stay together. Do not walk off as a group without a plan. The ship's Minimum Safe Manning Document and local port rules may still require a minimum crew on board. Leaving without arranged repatriation can weaken your position.
  2. Write down the facts each day. Record:
    • the date wages were last paid
    • stores remaining
    • fuel and water levels
    • generator hours
    • medical problems
    • every message from the owner, manager and agent
  3. Collect your documents. Get copies of:
    • your SEA
    • wage slips or portage bills
    • bank statements showing missed payments
    • your CDC or seaman's book
    • your passport
    • the financial security certificate
  4. Contact the financial security provider directly, using the details on the certificate. State that you are claiming under MLC Standard A2.5.2. Name the ship, IMO number, flag, port and number of crew. Attach the evidence. A union or the ITF can act as your nominated representative if the crew agrees.
  5. Notify the authorities in parallel:
    • Flag State: it has primary responsibility for repatriation when the owner fails.
    • Port State authority: usually through port State control or the harbour master.
    • Your own country:
    • Indian seafarers: the Directorate General of Shipping (DG Shipping).
    • Filipino seafarers: the Department of Migrant Workers (DMW) and OWWA, plus your manning agency.
    • Check the DG Shipping website (dgshipping.gov.in) and the DMW/OWWA websites for the current complaint or assistance channel, as these change.
  6. Contact a welfare body. ITF inspectors, your union, port welfare centres and the SeafarerHelp 24-hour helpline run by ISWAN can all help. They can also make sure the case is reported to the ILO/IMO Joint Database on Abandonment of Seafarers.
  7. Don't sign anything. Do not sign releases, quitclaims or "final settlement" papers for partial payment until someone you trust has read them.

Worked scenario: A bulk carrier is anchored off a West African port. Wages were last paid 10 weeks ago and the manager has stopped answering. Provisions will last eight days.

  • The two-month wage test is already met, so this is abandonment.
  • The master or a crew representative should email the P&I club named on the certificate the same day, with evidence of the missed wages and the stores level.
  • The flag State and the seafarers' national administrations should be copied in.
  • Request essential needs first (food, water, fuel). Request wages and repatriation alongside.

Who else is responsible: flag, port, nationality and your agency

MLC Regulation 2.5 and Standard A2.5.1 set up a chain of responsibility:

  • Shipowner first. The owner must pay for repatriation. It cannot ask you for an advance or take the cost from your wages, except where you have been found in serious default of your obligations.
  • Flag State next. If the owner fails to arrange and pay for repatriation, the flag State must do it. It can then recover the cost from the owner.
  • State of repatriation or nationality as backup. If the flag State also fails, the State from which you are being repatriated or your country of nationality may repatriate you and recover the cost from the flag State. Any State that has paid repatriation costs may detain, or ask for the detention of, the shipowner's ships until it is repaid (Standard A2.5.1, paras 5–6).
  • Recruitment and placement services (RPS). Your manning agency also carries duties. Countries that run these services must make them maintain insurance or an equivalent system. It compensates seafarers for money lost when the agency or the shipowner fails to meet its obligations to them (Standard A1.4).
    • India: DG Shipping licenses these agencies as RPSL agencies.
    • Philippines: DMW licenses manning agencies. Under Philippine law the local agency is generally held jointly liable with its foreign principal.

The Joint ILO–IMO Tripartite Working Group adopted Guidelines on how to deal with seafarer abandonment cases in December 2022. They are linked from the ILO/IMO Joint Database on Abandonment of Seafarers.

Common reasons claims stall, and how to avoid them

  • No evidence of unpaid wages.
    • Why it stalls: Insurers want proof.
    • What to do: Keep wage slips, bank statements and the SEA. If allotments were paid to family, get the family's bank records too.
  • Two-month threshold not met.
    • Why it stalls: The claim was filed too early.
    • What to do: Use the on-board complaint procedure and the flag State in the meantime, and claim when the threshold is met. If food, water or repatriation fail, those are separate triggers, so don't wait.
  • Wages claimed beyond four months.
    • Why it stalls: That is above the cap.
    • What to do: Claim the four months from the insurer. Pursue the rest through ship arrest, maritime liens or national courts, with legal or union help.
  • Wrong or expired provider.
    • Why it stalls: Cover changed when the owner switched clubs.
    • What to do: Check the dates on the certificate. Ask the flag State to confirm who holds current cover.
  • Claim made by someone with no authority to act for you.
    • Why it stalls: The person was never nominated.
    • What to do: Give a short signed crew authorisation naming the union or ITF representative.
  • Flag State not bound by the 2014 amendments (or not party to MLC).
    • Why it stalls: The ship may carry no compulsory cover.
    • What to do: Check NORMLEX for the flag's MLC status and acceptance of the 2014 amendments. The port State and nationality-State routes still apply.
  • Crew signed a quitclaim.
    • Why it stalls: The paper may be used to argue the claim is settled.
    • What to do: Do not sign under pressure. Get advice first.

Oral exam corner: what the examiner will ask

Abandonment is a common topic in MLC questions. Be ready for these:

  • "Define abandonment under MLC." Give the three triggers: no repatriation, no necessary support, or ties cut, including two months of unpaid wages.
  • "What does the financial security cover?" Up to four months of outstanding wages and entitlements, repatriation costs and essential needs. Say "Standard A2.5.2".
  • "As master, how would you know the ship is covered?" It is posted on board in English as Appendix A2-I evidence. It is checked during flag inspection, under the DMLC and by PSC.
  • "Can the cover be cancelled mid-voyage?" Not without at least 30 days' prior notice to the flag State.
  • "Who repatriates the crew if the owner and the flag fail?" The State from which they are being repatriated or their country of nationality may repatriate them and recover the cost from the flag State. Any State that has paid repatriation costs may detain, or ask for the detention of, the shipowner's ships until it is repaid (Standard A2.5.1, paras 5–6).
  • "Master's actions?" Keep the ship safe and manned, and record the facts. Inform the provider, the flag State, the port authority and the crew's national administrations. Keep the crew informed and look after their welfare.
Key points
  • Abandonment means the owner fails to pay for repatriation, fails to provide support, or cuts ties, including two months of unpaid wages.
  • Financial security must cover up to four months of outstanding wages, repatriation costs and essential needs such as food, water, fuel and medical care.
  • The certificate must be posted conspicuously on board in English, so photograph it when you join.
  • You or your nominated representative can claim directly from the provider, and assistance must be prompt.
  • If the owner fails, the flag State must repatriate you. If the flag State also fails, the State you are repatriated from or your country of nationality may step in.
  • Do not abandon the ship or sign quitclaims without advice, and keep written records and copies of documents.
  • Indian seafarers should also notify DG Shipping. Filipino seafarers should notify DMW, OWWA and their manning agency, and check those official websites for current channels.

Frequently asked questions

How many months of unpaid wages before I am legally abandoned?

Under MLC Standard A2.5.2, failure to pay contractual wages for at least two months counts as the owner cutting ties, which is abandonment. You don't have to wait two months if repatriation or essential support has already failed. Those are separate triggers on their own.

Will the P&I club pay all my unpaid wages?

The MLC financial security only has to cover outstanding wages and entitlements up to four months. You can still claim anything above that by other legal routes, such as ship arrest or national courts, usually with union or legal help. Being paid by the insurer does not cancel those other rights.

Where do I find the insurer's contact details?

On the financial security certificate, which must be posted somewhere conspicuous on board in English. It shows the provider's name and contact details and the validity period. If you can't find it, the flag State can tell you who holds the cover.

Can I leave the ship and fly home on my own?

You can, but it often weakens your claim and may leave the ship unsafely manned. Repatriation should be arranged through the owner, the insurer, the flag State or your national authorities, so the cost is covered and recorded. Get advice from your union, the ITF or your administration first.

What can Indian or Filipino seafarers do from home if a crew member is abandoned?

Family members can contact the national administration: DG Shipping for Indian seafarers, and DMW and OWWA for Filipino seafarers. They can also contact the licensed manning agency, a seafarers' union or the ITF, giving the ship's name, IMO number, location and crew list. Check the DG Shipping website (dgshipping.gov.in) and the DMW/OWWA websites for the current complaint or assistance channel, as these change.

Test yourself

Under MLC Standard A2.5.2, which of these on its own counts as abandonment?

Cutting ties, including failure to pay contractual wages for at least two months, is one of the three abandonment triggers (MLC 2006, Standard A2.5.2).

What is the maximum period of outstanding wages the abandonment financial security must cover?

Cover for outstanding wages and entitlements is limited to four months (MLC 2006, Standard A2.5.2).

Before the financial security can lapse during its validity period, the provider must give:

The security cannot end before its validity expires unless the provider has given the flag State at least 30 days' prior notice (MLC 2006, Standard A2.5.2).

If both the shipowner and the flag State fail to repatriate an abandoned crew, who may do it?

The State from which the crew are being repatriated or their country of nationality may repatriate them and recover the cost from the flag State. Any State that has paid repatriation costs may detain, or ask for the detention of, the shipowner's ships until it is repaid (MLC 2006, Standard A2.5.1, paras 5–6).

The financial security certificate on board must be:

Documentary evidence of the security must be posted in a conspicuous place on board, in English or with an English translation (MLC 2006, Standard A2.5.2 and Appendix A2-I).
