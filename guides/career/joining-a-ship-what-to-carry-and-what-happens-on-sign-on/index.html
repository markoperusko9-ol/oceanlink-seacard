Joining a ship: what to carry and what happens on sign-on

Quick answer

Carry valid originals of your passport, seaman's book (CDC or SIRB), CoC with flag endorsement, STCW certificates, medical certificate, visas and your signed employment agreement. Check that each one stays valid for the whole contract. At sign-on the Master or agent checks your documents, you sign articles or your SEA and join the crew list. You then get ship-specific safety and security familiarisation before you take any duties. Most failures at joining come from expired documents, mismatched names or a missing flag endorsement, and most of these can be sorted out before you leave home if you check early.

The document folder: what every seafarer must carry

Pack originals in one waterproof folder in your hand luggage, never in the checked bag. Keep scans on your phone and in your email as well. Before you lock the bag, check each item against the contract length, not just the joining date.

Document What to check
Passport Validity covers the contract plus a margin. Your company or the visa rules may set a minimum. Leave enough blank pages
Seaman's book (Indian CDC / Philippine SIRB) Valid and not damaged. Previous sign-on and sign-off entries complete
Certificate of Competency or proficiency Correct capacity for the rank you are joining in
Flag State endorsement Issued by the flag of this ship, or proof of application if that flag accepts it
STCW certificates (BST modules, PSCRB, AFF, MFA, security, tanker/gas) Not expired, and right for the ship type
ECDIS training (officers) ECDIS generic training (STCW), plus type-specific training or familiarisation as required by your company/flag
Medical fitness certificate Valid, from a doctor approved by your administration
Vaccination records (e.g. yellow fever) Needed if the trading area or a port requires them
Visas (e.g. US C1/D, transit visas) Needed for the route and for your flights to the joining port
Seafarers' Employment Agreement (SEA) / contract copy Signed. Terms match what you were offered
Letter of guarantee / OK-to-board letter from the agency Often requested by airlines and immigration at transit and arrival airports – carry it

Under MLC Standard A1.2, a medical certificate is normally valid for up to two years (one year for under-18s). Colour-vision certificates have their own maximum period. Your administration may set shorter periods, so go by what is printed on your certificate.

Country-specific steps: India and the Philippines

Indian seafarers

  • DG Shipping (Directorate General of Shipping) launched the e-Samudra portal on 7 April 2026. Log in, verify and update your profile (INDoS, mobile number, email) so you can use online services. Use e-Samudra unless the Directorate tells you otherwise for a specific service. You can reach it through the portal link on dgshipping.gov.in.
  • DG Shipping advises all seafarers to log in to e-Samudra and verify and update their INDoS profile so online services work smoothly.
  • Your RPSL agency or the shipping company enters your sign-on and sign-off in DG Shipping's online system. This builds your sea service record for CoC exams and revalidation.
  • After joining, log in and check that your sign-on date appears. If the entry is missing, contact the RPSL or company first. If they cannot fix it, raise it with DG Shipping through its grievance or crew branch channels. Raise it early, not when you are applying for an exam.
  • Join only through a licensed RPSL agency. Check the licence on the DG Shipping site before you pay for anything or travel.

Filipino seafarers

  • MARINA rules say Filipino seafarers must hold a valid SIRB at all times. Renew it before it lapses, not on board. SIRB rules have been updated over the years, so check marina.gov.ph for the latest requirements.
  • Your licensed manning agency processes your deployment with the Department of Migrant Workers (DMW). This covers contract processing and departure clearance.
  • Requirements and system names change, so check dmw.gov.ph and confirm with your agency before departure.
  • Your MARINA-issued CoC or CoP and the foreign flag endorsement must match your rank on board.

Other nationalities and flags: the same logic applies. Your national seafarer ID or record book, your administration's CoC, and the flag's endorsement. Procedures and processing times differ by administration.

Travel and arriving at the joining port

  1. Get the joining instructions in writing. You need the vessel name, IMO number, port agent's name and 24-hour phone number, the hotel, and the planned boarding time.
  2. At the departure airport, airlines and immigration often ask for the OK-to-board letter, especially if you hold a one-way ticket, so carry it. Seafarers travelling on a one-way ticket to join a ship get questioned often.
  3. In transit, check whether you need a transit visa. Some countries let crew transit visa-free with a seaman's book and letter, and others do not. Your agency should confirm this before you fly.
  4. On arrival, the port agent usually meets you and handles shore-side immigration for crew. In many ports you stay on a crew list or shore pass until you physically board.
  5. Medicines: carry personal medicines in their original packs with a prescription. Some common drugs are controlled in certain countries.
  6. Do not carry anything your company drug and alcohol policy bans, or anything you would have to declare to customs on the crew effects list without being able to explain it.

If the ship is delayed, stay in contact with the agent. Do not go to the port gate on your own.

What actually happens at sign-on: step by step

Worked scenario: you are joining a bulk carrier at anchor as Third Officer. You go out by launch with the agent.

  1. Boarding. You climb the pilot or accommodation ladder wearing a lifejacket if one is issued for the launch transfer. The gangway watch logs you in. Show ID.
  2. Report to the Master's office, usually with the Chief Officer present. Hand over your document folder.
  3. Document check. The Master compares your originals against the crew list. They check validity, names and dates of birth, the flag endorsement and the medical. Copies go into the ship's crew certificate file, which port State control inspectors will look at.
  4. Agreement. You sign the articles of agreement or the SEA, depending on flag and company system. Under MLC Standard A2.1, both you and the shipowner (or representative) must each hold a signed original. You must also have had the chance to review it and seek advice before signing. Do not sign blank or different terms.
  5. Crew list and record of service. You are added to the crew list. Your seaman's book is stamped or entered by the Master. Make sure the sign-on date is correct.
  6. Security and ID. Depending on the ship, you get a cabin key and a crew ID card, and you are briefed on access control under the Ship Security Plan.
  7. Muster list. Find your name, your emergency duties and your lifeboat or liferaft station. Then go and look at them physically.

The agent often takes your passport for immigration formalities. Ask for a receipt or make sure it goes into the ship's safe.

First days on board: familiarisation, handover and drills

Safety familiarisation comes first. Under STCW Regulation VI/1 and Section A-VI/1, everyone employed or engaged on board must receive safety familiarisation before being assigned shipboard duties. Section A-VI/6 adds security-related familiarisation. The company's SMS, written to ISM Code section 6, sets out how this is done and recorded, usually on a familiarisation checklist signed by you and your head of department.

A typical checklist covers:

  • how to raise an alarm and what each alarm signal means
  • your muster station, lifejacket and immersion suit, and escape routes
  • the location of fire extinguishers, fire alarm call points, EEBDs and the emergency stop
  • the watertight and fire door rules
  • the ship's permit-to-work, enclosed space and drug and alcohol policies

Handover from your reliever:

  • Deck: the status of charts and publications, outstanding PSC and audit items, LSA/FFA records if you are taking that duty, passwords, and keys.
  • Engine: the running hours and condition of your machinery, PMS jobs overdue, spares status, and known defects.
  • Get it in writing. A handover note protects both of you.

Drills and training:

  • Under SOLAS Chapter III, Regulation 19, on-board training in the use of the ship's life-saving appliances must be given as soon as possible, and not later than two weeks after you join.
  • If more than 25% of the crew have not taken part in drills on that ship in the previous month, abandon ship and fire drills must be held within 24 hours of leaving port.
  • Take part properly. The record goes in the official log or the company's drill log.

Hours of rest apply from the moment you sign on. Record them honestly.

Common reasons seafarers are stopped at joining, and how to avoid them

Problem What to do
A certificate expires during the contract Revalidate before you leave. Your company may refuse to send you, and PSC may detain the ship
Name spelt differently on passport, CDC/SIRB and CoC Get it corrected with the issuing authority before you join. Agents cannot fix this at the airport
No flag endorsement, or wrong capacity on it Confirm with the company that it is applied for. Rules on sailing with proof of application differ by flag
Tanker/gas endorsement missing for the ship type Your national DCE or equivalent must match the cargo
Medical certificate from a doctor your administration has not approved Use only the approved doctor list on your administration's website
Visa missing for transit or a scheduled port Check the route with the agency. US port calls need a crew (D) visa, usually issued as a combined C1/D. Joining or transiting through the US needs the C1 element
Sign-on not recorded in your national system Check your online profile within days and chase the RPSL or manning agency

On a medical certificate that expires during a voyage: MLC Standard A1.2 lets it stay in force until the next port of call where a recognised doctor is available, for up to three months. This is a safety net, not a plan. Many companies will not send you with a short-dated medical.

Oral exam corner: joining-ship questions examiners ask

Q: You join as OOW. Can you keep a watch the same evening? Only after the ship-specific familiarisation required by STCW and the company SMS is done, and the Master is satisfied you are ready. Bridge familiarisation should also cover the ship's ECDIS: you must be familiar with the installed ECDIS type, as required by your company SMS and flag.

Q: What must you check on the muster list? Your emergency duties, your survival craft and station, the alarm signals, and who replaces key people if they are injured. SOLAS requires the muster list to be posted in conspicuous places.

Q: Who holds the SEA originals? Both you and the shipowner (or its representative) hold a signed original under MLC Standard A2.1. A copy of the agreement and any applicable collective agreement must also be available on board for PSC.

Q: A new rating cannot find their immersion suit. What do you do? Stop and fix it now. Show them the suit, check the size, and record it on the familiarisation checklist. This is a basic familiarisation gap, and PSC inspectors test it.

Key points
  • Carry originals in your hand luggage and check each document's validity against the contract length, not the joining date.
  • The flag endorsement must be for the flag of the ship you are joining and for your actual rank.
  • Indian seafarers: keep INDoS current and check that the RPSL or company has recorded sign-on in the DG Shipping system (now e-Samudra).
  • Filipino seafarers: keep the SIRB valid at all times and complete DMW deployment steps through a licensed manning agency.
  • Under MLC, review your SEA before signing, and keep your own signed original.
  • Safety and security familiarisation must be done before you take any shipboard duties.
  • LSA on-board training must happen within two weeks of joining under SOLAS III/19.

Frequently asked questions

What documents do I need to join a ship?

You need your passport, seaman's book (CDC or SIRB), CoC or CoP with the flag State endorsement, STCW certificates, medical fitness certificate, any required visas and vaccination records, and your signed employment agreement. Your agency's joining letter helps at immigration. Carry originals and keep digital copies.

Can I join a ship if my certificate expires during the contract?

Most companies will not send you, and an expired certificate on board can lead to a PSC deficiency. MLC lets a medical certificate that expires during a voyage stay in force until the next port with a recognised doctor, for up to three months. There is no general automatic extension for other certificates. Any dispensation is at the flag State's discretion and is exceptional, so revalidate before you travel.

How do I check my sign-on was recorded by DG Shipping?

Log in to your seafarer profile on DG Shipping's portal (now e-Samudra) and check your sea service entries. The RPSL or shipping company makes the entry. If the entry is missing, contact the RPSL or company first. If they cannot fix it, raise it with DG Shipping through its grievance or crew branch channels.

Do I get time to read my contract before signing on board?

Yes. MLC Standard A2.1 says seafarers must be able to examine and seek advice on the agreement before signing. Ideally you review it at the agency before departure. Do not sign terms on board that differ from what you were offered.

What is safety familiarisation when joining a ship?

It is ship-specific instruction on alarms, muster stations, escape routes, safety equipment and key procedures. STCW requires it before you are assigned any duties. It is recorded on a company checklist signed by you and your head of department.

Test yourself

Under SOLAS III/19, when must on-board training in the ship's life-saving appliances be given to a newly joined crew member?

SOLAS Chapter III, Regulation 19 requires LSA on-board training as soon as possible and not later than two weeks after joining. The 24-hour rule is about drills when more than 25% of the crew are new.

When must a newly joined officer receive ship-specific safety familiarisation?

STCW Regulation VI/1 and Section A-VI/1 require safety familiarisation before a person is assigned shipboard duties, whatever their previous experience.

Under MLC 2006, who must hold a signed original of the Seafarers' Employment Agreement?

MLC Standard A2.1 paragraph 1(c) requires the shipowner and the seafarer to each have a signed original of the SEA.

Your medical certificate expires mid-voyage. What does MLC Standard A1.2 allow?

MLC Standard A1.2 lets an expiring medical certificate continue until the next port of call with a recognised practitioner, for a period not exceeding three months.

For an Indian seafarer, who normally enters the sign-on into the DG Shipping online system?

In DG Shipping's online system the RPSL agency or shipping company records sign-on and sign-off (DG Shipping, crew matters). The seafarer should check that the entry appears on their profile.
