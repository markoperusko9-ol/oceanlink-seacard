e-Migrate registration for Indian seafarers: how it works and how to avoid being stopped at immigration

Quick answer

Indian seafarers do not normally register on the e-Migrate portal the way ECR workers do. In most cases your DG Shipping-licensed RPSL, or the Indian shipowner for Indian-flag ships, files your engagement as Form-I through the DG Shipping e-governance system. That data goes to e-Migrate and creates an ARN that immigration can check. Your part is to keep your DGS profile correct, join only through a valid RPSL, and carry the Form-I/ARN printout when you fly.

What e-Migrate actually means for a seafarer

The e-Migrate portal (emigrate.gov.in) is the Ministry of External Affairs system that runs the Emigration Act, 1983. Under Section 22 of that Act, an Indian citizen needs emigration clearance from a Protector of Emigrants before going abroad for work, unless they are exempt. Holders of a CDC, along with qualifying sea and deck cadets, are in the ECNR (Emigration Check Not Required) category. So a seafarer does not get the classic ECR emigration clearance stamp.

For seafarers, e-Migrate is used differently. DG Shipping launched the e-Migrate system for seafarers through Merchant Shipping Notice No. 7 of 2017. The aim was to stop Indians being recruited for foreign-flag ships by unlicensed manning agents. The DGS e-governance system sends engagement data straight to e-Migrate. Immigration officers at Indian airports and seaports can then see that you were placed through a legitimate channel.

The point to remember: DG Shipping's position is that seafarers recruited through unauthorised RPS agencies are liable to be stopped at immigration. e-Migrate is how that rule is enforced.

Who files what: Form-I and the proposed revised forms

The record created online is called Form-I. In most cases the recruiter or owner files it, not you. DG Shipping accepts this data through these channels:

Your situation Who files Form
Indian seafarer joining a foreign-flag ship RPSL company Form-I (Form-1 under the proposed 2025 revision)
Indian seafarer joining an Indian-flag ship Indian shipowner or RPSL Form-I (Form-1A under the proposed 2025 revision)
Non-seafarer travelling to a ship (surveyor, superintendent, etc.) Non-RPSL route set by DGS Form-1B under the proposed 2025 revision

Form-I holds:

  • name of the recruiting agency
  • vessel name, flag and IMO number
  • your name, INDoS number and CDC number
  • date of departure from India and date of sign-on

In November 2025 DG Shipping published a draft circular proposing a revised Form-1, Form-1A and Form-1B, with the e-Migrate ARN shown on the form. Check dgshipping.gov.in to see whether it has been issued in final form before relying on these form names. DG Shipping has also announced on its official X account that the e-Migrate ARN is being linked with the passport. Check the DGS e-Migrate service page for the current verification method. Form names and formats change, so check the latest Crew Branch circulars on dgshipping.gov.in before relying on any one version.

The 2017 notice also set up a limited third route for certain categories, where data is entered directly on the DGS website rather than by an RPSL. If you think you don't fit the RPSL or Indian-owner routes, ask the DGS Crew Branch before you book travel.

Step by step: from job offer to immigration counter

  1. Get your DGS profile in order. Register and update your seafarer profile on the DG Shipping e-governance system (check dgshipping.gov.in for the current portal name). Check that your INDoS, CDC, passport number, date of birth and name spelling match your documents exactly.
  2. Check that the recruiter is licensed. DG Shipping publishes the live status of every RPSL company: valid, invalid or temporarily blocked. If the agency is not valid on the DGS site, stop there.
  3. Check the ship. DGS has issued circulars that bar RPSLs from placing Indian crew on named foreign vessels with records of abandonment or unpaid wages. Search the latest DGS circulars for the vessel's name or IMO number.
  4. If you hold a foreign CoC, ask the RPSL to confirm in writing that the latest DGS circulars allow you to be engaged.
  5. Read and sign your SEA. Do this before the agency files your data.
  6. The RPSL or owner files Form-I on the DGS system with your final vessel and travel details. The system forwards it to e-Migrate and an ARN is created.
  7. Get a copy of Form-I showing the ARN. Check every field against your passport and tickets. Ask the RPSL how the ARN can be verified, and check the DGS e-Migrate service page for the current method.
  8. Carry it to the airport or seaport with your passport, CDC and joining papers. Show it at immigration if asked.

Documents to carry when you fly out to join

Your company's joining checklist comes first. For the e-Migrate side, these are the papers immigration officers usually look for:

  • Passport. It must be valid, and it must be the same passport whose number is on your Form-I.
  • CDC (Indian seaman's book). This is what puts you in the ECNR seafarer category.
  • Printed Form-I with the e-Migrate ARN, or the revised form if the proposed 2025 revision has been issued in final form.
  • Joining or appointment letter from the RPSL or owner, naming the vessel and port.
  • Visa or OK-to-Board letter, if the joining country requires one.
  • Signed SEA or a copy of the articles, as your company decides.
  • CoC, endorsements and STCW certificates. These are mainly for the ship and port state, but keep them in your hand luggage.

Keep a soft copy of everything as well. If an officer asks you to verify the ARN, the agency's operations contact should answer the phone. Get their number before you leave home.

Common reasons seafarers get stopped, and the fix

Problem at immigration Usual cause What to do
"No e-Migrate record found" The agency never filed Form-I, or filed it under a different passport number Call the RPSL straight away. They must file or correct it on the DGS system.
Name or date-of-birth mismatch DGS profile, CDC and passport don't match (initials, surname order) Correct the DGS profile and documents before your next job. A last-minute fix is often not possible.
Old passport on record Passport renewed but the DGS profile was not updated Update the profile and ask the RPSL to refile.
Recruiter not valid or blocked Licence expired, suspended or never issued Do not travel. Report it to DG Shipping.
Vessel on a DGS restricted or blacklisted list The ship is barred from employing Indian crew Do not join. Ask the agency for another vessel.

A "sub-agent" who says they will "manage immigration" without a valid RPSL licence is the oldest warning sign of a fake job.

Worked scenarios

Scenario 1: 3rd Officer, foreign-flag bulk carrier, joining in Singapore. The licensed RPSL files Form-I with the vessel's IMO number, flag, your INDoS and CDC, and your departure date. You get the printout with the ARN. At Mumbai immigration you show your passport, CDC and Form-I. The record matches your passport and you go through. This is the normal route.

Scenario 2: Flight rebooked by two days. If your travel date or port changes, tell the RPSL and ask whether the record needs updating. Don't assume the old printout will be accepted.

Scenario 3: A friend's contact offers a tanker job via WhatsApp. "No agency needed", visa arranged, pay the processing charge. No RPSL means no Form-I, and without a valid record immigration can stop you. Check the name on the DGS RPSL status list. If it isn't there, walk away and report it.

Scenario 4: Engine cadet, first ship, Indian-flag tanker. If you join an Indian-flag ship abroad (flying out of India), the Indian owner files the e-Migrate data (Form-1A under the proposed 2025 revision). If you join in an Indian port, sign-on is through the Articles of Agreement. Make sure your INDoS and CDC are active either way. New cadets are often caught out by a CDC that hasn't been issued yet.

Self-check before you leave home

Ask yourself these before you leave home. Every one of them has stopped seafarers at the airport.

  • Q: Who filed my Form-I, and is their licence valid today? A: Name the RPSL and confirm its status on the DGS website.
  • Q: Does the passport number on Form-I match the passport in my hand? A: It must match exactly. The e-Migrate record is matched to your passport details.
  • Q: Is the vessel on any DGS restricted list? A: You checked the latest circulars.
  • Q: Do my name and date of birth match on my passport, CDC, INDoS and DGS profile? A: Yes, character for character.
  • Q: If immigration asks, can someone verify my ARN right now? A: You have the agency's operations number and a soft copy.
  • Q: Am I ECR or ECNR, and why? A: As a CDC holder, ECNR. The e-Migrate record is a DGS recruitment control, not ECR emigration clearance.

The Ministry of External Affairs has also published a draft Overseas Mobility (Facilitation and Welfare) Bill, 2025 to replace the Emigration Act, 1983. If it becomes law, registration rules may change. Until then, follow the current DGS circulars.

Key points
  • Seafarers don't usually self-register on emigrate.gov.in. In most cases the RPSL or Indian shipowner files Form-I through the DG Shipping system.
  • The e-Migrate system for seafarers was launched by Merchant Shipping Notice No. 7 of 2017 to stop recruitment by unlicensed agents.
  • If you are engaged through an unregistered recruiter, you can be stopped at Indian immigration.
  • DG Shipping has announced that the e-Migrate ARN is being linked with the passport, so your DGS profile must show your current passport.
  • Check the RPSL's live status and the DGS vessel restriction circulars before accepting any job.
  • Carry your passport, CDC, the Form-I printout with ARN, and your joining letter at the airport or seaport.
  • Form names and procedures change, and a revised Form-1/1A/1B is only proposed in draft, so always check the latest Crew Branch circulars on dgshipping.gov.in.

Frequently asked questions

Do Indian seafarers need to register on emigrate.gov.in themselves?

Normally, no. In most cases your licensed RPSL, or the Indian shipowner for Indian-flag ships, files your engagement as Form-I on the DG Shipping e-governance system, and it goes to e-Migrate automatically. What you must do is keep your DGS profile accurate and make sure the form has actually been filed.

Is a seafarer with a CDC ECR or ECNR?

Under MEA guidance, CDC holders and qualifying sea and deck cadets are in the ECNR category, so you don't need the ECR emigration clearance that some workers need. The e-Migrate record for seafarers is a separate DG Shipping control on recruitment, and immigration checks it.

What is the e-Migrate ARN on my Form-I?

It is the Application Reference Number created when your engagement data reaches e-Migrate. DG Shipping announced on its official X account that the ARN is being linked with the passport. Check the DGS e-Migrate service page for the current verification method, and make sure the record relates to the passport you are actually travelling on.

Can I join a foreign ship through an agent who is not an RPSL?

In most cases data is filed by a licensed RPSL or an Indian shipowner. MS Notice 7 of 2017 also allows a limited self-entry route for certain categories, so confirm with the DGS Crew Branch if you are not joining through an RPSL or Indian owner. Seafarers recruited through unauthorised agencies are liable to be stopped at immigration, so check the recruiter's live status on the DG Shipping website before you sign anything or pay anyone.

What should I do if immigration says my e-Migrate record is not found?

Call the RPSL's operations contact at once and ask them to check, file or correct Form-I on the DGS system. The usual causes are a different passport number on record, changed travel details, or a form that was never submitted. Don't try to get through without a valid record.

Test yourself

Who normally creates the e-Migrate record for an Indian officer joining a foreign-flag ship?

Under Merchant Shipping Notice No. 7 of 2017, RPSLs file seafarer engagement data (Form-I) through the DGS system, and it goes to e-Migrate.

Which instrument launched the e-Migrate system for seafarers?

DG Shipping launched the e-Migrate system for seafarers through MS Notice No. 7 of 2017 to curb recruitment by unlicensed agents.

What emigration category is a CDC holder placed in under MEA guidance?

The MEA ECNR list under the Emigration Act, 1983 includes seamen who hold a CDC and qualifying sea and deck cadets.

Your passport was renewed last month and immigration finds no match for your ARN. What is the most likely cause?

The e-Migrate record is matched to your passport details, so the profile and Form-I must show your current passport. INDoS does not change with a new passport.

Before accepting a job, what is the best official check that the recruiter is legitimate?

DG Shipping publishes each RPSL's live status as valid, invalid or temporarily blocked. Only valid RPSLs can file Form-I.
