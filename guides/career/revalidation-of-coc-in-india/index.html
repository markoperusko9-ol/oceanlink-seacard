Revalidation of CoC in India: a practical guide for officers and engineers

Quick answer

An Indian Certificate of Competency is valid for five years. To revalidate it you must meet the continued-competence rules in STCW Regulation I/11, as applied in India by the Merchant Shipping (STCW) Rules, 2014 and DG Shipping's training and examination manual. In practice that means having qualifying sea service or equivalent service, completing the approved refresher and updating course, and applying online to the Mercantile Marine Department (MMD). Get the timing of the course and application right and the new five years can run from your old expiry date, so you lose no validity.

What revalidation is and when it falls due

DG Shipping issues CoCs for deck officers, engineers and ETOs with a validity of five years. Before you can carry on serving at sea in that capacity, you must show that you are still competent. That process is revalidation.

The international basis is STCW Regulation I/11. It requires every master, officer and radio operator to revalidate at intervals of no more than five years. To do so you must:

  • meet the medical fitness standards of Regulation I/9, and
  • establish continued professional competence under Section A-I/11 of the STCW Code.

India applies this through Rule 14 of the Merchant Shipping (STCW) Rules, 2014. The detail is set out in DG Shipping's training and examination manual and in its circulars.

Timing matters. DG Shipping's circular on refresher and updating training for revalidation states two things:

  • A CoC is normally revalidated for five years from the date you complete the refresher and updating course.
  • If you complete the course within six months before your CoC expires, the new five years run from the expiry date.

DG Shipping's FAQ phrases this as applying for revalidation within six months before expiry. To be safe, complete the course and apply inside the window.

If you do the course too early, you give away months of validity. If you leave it too late, you risk being stuck ashore with an expired certificate when a joining call comes. Older DG Shipping notices used different windows, so check the current position on dgshipping.gov.in before you book.

Eligibility: the sea-service routes

Section A-I/11 of the STCW Code sets out the ways to prove continued competence. DG Shipping's revalidation circular reflects the main ones:

Route What you need
Sea service (long) Approved seagoing service performing functions appropriate to the CoC held, at least 12 months in total in the preceding five years
Sea service (recent) At least three months in total in the six months immediately before revalidating
Equivalent service Shore service accepted as equivalent to seagoing service, such as qualifying service with a maritime administration or in marine-related roles. DG Shipping specifies what counts and for how long
No or partial sea time The STCW Code also allows an approved test, an approved course, or a period of approved supernumerary or lower-rank service. DG Shipping decides which applies and what extra training or assessment you need

Three practical points:

  • Rank matters. The service must suit the certificate you hold. A Class II deck CoC is revalidated on service as an officer, not as a rating.
  • Shore service is assessed case by case. If you have been a superintendent, a surveyor or a faculty member, do not assume it counts. Check the list in the current circular, or ask the MMD before applying.
  • Records must agree. DG Shipping's seafarer FAQ says service with ship-owning companies is acceptable if supported by documents. Make sure the service is supported by documents, and that your CDC sign-on and sign-off entries and testimonials all agree with each other.

Tanker endorsements, flag endorsements and other certificates

Revalidating your CoC does not automatically revalidate everything attached to it.

  • Tanker certificates (DCE / CoP). Regulation I/11 adds a separate five-yearly continued-competence check for masters and officers serving on tankers. Under Section A-I/11, paragraph 3, you need at least three months of approved service on the relevant tanker type in the previous five years, or approved training instead. DG Shipping handles revalidation of dangerous cargo endorsements as a separate online application. If you lack three months of service on that tanker type in the preceding five years, DG Shipping's FAQ says you must redo the relevant advanced tanker course(s) before revalidating a Level 2 DCE. For oil tankers, those courses are TASCO and LCHS. Check the current requirement for your tanker type.
  • Flag state endorsements. An endorsement issued under Regulation I/10 cannot outlast the national certificate it recognises. Once your Indian CoC is revalidated, apply for a fresh endorsement to the flag you serve under, such as Panama, Liberia or the Marshall Islands. Each flag has its own process and requirements.
  • GMDSS and other certificates. DG Shipping's FAQ says you can submit the GMDSS CoC application at the same time as the CoC revalidation, provided all the other requirements are complete.
  • STCW certificates of proficiency. Basic training, PSCRB, AFF and similar certificates have their own refresher cycles under Chapter VI. They are separate from the CoC course. Crewing departments and port state control officers will check them, so revalidate them together where you can.

Step-by-step: applying to the MMD

  1. Check eligibility. Add up your sea service from your CDC and discharge records against the routes above. Note your CoC expiry date and work backwards to find the six-month window.
  2. Book the approved refresher and updating course. Use only a DG Shipping-approved institute. Confirm that the institute uploads your course certificate to the DG Shipping database, because the MMD checks the course online.
  3. Update your seafarer profile. Log in to DG Shipping's online seafarer services, linked from dgshipping.gov.in. Check your name, date of birth, INDoS number, photo, signature and sea-service entries. The Directorate is moving services from its legacy e-governance system to a newer platform, so use whichever portal the website currently directs you to.
  4. Fill in the application. In the seafarer profile, choose the CoC and CoC revalidation option, enter the details and generate an application number.
  5. Upload scans of the supporting documents (see the checklist below).
  6. Pay the fee online through the portal's e-payment system. The portal shows the fee at the time of applying.
  7. Track the application and respond to queries. The MMD may ask for clarification or for a document to be uploaded again. Answer through the portal promptly.
  8. Collect the endorsement. DG Shipping's FAQ says you do not have to hand in your CoC hard cover. The MMD endorses a revalidation sticker and sends it to you, and you fix it in the CoC's additional pages. This is the process described in DG Shipping's FAQ. Check the current portal for how the revalidated CoC is now issued.

You do not have to apply through your training institute. DG Shipping's FAQ confirms you can apply to the MMD directly once you have finished the course.

Documents checklist

Requirements vary by department and by the route you are using, so check the list shown on the portal. Typically you will need:

  • your current CoC (relevant pages)
  • your CDC, with sign-on and sign-off entries covering the service you are claiming
  • your passport
  • the course certificate for the refresher and updating course
  • testimonials or a sea-service certificate from the company, where the CDC alone does not prove rank or ship type
  • for equivalent service, an employer letter that sets out your role and dates
  • a valid medical fitness certificate from a DG-approved doctor
  • your STCW certificates of proficiency, plus ECDIS and security certificates where relevant to your rank

According to DG Shipping's FAQ, an expired CDC or passport does not stop you applying to the MMD. You will still need both to be valid before joining a ship.

Common rejections and how to avoid them

Many delays are paperwork problems rather than eligibility problems:

  • The course is not showing online. The institute has not uploaded your certificate. Get confirmation from them before you apply.
  • Sea service is short or unverifiable. Typical causes are gaps in CDC entries, an unstamped sign-off, or service that cannot be verified. Collect testimonials before you leave the company.
  • Rank does not match. The service claimed is in a capacity that does not suit the CoC held.
  • Personal details do not match. Your name spelling or date of birth differs across your passport, CDC, CoC and INDoS record. Fix the profile first.
  • Scans are poor. Documents are blurred or cropped, or multi-page documents are uploaded as single images. Upload clear colour PDFs.
  • The course is stale. DG Shipping's notice on revalidation and up-gradation states that, for the course-based revalidation routes, courses attended more than 12 months before applying will not be accepted. Apply soon after you finish the course.
  • The tanker endorsement is forgotten. The CoC is revalidated but the DCE has expired, so a tanker company will not sign you on.

Worked scenarios

Scenario 1: well-timed second engineer. His CoC expires in November. He has 22 months of service as second engineer in the last five years. He does the refresher course in July, inside the six-month window, and applies online straight away. The revalidation runs five years from the November expiry date, so he loses nothing.

Scenario 2: early bird. A chief officer finishes the course ten months before expiry because she is between contracts. She meets the sea-service requirement, but the course is outside the six-month window. Her validity normally runs from the course completion date. Her new expiry is therefore about ten months earlier than if she had done the course inside the six-month window.

Scenario 3: shore stint. A master spent four years ashore as a marine superintendent and sailed only briefly. Before booking anything, he checks the current DG Shipping circular to see whether his shore role counts as equivalent service. He also asks the MMD whether he needs extra training, a test or supernumerary service. This saves him paying for a course that would not make him eligible on its own.

Scenario 4: tanker officer back from bulk carriers. His CoC revalidates without trouble, but he has had no oil tanker service in five years. Before rejoining a tanker he must redo the relevant advanced tanker courses (for oil tankers, TASCO and LCHS according to DG Shipping's FAQ). He must also revalidate his DCE separately. He checks the current requirement on the DG Shipping portal first.

Oral exam corner: what examiners like to ask

Revalidation itself has no oral exam, but examiners in higher-grade orals often test whether you understand it:

  • "Which STCW regulation covers revalidation, and what are its two limbs?" Regulation I/11. The two limbs are medical fitness under I/9 and continued professional competence under A-I/11.
  • "Give two ways to prove continued competence." Twelve months of service in five years, or three months in the last six months. You can also mention equivalent service, an approved test, an approved course, or supernumerary service.
  • "Is a tanker officer's revalidation any different?" Yes. There is an extra five-yearly check for tankers under A-I/11, paragraph 3.
  • "Can a flag endorsement outlast your national CoC?" No. Regulation I/10 links its validity to the underlying certificate.
Key points
  • Indian CoCs are valid for five years and must be revalidated under STCW Regulation I/11 and Rule 14 of the Merchant Shipping (STCW) Rules, 2014.
  • The main route is 12 months of approved service in the last five years, or three months in the last six months.
  • Complete the refresher and updating course and apply within six months before expiry so the new validity runs from the old expiry date.
  • Apply online through DG Shipping's seafarer services and pay the fee online, then check the current portal for how the revalidated CoC is issued.
  • Tanker endorsements (DCE) and flag endorsements need their own revalidation or renewal.
  • Common causes of delay include unverified sea service, courses not uploaded and mismatched personal details.
  • Fees, portals and windows change, so always check dgshipping.gov.in before applying.

Frequently asked questions

How long before expiry should I revalidate my CoC in India?

DG Shipping's circular says that if you complete the refresher and updating course within six months before expiry, the new five years run from the expiry date. Its FAQ phrases this as applying within six months before expiry, so complete the course and apply inside that window. If you do it earlier, validity normally runs from the course completion date. Check the current circular, because the window has changed over the years.

I don't have 12 months of sea time in five years. Can I still revalidate?

Possibly. Three months of service in the six months before revalidating also qualifies. Some shore service can count as equivalent, and STCW also allows an approved test, an approved course or supernumerary service. DG Shipping decides which of these you need, so ask the MMD before booking courses.

Do I need to send my original CoC to the MMD?

DG Shipping's FAQ says you do not have to hand in the hard cover. Under that process the MMD endorses a revalidation sticker and sends it to you, and you fix it in the CoC's additional pages. DG Shipping is moving to a new portal, so check there for how the revalidated CoC is now issued.

My passport or CDC has expired. Can I still apply?

Yes. DG Shipping's seafarer FAQ says a valid CDC or passport is not mandatory for an MMD application. You will still need both to be valid before you join any ship.

Does CoC revalidation also renew my DCE and Panama/Liberia endorsement?

No. Tanker endorsements have their own five-yearly competence requirement and a separate online application. A flag endorsement cannot outlast your CoC, so you must apply to each flag administration again after revalidating.

Test yourself

Which STCW regulation governs revalidation of certificates of competency?

Regulation I/11 sets out revalidation, while I/9 covers medical standards and I/10 covers recognition (flag endorsements). Ref: STCW 1978 as amended, Regulation I/11.

Which of the following alone meets the sea-service route for continued professional competence?

Section A-I/11 accepts 12 months in total in five years, or three months in the six months immediately before revalidation. Ref: STCW Code, Section A-I/11.

Your CoC expires in October and you complete the refresher course in June. From when is it normally revalidated?

A course completed within six months before expiry lets the validity run from the expiry date. Ref: DG Shipping circular on refresher and updating training for CoC revalidation.

A revalidated CoC holder's Liberian endorsement has expired. What is true?

An endorsement under Regulation I/10 cannot remain valid beyond the certificate it recognises, and only the flag issues it. Ref: STCW 1978 as amended, Regulation I/10.

What extra requirement applies to officers continuing to serve on tankers?

Regulation I/11 requires masters and officers on tankers to establish continued professional competence for tankers at intervals not exceeding five years, in accordance with Section A-I/11, paragraph 3 (three months' tanker service in five years or approved training). Ref: STCW 1978 as amended, Regulation I/11; STCW Code, Section A-I/11.
