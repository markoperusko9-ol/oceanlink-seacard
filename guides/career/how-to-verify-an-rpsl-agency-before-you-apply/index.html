How to verify an RPSL agency before you apply

Quick answer

Ask the agency for its RPSL number, then check it yourself on the official Directorate General of Shipping (DGS) website. Only deal with it if the status shows Valid and the details shown match what the agency told you. Agencies marked Invalid or Temporarily Blocked are off-limits, a licensed agency must not charge you for recruitment or placement, and the DGS has warned that sea service obtained through unlicensed agents may not be accepted.

What an RPSL is and why it protects you

RPSL stands for Recruitment and Placement Services Licence. In India it is issued under the Merchant Shipping (Recruitment and Placement of Seafarers) Rules, 2016. These rules put Regulation 1.4 of the Maritime Labour Convention 2006 into Indian law. Under the rules, nobody may recruit or place a seafarer in India for an Indian or foreign shipowner, directly or indirectly, without being registered and licensed.

The licence comes with conditions that protect you. The agency must lodge a bank guarantee with the regulator under the 2016 Rules (see the Rules for the amounts). Under MLC, a licensed agency must also have insurance or an equivalent arrangement to compensate seafarers for money they lose if the agency or the shipowner fails to meet its obligations to them. If you go through an unlicensed agent, none of this applies.

Where to check: the Directorate General of Shipping (DGS) publishes the RPSL list on its official website, dgshipping.gov.in. The 2016 rules were made under the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958, so watch the DGS crew circulars for any amendments or replacement rules.

Step-by-step: checking the licence on the official portal

  1. Ask for the RPSL number in writing. Licence numbers usually start with RPSL- followed by an office code. The exact format may vary. If the agency is reluctant to give the number, treat that as your first warning.
  2. Go to the official site yourself. Type dgshipping.gov.in into your browser and find the RPSL list under the Crew Division section. Menu names can change. Don't click a link the agent sends you. Third-party apps and blogs copy this list, but their copies can be out of date. Under DGS Circular 55 of 2025, the regulator's website shows each RPSL's status live.
  3. Check whatever details the portal shows. This may include the licence number, company name, city and validity. Make sure each one matches what the agent told you. The fields shown may vary.
  4. Read the status. It must say Valid. If the portal shows a validity or expiry date, make sure it runs past your expected joining date.
  5. Use contact details from an official source. Take them from the regulator's listing if it shows them, or from the company's official registered office. Don't use the mobile number on a WhatsApp forward. Contact the company and ask it to confirm that it is recruiting you.
  6. Screenshot the result with the date showing. Keep it with your other joining papers.

Do all of this before you send your CDC, passport, CoC or any money. The DGS advisory says plainly that you should not interact with an Invalid or Temporarily Blocked company at all: no documents, no offer letters, no payments.

Reading the result: what each status means

Status shown What it means for you
Valid The licence is in force. You can go on to the further checks below.
Invalid The licence is not currently valid. Do not deal with the company.
Temporarily Blocked The regulator has blocked the licence for now (the reason is not always shown). Do not deal with the company until it shows Valid again.
No match Recheck the spelling and the licence number. If the company still does not appear, treat it as unlicensed. It may be a sub-agent trading on someone else's licence.

One common excuse is "our licence is under renewal". The portal shows the status today, and that is the only status that counts. An agency that is still allowed to recruit will show as Valid. The regulator has also warned against licensed companies selling or sharing their RPSL with private agents. A valid number on a visiting card proves nothing unless the registered company itself confirms that it is recruiting you.

Checks beyond the licence number

A valid licence is where your checks start. Before you sign anything, also check the following:

  • No recruitment fees. Under MLC Standard A1.4, a licensed agency must not charge you, directly or indirectly, for recruitment or placement. The only costs you can be asked to meet are personal documents the law requires, such as your medical certificate, seaman's book and passport. Visas are the shipowner's cost. If someone demands a "processing fee", "security deposit" or "guaranteed-job package", walk away.
  • The vessel is not banned. The regulator publishes circulars listing foreign vessels that are blacklisted or restricted from employing Indian crew, mostly because of abandonment and unpaid wages. Licensed agencies are not allowed to place you on them. Check the IMO number yourself against the latest crew circulars.
  • Your SEA. Read the full Seafarer Employment Agreement before you sign. Check that the vessel name and IMO number are the same as the ones you were offered.
  • Joining papers. Check the current DGS crew circulars for any documents you must carry when you leave India to join a ship. Make sure they come from the licence holder and that your details on them are correct.
  • A real office. A licensed agency has a registered address. If you can, visit before you hand over original documents.

Red flags that should stop you

  • The agent contacts you only by WhatsApp or social media, uses a personal email address and has no landline.
  • They want money before you have an offer letter, or they say a payment "guarantees" you a job.
  • The RPSL number they quote belongs to a different company name or city on the portal.
  • They tell you to fly out and join a different ship from the one on your paperwork.
  • Joining papers that come from a company that is not the licence holder.
  • Pressure to sign a contract on the airport day without reading it.
  • They promise that your sea time will "count for your CoC". The DGS has warned that sea service obtained through unlicensed agents may not be accepted. Check the current position with the DGS before relying on it. If the service is not accepted, you could lose qualifying time for your Second Mate or MEO Class 4 exam.

Worked scenarios

Scenario 1: the cadet with a WhatsApp offer. A deck cadet gets a message offering a berth on a general cargo ship for a "training fee". The RPSL number given is real, but the portal lists it under a different company in another city. Correct action: stop and pay nothing. This looks like a borrowed or shared licence. Contact the real licence holder using details from an official source. If it denies any link with the agent, report it.

Scenario 2: the AB with a blocked agency. An AB is about to sign with an agency he has used before. When he checks the portal, it now shows Temporarily Blocked. Correct action: do not sign or send documents until the portal shows Valid again. A past good experience counts for nothing while the licence is blocked.

Scenario 3: the 3/O and the vessel switch. A third officer's offer is for Vessel A. At the hotel abroad, the agent tells him to join Vessel B instead. Correct action: refuse until the licensed agency issues a fresh SEA and joining papers for Vessel B, sent from its official email address. Check Vessel B against the blacklist circulars first.

Oral exam angle: questions examiners like

RPSL comes up in MMD orals and company interviews, usually under MLC and crew welfare. Have short answers ready:

  • Which MLC regulation covers recruitment and placement? Regulation 1.4 and Standard A1.4.
  • What may a seafarer lawfully be charged? Only the cost of the personal documents the law requires (medical certificate, seaman's book, passport and similar). Never a placement fee.
  • How does the agency protect the seafarer financially? Under MLC, through insurance or an equivalent system. In India, also through the bank guarantee lodged with the regulator.
  • Where do you verify a licence? On the RPSL list on the official DG Shipping website, using its live status.

Filipino seafarers: the same idea applies at home. Manning agencies in the Philippines are licensed by the Department of Migrant Workers (DMW), which took over this role from the POEA. Check the agency's licence status on the DMW's official website, or at a DMW office, before you submit documents. Processing details change, so confirm the current steps with the DMW directly.

If you have already paid or joined through an unlicensed agent

  • Stop all payments. Keep receipts, chat logs, bank transfer records and the agent's details.
  • Report it to the regulator. DGS Circular 55 of 2025 gives contact points for reporting fraudulent recruitment. Use those, or the Crew Division contacts on the official DGS website.
  • Use the national grievance route if needed. The Government of India's CPGRAMS portal takes complaints against central government bodies and on matters within their remit.
  • File a police complaint for cheating if you lost money. The regulator can act against a licensed RPSL, but it has limited power over agents with no licence.
  • If you are on board, contact a seafarers' union or welfare body, or the nearest Indian mission. Ask the master to help you keep copies of your SEA and wage accounts.
Key points
  • Under the 2016 rules, only an RPSL holder may recruit or place seafarers in India.
  • Check the licence yourself on the official DG Shipping website (dgshipping.gov.in), never through a link the agent sends.
  • Deal only with Valid agencies. Invalid or Temporarily Blocked means no documents, no offers and no payments.
  • Under MLC Standard A1.4, licensed agencies must not charge you recruitment or placement fees.
  • Check that the vessel is not on a regulator blacklist or restricted list before you sign.
  • Sea service obtained through unlicensed agents may not be accepted, so check the current position with the DGS.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I check whether an RPSL agency is genuine?

Check on the official Directorate General of Shipping website (dgshipping.gov.in), in the RPSL list under the Crew Division section. It shows each licence's status live. Check whatever details it shows, such as licence number, company name, city and validity, against what the agent told you.

Can an RPSL agency charge me a fee for a job?

No. Under MLC Standard A1.4, licensed agencies may not charge seafarers for recruitment or placement. The only costs you can be asked to pay are personal documents required by law, such as your medical certificate, CDC and passport. If an agency asks for a fee or deposit for the job itself, that is a serious warning sign.

What does 'Temporarily Blocked' mean on the RPSL list?

It means the regulator has blocked the licence for now, and the reason is not always shown. The DGS advisory tells seafarers not to share documents, accept offers or pay money to Invalid or Temporarily Blocked companies. Do not deal with the company until it shows Valid again, or use a different licensed agency.

Will my sea time count if I joined through an unregistered agent?

The DGS has warned that sea service obtained through unlicensed agents may not be accepted. Check the current position with the DGS before relying on it for your CoC exam eligibility. The safest course is to join only through a valid RPSL holder.

The agent says their licence is under renewal. Is that acceptable?

No. The status on the official portal today is the only one that counts. If the portal does not show Valid, the agency should not be recruiting you. Wait until it does.

Test yourself

Which instrument sets the international rules for seafarer recruitment and placement services?

MLC 2006 Regulation 1.4 and Standard A1.4 cover recruitment and placement. India applies them through the Merchant Shipping (Recruitment and Placement of Seafarers) Rules, 2016.

The portal shows an agency as 'Temporarily Blocked'. What should you do?

DGS Circular 55 of 2025 says there should be no interaction, document submission, offer acceptance or payment with Invalid or Temporarily Blocked RPSL companies.

Which of these may a licensed agency lawfully ask you to pay for yourself?

MLC Standard A1.4 lets national law allow seafarers to pay for required personal documents such as the medical certificate. Visas are the shipowner's cost, and placement fees are banned.

Under MLC Standard A1.4, how must a licensed recruitment and placement service protect seafarers against monetary loss if it or the shipowner fails its obligations?

MLC 2006 Standard A1.4 requires a system of protection, by way of insurance or an equivalent appropriate measure, to compensate seafarers for monetary loss.

An agent gives you a real RPSL number, but the portal lists it under a different company. What is the most likely explanation?

DG Shipping has warned against RPSL holders selling or sharing their licence with unauthorised agents. Confirm with the registered company through official contact details.
