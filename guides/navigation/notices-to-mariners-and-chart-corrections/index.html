Notices to Mariners and chart corrections: a practical exam guide

Quick answer

Notices to Mariners (NMs) are the hydrographic office's official updates that keep charts and nautical publications up to date, as SOLAS Chapter V requires. Permanent NMs go on the paper chart in waterproof ink. Temporary and Preliminary (T&P) notices go on in pencil. Every correction is recorded on the chart and in the correction log, and ENCs are updated through the ECDIS update service. Examiners expect you to know the weekly NM layout, the order of work, how to spot a missed correction, and the difference between NMs and navigational warnings.

Why charts must be corrected, and who does it

SOLAS Chapter V requires every ship to carry nautical charts and publications adequate for the intended voyage, and to keep them up to date. An uncorrected chart has several consequences:

  • It is a SOLAS deficiency that port State control may treat as grounds for detention.
  • It is a finding in a vetting inspection.
  • It is potential evidence of unseaworthiness in a casualty investigation.

On most ships the Second Officer is the navigating officer and handles chart and publication corrections. The exact split of duties is set by the company SMS, and the Master remains responsible overall. Cadets often do the cutting and pasting under supervision. Whoever applies a correction, the officer responsible must check it.

There are three separate streams of chart information. Keep them distinct in your head:

  • Notices to Mariners: the formal, scheduled updates to charts and publications.
  • Navigational warnings (NAVAREA, coastal/NAVTEX, local): urgent information broadcast before, or instead of, an NM. NAVAREA and coastal (NAVTEX) warnings are coordinated under the IMO/IHO World-Wide Navigational Warning Service. Local warnings are issued by port and harbour authorities to supplement them.
  • New Editions and New Charts: these replace the chart outright and incorporate all earlier NMs.

What is in the weekly Notices to Mariners

Most ocean-going ships use ADMIRALTY products, so this guide uses the UKHO system. Other hydrographic offices issue their own notices, for example the US NGA weekly NtM and national notices from India and many other States. Their layouts differ, so always read the explanatory notes of the edition you hold.

The ADMIRALTY weekly edition is published every week, numbered by week and year (for example Weekly 12/25). Individual notices are numbered in sequence through the year (for example 1500/25). The broad structure is:

Section Content
I Explanatory notes and an index of charts affected
II Updates to ADMIRALTY paper charts, including T&P notices
III Reprints of NAVAREA I warnings and lists of warnings in force
IV Updates to Sailing Directions
V Updates to the List of Lights and Fog Signals
VI Updates to the List of Radio Signals

The UKHO has adjusted the layout over the years, so check Section I of the copy on board rather than relying on memory.

Supporting publications:

  • Annual Summary of Notices to Mariners (NP247). Part 1 holds the Annual Notices, standing information such as distress, piracy and regulatory notes that are reissued every year. Part 2 summarises T&P notices in force and Sailing Directions updates.
  • Cumulative List of Notices to Mariners (NP234). Issued twice a year. It lists the current edition date of each chart and the NMs affecting it. This is your tool for proving a chart is fully corrected.
  • How to Keep Your ADMIRALTY Products Up-to-Date (NP294). The UKHO guidance on correcting technique.
  • Chart Correction Log and Folio Index (NP133A), or the company's electronic equivalent. This is the record of which charts are held and what has been applied.

Step-by-step: correcting a paper chart

  1. Receive and check the weekly NM. Confirm you have every week in sequence. A missing week means missing corrections.
  2. Use the Section II index to list the charts affected. Cross-check against your correction log to see which of those charts you actually hold.
  3. Check the chart edition. If a New Edition has been published, the NM does not apply to your old copy. Order the new edition and mark the old one as cancelled or superseded according to your SMS.
  4. Check the previous update. Each ADMIRALTY chart notice shows the number of the last NM that affected that chart. It must match the last correction recorded on your chart. If it does not, you have missed one, so stop and find it.
  5. Note the horizontal datum stated in the notice header. Positions in the NM refer to the chart's own datum.
  6. Apply the correction with the symbols from the chart symbols publication (NP5011 for ADMIRALTY charts):
    • Permanent NMs go in waterproof ink of the colour recommended in NP294.
    • T&P notices go in pencil.
    • Block corrections (chartlets) are cut to shape, aligned on the graticule and detail, and pasted down.
    • Deletions are struck through neatly. Never erase or obliterate the original.
  7. Record it on the chart. Write the year and NM number in the small-corrections space in the bottom-left margin. Permanent NMs go in ink and T&Ps in pencil.
  8. Record it in the correction log against the chart.
  9. Correct the publications from Sections IV to VI: lights, radio signals and sailing directions. Paste-in slips or e-NP updates are both acceptable, as your ship uses.
  10. Remove cancelled T&Ps. When a T&P is cancelled or replaced, rub out the pencil entry and its margin record.

Good practice is to start with the charts for the current and next voyage. Do not batch corrections until the port before arrival.

Worked example

Fictitious data for training only.

You hold Chart 9999. Its margin shows the last correction as 2024-3456. The Weekly NM 12/25 Section II index lists Chart 9999 against two notices.

NM 1500/25. Header: Chart 9999, previous update 3456/24, WGS84 datum. The previous update matches your margin, so no correction has been missed.

The notice says: insert a wreck symbol with a depth of 14.2 metres at 12°34'.50N, 072°10'.20E.

Method:

  • Take 34'.50 off the latitude scale at the side of the chart, in the same area as the position.
  • Take 10'.20 off the longitude scale.
  • Mark the intersection lightly in pencil.
  • Draw the correct wreck-with-depth symbol from NP5011 in ink.
  • Erase the pencil mark.

Then:

  • Margin: add 2025-1500 after 2024-3456.
  • Log: enter 1500/25 against Chart 9999.

NM 1520(T)/25. The notice says: a light buoy is temporarily established for survey work.

  • Plot the buoy in pencil.
  • Add (T) 2025-1520 in pencil in the margin.
  • Enter it in the T&P section of the log.

Weeks later the NM cancels 1520(T)/25. Erase both pencil entries and note the cancellation in the log.

Mismatch case. Suppose a later notice, NM 1800/25, gives previous update 1650/25, but your last ink entry is 1500/25. You have missed 1650/25.

  • Check NP234 or the UKHO online notices archive.
  • Retrieve 1650/25 and apply it.
  • Only then apply 1800/25.

Apply notices in number order so each previous update reference chains correctly.

ENCs and ECDIS

On ships using ECDIS as the primary means of navigation, the legal obligation still applies. The ENC portfolio must be up to date.

  • ENC updates arrive as update files from the chart service, by e-mail, download or media, according to the provider. They are loaded into ECDIS, usually every week.
  • After loading, check the ECDIS chart status or update report. Confirm that every cell in the folio is at the latest edition and update number, with nothing rejected. Note any errors.
  • T&P information on ENCs. Some ENC producers include T&P notices in the cell and some do not. Within the ADMIRALTY service, the ADMIRALTY Information Overlay can display T&P and other information that may be missing from the ENC. Know your system and display it during passage planning.
  • Manual updates. Navigational warnings not yet in an ENC update can be entered as manual updates or mariner's notes. Remove them when they expire or when the official update arrives.
  • Licences and permits. An expired licence or permit can leave cells unusable or not updated. Check validity before departure.
  • Back-up arrangements. Back-up paper charts, if carried, must be corrected to the same standard.

Common mistakes inspectors and examiners find

  • A missing weekly edition, so a whole week of corrections was never applied.
  • The previous update reference was never checked, so missed corrections go unnoticed.
  • T&P notices plotted in ink, or cancelled T&Ps still shown on the chart.
  • Corrections applied but not recorded in the margin or log, or recorded but not applied.
  • NMs applied to a superseded edition while the new edition sits unopened.
  • Publications forgotten. Charts are corrected but the List of Lights and List of Radio Signals are months behind.
  • Navigational warnings in force for the route not plotted, on the assumption that the NM will cover them.
  • ENC updates loaded without checking the update status report.
  • Chartlets pasted out of alignment, or the old detail scraped away rather than neatly struck through.

Oral exam questions you should be able to answer

  • What is the difference between a T&P notice and a permanent NM, and how is each applied? Permanent notices go in ink. T&Ps go in pencil and are removed when cancelled. Both are recorded in the margin and the log.
  • How do you know a chart is fully corrected?
    • The edition is current.
    • The previous update chain is unbroken up to the latest weekly NM.
    • The record agrees with NP234 and the log.
  • What is in the Annual Summary, and why read Part 1? It holds the Annual Notices, standing information that may not appear anywhere else in the weekly editions.
  • A New Edition is published. What happens to NMs issued before its date? They are already incorporated. Only later NMs are applied.
  • How does urgent information reach you before an NM? Through navigational warnings:
    • NAVAREA warnings via EGC (Inmarsat SafetyNET / Iridium SafetyCast).
    • Coastal warnings via NAVTEX, or via EGC where there is no NAVTEX coverage.
    • Local warnings from the port authority or VTS.
  • Where do you record a correction on an ADMIRALTY chart? In the bottom-left margin, as year and NM number, and in the correction log.
  • How do you keep ENCs corrected, and how do you prove it? By loading the weekly updates and checking the ECDIS update status report.
  • Which regulation requires up-to-date charts? SOLAS Chapter V, Regulation 27 requires charts and publications to be adequate and up to date. The carriage requirements are in Regulation 19.2.1.4. Expect to discuss this in terms of seaworthiness and the passage plan.

Detailed oral practice differs between administrations. Prepare from your own administration's syllabus as well as this list.

Key points
  • SOLAS Chapter V requires charts and publications to be adequate and kept up to date for the intended voyage.
  • Permanent NMs go on in waterproof ink. T&P notices go on in pencil and are removed when cancelled.
  • Always check the previous update number in each notice against the last correction on your chart.
  • Record every correction in the chart's bottom-left margin and in the correction log.
  • NMs issued before a New Edition date are already incorporated. Apply only later notices.
  • Navigational warnings carry urgent information. Plot those in force for your route; do not wait for the NM.
  • On ECDIS, load ENC updates weekly and verify the update status report. Know how your system shows T&P information.

Frequently asked questions

How often are Admiralty Notices to Mariners published?

ADMIRALTY Notices to Mariners are published in weekly editions, and the UKHO makes them available online. Individual notices are numbered in sequence through the calendar year. The Annual Summary (NP247) and the six-monthly Cumulative List (NP234) support the weekly editions.

What colour ink is used for chart corrections?

Permanent corrections are made in waterproof ink of the colour the UKHO recommends in NP294, How to Keep Your ADMIRALTY Products Up-to-Date. T&P notices are always inserted in pencil so they can be removed when cancelled. Company SMS instructions may add detail, so follow those as well.

What is a T&P notice?

A Temporary or Preliminary notice, marked (T) or (P) after the NM number. A Temporary notice covers a change that will be removed later, such as a survey buoy. A Preliminary notice gives advance warning of a change that will later be confirmed by a permanent NM or New Edition. Both are plotted and recorded in pencil and removed when cancelled.

How do I find a correction I have missed?

The previous update reference in each ADMIRALTY chart notice shows the last NM that affected that chart. If it does not match your chart, look up the chart in the Cumulative List (NP234) or the UKHO online notices archive to identify and retrieve the missing notice. Apply it before any later ones.

Do I still need Notices to Mariners if the ship uses ECDIS?

Yes. ENCs are corrected through update files rather than by hand, but you must still load them and verify the update status. You must also check how T&P information is shown and keep the publications corrected. Any back-up paper charts must also be corrected to the same standard.

Test yourself

How is a Temporary NM applied to a paper chart?

T&P notices are plotted and recorded in pencil so they can be erased when cancelled. Reference: UKHO NP294.

An NM for your chart shows 'previous update 1650/25', but your last recorded correction is 1500/25. What should you do?

A mismatch means a correction has been missed. Retrieve it via NP234 or the UKHO archive and apply it before the later notice. Reference: UKHO NP294; SOLAS Chapter V, Regulation 27.

A New Edition of a chart is published with a date in March. Which NMs must be applied to it?

A New Edition incorporates earlier notices, so only later NMs apply. Reference: UKHO NP294.

Where are the standing Annual Notices to Mariners found?

NP247 Part 1 contains the Annual Notices. NP234 lists chart editions and the NMs affecting them. Reference: UKHO NP247.

Which statement about ENC corrections is correct?

ENCs must be kept up to date by applying official updates and verifying the result. T&P inclusion varies by ENC producer. Reference: SOLAS Chapter V, Regulation 27.
