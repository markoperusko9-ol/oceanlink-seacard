ECDIS Safety Contour and Safety Depth: A Practical Guide for Deck Officers

Quick answer

The safety contour is the line ECDIS uses to separate safe from unsafe water. It drives the depth shading and the anti-grounding and route-check alarms. The safety depth only makes soundings equal to or shallower than your value stand out on the display. Both values come from your draft, squat and the company's under-keel clearance policy. ECDIS cannot draw a contour that does not exist in the ENC, so it moves up to the next deeper contour that the chart data actually contains.

What the two settings actually do

These two values are entered on the same settings page, so they are easily confused. In practice they do quite different jobs.

Safety contour - This is the depth contour ECDIS treats as the boundary between safe and unsafe water for your ship. - It is drawn as a highlighted line and forms part of the Display Base. Display Base is the minimum information that cannot be removed from the screen. - Depth area shading is built around it. Water shallower than the safety contour is shown in a blue 'unsafe' tint. - The IMO performance standards require an indication if a planned route crosses the safety contour, and an alarm during route monitoring if own ship will cross it within the set look-ahead time.

Safety depth - This only affects how spot soundings are displayed. - Soundings equal to or shallower than the safety depth are shown in a bold, prominent colour. Deeper soundings are shown in a fainter grey. - It does not draw a line and does not change the shading. - Under the performance standards, it is not the setting that generates the grounding alarm.

Spot soundings are not part of Display Base. You will only see them, and so only benefit from the safety depth highlighting, if the display category includes them. On most equipment this is 'All' or 'Other' with soundings switched on.

The ECDIS will normally ask for two more values: the shallow contour and the deep contour. Together with the safety contour, these give the four-shade depth display. Many officers set the shallow contour at or near the draft and the deep contour at a comfortably deeper value. Your company's procedures may specify both.

Why the contour you see is not always the value you typed

This is the point examiners probe most often.

An ENC only contains the depth contours the hydrographic office chose to encode. Typical intervals include values such as 5, 10, 15, 20, 30 and 50 m. Which contours are present depends on the producer, the area and the compilation scale of the cell.

ECDIS cannot invent a contour between two encoded ones. Here is what happens when you enter a safety contour value:

  1. ECDIS looks for an encoded contour equal to your value.
  2. If none exists, it uses the next deeper contour available in the chart data.
  3. If that deeper contour is missing from a particular cell, it moves up again to the next deeper one, and so on.

The safe-side consequences:

  • The displayed safety contour can change as you move from one cell to another, even though your setting has not changed.
  • In shallow or confined areas the displayed contour may sit well inside water that is actually deep enough for you. This produces alarms on a route that is genuinely safe.
  • If you enter no value at all, the IHO presentation standard provides a default of 30 m. That is sensible for a deep-draught ship in open water but meaningless in a river approach.

The safety depth behaves differently. It is compared directly against each sounding value, so it works to the decimal you enter.

Working out your values: step by step

The exact formula belongs to your company's SMS and passage planning procedures. Some companies add allowances that others do not, and some forbid using height of tide in the safety contour. The structure below is common across most fleets.

  1. Take the deepest static draft for the leg, allowing for trim. Use the draft you will actually have on that leg. A ballast passage and a laden passage need different settings.
  2. Add the expected squat at the planned speed in that water depth. Use the ship's squat data or the company's method. See the related guide on squat.
  3. Add the required UKC. This comes from the company policy, which is usually stated as a fixed figure, a percentage of draft, or both. Local port or pilotage rules may require more.
  4. Add any other allowances your SMS requires. Examples include wave response or heel in exposed water, and an allowance for chart accuracy based on the CATZOC (zone of confidence) of the survey data.
  5. Decide whether height of tide may be used. If your procedures allow it for a specific tidal window, subtract the predicted height of tide to get the required charted depth. Treat this with caution. The ECDIS shading has no idea of the time or the tide.
  6. Enter the result as the safety depth.
  7. Enter the same value, or the company-specified value, as the safety contour. Note which contour the ECDIS actually displays in each cell along the route.
  8. Run the route check. Investigate every alarm against the chart and your UKC calculation, and record how each alarm was resolved in the passage plan.
  9. Reset the values whenever the draft or the area changes and the settings are no longer appropriate. Examples are after loading or ballasting, or when entering a river or port area.

Worked example

The figures below are illustrative. Your own allowances will come from your SMS.

Ship and leg - Deepest static draft: 10.5 m - Squat at planned 8 knots in the approach channel: 0.6 m - Company minimum UKC for this area (example figure): 1.0 m - Chart quality allowance required by the SMS (example figure): 0.5 m

Required minimum depth = 10.5 + 0.6 + 1.0 + 0.5 = 12.6 m

Settings entered - Safety depth: 12.6 m. A sounding of 12.4 m appears bold, and a sounding of 13.0 m appears grey. - Safety contour: 12.6 m. The ENC contains 10 m, 15 m and 20 m contours, so ECDIS displays the 15 m contour as the safety contour.

The approach channel The channel is charted as a dredged area maintained to 13.5 m.

  • 13.5 m is deeper than the 12.6 m you need, so the channel is safe for this draft without counting on tide. ECDIS normally shades it as safe water.
  • The channel edges, however, border 10–15 m depth areas. These are shaded unsafe, and the displayed safety contour runs along their boundaries.
  • Where the route enters or leaves the channel, or where the look-ahead sector overlaps the channel edges, the ECDIS raises safety contour alarms.
  • Exact presentation depends on how the cell is encoded, so check it on screen.

The wrong fix is to drop the safety contour to 10 m so the alarms stop. The 10 m line is shallower than your 12.6 m requirement. The 10–15 m areas along the channel edges would then be shaded safe. A 12 m shoal patch just outside the channel would appear on the safe side, and the look-ahead would stay silent as you approached it.

The right approach has three parts: - Keep 12.6 m. - Verify the channel depth against the ENC and the latest notices or port information. - Document in the passage plan that the alarms on this leg have been assessed and why the leg is safe.

Watch the safety-depth soundings closely, because these are what reveal a 12 m patch. Some systems also let you mark no-go or caution areas manually, which helps here.

If the tide were needed Suppose the draft were deeper and the SMS allowed tide for this transit, with a predicted height of tide of 1.8 m. The required charted depth would be the required minimum depth less 1.8 m. The displayed contour would still be limited to the encoded contours. The tidal window and its basis must therefore be stated in the plan, not hidden in the ECDIS settings.

Shading, isolated dangers and settings that interact

  • Two-shade or four-shade. Two shades show only safe and unsafe either side of the safety contour. Four shades add the shallow and deep contours for more depth detail. Both are standard options.
  • Isolated dangers. A wreck, rock or obstruction shallower than your safety contour that lies inside otherwise safe water is shown with the isolated danger symbol. This symbol is part of the Display Base. Changing the safety contour changes which dangers qualify, which is another reason not to set it too shallow.
  • Shallow pattern. Some systems can add a hatched pattern over unsafe water to make it obvious at night or under dimmed palettes.
  • Overscale and SCAMIN. Zooming beyond the compilation scale does not add detail. SCAMIN can also hide objects at smaller display scales. Check critical areas at an appropriate scale.
  • Data quality. Shading is only as good as the survey behind it. Check CATZOC for the cells you rely on.
  • Raster charts (RCDS mode). Raster charts carry no depth data that ECDIS can interpret. The safety contour, depth shading and grounding alarms based on chart data do not function. You must compensate with manual methods and appropriate paper chart backup, in line with flag state acceptance.
  • Look-ahead settings. The crossing alarm depends on the look-ahead time or distance and on the sector you have set. A very short look-ahead gives warnings too late to act on.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving the 30 m default in coastal waters. This floods the screen with blue and causes alarm fatigue.
  • Setting the safety contour shallow to silence alarms. This is the classic grounding chain in accident reports.
  • Forgetting to change the values after loading, discharging or ballasting.
  • Believing the safety depth triggers the grounding alarm.
  • Not realising the displayed contour has jumped deeper, or that it differs between adjacent cells.
  • Using a display category that hides spot soundings, which makes the safety depth setting useless.
  • Applying height of tide in the ECDIS settings without stating the tidal window in the plan.
  • Accepting route-check alarms in bulk without checking each one.
  • Different officers using different settings on the same voyage because the values were never written into the passage plan and handover notes.

Oral exam questions you should be ready for

Examiners at OOW and Chief Mate level commonly ask questions like these. Answer briefly and correctly, then expand if asked.

  • What is the difference between safety contour and safety depth? The contour gives the line, the shading and the alarm. The depth only highlights soundings.
  • You enter 12.6 m. What does the ECDIS display? The next deeper contour encoded in the ENC, for example 15 m. It may differ between cells.
  • How do you calculate the values? Draft plus squat plus required UKC plus SMS allowances, less height of tide only if procedures permit.
  • Your route through a dredged channel gives safety contour alarms. What do you do? Verify the depths, confirm the UKC, and document the assessment. Never lower the setting below the requirement.
  • What is the default safety contour if none is selected? 30 m.
  • What is in the Display Base? Coastline, own ship's safety contour, isolated dangers in safe water, traffic routeing, scale and range, units, and display mode. Expect to be asked to list the main items.
  • What happens in RCDS mode? No automatic depth-based alarms, so manual safeguards and suitable backup are needed.
  • When would you change the settings during a voyage? On a change of draft, a change of area or UKC requirement, or on entering pilotage waters, as per the plan.
Key points
  • The safety contour drives the depth shading and the grounding and route-check alarms. The safety depth only highlights soundings at or shallower than its value.
  • ECDIS displays the next deeper contour encoded in the ENC when your exact value does not exist.
  • If no safety contour is set, the IHO default is 30 m, which is unsuitable for most coastal work.
  • Base both values on draft plus squat plus required UKC plus any SMS allowances.
  • Never lower the safety contour to silence alarms. Assess and document each alarm instead.
  • Update the settings whenever the draft or area changes, and record them in the passage plan.
  • Raster chart mode gives no depth-based alarms, so manual safeguards are essential.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between safety contour and safety depth in ECDIS?

The safety contour is a highlighted depth line that separates safe from unsafe water. It drives the shading and the anti-grounding and route-check alarms. The safety depth only changes how spot soundings look, making those equal to or shallower than your value bold. It draws no line and is not the basis of the crossing alarm.

What value should I set as the safety contour?

Generally use your deepest draft plus squat plus the required UKC and any allowances your SMS specifies. Some companies allow height of tide to be subtracted for a defined window and some do not. Follow your company's procedure and record the value in the passage plan.

Why does ECDIS show a safety contour deeper than the value I entered?

An ENC only contains the contours the hydrographic office encoded, for example 10, 15 and 20 m. If your value lies between two of them, ECDIS uses the next deeper available contour. This is conservative by design and may vary between adjacent cells.

Does the safety depth setting trigger a grounding alarm?

Not under the IMO performance standards. The crossing alarm is tied to the safety contour. The safety depth helps you spot shallow soundings visually, which is why you should display soundings in confined waters.

Do safety contour alarms work when using raster charts?

No. Raster charts are scanned images with no depth data that ECDIS can interpret, so there is no safety contour function or depth-based alarm. Use manual methods such as no-go areas, parallel indexing and close monitoring, and keep the backup arrangements your flag state accepts.

Test yourself

You set a safety contour of 12.6 m. The ENC has contours at 10 m, 15 m and 20 m. Which contour does ECDIS display as the safety contour?

ECDIS cannot create contours. It uses the next deeper contour available in the ENC data. Reference: IHO S-52 presentation rules applied under IMO Res. MSC.232(82).

What is the function of the safety depth setting?

Safety depth only changes how spot soundings are displayed. The line, shading and alarms belong to the safety contour. Reference: IHO S-52; IMO Res. MSC.232(82).

If the mariner does not select a safety contour, what default does ECDIS apply?

The IHO presentation standard specifies a 30 m default when no safety contour has been selected. Reference: IHO S-52.

Which of the following is part of the ECDIS Display Base and cannot be removed from the screen?

Own ship's safety contour is a Display Base item. Spot soundings, light descriptions and magnetic variation are in All Other Information. Reference: IMO Res. MSC.232(82), Appendix 2; IHO S-52.

When is the isolated danger symbol shown on ECDIS?

The symbol marks hazards shallower than the safety contour that sit inside water deeper than the safety contour. Reference: IHO S-52 conditional symbology.
