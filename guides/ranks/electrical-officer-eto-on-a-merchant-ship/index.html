Electrical Officer (ETO) on a Merchant Ship

Quick answer

The Electro-Technical Officer (ETO) is the ship's specialist for electrical, electronic and control systems. The ETO normally works days under the Chief Engineer and holds a certificate of competency under STCW Regulation III/6. Entry routes, sea-time rules and exam formats are set by each administration, so always check your own flag's or country's requirements.

What the ETO actually does

Since the 2010 Manila amendments to STCW, the ETO has been a recognised officer rank with its own certificate of competency. Before that, many ships carried an "electrical officer" or "electrician" whose qualifications were set by the company or the flag. You will still hear both titles on board. In this guide, ETO means an officer certificated under Regulation III/6.

The job covers everything that runs on electrons, from the main switchboard to the bridge console:

  • Power generation and distribution: generators, main and emergency switchboards, power management system, preferential tripping, shore connection, transformers and distribution boards.
  • Motors and drives: pumps, fans, compressors, steering gear motors, thrusters, winches and cranes, including starters, frequency drives and soft starters.
  • Automation and control: alarm and monitoring systems, PLCs, engine control systems, sensors and transmitters, safety shutdowns.
  • Bridge and navigation electronics: radar, gyro, autopilot, ECDIS hardware, echo sounder, speed log, AIS and VDR. This applies where company policy assigns them to the ETO. Some systems are only serviced by authorised shore technicians.
  • Safety systems: fire detection, emergency lighting, batteries and UPS, watertight door and fire door controls, public address and alarms.
  • Ship-type specialities: reefer containers on container ships, cranes and hatch covers on bulkers, cargo ramps on car carriers, cargo and gas detection systems on tankers, DP and thruster systems offshore, and high-voltage plant on cruise ships, LNG carriers and many large ships.

The ETO also keeps the planned maintenance records for electrical equipment, manages electrical spares and isolates equipment safely for other people's work.

Working pattern, reporting line and the team

Watch pattern. On most ships the ETO is a day worker, not a watchkeeper. Typical hours run from morning to late afternoon with the engine department. Hours of work and rest still fall under the flag's rules implementing STCW and MLC 2006. On unmanned machinery space (UMS) ships, the ETO is usually on call for electrical and automation alarms. Some companies also put the ETO on the duty roster. Call-outs at night count against rest hours and must be recorded honestly.

An ETO certificate does not by itself qualify you to keep an engineering watch. You can stand as officer in charge of an engineering watch only if you also hold the relevant III/1 certificate.

Reporting line. The usual set-up is:

Direction Who
Reports to Chief Engineer, with day-to-day work coordinated through the Second Engineer
Works closely with Deck officers on bridge equipment and cargo gear, the Chief Officer on cargo-related systems
Supervises (where carried) Electro-technical rating / electrician (STCW III/7), electrical cadet or trainee ETO

The exact arrangement is set by the company's Safety Management System and the ship's organisation chart. On large passenger ships there may be a team of several ETOs under a senior or chief ETO.

Is an ETO always carried? No. Whether an ETO is required depends on the ship's Minimum Safe Manning Document issued by the flag. Many ships carry one for operational reasons even where it is not required.

Certificates and competencies

The core certificate is the Certificate of Competency as Electro-Technical Officer under STCW Regulation III/6. The competence standard is in Section A-III/6. It applies to ETOs on seagoing ships with main propulsion machinery of 750 kW or more. The minimum age is 18.

The administration sets the rest:

  • the approved education and training programme
  • the onboard training and sea-service requirement
  • the written and oral examinations

These vary by country, so do not rely on another country's figures.

The A-III/6 standard is grouped under three functions:

  • electrical, electronic and control engineering
  • maintenance and repair
  • controlling the operation of the ship and care for persons on board

Other documents typically required or expected (check your flag and company):

  • Basic Safety Training: PST, FPFF, EFA and PSSR (Section A-VI/1)
  • Proficiency in Survival Craft and Rescue Boats (A-VI/2)
  • Advanced Fire Fighting (A-VI/3)
  • Medical First Aid (A-VI/4)
  • Security awareness or designated security duties (A-VI/6)
  • Valid medical fitness certificate (Regulation I/9 and MLC 2006)
  • Flag State endorsement when serving under a foreign flag
  • Basic tanker or gas training (Chapter V) where your duties on that ship type require it
  • High Voltage training: often asked for as a separate course certificate, even though HV content is part of the ETO standard
  • Engine Room Resource Management, and type-specific or maker courses the company requires

The ETO certificate is revalidated under Regulation I/11 like other CoCs. The administration defines the sea-service or alternative routes for revalidation.

How people reach the rank and where it leads

Common entry routes (details differ by administration):

  1. Graduate route. Many candidates hold an electrical, electronics or marine engineering diploma or degree.
  2. Pre-sea course. They then complete an administration-approved pre-sea ETO course.
  3. Onboard training. They sail as trainee ETO or electrical cadet with a training record book.
  4. Assessment. They then sit the administration's examinations for the III/6 certificate.

Rating route. An electrician or electro-technical rating (III/7) gains sea service and completes the approved training and exams for III/6.

Transitional cases. Experienced shipboard electricians certificated under older national systems may have had specific conversion arrangements. These were set by their administration.

Indian candidates will need an INDoS number and DG Shipping-approved courses. Filipino candidates follow MARINA's requirements. Read the current circulars rather than forum advice.

The next step up. STCW has only one level of ETO certificate. Progression comes through experience and responsibility, not a higher STCW grade:

  • Senior, chief or staff ETO: company titles, mainly on cruise ships, LNG carriers, offshore and DP vessels.
  • Specialist roles: HV, DP or automation specialist positions with offshore and cruise operators.
  • Shore roles: technical superintendent, service engineer with an equipment maker, or class or yard roles.
  • Crossing to engineering CoCs: some ETOs move to engineering certificates. Whether any ETO sea time counts towards a III/1 certificate is decided by the administration. Ask before you plan on it.

What crewing agencies look for in an ETO CV

Superintendents scan an ETO CV for evidence that you can keep the plant running without shore help. Put these up front:

  • CoC details: certificate type (III/6), issuing administration, number and expiry. Add flag endorsements held.
  • Sea service by rank, vessel type, flag, GT and kW, with exact sign-on and sign-off dates. Unexplained gaps raise questions.
  • System experience in plain terms:
    • HV or LV and system voltage
    • generator configuration and power management system
    • shaft generator
    • frequency drives
    • integrated automation system (make, if you know it)
    • reefer count on container ships
    • DP class and thruster type offshore
  • HV experience: switching, earthing and permits on HV plant. Say whether you held authorised person duties under the company's HV procedures.
  • Courses and validity: STCW basic and advanced courses, HV, ERM, tanker or gas training and any maker courses. Keep every date current. An expired course can stop a joining.
  • References and appraisals from Chief Engineers. Good reliefs and repeat contracts with one company count for a lot.
  • Medical, passport, seaman's book and visas valid well beyond the intended contract length.

Keep it to two pages and use one consistent date format. Do not inflate experience. A superintendent's technical interview will find it quickly.

Worked scenario: earth fault alarm at sea

It is 0200 on a UMS ship and the call bell rings: "440 V earth fault" on the main switchboard. Ship's LV systems are commonly insulated neutral. A single earth fault normally gives an alarm but does not trip the supply, so essential services keep running. A second fault on another phase would create a phase-to-phase short through earth, so the first fault must be found.

Typical approach (always follow your SMS and the Chief Engineer's standing orders):

  1. Report. Tell the duty engineer, and the Chief Engineer if required. Check the bridge is not affected.
  2. Read the indication. Check the earth lamps or insulation monitor to see which phase is affected and how low the reading is.
  3. Check recent changes. Ask what was started or switched recently. Wash-down areas, deck lighting, galley equipment and reefer sockets in rain are frequent culprits.
  4. Isolate one circuit at a time. Agree with the bridge and duty engineer before isolating anything. Begin with non-essential circuits and watch the monitor recover. Never isolate steering, navigation or propulsion-critical supplies without the bridge's knowledge and agreement.
  5. Once the faulty circuit is found, lock out and tag out, prove dead, and insulation-test the cable and the equipment separately.
  6. Repair or keep it isolated until it can be repaired safely. Then record the fault, readings and action in the electrical log and PMS.

This is a favourite oral exam and interview scenario because it tests system knowledge, safe isolation and communication together.

Oral exam and interview questions to prepare

Examiners and superintendents ask these often. Be ready to answer in your own words.

  • What conditions must be met before closing a generator breaker onto a live board? Voltage, frequency, phase sequence and phase angle must all match. In practice the incoming machine runs slightly fast and you close just before the synchroscope reaches the in-phase position.
  • What does the reverse power trip protect? It stops a generator being driven as a motor by the busbar (for example after a prime mover fault or loss of fuel). This protects the prime mover from damage and prevents the generator from drawing power from the other sets.
  • What is preferential tripping? On generator overload, non-essential loads are shed in stages to keep essential services supplied.
  • How do you insulation-test a motor safely? Isolate, lock and tag, and prove dead. Disconnect electronics that the test voltage could damage. Test phase-to-earth and phase-to-phase. Discharge after the test and record the readings.
  • How is the emergency source started and tested? Expect questions on automatic start, transfer to the emergency switchboard and SOLAS Chapter II-1 Part D principles. Also cover battery checks and the ship's test schedule.
  • What makes HV work different? You need permit-to-work, isolation and earthing under the company's HV safety rules, by authorised persons only, and must never assume a cable is dead.

Practical tips on board:

  • Learn the single-line diagram in your first week.
  • Label spares properly.
  • Photograph terminations before you disturb them.
  • Never bypass a safety device without a written, authorised override.
Key points
  • The ETO is an STCW officer rank certificated under Regulation III/6, with the competence standard in Section A-III/6.
  • Most ETOs are day workers who report to the Chief Engineer and are on call for electrical and automation alarms.
  • An ETO certificate alone does not qualify you to keep an engineering watch.
  • Whether an ETO must be carried depends on the flag's Minimum Safe Manning Document.
  • Training routes, sea-service rules and exams are set by each administration, so check your own.
  • STCW has one ETO level; progression is to senior, specialist or shore roles.
  • A strong CV lists the actual systems handled: HV, power management, drives, automation, reefers and DP.

Frequently asked questions

Is an Electro-Technical Officer the same as a ship's electrician?

Not exactly. The ETO is an officer certificated under STCW Regulation III/6. A ship's electrician or electro-technical rating is a rating, and the rating certificate is under Regulation III/7. Some companies still use 'electrical officer' or 'electrician' loosely, so check what certificate the position actually requires.

Does an ETO keep watches in the engine room?

Usually not. The ETO is normally a day worker on call for electrical and automation alarms, though company duty rosters vary. To stand as officer in charge of an engineering watch you need the relevant Regulation III/1 certificate as well.

What is the promotion path after ETO?

STCW has only one ETO certificate level, so there is no higher STCW grade to sit. Progression is through company roles such as senior or chief ETO, specialist HV, DP or automation positions, or shore jobs like superintendent and service engineer. Some ETOs move to engineering certificates, but sea-time credit for that is decided by the administration.

Do I need a separate high voltage course as an ETO?

High voltage content is part of the ETO competence standard. Many companies and some administrations still expect a separate HV course certificate, especially for ships with HV plant. Check the company's requirements and your administration's rules before joining.

Can an ETO get a job on a tanker or gas carrier without tanker courses?

On tankers and gas carriers, STCW Chapter V training is required for seafarers whose duties relate to cargo or cargo equipment. In practice most companies require at least the basic tanker or gas course for ETOs. The exact requirement depends on your duties, the flag and the company SMS.

Test yourself

Under which STCW regulation is the Electro-Technical Officer certificate of competency issued?

Regulation III/6 covers the ETO; III/7 is the electro-technical rating and III/1 the engineering watch officer. Reference: STCW Convention, Regulation III/6 and Code Section A-III/6.

On an insulated-neutral 440 V ship's system, what normally happens when a single earth fault occurs on one phase?

An insulated neutral system tolerates a single earth fault with an alarm, keeping essential services running. A second fault on another phase can cause a short circuit, so the first must be found. Reference: SOLAS Chapter II-1 Part D principles on electrical installations.

What is the main purpose of a reverse power relay on a ship's generator?

Reverse power protection trips the breaker if power flows into the generator, stopping it acting as a motor. Load shedding is the job of preferential tripping. Reference: STCW Code Section A-III/6 knowledge of generator protection.

In the usual shipboard organisation, to whom does the ETO report?

The ETO normally reports to the Chief Engineer, with daily work coordinated through the Second Engineer. The exact arrangement is defined in the company SMS. Reference: ISM Code, Section 3 (company responsibilities and authority).

What must be done before insulation-testing a motor with a megger?

Insulation testing applies a high test voltage, so the circuit must be safely isolated and proven dead first. Sensitive electronics must be disconnected. Reference: STCW Code Section A-III/6 (maintenance and repair, safe working practices).
