Messman on a merchant ship: role, certificates and career path

Quick answer

A messman is an entry-level catering rating who serves meals, cleans the messes, pantries and galley, and helps the cook. The rank needs STCW basic training, security awareness, a valid seafarer medical certificate and identity documents. Some administrations or companies also require a food-handling or catering course. With experience and the right qualifications, the usual next step is steward or second cook, and later chief cook.

What a messman actually does

The messman is the backbone of the catering department. On most cargo ships the department is small: a chief cook and one or two messmen or stewards. On some ships one person does both the messman and steward work. Titles vary by company and crewing market. You will see messman, mess boy, general steward (GS) and utility.

Typical daily duties:

  • Meal service: laying tables, serving breakfast, lunch and dinner in the officers' and crew messes, and clearing away.
  • Pantry and dishwashing: washing crockery, cutlery and galley utensils, and keeping pantries stocked with tea, coffee, bread and snacks for the night watches.
  • Cleaning: messes, pantries, galley decks, alleyways and common rooms. On many ships this also includes the Master's and senior officers' cabins and the hospital. Exact areas are set by the company and the chief cook.
  • Galley support: washing and cutting vegetables, basic preparation and keeping work surfaces clean, all under the cook's direction.
  • Laundry and linen: issuing, collecting and washing bed linen and towels.
  • Provisions: helping take stores on board, stowing them in dry stores, chiller and freezer rooms, and rotating stock so older items are used first.
  • Garbage: segregating galley and mess waste according to the ship's garbage management plan.

Small habits make a big difference here. Keep cold room doors closed. Report a fridge running warm. Never leave a wet galley deck.

Working pattern, reporting line and emergency duties

A messman is a day worker, not a watchkeeper. The day is built around meal times. That usually means an early start to prepare breakfast and a finish after dinner is cleared, with breaks between meals. Port days, provisioning and inspections can stretch the day.

Hours of rest still apply to you. Under both the MLC and STCW, you must have:

  • at least 10 hours of rest in any 24-hour period, and
  • at least 77 hours in any 7-day period.

Rest may be split into no more than two periods, and one of them must be at least 6 hours long. Under the MLC some flag states use maximum hours of work instead of minimum hours of rest. However, because you have designated safety duties on the muster list, the STCW minimum rest hours still apply to you. Either way, your hours must be recorded honestly on the ship's rest-hour sheets. Sign them only if they are correct.

Who you report to:

Level Role
Direct supervisor Chief cook (or chief steward on larger ships)
Department oversight Master, or an officer the Master delegates. Often the chief officer handles provisions and inspections, but this varies by company.
Reports to the messman Normally nobody. Messman is an entry rank.

Emergency duties: your name is on the muster list like everyone else. Catering ratings are often assigned to tasks such as:

  • the first-aid or stretcher party,
  • bringing blankets and extra provisions to the survival craft,
  • closing galley ventilation, or
  • checking accommodation.

Your exact duty is on the muster list and your lifejacket or cabin card. Learn it during familiarisation on your first day.

Certificates and documents you need

Requirements come from the STCW Convention, the MLC 2006 and your flag state. Exact course approvals, validity rules and local extras differ by administration. Check with your national maritime authority and the crewing agency.

Core STCW requirements (every seafarer with designated safety or pollution-prevention duties):

  • Basic training, made up of four courses:
    • Personal Survival Techniques (PST)
    • Fire Prevention and Fire Fighting (FPFF)
    • Elementary First Aid (EFA)
    • Personal Safety and Social Responsibilities (PSSR)
  • You must show continued proficiency in PST and FPFF every five years. Your administration sets how this is done, usually through a refresher course.
  • Ship security training:
    • Security awareness is needed for all seafarers.
    • If the company gives you designated security duties, such as gangway watch in port, you need the higher Seafarers with Designated Security Duties certificate instead.

MLC and identity documents:

  • A valid seafarer medical fitness certificate. Under the MLC this is valid for a maximum of two years, or one year if you are under 18.
  • Passport and seaman's book (continuous discharge certificate). Indian seafarers also need an INDoS number.
  • A signed seafarer employment agreement.

Catering-specific:

The MLC requires catering staff to be properly trained or instructed for their positions. It sets formal qualification requirements for the ship's cook, not the messman. However:

  • some administrations and many companies require a food safety, food hygiene or catering ratings course for messmen, and
  • some charterers and cruise operators have their own requirements.

Trade-dependent: a yellow fever vaccination certificate (where trading areas require it) and, for some routes, crew visas such as the US C1/D.

Hygiene and garbage: where messmen get caught out

Port State Control officers and flag inspectors look closely at galleys, cold rooms and garbage. The MLC requires frequent documented inspections of food and water supplies, storerooms and the galley. These are carried out by or under the authority of the Master. As the messman, your work is what they see.

Practical standards to keep:

  • Wash hands before handling food and after cleaning toilets or handling garbage.
  • Keep raw and cooked food separate, and use the colour-coded chopping boards if the galley has them.
  • Label and date opened items. Use older stock first.
  • Know the cold room man-trapped alarm and how the inside door release works before you go in alone.
  • Report any illness, especially stomach upsets, to the cook or officer before handling food.

Garbage under MARPOL Annex V:

  • Discharge of plastics into the sea is prohibited everywhere. This includes plastic packaging, cling film and synthetic ropes.
  • Food waste discharge is allowed only under specific conditions of distance from land and ship's position, and it is more restricted in special areas.
  • Cooking oil is a separate garbage category with its own disposal route.
  • Follow the ship's garbage management plan and the bin labels exactly. If in doubt, ask. Never decide on your own to throw something overboard.

Worked scenario: a crew member mixes food scraps with plastic wrappers in one bag.

  1. Do not put the bag in the food waste bin or the comminuter.
  2. Separate the contents, wearing gloves.
  3. Put the plastics in the plastics bin.
  4. Tell the cook (and the responsible officer if needed) so the garbage is disposed of correctly and the garbage record stays accurate.

Mixed garbage must be treated under the stricter category's rules.

How to get in, and where you go next

Entry routes:

  • Direct entry after completing basic training, security training, the medical and the documents above. Some companies or administrations also require a catering course.
  • Hotel and restaurant experience ashore is a real advantage. Hospitality graduates often join this way.
  • Cadetship or trainee programmes run by some companies and training institutes for catering ratings.

Typical progression:

  1. Messman / general steward
  2. Steward or second cook: more cooking responsibility and sometimes provision accounts.
  3. Chief cook: requires a ship's cook certificate or equivalent recognised by your flag state. Under the MLC, a ship's cook must be at least 18.
  4. Chief steward or catering officer: mainly on larger crews, passenger ships and offshore units.

Sea time requirements and course content for each step differ by administration and company. Keep every discharge entry and sign-off record clean.

Moving to another department: some messmen move across to deck or engine as an ordinary seaman or wiper. Catering sea time does not usually count towards a watchkeeping rating certificate. Check the specific rules of your administration before planning on it.

What crewing agencies look for in a messman CV

Agencies screen fast. Make the important things easy to find.

  • Certificates table with certificate number, issuing authority and expiry date:
    • basic training (all four courses)
    • security certificate
    • medical
    • passport
    • seaman's book
    • any food hygiene or catering course
  • Sea service table listing:
    • vessel name and type (bulk, tanker, container and so on)
    • flag and GRT/GT
    • rank
    • sign-on and sign-off dates
    • crew nationality mix
  • Shore experience: hotel, restaurant or institutional kitchen work, with dates.
  • Language: working English is expected. Mention other languages, which help on mixed crews.
  • References: chief cooks or Masters who will confirm your work.
  • Clean record: no unexplained gaps or early sign-offs. If a contract ended early, give a short honest reason.

A warning: under the MLC, recruitment and placement services must not charge seafarers fees for finding them a job. Be wary of anyone asking for money to guarantee a vessel. Check that the agency is licensed by your national authority.

Interview questions and practical tips

Interviews for messmen are usually short and practical. Expect questions like these.

  • "How do you segregate garbage on board?"
    • Follow the garbage management plan and bin labels.
    • Plastics never go overboard.
    • Food waste goes only as permitted.
    • Cooking oil is kept separately.
  • "What is your muster station duty if the general alarm sounds while you are serving lunch?"
    • Stop service and secure the galley if your duty requires it.
    • Collect your lifejacket and go to your station.
    • Do the task on your muster card.
  • "How many hours of rest must you get?"
    • At least 10 in any 24 hours and 77 in any 7 days.
    • No more than two rest periods, one of at least 6 hours.
  • "What do you do if you find the freezer temperature high?"
    • Tell the cook or responsible officer immediately.
    • Keep the door closed.
    • Do not refreeze thawed food without the cook's decision.

Practical tips from experience:

  • Learn the names and meal habits of the crew in the first week. A good messman is noticed quickly.
  • Wear safety shoes in the galley. Burns, cuts and slips are common catering injuries.
  • Secure everything before bad weather: pots, crockery, chairs and stores.
  • Ask the cook to teach you one dish at a time. That is your path to second cook.
  • Keep copies of every certificate and discharge in your bag and in digital form.
Key points
  • A messman is an entry-level catering day worker who reports to the chief cook, with the Master overseeing the department.
  • Basic training (PST, FPFF, EFA, PSSR), security training, a valid medical certificate and identity documents are the core requirements.
  • Some administrations and companies also require a food hygiene or catering course; check locally.
  • MLC and STCW rest hours apply to you: at least 10 in any 24 hours and 77 in any 7 days.
  • Never discharge plastics into the sea under MARPOL Annex V; follow the garbage management plan exactly.
  • The usual progression is messman, then steward or second cook, then chief cook with a recognised ship's cook certificate.
  • Recruitment agencies must not charge seafarers placement fees under the MLC.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need STCW certificates to work as a messman?

Yes. Messmen normally have designated safety duties on the muster list, so they need STCW basic training and the security training appropriate to their duties. You also need a valid seafarer medical certificate. Some administrations and companies add a food hygiene or catering course, so check with your maritime authority and agency.

Is a messman a watchkeeping rating?

No. A messman is a day worker whose hours are organised around meal times, not bridge or engine watches. The MLC and STCW rest-hour rules still apply and must be recorded. You also have assigned emergency duties on the muster list.

How can a messman become a ship's cook?

Gain sea time as messman and then steward or second cook, learning from the chief cook. Then obtain a ship's cook certificate or equivalent recognised by your flag state. Under the MLC, a ship's cook must be at least 18. Sea time and course requirements depend on your administration.

What is the difference between a messman and a steward?

Both are catering ratings, and on small crews one person may do both jobs. Generally the messman focuses on serving, cleaning and dishwashing. The steward may take on more responsibility for cabins, linen, stores or cooking support. Exact duties depend on the company and ship.

Can a messman switch to the deck or engine department?

It is possible, but catering sea time usually does not count towards watchkeeping rating certificates. You would normally need to meet the deck or engine rating requirements of your administration, including the relevant sea service and training. Plan with your agency and check your national rules first.

Test yourself

What is the minimum rest a messman must have in any 24-hour period under the MLC and STCW?

At least 10 hours of rest in any 24 hours and 77 hours in any 7 days are required (MLC Standard A2.3; STCW Code Section A-VIII/1).

Under MARPOL Annex V, what is the rule for plastic galley waste such as cling film and packaging?

Discharge of all plastics into the sea is prohibited (MARPOL Annex V, Regulation 3), and mixed garbage takes the stricter rule under the Annex.

Which section of the STCW Code sets the standard for basic training (PST, FPFF, EFA, PSSR)?

Basic safety training is covered by STCW Code Section A-VI/1. A-II/1 is for deck officers, A-VIII/1 is fitness for duty and A-III/4 is for engine watch ratings.

Under the MLC, who carries out or authorises the documented inspections of food, water, storerooms and galley?

Frequent documented inspections are carried out by or under the authority of the Master (MLC Standard A3.2).

What is the minimum age for employment as a seafarer under the MLC 2006?

The MLC sets 16 as the minimum age for seafarers. Some work, such as ship's cook and night work, is restricted for those under 18 (MLC Standard A1.1, Standard A3.2).
