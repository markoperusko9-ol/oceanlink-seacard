Pumpman on a Merchant Ship: Duties, Certificates and Career Path

Quick answer

The pumpman is a senior rating on oil, product and chemical tankers who operates and maintains the cargo pumps, pipelines, valves and tank cleaning gear. During cargo operations they usually work under the Chief Officer, and for maintenance under the engine department. At minimum they need STCW basic safety training and tanker basic training. Many companies and administrations expect more, so check the flag, the company SMS and the charterer's requirements.

What a pumpman actually does

The pumpman is the hands-on specialist for the cargo system. Most of the job is keeping the cargo plant ready, so that loading, discharging and tank cleaning go to plan without leaks, trips or delays.

Typical duties include:

  • Cargo pumps: preparing, running, monitoring and maintaining them. On crude carriers and many large product tankers these are often steam turbine-driven centrifugal pumps in a pump room. On most chemical and many product tankers they are individual in-tank pumps. These are either submerged hydraulically driven pumps, or deepwell pumps with an electric or hydraulic motor on deck.
  • Stripping and draining: stripping pumps, eductors and line draining, so that the ship completes discharge with minimum ROB.
  • Pump room routines: bilge checks, gland and seal inspection, bearing temperatures, and ventilation running. Fixed gas detection and alarms are monitored from the cargo control room, but anyone in the pump room must leave immediately and report if an alarm sounds. The pumpman is often the first to notice something wrong.
  • Valves and pipelines: hydraulic valve actuators, the remote valve control power pack, pressure/vacuum valves, manifold blanks, gaskets and strainers.
  • Tank cleaning and COW: rigging and maintaining tank cleaning machines, and assisting with crude oil washing where it is fitted.
  • Submerged pump checks: on submerged pumps, carrying out cofferdam purging and reporting the findings.
  • Cargo heating: heating coil checks, steam traps and coil pressure testing.
  • Ballast system: pumps and valves, which on many tankers share the cargo control arrangements.
  • Hoses and manifold: hose and reducer handling, manifold connection and disconnection, sampling, and drip tray management.
  • Inert gas system: assisting with deck seal, non-return valves and pressure/vacuum breaker checks, as directed by officers.

The exact split of tasks between the pumpman, the fitter, the bosun and the engineers depends on the company and the ship type. Read your job description in the SMS on joining.

Reporting lines and working pattern

Who you report to:

Situation Usual line manager
Cargo, ballast, tank cleaning operations Chief Officer, or the duty officer in the cargo control room
Machinery maintenance on cargo pumps and drives Chief Engineer or 2nd Engineer, as the company assigns
Day-to-day deck work outside cargo ops Chief Officer, often coordinated through the bosun

Some companies place the pumpman firmly in the deck department, some in the engine department, and some split it as in the table. Your contract and the ship's organisation chart settle it.

Who reports to you: usually nobody formally. You will often direct an AB, OS, oiler or wiper assisting with a job, so you need to brief and supervise them clearly.

Working pattern:

  • At sea, the pumpman is normally a day worker, with overtime when maintenance demands it.
  • In port, during cargo operations, the pumpman is often on call around the clock. Expect to be called for pump start-ups, stripping, line changes and problems.
  • Rest hours still apply. Under STCW and MLC, the minimum is 10 hours' rest in any 24-hour period and 77 hours in any 7-day period. Exceptions are only allowed within the strict limits those instruments set.
  • On short-haul product and chemical trades with frequent ports, managing fatigue is a real part of the job. Plan your rest with the Chief Officer before a long discharge, not halfway through it.

Certificates and competencies

Requirements are set by the flag administration and then built on by the company and the charterer. What follows is the general STCW framework, not any one country's list.

STCW minimum for tanker cargo duties:

  • Basic safety training under STCW Section A-VI/1.
  • Security training under STCW Section A-VI/6, at the level matching any security duties you are assigned.
  • Basic Training for Oil and Chemical Tanker Cargo Operations (STCW Regulation V/1-1 and Section A-V/1-1). This is required for seafarers assigned specific duties related to cargo or cargo equipment on oil or chemical tankers.
  • The resulting certificate of proficiency. Some administrations issue it as a tanker or Dangerous Cargo Endorsement.

Advanced tanker training:

  • STCW requires advanced oil or chemical tanker training for those with immediate responsibility for cargo operations.
  • Whether a pumpman must hold it varies by administration and company.
  • Many operators prefer or require it, especially on chemical tankers. Check before you apply.

Watch rating certificate: many pumpmen hold one of these, and some companies require it:

  • Rating Forming Part of an Engineering Watch (A-III/4)
  • Able Seafarer Engine (A-III/5)
  • The deck equivalents

Other documents:

  • Valid medical fitness certificate
  • Seafarer's identity and discharge book
  • Passport
  • Any visas the trade needs

Practical competencies employers expect:

  • Understanding centrifugal and positive displacement pump behaviour, including cavitation, NPSH and loss of suction.
  • Dismantling and reassembling pumps, seals and valves to the maker's manual.
  • Reading pipeline and P&ID drawings.
  • Basic hydraulics, and steam where turbines are fitted.
  • Gas testing with portable instruments.
  • Enclosed space and pump room entry procedures.
  • Permit to work discipline.
  • Welding and fitting skills are a bonus on many ships.

Worked scenarios from the job

Scenario 1: cofferdam purge shows cargo. On a chemical tanker with submerged hydraulic pumps, you purge a pump cofferdam and get cargo out instead of just a little air or hydraulic oil.

  1. Stop and contain it.
  2. Note the volume, appearance and which pump.
  3. Inform the Chief Officer straight away. Cargo in the cofferdam points to cargo seal leakage.
  4. Check the cargo's hazards on the safety data sheet before handling the drained liquid.
  5. Follow the pump maker's instructions on purge frequency and the limits for continued operation.
  6. Log the result. A trend of rising leakage is what lets the ship plan a seal change rather than suffer a failure.

Scenario 2: pump room entry before a routine check.

  1. Confirm the pump room ventilation has been running as the SMS requires.
  2. Test the atmosphere with a calibrated portable instrument, at different levels, especially the bottom.
  3. Get the duty officer's permission and agree a communication method.
  4. Log the entry as the company procedure requires.

The fixed gas detection system does not replace a personal check before entry. Leave immediately if an alarm sounds or you feel unwell.

Scenario 3: losing suction near the end of discharge.

Pump discharge pressure starts hunting and the motor or turbine load fluctuates. That points to the pump losing suction.

  1. Reduce the rate as instructed by the officer in charge.
  2. Trim and list to assist draining, if the Chief Officer agrees.
  3. Change over to stripping.

Running a pump dry or cavitating for long damages seals and bearings. Good pumpmen hear it before the gauges show it.

How people become pumpmen and where they go next

Common routes in:

  • Engine ratings (motorman, oiler, fitter) with tanker experience. Pump and machinery skills transfer directly.
  • Deck ratings (AB) on tankers who have worked closely with cargo gear and shown aptitude.
  • Fitters moving across within the same tanker company. This is a common path because companies like promoting known people.

There is no single STCW "pumpman certificate". Promotion is usually a company decision based on:

  • Tanker sea time
  • The basic tanker certificate of proficiency or endorsement
  • Appraisals
  • Often an internal assessment or interview by the marine or technical superintendent

Next steps after pumpman vary:

  • Senior pumpman on larger or more complex tankers. Examples are VLCCs, or parcel chemical tankers with many segregations.
  • Studying for an engineering CoC (OICEW) or, less often, a deck CoC. Whether your rating sea time counts towards it, and how much, depends entirely on the administration. Check with your administration before you plan around it.
  • Shore roles. These include terminal or tank cleaning work, cargo surveying, and service work with pump manufacturers. They usually call for extra qualifications or employer-specific training.

What crewing agencies look for in a pumpman CV

  • Tanker type and size, clearly listed: crude, product or chemical, and the size (for example VLCC, Aframax, MR). Chemical and crude experience are not interchangeable in a manager's eyes.
  • Pump systems you have actually worked on: steam turbine pump room plant, submerged hydraulic pumps or deepwell pumps. Name the maker where you can, and say whether the drive was hydraulic or electric.
  • Specific jobs done: seal changes, pump overhauls, valve actuator repairs, heating coil repairs, COW or tank cleaning operations. This is far stronger than "maintained cargo pumps".
  • Valid documents with expiry dates: tanker certificate of proficiency or endorsement, STCW basic training, medical, and any advanced tanker training.
  • Continuous record with the same operators: tanker operators value retention and clean appraisals.
  • Vetting awareness: experience on ships trading under oil-major vetting. Inspectors may talk to crew about pump room safety and procedures, so confidence here counts.
  • References or appraisals from Chief Officers and Chief Engineers.

Keep it to two pages, put the sea service table first, and make sure the dates match your discharge book exactly.

Oral-exam style questions to prepare for

Superintendents and senior officers often test a pumpman candidate verbally. Be ready to answer:

  • "Why does a centrifugal pump lose suction near the end of discharge, and what do you do?" Cover falling suction head, vapour and air entrainment, reducing the rate, trim, and changing to stripping.
  • "What precautions before entering the cargo pump room?" Cover ventilation, atmosphere testing, permission, communication, the SMS procedure, and why gases collect low down.
  • "What does cargo in a cofferdam purge tell you?" Cargo seal leakage. Report it, trend it, and follow the maker's limits.
  • "Which trips and alarms protect a turbine-driven cargo pump?" Typical answers are overspeed, low lube oil pressure, and high casing or bearing temperature. Then add "as per the ship's specific plant", because arrangements differ.
  • "Why isolate and lock out before working on a pump?" Explain energy isolation (hydraulic, electrical, steam), draining and gas-freeing lines, and the permit.
  • "What is your role in a cargo spill at the manifold?" Raise the alarm, stop the operation as directed, contain the spill using the SOPEP equipment, and follow the Chief Officer's orders.

Answer from what you have actually done on board. Examiners can tell rehearsed theory from experience.

Key points
  • The pumpman runs and maintains the cargo pumps, pipelines, valves and tank cleaning gear on oil, product and chemical tankers.
  • During cargo operations the pumpman usually reports to the Chief Officer; for maintenance, often to the engine department, depending on the company.
  • Basic Training for Oil and Chemical Tanker Cargo Operations, evidenced by a certificate of proficiency or endorsement issued by the administration, is the core STCW requirement.
  • Whether advanced tanker training is required varies by administration and company, so check before applying.
  • Never enter the pump room without ventilation, a personal atmosphere test and permission, whatever the fixed gas detection shows.
  • The STCW and MLC minimum rest hours still apply during long cargo operations.
  • A strong CV names the tanker types, sizes and pump systems you have actually worked on.

Frequently asked questions

Is pumpman an officer or a rating?

A pumpman is a senior rating, not an officer. They usually rank alongside the bosun and fitter and do not hold a certificate of competency as an officer. The exact place in the hierarchy depends on the company's organisation chart.

Which certificate do I need to work as a pumpman on a tanker?

As a minimum you need STCW basic safety training and Basic Training for Oil and Chemical Tanker Cargo Operations. This is evidenced by a certificate of proficiency, which some administrations issue as a tanker or Dangerous Cargo Endorsement. Many companies and some administrations also expect advanced oil or chemical tanker training. A watchkeeping rating certificate is often wanted too, so check the specific requirements.

Do LNG and LPG ships carry a pumpman?

Practice varies. Many gas carriers use a cargo or gas engineer, often an officer, to look after cargo machinery, while some LPG operators do carry a pumpman. The pumpman rank is most common on oil, product and chemical tankers.

How do I get promoted from fitter or oiler to pumpman?

Build tanker sea time and get your basic tanker certificate of proficiency or endorsement. Volunteer for cargo pump and valve maintenance jobs, and ask for this experience to be noted in your appraisals. Promotion is usually a company decision, often after an interview or assessment by a superintendent.

Can a pumpman become an engineer officer?

Yes, some pumpmen go on to study for an engineering watchkeeping CoC. Whether rating sea time counts, and how much, depends on your administration's rules and any training programme it requires. Confirm with your administration or an approved training institute before planning around it.

Test yourself

During a cargo discharge, who does the pumpman normally take operational instructions from?

The Chief Officer is responsible for cargo operations under the Master, and the pumpman acts on their instructions or those of the duty officer. This follows the ship's SMS organisation under the ISM Code.

Which STCW training is the core requirement for a rating assigned cargo equipment duties on an oil or chemical tanker?

STCW Regulation V/1-1 and Section A-V/1-1 require basic tanker training for seafarers with specific duties related to cargo or cargo equipment on these ships.

The fixed gas detection panel shows normal readings in the pump room. What must you do before entering?

A fixed system does not replace a pre-entry atmosphere check with a portable instrument (SOLAS XI-1/7 requires ships to carry suitable instruments) and the company enclosed space entry procedure in the SMS.

Purging the cofferdam of a submerged cargo pump produces cargo liquid. What does this most likely indicate?

Cargo in the cofferdam points to cargo seal leakage. It must be reported to the Chief Officer and assessed against the pump maker's limits.

What are the minimum rest hours for a seafarer under STCW and MLC?

STCW Code Section A-VIII/1 and MLC 2006 Standard A2.3 set a minimum of 10 hours' rest in any 24 hours and 77 hours in any 7 days.
